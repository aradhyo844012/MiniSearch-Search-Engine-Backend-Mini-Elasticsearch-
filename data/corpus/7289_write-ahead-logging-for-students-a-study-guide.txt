Write-Ahead Logging for Students: A Study Guide

This study guide prepares you for exams, labs and interviews that cover Write-Ahead Logging.

Learning goals:
1. Explain Write-Ahead Logging in your own words.
2. Describe log records, checkpoints, crash recovery, and fsync.
3. Give examples from surviving crashes and feeding replication streams.
4. Name three problems to watch for.

Start with the definition: Write-Ahead Logging is a technique that records changes in a log before they are applied to the main data. Then draw writing a plan in a notebook before changing the real blueprint on paper and label the four terms from the goals above.

Self-test questions: Why is disabling fsync for speed a problem? How does checkpoints relate to crash recovery? When would you choose PostgreSQL WAL or SQLite journal?

Study habit worth keeping: test crash recovery by killing the process on purpose.

When you feel ready, compare Write-Ahead Logging with Connection Pooling and write down three differences.
