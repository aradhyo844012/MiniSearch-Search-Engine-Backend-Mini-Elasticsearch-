How to Learn Decision Trees in 30 Days

You do not need months to become comfortable with Decision Trees. A focused plan of thirty days is enough to build a solid base. Put simply, Decision Trees covers models that make predictions by asking a sequence of simple questions about the data. Learning it well pays off across many areas of computing.

Week 1: learn splits and entropy and Gini impurity, and write a one-page summary.
Week 2: practice with scikit-learn on a small task such as classifying customers.
Week 3: build a mini project around explaining predictions.
Week 4: review common mistakes such as deep trees that overfit, and explain Decision Trees to a friend.

A model is only as good as the data used to train and test it.

In practice you can start with scikit-learn, then explore XGBoost or Graphviz as your needs grow.

In short, careful study of Decision Trees pays off. Spend time on splits and entropy and Gini impurity, and the rest will follow.
