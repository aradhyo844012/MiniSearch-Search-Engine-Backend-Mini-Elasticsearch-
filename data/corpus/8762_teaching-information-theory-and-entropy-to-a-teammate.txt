Teaching Information Theory and Entropy to a Teammate

Explaining Information Theory and Entropy to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: measuring how surprised you are by each new message. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Information Theory and Entropy is the mathematics of measuring and compressing information. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving data compression, naming bits and entropy as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain compression limits in Information Theory and Entropy back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, confusing information with meaning, and suggest this habit: compute the entropy of a short text by hand.
