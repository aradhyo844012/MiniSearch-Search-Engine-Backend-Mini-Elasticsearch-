Network Troubleshooting for Students: A Study Guide

This study guide prepares you for exams, labs and interviews that cover Network Troubleshooting.

Learning goals:
1. Explain Network Troubleshooting in your own words.
2. Describe protocol layers, ping and traceroute, DNS checks, and packet captures.
3. Give examples from fixing office connectivity and diagnosing slow apps.
4. Name three problems to watch for.

Start with the definition: Network Troubleshooting is a step-by-step process for finding out why a network connection fails or is slow. Then draw a doctor who checks the heart, lungs and blood pressure in order on paper and label the four terms from the goals above.

Self-test questions: Why is changing several things at once a problem? How does ping and traceroute relate to DNS checks? When would you choose ping or traceroute?

Study habit worth keeping: work from the bottom layer upward.

When you feel ready, compare Network Troubleshooting with Wi-Fi Networks and write down three differences.
