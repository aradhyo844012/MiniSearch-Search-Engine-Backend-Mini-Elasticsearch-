LSM Trees vs Graph Databases: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study LSM Trees or Graph Databases first. Both sit in the area of Storage Engines and Databases, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

LSM Trees is storage structures that buffer writes in memory and merge them into sorted files on disk, and it is closely tied to memtable and sorted string tables. Graph Databases, by contrast, is databases that store data as nodes and relationships and query connections directly, and it is closely tied to nodes and edges.

Choose LSM Trees first if you care about write-heavy databases or time-ordered data. Choose Graph Databases first if you care about social networks or recommendation engines.

Think of LSM Trees as a mail room that sorts incoming letters in batches rather than one by one, and of Graph Databases as a city map where intersections are records and roads are relationships. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With LSM Trees, avoid read amplification. With Graph Databases, avoid using them for simple tabular data.

A good habit for LSM Trees is to watch compaction metrics before blaming the application. A good habit for Graph Databases is to limit traversal depth in every exploratory query.
