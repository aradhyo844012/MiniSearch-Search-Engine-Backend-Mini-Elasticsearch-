Teaching Combinatorics to a Teammate

Explaining Combinatorics to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: counting the ways to seat guests at a table. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Combinatorics is the art of counting arrangements and selections. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving analyzing algorithms, naming permutations and combinations as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain the pigeonhole principle in Combinatorics back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, double counting, and suggest this habit: test counting formulas on tiny cases first.
