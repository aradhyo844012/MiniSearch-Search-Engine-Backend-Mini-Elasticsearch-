A Beginner's Guide to Site Reliability

This guide is written for readers who are new to Site Reliability and want a clear starting point. If you build software for long enough, you will run into Site Reliability. The term refers to the discipline of keeping services dependable using engineering practices.

Automation turns risky manual steps into repeatable and reviewable processes.

To understand Site Reliability, start with service level objectives. Then move on to error budgets, and finally look at incident response and postmortems. A good way to remember service level objectives is to connect it with a concrete example from your own code.

People use Site Reliability for running production services, for reducing outages, and for planning on-call rotations.

In practice you can start with PagerDuty, then explore Prometheus or runbooks as your needs grow.

Beginners often stumble on three things. First: alerting on everything. Second: blaming people instead of systems. Third: no written postmortems.

Practice service level objectives with small exercises until it feels natural. Automate repetitive steps so that results are easy to reproduce. Read the official documentation for Prometheus; it often explains error budgets better than blog posts do.

Keep experimenting, keep notes, and revisit Site Reliability whenever a new project gives you a reason.
