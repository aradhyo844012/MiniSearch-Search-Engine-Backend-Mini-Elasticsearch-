Combining Statistical Significance with Data Anonymization in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Statistical Significance and Data Anonymization can work together in a single project.

Statistical Significance is a measure of how unlikely a result would be if there were no real effect. Data Anonymization is removing or altering personal details in data so people cannot be identified.

Imagine a project that involves judging experiment results and sharing datasets with researchers. Statistical Significance brings p-values, while Data Anonymization brings masking.

A sensible plan is to start with SciPy for the Statistical Significance part and ARX for the Data Anonymization part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: p-hacking on one side, and removing too little on the other.

Picture the whole as asking how surprised you should be if a coin were actually fair, working alongside blurring faces in a crowd photo while keeping the scene.

Two habits help: first, always report the effect size alongside the p-value; second, test whether combining fields could identify a person.
