Teaching Statistical Significance to a Teammate

Explaining Statistical Significance to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: asking how surprised you should be if a coin were actually fair. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Statistical Significance is a measure of how unlikely a result would be if there were no real effect. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving judging experiment results, naming p-values and the null hypothesis as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain confidence intervals in Statistical Significance back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, mistaking significance for importance, and suggest this habit: always report the effect size alongside the p-value.
