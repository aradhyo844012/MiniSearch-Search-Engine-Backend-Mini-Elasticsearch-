Relational Databases vs Redis Caching: Key Differences

Relational Databases and Redis Caching both belong to the area of Databases, but they solve different problems. This article compares them side by side so you can choose with confidence.

The term Relational Databases refers to systems that store data in tables with rows, columns and defined relationships. Its core ideas are tables and rows, primary keys, and foreign keys.

The term Redis Caching refers to using an in-memory data store to keep frequently needed results close to the application. Its core ideas are key-value pairs, expiration times, and eviction policies.

The main difference is focus. Relational Databases puts the emphasis on tables and rows and primary keys, while Redis Caching puts the emphasis on key-value pairs and expiration times.

Reach for Relational Databases when your task involves storing orders and customers or reporting and analytics. Reach for Redis Caching when your task involves caching API responses or storing session data.

Both have risks. With Relational Databases, watch for this: skipping constraints. With Redis Caching, watch for this: serving stale data.

In practice you do not always have to choose. Many projects use Relational Databases alongside Redis Caching, with tools such as PostgreSQL and Redis. Most slow pages in data-driven applications trace back to a handful of slow queries.
