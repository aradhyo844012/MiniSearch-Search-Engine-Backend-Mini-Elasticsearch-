Combining Autoscaling with Log Aggregation in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Autoscaling and Log Aggregation can work together in a single project.

Autoscaling is automatically adding or removing computing resources as demand changes. Log Aggregation is collecting logs from many machines into one searchable place.

Imagine a project that involves handling traffic spikes and troubleshooting across services. Autoscaling brings scaling metrics, while Log Aggregation brings shippers.

A sensible plan is to start with Kubernetes HPA for the Autoscaling part and Elasticsearch for the Log Aggregation part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: flapping because of short spikes on one side, and unstructured logs on the other.

Picture the whole as a restaurant opening extra tables when a crowd arrives, working alongside gathering notes from every desk into a single searchable binder.

Two habits help: first, load test the system to find realistic thresholds; second, use structured JSON logs so that fields are searchable.
