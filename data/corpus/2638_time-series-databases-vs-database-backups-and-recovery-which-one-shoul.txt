Time-Series Databases vs Database Backups and Recovery: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Time-Series Databases or Database Backups and Recovery first. Both sit in the area of Storage Engines and Databases, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Time-Series Databases is databases optimized for storing and querying data points ordered by time, and it is closely tied to timestamps and retention policies. Database Backups and Recovery, by contrast, is the practice of copying data regularly so that it can be restored after failures, and it is closely tied to full and incremental backups and recovery point objective.

Choose Time-Series Databases first if you care about server metrics or sensor readings. Choose Database Backups and Recovery first if you care about recovering deleted tables or migrating servers.

Think of Time-Series Databases as a long logbook where every line starts with the time, and of Database Backups and Recovery as a spare house key kept at a trusted neighbor's home. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Time-Series Databases, avoid high-cardinality tags. With Database Backups and Recovery, avoid never testing restores.

A good habit for Time-Series Databases is to include a time range in every query. A good habit for Database Backups and Recovery is to schedule a restore drill on a regular basis.
