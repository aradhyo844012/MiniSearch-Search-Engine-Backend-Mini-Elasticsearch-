Combining Push Notifications with Offline-First Apps in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Push Notifications and Offline-First Apps can work together in a single project.

Push Notifications is messages sent to a user's device from a server even when the app is closed. Offline-First Apps is apps designed to work without a network and sync when a connection returns.

Imagine a project that involves order updates and field service tools. Push Notifications brings device tokens, while Offline-First Apps brings local storage.

A sensible plan is to start with Firebase Cloud Messaging for the Push Notifications part and SQLite for the Offline-First Apps part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: sending too many messages on one side, and unbounded local data on the other.

Picture the whole as a courier who knocks on your door when a package arrives, working alongside writing in a notebook and mailing the pages later.

Two habits help: first, ask for permission at a moment when the user understands the value; second, show users which changes have not synced yet.
