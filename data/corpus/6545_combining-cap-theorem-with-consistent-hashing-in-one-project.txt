Combining CAP Theorem with Consistent Hashing in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how CAP Theorem and Consistent Hashing can work together in a single project.

CAP Theorem is a result stating that a distributed system cannot guarantee consistency, availability and partition tolerance all at once. Consistent Hashing is a way of mapping keys to servers so that adding or removing servers moves very few keys.

Imagine a project that involves choosing database types and distributed caches. CAP Theorem brings consistency, while Consistent Hashing brings hash ring.

A sensible plan is to start with Apache Cassandra for the CAP Theorem part and Redis Cluster for the Consistent Hashing part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: ignoring latency trade-offs in normal operation on one side, and uneven load on the other.

Picture the whole as a phone tree that must choose between giving old information or staying silent when a line is cut, working alongside seats around a round table where a new guest only displaces their neighbor.

Two habits help: first, ask what your users prefer during a failure: stale answers or errors; second, use many virtual nodes per server to even out the load.
