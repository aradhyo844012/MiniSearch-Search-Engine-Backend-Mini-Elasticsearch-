Data Anonymization Explained Simply

If Data Anonymization sounds intimidating, relax. It is removing or altering personal details in data so people cannot be identified, and the idea is simpler than the name suggests.

Think of Data Anonymization as blurring faces in a crowd photo while keeping the scene. Keep that picture in mind as you read on.

The vocabulary has four main pieces: masking, generalization, k-anonymity, and differential privacy. You do not need to master them all at once. Start with masking, then add generalization.

People reach for Data Anonymization when they work on sharing datasets with researchers, testing with production-like data, and meeting privacy rules.

One practical tip is to test whether combining fields could identify a person.
