Encryption at Rest vs Single Sign-On: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Encryption at Rest or Single Sign-On first. Both sit in the area of Application Security, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Encryption at Rest is protecting stored data by encrypting it on disk or in a database, and it is closely tied to symmetric keys and key management. Single Sign-On, by contrast, is letting users log in once and access several applications, and it is closely tied to identity provider and SAML.

Choose Encryption at Rest first if you care about protecting backups or meeting compliance rules. Choose Single Sign-On first if you care about company intranets or reducing password fatigue.

Think of Encryption at Rest as a locked safe inside a locked room, and of Single Sign-On as a festival wristband that opens every gate. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Encryption at Rest, avoid storing keys next to the data. With Single Sign-On, avoid a single point of failure.

A good habit for Encryption at Rest is to keep keys in a separate service from the data. A good habit for Single Sign-On is to plan for identity provider outages.
