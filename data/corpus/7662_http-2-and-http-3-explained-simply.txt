HTTP/2 and HTTP/3 Explained Simply

Here is HTTP/2 and HTTP/3 without the jargon: it is newer versions of the web protocol that load pages faster over fewer connections.

Think of HTTP/2 and HTTP/3 as a multi-lane highway replacing a single-lane road. Keep that picture in mind as you read on.

The vocabulary has four main pieces: multiplexing, header compression, QUIC, and server push. You do not need to master them all at once. Start with multiplexing, then add header compression.

People reach for HTTP/2 and HTTP/3 when they work on faster page loads, improving mobile performance, and reducing connection overhead.

One practical tip is to keep a working HTTP/1.1 fallback.

After HTTP/2 and HTTP/3, a natural next topic is Reverse Proxies.
