Hexagonal Architecture in Real Projects

Reading about Hexagonal Architecture is useful, but the ideas make more sense in context. These three scenarios show how it appears in real projects.

Scenario one: testable business logic. Here, ports drives the main design choices, and the team needs to guard against too many layers for small apps.

Scenario two: swapping databases. This time adapters matters most, and tools such as Spring help keep things manageable.

Scenario three: supporting several interfaces. The key idea is the core domain, and the biggest risk is leaking framework types into the core.

Across all three scenarios, Hexagonal Architecture stays the same idea: a design that keeps core logic separate from outside systems through ports and adapters. Only the details change.
