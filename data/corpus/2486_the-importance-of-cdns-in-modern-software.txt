The Importance of CDNs in Modern Software

It is easy to underestimate CDNs until something goes wrong without it. The term CDNs refers to networks of servers around the world that deliver static content from a location close to the user. This article explains the idea in plain language and points out where it fits in real projects.

Every web request crosses several layers of protocols before it reaches an application.

You will see CDNs whenever people work on serving images and scripts or speeding up global sites. It also helps with absorbing traffic spikes.

Beginners often stumble on three things. First: caching private data. Second: forgetting to purge old files. Third: misconfigured cache headers.

A solid grasp of CDNs also makes HTTP and HTTPS easier to understand.

Write a few tests that show how CDNs should behave. Tests double as documentation for your teammates. Keep an eye on this risk during code review: caching private data.

Keep experimenting, keep notes, and revisit CDNs whenever a new project gives you a reason.
