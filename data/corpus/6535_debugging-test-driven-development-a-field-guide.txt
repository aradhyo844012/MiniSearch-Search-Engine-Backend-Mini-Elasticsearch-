Debugging Test-Driven Development: A Field Guide

When something goes wrong around Test-Driven Development, resist the urge to guess. Work through the problem in order.

Step 1: reproduce the problem with the smallest possible example.
Step 2: list the usual suspects: writing tests that are too large, skipping the refactor step, and testing implementation details.
Step 3: revisit red, green, refactor and small steps, since misunderstandings there cause many bugs.
Step 4: use pytest or similar tools to look inside.
Step 5: write a test that fails because of the bug and passes after the fix.

Many bugs involving Test-Driven Development in work such as designing small modules come down to the first suspect on the list. Check the simple causes before suspecting anything exotic.

Finally, keep each red-green cycle to a few minutes. It prevents a surprising number of repeat problems.
