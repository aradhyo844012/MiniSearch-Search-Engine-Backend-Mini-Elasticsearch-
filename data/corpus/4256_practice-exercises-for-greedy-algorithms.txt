Practice Exercises for Greedy Algorithms

These exercises build real understanding of Greedy Algorithms. Do them in order, and write down your answers.

Exercise 1: List three situations from your own experience that resemble interval scheduling or Huffman coding.
Exercise 2: Compare Python sorted and unit tests for making change with standard coins and write down your conclusion.
Exercise 3: Pick pen and paper proofs and complete its getting-started tutorial.
Exercise 4: Create a small example that deliberately triggers this problem: assuming greedy always works. Then fix it.
Exercise 5: Explain how exchange arguments and local versus global optimum differ, using a drawing.

When you finish, compare your answers with the definition: Greedy Algorithms is algorithms that build a solution by always taking the locally best choice.
