HTTP and HTTPS vs WebSockets: Key Differences

HTTP and HTTPS and WebSockets both belong to the area of Networking, but they solve different problems. This article compares them side by side so you can choose with confidence.

The term HTTP and HTTPS refers to the request and response protocol of the web, with HTTPS adding encryption. Its core ideas are methods, status codes, and headers.

The term WebSockets refers to a protocol that keeps a two-way connection open between a browser and a server. Its core ideas are persistent connections, messages, and heartbeats.

The main difference is focus. HTTP and HTTPS puts the emphasis on methods and status codes, while WebSockets puts the emphasis on persistent connections and messages.

Reach for HTTP and HTTPS when your task involves loading web pages or calling APIs. Reach for WebSockets when your task involves chat applications or live dashboards.

Both have risks. With HTTP and HTTPS, watch for this: mixing up status codes. With WebSockets, watch for this: forgetting reconnection logic.

In practice you do not always have to choose. Many projects use HTTP and HTTPS alongside WebSockets, with tools such as curl and Socket.IO. Network failures are normal, so well-designed software expects timeouts and retries.
