Combining Endianness with Interrupts in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Endianness and Interrupts can work together in a single project.

Endianness is the order in which a computer stores the bytes of a multi-byte number. Interrupts is signals that make a processor pause its work to handle an urgent event.

Imagine a project that involves network protocols and keyboard and mouse input. Endianness brings big-endian, while Interrupts brings interrupt handlers.

A sensible plan is to start with Python struct for the Endianness part and Linux /proc/interrupts for the Interrupts part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: assuming all machines are alike on one side, and race conditions with shared data on the other.

Picture the whole as writing a date as day-month-year or as year-month-day, working alongside a phone call interrupting your reading.

Two habits help: first, always specify byte order in file and protocol formats; second, keep interrupt handlers short and defer the heavy work.
