The Trade-offs of Cross-Site Scripting

Every design choice has a cost, and Cross-Site Scripting is no exception. Put simply, Cross-Site Scripting covers a vulnerability where attackers get a site to run their scripts in other users' browsers. Learning it well pays off across many areas of computing.

On the positive side, Cross-Site Scripting is well suited to reviewing web forms and securing comment sections. On the other hand, it is not free. Keep these risks in mind: inserting user text as raw HTML; trusting client-side filtering; missing content security policy. Whether the trade is worth it depends on how much reflected XSS and stored XSS matter to your project.
