Mistakes to Avoid with Domain-Driven Design

Everyone learns Domain-Driven Design partly through mistakes. Here are three worth avoiding.

Mistake one: using it for simple CRUD apps. It usually appears when someone skips ubiquitous language and jumps straight to implementation. The fix is to slow down and review the basics.

Mistake two: models that never match the business. It tends to be noticed late, often during testing of complex business systems. A short checklist during review catches it early.

Mistake three: ignoring context boundaries. It is easy to miss because everything seems to work at first. Keep an eye on entities and value objects to spot it.

If you remember only one rule, make it this: agree on shared terms with domain experts before drawing classes.
