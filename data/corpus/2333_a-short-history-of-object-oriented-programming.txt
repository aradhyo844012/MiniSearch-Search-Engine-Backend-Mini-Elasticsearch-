A Short History of Object-Oriented Programming

Ideas behind Object-Oriented Programming developed gradually as engineers ran into practical limits and shared what they learned. Many students first meet Object-Oriented Programming in a classroom, but it matters just as much in production systems. The term refers to a style of programming that organizes code into objects that combine data and behavior.

Every language has idioms that experienced developers recognize at a glance.

To understand Object-Oriented Programming, start with classes. Then move on to inheritance, and finally look at encapsulation and polymorphism. A good way to remember classes is to connect it with a concrete example from your own code.

A solid grasp of Object-Oriented Programming also makes Python Generators easier to understand.

Keep experimenting, keep notes, and revisit Object-Oriented Programming whenever a new project gives you a reason.
