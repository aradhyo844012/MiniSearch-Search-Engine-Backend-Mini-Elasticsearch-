Debugging Encryption at Rest: A Field Guide

When something goes wrong around Encryption at Rest, resist the urge to guess. Work through the problem in order.

Step 1: reproduce the problem with the smallest possible example.
Step 2: list the usual suspects: storing keys next to the data, no key rotation, and assuming it stops every kind of attack.
Step 3: revisit symmetric keys and key management, since misunderstandings there cause many bugs.
Step 4: use LUKS or similar tools to look inside.
Step 5: write a test that fails because of the bug and passes after the fix.

Many bugs involving Encryption at Rest in work such as protecting backups come down to the first suspect on the list. Check the simple causes before suspecting anything exotic.

Finally, keep keys in a separate service from the data. It prevents a surprising number of repeat problems.
