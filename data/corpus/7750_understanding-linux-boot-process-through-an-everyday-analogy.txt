Understanding Linux Boot Process Through an Everyday Analogy

Abstract ideas are easier to hold onto when you attach them to something familiar. So picture a theater crew setting the stage in order before the curtain rises.

In that picture, firmware is the first rule you follow, bootloader is the next step, and kernel initialization and the init system keep everything running smoothly.

Every analogy breaks down eventually. Real Linux Boot Process is the sequence of steps from powering on a machine to a running operating system, and it involves details such as overwriting the bootloader that no everyday picture captures.

Use the analogy to remember the shape of the idea, and use documentation for GRUB to learn the exact behavior. The same picture helps when you explain troubleshooting machines that do not start to a friend.
