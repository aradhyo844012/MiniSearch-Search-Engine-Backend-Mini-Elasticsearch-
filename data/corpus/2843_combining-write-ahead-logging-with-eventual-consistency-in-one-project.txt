Combining Write-Ahead Logging with Eventual Consistency in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Write-Ahead Logging and Eventual Consistency can work together in a single project.

Write-Ahead Logging is a technique that records changes in a log before they are applied to the main data. Eventual Consistency is a guarantee that replicas will agree on the same value if updates stop for long enough.

Imagine a project that involves surviving crashes and global social feeds. Write-Ahead Logging brings log records, while Eventual Consistency brings replication delay.

A sensible plan is to start with PostgreSQL WAL for the Write-Ahead Logging part and Amazon DynamoDB for the Eventual Consistency part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: log files filling the disk on one side, and unresolved write conflicts on the other.

Picture the whole as writing a plan in a notebook before changing the real blueprint, working alongside news spreading through a village until everyone has heard it.

Two habits help: first, test crash recovery by killing the process on purpose; second, decide up front how your app handles stale reads.
