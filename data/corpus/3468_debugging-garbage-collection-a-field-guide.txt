Debugging Garbage Collection: A Field Guide

When something goes wrong around Garbage Collection, resist the urge to guess. Work through the problem in order.

Step 1: reproduce the problem with the smallest possible example.
Step 2: list the usual suspects: creating huge numbers of short-lived objects, holding references longer than needed, and ignoring pause times in latency-sensitive code.
Step 3: revisit reachability and generations, since misunderstandings there cause many bugs.
Step 4: use JVM GC logs or similar tools to look inside.
Step 5: write a test that fails because of the bug and passes after the fix.

Many bugs involving Garbage Collection in work such as managed languages like Java and Python come down to the first suspect on the list. Check the simple causes before suspecting anything exotic.

Finally, measure allocation rates before tuning collector settings. It prevents a surprising number of repeat problems.
