Is Reading Documentation Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Reading Documentation, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Reading Documentation is the skill of finding accurate answers in official guides and references.

It is a good fit when your work involves learning new libraries or solving errors, and when your team understands search.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn tutorials versus references, or when you cannot manage these risks: reading the wrong version, skipping the overview, and copying examples blindly.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether examples and version numbers are part of the answer. If they are, Reading Documentation is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with verifying answers from other sources and check that the documentation version matches your installed version.
