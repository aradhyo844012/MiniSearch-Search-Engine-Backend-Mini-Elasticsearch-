Background Jobs vs Object-Relational Mappers: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Background Jobs or Object-Relational Mappers first. Both sit in the area of Backend Engineering, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Background Jobs is tasks that run outside the request cycle so users do not have to wait for them, and it is closely tied to queues and workers. Object-Relational Mappers, by contrast, is libraries that map database tables to objects in code, and it is closely tied to models and queries.

Choose Background Jobs first if you care about sending emails or resizing images. Choose Object-Relational Mappers first if you care about building CRUD apps quickly or switching databases.

Think of Background Jobs as dropping laundry at a service and collecting it later, and of Object-Relational Mappers as a translator between the language of tables and the language of objects. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Background Jobs, avoid jobs that are not idempotent. With Object-Relational Mappers, avoid N+1 queries.

A good habit for Background Jobs is to give every job a timeout and a retry limit. A good habit for Object-Relational Mappers is to log the generated SQL during development.
