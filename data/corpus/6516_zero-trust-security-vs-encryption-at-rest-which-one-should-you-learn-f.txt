Zero Trust Security vs Encryption at Rest: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Zero Trust Security or Encryption at Rest first. Both sit in the area of Application Security, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Zero Trust Security is a model that treats every request as untrusted until it is verified, and it is closely tied to identity verification and least privilege. Encryption at Rest, by contrast, is protecting stored data by encrypting it on disk or in a database, and it is closely tied to symmetric keys and key management.

Choose Zero Trust Security first if you care about remote work access or protecting internal services. Choose Encryption at Rest first if you care about protecting backups or meeting compliance rules.

Think of Zero Trust Security as a building where every door checks your badge, not just the front entrance, and of Encryption at Rest as a locked safe inside a locked room. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Zero Trust Security, avoid treating it as a product to buy. With Encryption at Rest, avoid storing keys next to the data.

A good habit for Zero Trust Security is to start with strong identity and device checks. A good habit for Encryption at Rest is to keep keys in a separate service from the data.
