Vector Graphics (SVG) for Students: A Study Guide

This study guide prepares you for exams, labs and interviews that cover Vector Graphics (SVG).

Learning goals:
1. Explain Vector Graphics (SVG) in your own words.
2. Describe paths, the viewBox, fill and stroke, and transforms.
3. Give examples from logos and icons.
4. Name three problems to watch for.

Start with the definition: Vector Graphics (SVG) is images described by shapes and paths so they stay sharp at any size. Then draw a recipe for drawing a picture rather than a photo of it on paper and label the four terms from the goals above.

Self-test questions: Why is overly complex paths a problem? How does the viewBox relate to fill and stroke? When would you choose Inkscape or SVGO?

Study habit worth keeping: set a viewBox so the image scales cleanly.

When you feel ready, compare Vector Graphics (SVG) with Rasterization and write down three differences.
