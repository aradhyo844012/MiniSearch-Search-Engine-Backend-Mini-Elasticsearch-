Best Practices for Zipf's Law

The practices below come from patterns that tend to work well across many teams and projects. Many students first meet Zipf's Law in a classroom, but it matters just as much in production systems. The term refers to the observation that a word's frequency is roughly inversely related to its rank in natural language.

1. Automate repetitive steps so that results are easy to reproduce.
2. Write a few tests that show how Zipf's Law should behave. Tests double as documentation for your teammates.
3. Start small by applying Zipf's Law to one case, such as analyzing text corpora, before rolling it out more widely.
4. Ask a teammate to explain log-log plots back to you; teaching is a quick way to find gaps.
5. Measure before you optimize. Use matplotlib or a simple timer to see where time is actually spent.

Popular tools and libraries include matplotlib, NumPy, and pandas.

A solid grasp of Zipf's Law also makes Edit Distance easier to understand.

In short, careful study of Zipf's Law pays off. Spend time on rank and frequency and power laws, and the rest will follow.
