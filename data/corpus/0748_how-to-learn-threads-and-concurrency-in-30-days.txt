How to Learn Threads and Concurrency in 30 Days

You do not need months to become comfortable with Threads and Concurrency. A focused plan of thirty days is enough to build a solid base. Many students first meet Threads and Concurrency in a classroom, but it matters just as much in production systems. The term refers to ways of making progress on several tasks at once within a program.

Week 1: learn threads and locks, and write a one-page summary.
Week 2: practice with Python threading on a small task such as handling many requests.
Week 3: build a mini project around keeping interfaces responsive.
Week 4: review common mistakes such as forgetting to protect shared data, and explain Threads and Concurrency to a friend.

Threads and Concurrency and Deadlocks are closely related, so learning both gives a fuller picture of Operating Systems.

In practice you can start with Python threading, then explore Java ExecutorService or asyncio as your needs grow.

In short, careful study of Threads and Concurrency pays off. Spend time on threads and locks, and the rest will follow.
