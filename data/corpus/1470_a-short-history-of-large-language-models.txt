A Short History of Large Language Models

Ideas behind Large Language Models developed gradually as engineers ran into practical limits and shared what they learned. Many students first meet Large Language Models in a classroom, but it matters just as much in production systems. The term refers to very large neural networks trained on text that can generate and analyze language.

Simple baselines are worth building first because they show what improvement is realistic.

Four ideas sit at the heart of Large Language Models: tokens, attention, pretraining, and prompts. Each one builds on the one before it. Sketching prompts on paper before coding helps many learners see how the pieces fit.

A solid grasp of Large Language Models also makes Linear Regression easier to understand.

In short, careful study of Large Language Models pays off. Spend time on tokens and attention, and the rest will follow.
