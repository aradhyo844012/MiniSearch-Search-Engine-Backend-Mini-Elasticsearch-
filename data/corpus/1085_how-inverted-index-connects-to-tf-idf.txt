How Inverted Index Connects to TF-IDF

Inverted Index and TF-IDF are closely connected parts of Search and Retrieval. Seeing how they fit together helps you learn both faster.

A project involving full-text search may also involve ranking search results. In such a project, vocabulary and term frequency often influence each other.

Start with Inverted Index: the term refers to a data structure that maps each term to the documents that contain it. Then look at TF-IDF, which refers to a scoring method that weights terms by how often they appear in a document and how rare they are overall.

A useful exercise is to take one example, such as log search, and explain how inverse document frequency would change the design.

Common problems are shared too. For Inverted Index, watch for this: building the index without normalizing text. For TF-IDF, watch for this: ignoring document length. Real query logs reveal what people actually look for, which is rarely what designers expect.

Spend a few hours on each topic, then combine them in one small project using Lucene or scikit-learn TfidfVectorizer.
