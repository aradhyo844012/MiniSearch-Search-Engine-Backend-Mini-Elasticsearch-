Combining Random Forests with Convolutional Neural Networks in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Random Forests and Convolutional Neural Networks can work together in a single project.

Random Forests is ensembles of many decision trees whose predictions are combined. Convolutional Neural Networks is neural networks that scan images with small filters to detect patterns.

Imagine a project that involves tabular predictions and image classification. Random Forests brings bagging, while Convolutional Neural Networks brings filters.

A sensible plan is to start with scikit-learn for the Random Forests part and PyTorch for the Convolutional Neural Networks part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: very large forests being slow on one side, and ignoring data augmentation on the other.

Picture the whole as asking many different experts and taking the majority view, working alongside a magnifying glass sweeping across a photo looking for edges and shapes.

Two habits help: first, tune the number of trees only after the basics work; second, start from a pretrained model.
