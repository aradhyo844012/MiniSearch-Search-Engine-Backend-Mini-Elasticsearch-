Mistakes to Avoid with Memory Hierarchy

Everyone learns Memory Hierarchy partly through mistakes. Here are three worth avoiding.

Mistake one: ignoring locality. It usually appears when someone skips registers and jumps straight to implementation. The fix is to slow down and review the basics.

Mistake two: excessive paging. It tends to be noticed late, often during testing of designing efficient programs. A short checklist during review catches it early.

Mistake three: treating all memory accesses as equal. It is easy to miss because everything seems to work at first. Keep an eye on disk to spot it.

If you remember only one rule, make it this: reduce how often your program goes down to the slower layers.
