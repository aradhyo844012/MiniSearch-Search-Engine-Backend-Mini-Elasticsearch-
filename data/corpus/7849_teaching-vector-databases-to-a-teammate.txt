Teaching Vector Databases to a Teammate

Explaining Vector Databases to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: a map where similar ideas sit near each other. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Vector Databases is databases built to store embeddings and find the most similar ones quickly. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving semantic search, naming embeddings and approximate nearest neighbors as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain distance metrics in Vector Databases back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, mixing embedding models, and suggest this habit: measure recall on a small labeled test set.
