Debugging Technical Writing: A Field Guide

When something goes wrong around Technical Writing, resist the urge to guess. Work through the problem in order.

Step 1: reproduce the problem with the smallest possible example.
Step 2: list the usual suspects: assuming too much knowledge, burying the main point, and long unbroken paragraphs.
Step 3: revisit audience and structure, since misunderstandings there cause many bugs.
Step 4: use Markdown or similar tools to look inside.
Step 5: write a test that fails because of the bug and passes after the fix.

Many bugs involving Technical Writing in work such as documentation come down to the first suspect on the list. Check the simple causes before suspecting anything exotic.

Finally, state the main point in the first two sentences. It prevents a surprising number of repeat problems.
