Debugging Fine-Tuning Models: A Field Guide

When something goes wrong around Fine-Tuning Models, resist the urge to guess. Work through the problem in order.

Step 1: reproduce the problem with the smallest possible example.
Step 2: list the usual suspects: overfitting on small datasets, forgetting general abilities, and low-quality training data.
Step 3: revisit pretrained weights and training data, since misunderstandings there cause many bugs.
Step 4: use Hugging Face or similar tools to look inside.
Step 5: write a test that fails because of the bug and passes after the fix.

Many bugs involving Fine-Tuning Models in work such as domain-specific language tasks come down to the first suspect on the list. Check the simple causes before suspecting anything exotic.

Finally, start with a small, clean dataset and a held-out test set. It prevents a surprising number of repeat problems.
