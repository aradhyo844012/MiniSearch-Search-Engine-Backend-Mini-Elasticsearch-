Hashing vs Encryption vs Threat Modeling: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Hashing vs Encryption or Threat Modeling first. Both sit in the area of Application Security, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Hashing vs Encryption is the difference between one-way digests and reversible protection of data, and it is closely tied to one-way functions and keys. Threat Modeling, by contrast, is a structured way of identifying what could go wrong in a system and how to prevent it, and it is closely tied to assets and attackers.

Choose Hashing vs Encryption first if you care about storing passwords or verifying file integrity. Choose Threat Modeling first if you care about designing new features or reviewing architecture.

Think of Hashing vs Encryption as a paper shredder compared with a locked suitcase, and of Threat Modeling as walking around a house asking where a burglar might get in. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Hashing vs Encryption, avoid encrypting passwords instead of hashing them. With Threat Modeling, avoid doing it once and never again.

A good habit for Hashing vs Encryption is to ask whether you will ever need the original value back. A good habit for Threat Modeling is to draw the data flow diagram first.
