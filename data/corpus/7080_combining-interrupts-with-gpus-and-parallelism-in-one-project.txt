Combining Interrupts with GPUs and Parallelism in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Interrupts and GPUs and Parallelism can work together in a single project.

Interrupts is signals that make a processor pause its work to handle an urgent event. GPUs and Parallelism is processors with thousands of small cores that handle many similar calculations at once.

Imagine a project that involves keyboard and mouse input and training neural networks. Interrupts brings interrupt handlers, while GPUs and Parallelism brings threads and blocks.

A sensible plan is to start with Linux /proc/interrupts for the Interrupts part and CUDA for the GPUs and Parallelism part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: race conditions with shared data on one side, and branch-heavy code on the other.

Picture the whole as a phone call interrupting your reading, working alongside a stadium of workers each doing one small identical task.

Two habits help: first, keep interrupt handlers short and defer the heavy work; second, minimize copies between CPU and GPU memory.
