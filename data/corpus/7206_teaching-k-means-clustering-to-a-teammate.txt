Teaching K-Means Clustering to a Teammate

Explaining K-Means Clustering to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: dividing students into study groups around the most central member. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: K-Means Clustering is an algorithm that groups data points into a chosen number of clusters around central points. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving customer segmentation, naming centroids and distance measures as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain choosing k in K-Means Clustering back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, choosing k blindly, and suggest this habit: scale features before clustering.
