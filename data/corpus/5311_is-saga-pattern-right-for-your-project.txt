Is Saga Pattern Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Saga Pattern, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Saga Pattern is a way of managing long business operations across services using a sequence of local steps and compensating actions.

It is a good fit when your work involves order processing or travel bookings, and when your team understands local transactions.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn compensating actions, or when you cannot manage these risks: missing compensations, steps that are not idempotent, and flows that are hard to trace.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether orchestration and choreography are part of the answer. If they are, Saga Pattern is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with payment workflows and make every step and its compensation idempotent.
