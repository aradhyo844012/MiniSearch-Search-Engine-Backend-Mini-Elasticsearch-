The Trade-offs of OAuth 2.0

Every design choice has a cost, and OAuth 2.0 is no exception. The term OAuth 2.0 refers to a framework that lets applications access resources on a user's behalf without sharing passwords. This article explains the idea in plain language and points out where it fits in real projects.

On the positive side, OAuth 2.0 is well suited to sign in with another account and API access for third-party apps. On the other hand, it is not free. Keep these risks in mind: loose redirect URI checks; requesting excessive scopes; storing tokens insecurely. Whether the trade is worth it depends on how much authorization server and access tokens matter to your project.

Three issues come up again and again: loose redirect URI checks; requesting excessive scopes; and storing tokens insecurely. Knowing them in advance saves hours of debugging.

Write a few tests that show how OAuth 2.0 should behave. Tests double as documentation for your teammates. Read the official documentation for Keycloak; it often explains access tokens better than blog posts do. Automate repetitive steps so that results are easy to reproduce.

In short, careful study of OAuth 2.0 pays off. Spend time on authorization server and access tokens, and the rest will follow.
