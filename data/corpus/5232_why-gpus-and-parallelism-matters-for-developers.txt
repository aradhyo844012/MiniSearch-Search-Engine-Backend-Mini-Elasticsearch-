Why GPUs and Parallelism Matters for Developers

Developers who understand GPUs and Parallelism make better decisions, because GPUs and Parallelism is processors with thousands of small cores that handle many similar calculations at once.

Three situations show why it matters. First, training neural networks. Second, rendering graphics. Third, scientific simulations. In each case, a solid grasp of threads and blocks and kernels separates a quick fix from a lasting solution.

Ignoring it has a cost. Teams run into these problems: slow data transfers, branch-heavy code, and launching tiny workloads.

You will not need to build everything yourself; CUDA, PyTorch, and OpenCL cover most needs.
