Is Retrieval-Augmented Generation Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Retrieval-Augmented Generation, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Retrieval-Augmented Generation is a technique that gives a language model relevant documents to read before it answers.

It is a good fit when your work involves company knowledge assistants or question answering over documents, and when your team understands retrieval.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn chunking, or when you cannot manage these risks: poor chunking, irrelevant retrieved text, and no evaluation of the answers.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether prompts with context and grounding are part of the answer. If they are, Retrieval-Augmented Generation is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with support bots and evaluate retrieval quality separately from answer quality.
