A Short History of Two-Factor Authentication

Ideas behind Two-Factor Authentication developed gradually as engineers ran into practical limits and shared what they learned. Put simply, Two-Factor Authentication covers requiring a second proof of identity in addition to a password. Learning it well pays off across many areas of computing.

Attackers usually choose the easiest weakness, not the most interesting one.

The vocabulary of Two-Factor Authentication is small. Learn one-time codes, authenticator apps, hardware keys, and backup codes, and most documentation will start to make sense. Sketching backup codes on paper before coding helps many learners see how the pieces fit.

A solid grasp of Two-Factor Authentication also makes Secure Coding easier to understand.

In short, careful study of Two-Factor Authentication pays off. Spend time on one-time codes and authenticator apps, and the rest will follow.
