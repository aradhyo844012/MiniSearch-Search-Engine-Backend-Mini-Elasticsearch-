Is Raft Consensus Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Raft Consensus, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Raft Consensus is an algorithm that lets a group of servers agree on a shared log of operations.

It is a good fit when your work involves configuration stores or distributed locks, and when your team understands leader election.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn log replication, or when you cannot manage these risks: even-sized clusters, slow disks causing elections, and network delays above election timeouts.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether terms and majority quorum are part of the answer. If they are, Raft Consensus is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with replicated state machines and use an odd number of nodes.
