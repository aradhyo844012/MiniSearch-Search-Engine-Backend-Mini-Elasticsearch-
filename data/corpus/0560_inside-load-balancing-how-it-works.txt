Inside Load Balancing: How It Works

This article walks through what happens inside Load Balancing, step by step. If you build software for long enough, you will run into Load Balancing. The term refers to spreading incoming requests across several servers so no single server is overwhelmed.

To understand Load Balancing, start with round robin. Then move on to health checks, and finally look at sticky sessions and layer 4 and layer 7. Understanding health checks first often makes sticky sessions much easier to reason about.

First, round robin sets the stage for everything else. Next, health checks builds on it. After that, sticky sessions comes into play, and finally layer 4 and layer 7 ties the pieces together. Seeing the sequence once, with a tiny example, makes the whole topic easier to remember.

Network failures are normal, so well-designed software expects timeouts and retries. Latency is limited by distance and by the number of round trips a request needs.

If you are comfortable with Load Balancing, explore WebSockets next. The two often appear in the same project.

In short, careful study of Load Balancing pays off. Spend time on round robin and health checks, and the rest will follow.
