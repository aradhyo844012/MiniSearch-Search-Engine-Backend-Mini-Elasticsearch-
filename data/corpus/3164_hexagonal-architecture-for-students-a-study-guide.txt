Hexagonal Architecture for Students: A Study Guide

This study guide prepares you for exams, labs and interviews that cover Hexagonal Architecture.

Learning goals:
1. Explain Hexagonal Architecture in your own words.
2. Describe ports, adapters, the core domain, and dependency direction.
3. Give examples from testable business logic and swapping databases.
4. Name three problems to watch for.

Start with the definition: Hexagonal Architecture is a design that keeps core logic separate from outside systems through ports and adapters. Then draw a power strip that lets any plug connect to the same core on paper and label the four terms from the goals above.

Self-test questions: Why is too many layers for small apps a problem? How does adapters relate to the core domain? When would you choose Spring or FastAPI?

Study habit: keep framework imports out of the core domain.

When you feel ready, compare Hexagonal Architecture with Event-Driven Architecture and write down three differences.
