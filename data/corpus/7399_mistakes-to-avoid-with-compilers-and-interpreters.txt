Mistakes to Avoid with Compilers and Interpreters

Everyone learns Compilers and Interpreters partly through mistakes. Here are three worth avoiding.

Mistake one: assuming interpreted means slow without measuring. It usually appears when someone skips lexing and jumps straight to implementation. The fix is to slow down and review the basics.

Mistake two: ignoring compiler warnings. It tends to be noticed late, often during testing of running Python and JavaScript. A short checklist during review catches it early.

Mistake three: confusing compile-time and run-time errors. It is easy to miss because everything seems to work at first. Keep an eye on bytecode to spot it.

If you remember only one rule, make it this: turn on compiler warnings and treat them seriously.
