Two Pointers and Sliding Window vs Skip Lists: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Two Pointers and Sliding Window or Skip Lists first. Both sit in the area of Algorithms in Practice, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Two Pointers and Sliding Window is techniques that scan arrays with two moving positions to avoid nested loops, and it is closely tied to left and right pointers and window boundaries. Skip Lists, by contrast, is layered linked lists that allow fast searching by skipping over many elements, and it is closely tied to multiple levels and random level assignment.

Choose Two Pointers and Sliding Window first if you care about finding pairs with a given sum or longest substring problems. Choose Skip Lists first if you care about ordered in-memory indexes or sorted sets in caches.

Think of Two Pointers and Sliding Window as a small frame that slides along a line of text, and of Skip Lists as an express train line layered over a local train line. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Two Pointers and Sliding Window, avoid moving the wrong pointer. With Skip Lists, avoid poor random number choices.

A good habit for Two Pointers and Sliding Window is to write down the window invariant before coding the loop. A good habit for Skip Lists is to draw several levels by hand to see how a search moves down.
