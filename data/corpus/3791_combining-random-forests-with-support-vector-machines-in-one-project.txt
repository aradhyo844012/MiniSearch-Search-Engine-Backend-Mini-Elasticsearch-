Combining Random Forests with Support Vector Machines in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Random Forests and Support Vector Machines can work together in a single project.

Random Forests is ensembles of many decision trees whose predictions are combined. Support Vector Machines is models that classify data by finding the boundary with the widest margin between classes.

Imagine a project that involves tabular predictions and text classification. Random Forests brings bagging, while Support Vector Machines brings margin.

A sensible plan is to start with scikit-learn for the Random Forests part and scikit-learn for the Support Vector Machines part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: very large forests being slow on one side, and poor kernel choices on the other.

Picture the whole as asking many different experts and taking the majority view, working alongside drawing the widest road between two neighborhoods.

Two habits help: first, tune the number of trees only after the basics work; second, scale features and try a linear kernel first.
