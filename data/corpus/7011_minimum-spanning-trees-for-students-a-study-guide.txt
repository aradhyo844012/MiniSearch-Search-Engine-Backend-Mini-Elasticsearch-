Minimum Spanning Trees for Students: A Study Guide

This study guide prepares you for exams, labs and interviews that cover Minimum Spanning Trees.

Learning goals:
1. Explain Minimum Spanning Trees in your own words.
2. Describe weighted graphs, Kruskal's algorithm, Prim's algorithm, and the cut property.
3. Give examples from designing cable networks and clustering data.
4. Name three problems to watch for.

Start with the definition: Minimum Spanning Trees is a set of edges that connects every node in a graph with the smallest possible total weight. Then draw laying the cheapest set of roads that still links every town on paper and label the four terms from the goals above.

Self-test questions: Why is using it on directed graphs a problem? How does Kruskal's algorithm relate to Prim's algorithm? When would you choose NetworkX or SciPy?

Study habit worth keeping: sort the edges once and reuse the order when testing variations.

When you feel ready, compare Minimum Spanning Trees with Greedy Algorithms and write down three differences.
