Is Image Compression Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Image Compression, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Image Compression is reducing the size of image files by removing redundancy or less visible detail.

It is a good fit when your work involves web images or photo storage, and when your team understands lossy and lossless formats.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn quantization, or when you cannot manage these risks: repeated recompression, the wrong format for graphics, and excessive quality loss.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether transform coding and quality settings are part of the answer. If they are, Image Compression is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with sending pictures over slow networks and pick the format by content: photos versus flat graphics.
