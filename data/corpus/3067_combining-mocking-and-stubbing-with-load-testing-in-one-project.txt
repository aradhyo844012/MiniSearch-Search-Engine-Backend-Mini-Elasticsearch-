Combining Mocking and Stubbing with Load Testing in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Mocking and Stubbing and Load Testing can work together in a single project.

Mocking and Stubbing is replacing real dependencies with controlled fakes during tests. Load Testing is measuring how a system behaves under expected and heavy traffic.

Imagine a project that involves isolating units and capacity planning. Mocking and Stubbing brings mocks, while Load Testing brings virtual users.

A sensible plan is to start with unittest.mock for the Mocking and Stubbing part and k6 for the Load Testing part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: tests that mirror the implementation on one side, and ignoring warm-up on the other.

Picture the whole as a crash test dummy standing in for a real passenger, working alongside a stress test that fills a stadium to see how the exits cope.

Two habits help: first, prefer simple fakes over deep mock chains; second, report p95 and p99 latency, not only averages.
