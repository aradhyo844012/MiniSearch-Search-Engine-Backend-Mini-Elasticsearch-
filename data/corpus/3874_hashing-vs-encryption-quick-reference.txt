Hashing vs Encryption Quick Reference

A one-screen reminder for Hashing vs Encryption. Bookmark it and return when the details fade.

What it is: the difference between one-way digests and reversible protection of data.
Key terms: one-way functions, keys, collision resistance, and reversibility.
Typical uses: storing passwords, verifying file integrity, and protecting data in transit.
Risks: encrypting passwords instead of hashing them, using broken hash functions, and confusing encoding with encryption.
Tools: SHA-256, AES, and OpenSSL.

Mental picture: a paper shredder compared with a locked suitcase.

Habit to build: ask whether you will ever need the original value back.
