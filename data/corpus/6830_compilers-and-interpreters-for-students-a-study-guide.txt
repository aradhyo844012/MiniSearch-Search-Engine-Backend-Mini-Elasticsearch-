Compilers and Interpreters for Students: A Study Guide

This study guide prepares you for exams, labs and interviews that cover Compilers and Interpreters.

Learning goals:
1. Explain Compilers and Interpreters in your own words.
2. Describe lexing, parsing, code generation, and bytecode.
3. Give examples from running Python and JavaScript and building native programs.
4. Name three problems to watch for.

Start with the definition: Compilers and Interpreters is programs that translate or run source code written in a high-level language. Then draw a translator who either translates a whole book first or interprets sentence by sentence on paper and label the four terms from the goals above.

Self-test questions: Why is assuming interpreted means slow without measuring a problem? How does parsing relate to code generation? When would you choose GCC or LLVM?

Study habit worth keeping: turn on compiler warnings and treat them seriously.

When you feel ready, compare Compilers and Interpreters with Boolean Logic and Gates and write down three differences.
