Debugging B-Trees on Disk: A Field Guide

When something goes wrong around B-Trees on Disk, resist the urge to guess. Work through the problem in order.

Step 1: reproduce the problem with the smallest possible example.
Step 2: list the usual suspects: random writes on fragmented pages, oversized keys, and ignoring page fill factor.
Step 3: revisit pages and branching factor, since misunderstandings there cause many bugs.
Step 4: use PostgreSQL or similar tools to look inside.
Step 5: write a test that fails because of the bug and passes after the fix.

Many bugs involving B-Trees on Disk in work such as database indexes come down to the first suspect on the list. Check the simple causes before suspecting anything exotic.

Finally, keep indexed keys short so more of them fit on a page. It prevents a surprising number of repeat problems.
