A Short History of Bootstrap

Ideas behind Bootstrap developed gradually as engineers ran into practical limits and shared what they learned. The term Bootstrap refers to a popular front-end toolkit with ready-made layout, components and utilities. This article explains the idea in plain language and points out where it fits in real projects.

Web pages must work across many browsers, devices and network conditions.

Four ideas sit at the heart of Bootstrap: grid system, components, utility classes, and theming variables. Each one builds on the one before it. Sketching theming variables on paper before coding helps many learners see how the pieces fit.

If you are comfortable with Bootstrap, explore HTML Semantics next. The two often appear in the same project.

None of this needs special hardware or expensive software. A laptop, curiosity and a few small experiments are enough to get comfortable with Bootstrap.
