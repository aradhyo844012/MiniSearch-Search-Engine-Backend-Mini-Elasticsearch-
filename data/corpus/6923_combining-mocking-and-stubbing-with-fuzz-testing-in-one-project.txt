Combining Mocking and Stubbing with Fuzz Testing in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Mocking and Stubbing and Fuzz Testing can work together in a single project.

Mocking and Stubbing is replacing real dependencies with controlled fakes during tests. Fuzz Testing is feeding programs random or malformed input to uncover crashes and security bugs.

Imagine a project that involves isolating units and testing parsers. Mocking and Stubbing brings mocks, while Fuzz Testing brings input generation.

A sensible plan is to start with unittest.mock for the Mocking and Stubbing part and AFL++ for the Fuzz Testing part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: tests that mirror the implementation on one side, and crashes that cannot be reproduced on the other.

Picture the whole as a crash test dummy standing in for a real passenger, working alongside shaking a machine hard to see what rattles loose.

Two habits help: first, prefer simple fakes over deep mock chains; second, keep every crashing input as a regression test.
