Troubleshooting NoSQL Databases Problems

When something breaks around NoSQL Databases, a calm and systematic approach saves the most time. When people talk about NoSQL Databases, they usually mean a broad family of databases that do not use the traditional relational table model. It appears in almost every serious software project, whether or not developers notice it.

Start by reproducing the problem with the smallest possible example. Then change one thing at a time and note what happens. Logs, tests and MongoDB usually reveal the cause faster than guessing.

Common culprits: assuming no schema means no design; weak consistency surprises; expensive cross-collection queries. If none of these explains the symptom, re-read the basics of document stores and key-value stores.

Most slow pages in data-driven applications trace back to a handful of slow queries. Backups are only useful if restoring from them has been tested.

Popular tools and libraries include MongoDB, Cassandra, and Redis.

In short, careful study of NoSQL Databases pays off. Spend time on document stores and key-value stores, and the rest will follow.
