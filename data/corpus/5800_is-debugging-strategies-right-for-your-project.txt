Is Debugging Strategies Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Debugging Strategies, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Debugging Strategies is systematic ways of finding and fixing the cause of unexpected program behavior.

It is a good fit when your work involves fixing production issues or learning unfamiliar code, and when your team understands reproducing the bug.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn isolating the cause, or when you cannot manage these risks: changing many things at once, guessing without evidence, and not writing a test afterward.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether forming hypotheses and binary search through code are part of the answer. If they are, Debugging Strategies is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with reducing repeat bugs and change one thing at a time and record the result.
