Decision Trees vs Overfitting: Key Differences

Decision Trees and Overfitting both belong to the area of Machine Learning, but they solve different problems. This article compares them side by side so you can choose with confidence.

The term Decision Trees refers to models that make predictions by asking a sequence of simple questions about the data. Its core ideas are splits, entropy and Gini impurity, and leaf nodes.

The term Overfitting refers to when a model learns the training data too closely and performs poorly on new data. Its core ideas are training and validation error, model complexity, and regularization.

The main difference is focus. Decision Trees puts the emphasis on splits and entropy and Gini impurity, while Overfitting puts the emphasis on training and validation error and model complexity.

Reach for Decision Trees when your task involves classifying customers or explaining predictions. Reach for Overfitting when your task involves diagnosing weak models or choosing simpler models.

Both have risks. With Decision Trees, watch for this: deep trees that overfit. With Overfitting, watch for this: evaluating only on training data.

In practice you do not always have to choose. Many projects use Decision Trees alongside Overfitting, with tools such as scikit-learn and scikit-learn. Reproducible experiments make it far easier to trust and improve a model.
