Understanding SSH and Remote Access Through an Everyday Analogy

Abstract ideas are easier to hold onto when you attach them to something familiar. So picture a private phone line with a secret handshake to a faraway office.

In that picture, key pairs is the first rule you follow, host keys is the next step, and port forwarding and config files keep everything running smoothly.

Every analogy breaks down eventually. Real SSH and Remote Access is a secure protocol for logging in to and controlling remote machines, and it involves details such as password logins left enabled that no everyday picture captures.

Use the analogy to remember the shape of the idea, and use documentation for OpenSSH to learn the exact behavior. The same picture helps when you explain administering servers to a friend.
