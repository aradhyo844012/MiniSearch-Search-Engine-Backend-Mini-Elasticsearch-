A Beginner's Guide to Phishing Defense

This guide is written for readers who are new to Phishing Defense and want a clear starting point. Many students first meet Phishing Defense in a classroom, but it matters just as much in production systems. The term refers to recognizing and reducing attacks that trick people into revealing information.

Never trust input that comes from outside your own code.

Four ideas sit at the heart of Phishing Defense: suspicious links, sender verification, email filtering, and user training. Each one builds on the one before it. A good way to remember suspicious links is to connect it with a concrete example from your own code.

You will see Phishing Defense whenever people work on protecting employees or securing shared mailboxes. It also helps with incident response drills.

Popular tools and libraries include SPF, DKIM, and DMARC.

Watch out for these problems: clicking before checking; reusing passwords; and ignoring reports from users.

Measure before you optimize. Use SPF or a simple timer to see where time is actually spent. Keep an eye on this risk during code review: clicking before checking. Automate repetitive steps so that results are easy to reproduce.

None of this needs special hardware or expensive software. A laptop, curiosity and a few small experiments are enough to get comfortable with Phishing Defense.
