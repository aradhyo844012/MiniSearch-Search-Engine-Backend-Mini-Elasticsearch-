The Future of ETL Pipelines

Predicting the future is risky, but the direction of ETL Pipelines is easy to describe in general terms. The term ETL Pipelines refers to processes that extract data from sources, transform it and load it into a destination. This article explains the idea in plain language and points out where it fits in real projects.

Writing down assumptions about the data prevents confusing surprises later. A chart is only useful if the reader can see the point within a few seconds.

Data work is mostly preparation, and cleaning often takes longer than analysis. Writing down assumptions about the data prevents confusing surprises later.

Expect tools such as Apache Airflow and dbt to keep improving, and expect newer ideas to keep building on extraction. The basics, though, tend to stay the same, so time spent on transformation and loading is rarely wasted. Anyone working on building reports will keep meeting ETL Pipelines for years to come.

Read the official documentation for dbt; it often explains transformation better than blog posts do. Ask a teammate to explain loading back to you; teaching is a quick way to find gaps.

None of this needs special hardware or expensive software. A laptop, curiosity and a few small experiments are enough to get comfortable with ETL Pipelines.
