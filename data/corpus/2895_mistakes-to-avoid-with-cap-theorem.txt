Mistakes to Avoid with CAP Theorem

Everyone learns CAP Theorem partly through mistakes. Here are three worth avoiding.

Mistake one: treating it as a three-way menu. It usually appears when someone skips consistency and jumps straight to implementation. The fix is to slow down and review the basics.

Mistake two: ignoring latency trade-offs in normal operation. It tends to be noticed late, often during testing of choosing database types. A short checklist during review catches it early.

Mistake three: assuming partitions never happen. It is easy to miss because everything seems to work at first. Keep an eye on trade-offs to spot it.

If you remember only one rule, make it this: ask what your users prefer during a failure: stale answers or errors.
