How to Learn Site Reliability in 30 Days

You do not need months to become comfortable with Site Reliability. A focused plan of thirty days is enough to build a solid base. The term Site Reliability refers to the discipline of keeping services dependable using engineering practices. This article explains the idea in plain language and points out where it fits in real projects.

Week 1: learn service level objectives and error budgets, and write a one-page summary.
Week 2: practice with PagerDuty on a small task such as running production services.
Week 3: build a mini project around reducing outages.
Week 4: review common mistakes such as alerting on everything, and explain Site Reliability to a friend.

Popular tools and libraries include PagerDuty, Prometheus, and runbooks.

Cloud resources are easy to create and just as easy to forget, which makes costs grow quietly. A system that cannot be observed cannot be reliably operated.

In short, careful study of Site Reliability pays off. Spend time on service level objectives and error budgets, and the rest will follow.
