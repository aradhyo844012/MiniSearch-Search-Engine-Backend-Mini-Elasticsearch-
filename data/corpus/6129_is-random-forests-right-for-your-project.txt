Is Random Forests Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Random Forests, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Random Forests is ensembles of many decision trees whose predictions are combined.

It is a good fit when your work involves tabular predictions or feature importance ranking, and when your team understands bagging.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn feature randomness, or when you cannot manage these risks: treating importance scores as causes, very large forests being slow, and ignoring class imbalance.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether voting and out-of-bag error are part of the answer. If they are, Random Forests is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with strong baseline models and tune the number of trees only after the basics work.
