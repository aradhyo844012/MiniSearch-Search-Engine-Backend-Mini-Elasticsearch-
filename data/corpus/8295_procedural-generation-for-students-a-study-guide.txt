Procedural Generation for Students: A Study Guide

This study guide prepares you for exams, labs and interviews that cover Procedural Generation.

Learning goals:
1. Explain Procedural Generation in your own words.
2. Describe random seeds, noise functions, rules and constraints, and reproducibility.
3. Give examples from roguelike dungeons and infinite terrain.
4. Name three problems to watch for.

Start with the definition: Procedural Generation is creating game content such as levels and terrain automatically with algorithms. Then draw a chef inventing endless dishes from a fixed set of ingredients on paper and label the four terms from the goals above.

Self-test questions: Why is levels that are unfair or unwinnable a problem? How does noise functions relate to rules and constraints? When would you choose Perlin noise or Wave Function Collapse?

Study habit worth keeping: log the seed so any generated level can be reproduced.

When you feel ready, compare Procedural Generation with Shaders and write down three differences.
