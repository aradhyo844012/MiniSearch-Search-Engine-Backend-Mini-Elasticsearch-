Overfitting vs Model Evaluation: Key Differences

Overfitting and Model Evaluation both belong to the area of Machine Learning, but they solve different problems. This article compares them side by side so you can choose with confidence.

The term Overfitting refers to when a model learns the training data too closely and performs poorly on new data. Its core ideas are training and validation error, model complexity, and regularization.

The term Model Evaluation refers to measuring how well a machine learning model works on data it has not seen. Its core ideas are accuracy, precision and recall, and confusion matrices.

The main difference is focus. Overfitting puts the emphasis on training and validation error and model complexity, while Model Evaluation puts the emphasis on accuracy and precision and recall.

Reach for Overfitting when your task involves diagnosing weak models or choosing simpler models. Reach for Model Evaluation when your task involves comparing models or choosing thresholds.

Both have risks. With Overfitting, watch for this: evaluating only on training data. With Model Evaluation, watch for this: using accuracy on imbalanced data.

In practice you do not always have to choose. Many projects use Overfitting alongside Model Evaluation, with tools such as scikit-learn and scikit-learn metrics. Reproducible experiments make it far easier to trust and improve a model.
