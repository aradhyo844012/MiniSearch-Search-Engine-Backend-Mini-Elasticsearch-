Choosing Tools for Random Forests

There is no single best tool for Random Forests. The right choice depends on your project, your team and your timeline.

scikit-learn is a common first choice, especially for tabular predictions. XGBoost suits teams that already work with similar technology, and LightGBM is worth a look when you need something different.

Judge any tool by four questions: Is the documentation clear? Is the community active? Does it handle feature importance ranking well? Can you leave it later if you need to?

Whichever tool you pick, test it on a small problem first, and watch for this: treating importance scores as causes.

As a rule of thumb, tune the number of trees only after the basics work, no matter which tool you use.
