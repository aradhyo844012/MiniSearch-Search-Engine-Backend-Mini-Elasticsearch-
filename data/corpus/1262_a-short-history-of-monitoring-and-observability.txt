A Short History of Monitoring and Observability

Ideas behind Monitoring and Observability developed gradually as engineers ran into practical limits and shared what they learned. When people talk about Monitoring and Observability, they usually mean collecting metrics, logs and traces so you can understand how a system behaves. It appears in almost every serious software project, whether or not developers notice it.

A system that cannot be observed cannot be reliably operated. Automation turns risky manual steps into repeatable and reviewable processes.

Four ideas sit at the heart of Monitoring and Observability: metrics, logs, traces, and alerts. Each one builds on the one before it. Sketching alerts on paper before coding helps many learners see how the pieces fit.

If you are comfortable with Monitoring and Observability, explore CI/CD Pipelines next. The two often appear in the same project.

None of this needs special hardware or expensive software. A laptop, curiosity and a few small experiments are enough to get comfortable with Monitoring and Observability.
