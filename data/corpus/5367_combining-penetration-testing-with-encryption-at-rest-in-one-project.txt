Combining Penetration Testing with Encryption at Rest in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Penetration Testing and Encryption at Rest can work together in a single project.

Penetration Testing is authorized attempts to find and exploit weaknesses in a system before real attackers do. Encryption at Rest is protecting stored data by encrypting it on disk or in a database.

Imagine a project that involves pre-launch security checks and protecting backups. Penetration Testing brings scoping, while Encryption at Rest brings symmetric keys.

A sensible plan is to start with OWASP ZAP for the Penetration Testing part and LUKS for the Encryption at Rest part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: ignoring the findings afterward on one side, and no key rotation on the other.

Picture the whole as hiring a locksmith to test your doors, working alongside a locked safe inside a locked room.

Two habits help: first, get written authorization before any test; second, keep keys in a separate service from the data.
