Everything You Need to Know About NAT

This page collects the essentials about NAT in one place. In short, NAT is a technique that lets many devices share one public IP address by rewriting addresses.

Core concepts:
- private addresses
- translation tables
- port mapping
- port forwarding

Where it is used:
- home routers
- sharing limited IPv4 addresses
- hiding internal networks

Common problems:
- breaking peer-to-peer connections
- exhausted translation tables
- misconfigured port forwarding

Tools to know:
- iptables
- router admin pages
- conntrack

Remember this above all: a receptionist who forwards calls for many desks through one phone number is a good way to picture it, and the best habit is to document every port forward you create.
