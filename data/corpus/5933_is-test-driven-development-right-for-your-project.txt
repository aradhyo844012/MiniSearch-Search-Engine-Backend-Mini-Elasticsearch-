Is Test-Driven Development Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Test-Driven Development, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Test-Driven Development is a workflow where you write a failing test first and then write code to make it pass.

It is a good fit when your work involves designing small modules or preventing regressions, and when your team understands red, green, refactor.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn small steps, or when you cannot manage these risks: writing tests that are too large, skipping the refactor step, and testing implementation details.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether test first and fast feedback are part of the answer. If they are, Test-Driven Development is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with gaining confidence for refactoring and keep each red-green cycle to a few minutes.
