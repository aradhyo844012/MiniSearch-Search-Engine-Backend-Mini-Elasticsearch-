Combining Distributed Locks with Vector Clocks in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Distributed Locks and Vector Clocks can work together in a single project.

Distributed Locks is mechanisms that ensure only one process at a time performs a critical action across machines. Vector Clocks is counters that let distributed processes track the order of events without a shared clock.

Imagine a project that involves running scheduled jobs once and detecting concurrent edits. Distributed Locks brings lease times, while Vector Clocks brings logical time.

A sensible plan is to start with Redis for the Distributed Locks part and Riak for the Vector Clocks part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: clock drift on one side, and confusing them with wall-clock time on the other.

Picture the whole as a single key to a shared meeting room that expires automatically, working alongside each person in a group chat keeping a tally of messages seen from everyone.

Two habits help: first, always use a timeout together with a fencing token; second, compare clocks entry by entry to detect true concurrency.
