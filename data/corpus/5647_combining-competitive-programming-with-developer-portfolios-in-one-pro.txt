Combining Competitive Programming with Developer Portfolios in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Competitive Programming and Developer Portfolios can work together in a single project.

Competitive Programming is solving algorithmic problems under time limits to build speed and problem-solving skills. Developer Portfolios is a collection of projects and writing that shows what a developer can build.

Imagine a project that involves interview preparation and applying for internships. Competitive Programming brings problem patterns, while Developer Portfolios brings projects.

A sensible plan is to start with Codeforces for the Competitive Programming part and GitHub Pages for the Developer Portfolios part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: skipping fundamentals on one side, and no explanation of decisions on the other.

Picture the whole as athletic training for problem solving, working alongside a shop window displaying your best work.

Two habits help: first, review problems you could not solve and study the editorial; second, feature a few polished projects rather than many half-finished ones.
