The Trade-offs of Tries

Every design choice has a cost, and Tries is no exception. When people talk about Tries, they usually mean tree structures that store strings character by character so shared prefixes are stored once. It appears in almost every serious software project, whether or not developers notice it.

On the positive side, Tries is well suited to autocomplete and spell checking. On the other hand, it is not free. Keep these risks in mind: high memory use for sparse data; forgetting end-of-word markers; assuming case-insensitive matching. Whether the trade is worth it depends on how much prefix sharing and nodes and edges matter to your project.

Tests double as documentation for your teammates. Keep notes on what worked. A short README explaining your decisions about Tries helps the next developer.

With steady practice, Tries will stop feeling abstract and become a tool you reach for naturally.
