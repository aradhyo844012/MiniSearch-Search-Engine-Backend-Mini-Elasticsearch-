The Trade-offs of Hash Tables

Every design choice has a cost, and Hash Tables is no exception. If you build software for long enough, you will run into Hash Tables. The term refers to a way of storing key-value pairs so that lookups take roughly constant time.

On the positive side, Hash Tables is well suited to implementing dictionaries and sets and caching computed results. On the other hand, it is not free. Keep these risks in mind: poor hash functions cause clustering; resizing is expensive at scale; iteration order is not always stable. Whether the trade is worth it depends on how much hash function and buckets matter to your project.
