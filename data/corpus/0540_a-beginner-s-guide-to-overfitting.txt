A Beginner's Guide to Overfitting

This guide is written for readers who are new to Overfitting and want a clear starting point. The term Overfitting refers to when a model learns the training data too closely and performs poorly on new data. This article explains the idea in plain language and points out where it fits in real projects.

A model is only as good as the data used to train and test it.

Ask a teammate to explain regularization back to you; teaching is a quick way to find gaps. Keep an eye on this risk during code review: evaluating only on training data.

To understand Overfitting, start with training and validation error. Then move on to model complexity, and finally look at regularization and cross-validation. Understanding model complexity first often makes regularization much easier to reason about.

People use Overfitting for diagnosing weak models, for choosing simpler models, and for tuning hyperparameters.

In practice you can start with scikit-learn, then explore early stopping or learning curves as your needs grow.

Beginners often stumble on three things. First: evaluating only on training data. Second: leaking test data. Third: adding complexity without evidence.

Read the official documentation for early stopping; it often explains model complexity better than blog posts do. Ask a teammate to explain regularization back to you; teaching is a quick way to find gaps. Write a few tests that show how Overfitting should behave. Tests double as documentation for your teammates.

Keep experimenting, keep notes, and revisit Overfitting whenever a new project gives you a reason.
