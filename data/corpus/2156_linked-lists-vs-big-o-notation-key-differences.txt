Linked Lists vs Big O Notation: Key Differences

Linked Lists and Big O Notation both belong to the area of Data Structures and Algorithms, but they solve different problems. This article compares them side by side so you can choose with confidence.

The term Linked Lists refers to chains of nodes where each node stores a value and a reference to the next node. Its core ideas are nodes, head and tail pointers, and singly and doubly linked lists.

The term Big O Notation refers to a way of describing how running time or memory grows as the input size grows. Its core ideas are upper bounds, constant, linear and logarithmic growth, and worst case.

The main difference is focus. Linked Lists puts the emphasis on nodes and head and tail pointers, while Big O Notation puts the emphasis on upper bounds and constant, linear and logarithmic growth.

Reach for Linked Lists when your task involves queues and stacks or undo histories. Reach for Big O Notation when your task involves comparing algorithms or predicting scaling problems.

Both have risks. With Linked Lists, watch for this: losing the head pointer. With Big O Notation, watch for this: ignoring constant factors entirely.

In practice you do not always have to choose. Many projects use Linked Lists alongside Big O Notation, with tools such as Python collections.deque and Python timeit. Most performance problems in everyday programs come from doing unnecessary work on large inputs.
