JWT vs Rate Limiting: Key Differences

JWT and Rate Limiting both belong to the area of Backend and APIs, but they solve different problems. This article compares them side by side so you can choose with confidence.

The term JWT refers to a compact token format that carries signed claims between a client and a server. Its core ideas are header, payload and signature, claims, and expiry.

The term Rate Limiting refers to controlling how many requests a client can make in a given period. Its core ideas are token bucket, fixed window, and sliding window.

The main difference is focus. JWT puts the emphasis on header, payload and signature and claims, while Rate Limiting puts the emphasis on token bucket and fixed window.

Reach for JWT when your task involves stateless API authentication or service-to-service calls. Reach for Rate Limiting when your task involves protecting APIs or stopping brute force attempts.

Both have risks. With JWT, watch for this: storing tokens in insecure places. With Rate Limiting, watch for this: limits that are too strict.

In practice you do not always have to choose. Many projects use JWT alongside Rate Limiting, with tools such as jwt.io and Redis. A backend turns requests from many clients into reliable, consistent responses.
