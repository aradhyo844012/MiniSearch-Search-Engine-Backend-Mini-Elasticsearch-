Inside Overfitting: How It Works

This article walks through what happens inside Overfitting, step by step. Put simply, Overfitting covers when a model learns the training data too closely and performs poorly on new data. Learning it well pays off across many areas of computing.

Four ideas sit at the heart of Overfitting: training and validation error, model complexity, regularization, and cross-validation. Each one builds on the one before it. A good way to remember training and validation error is to connect it with a concrete example from your own code.

First, training and validation error sets the stage for everything else. Next, model complexity builds on it. After that, regularization comes into play, and finally cross-validation ties the pieces together. Seeing the sequence once, with a tiny example, makes the whole topic easier to remember.

A model is only as good as the data used to train and test it. Simple baselines are worth building first because they show what improvement is realistic.

If you are comfortable with Overfitting, explore Model Evaluation next. The two often appear in the same project.

In short, careful study of Overfitting pays off. Spend time on training and validation error and model complexity, and the rest will follow.
