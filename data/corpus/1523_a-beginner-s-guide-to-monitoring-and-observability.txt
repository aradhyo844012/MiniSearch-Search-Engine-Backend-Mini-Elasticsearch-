A Beginner's Guide to Monitoring and Observability

This guide is written for readers who are new to Monitoring and Observability and want a clear starting point. Many students first meet Monitoring and Observability in a classroom, but it matters just as much in production systems. The term refers to collecting metrics, logs and traces so you can understand how a system behaves.

A system that cannot be observed cannot be reliably operated.

To understand Monitoring and Observability, start with metrics. Then move on to logs, and finally look at traces and alerts. A good way to remember metrics is to connect it with a concrete example from your own code.

A team working on detecting outages might adopt Monitoring and Observability after hitting the limits of a simpler approach. Other teams turn to it for finding slow requests or capacity planning.

In practice you can start with Prometheus, then explore Grafana or OpenTelemetry as your needs grow.

Three issues come up again and again: noisy alerts; logs without context; and dashboards nobody reads. Knowing them in advance saves hours of debugging.

Read the official documentation for Grafana; it often explains logs better than blog posts do. Automate repetitive steps so that results are easy to reproduce.

In short, careful study of Monitoring and Observability pays off. Spend time on metrics and logs, and the rest will follow.
