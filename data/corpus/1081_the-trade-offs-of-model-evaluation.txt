The Trade-offs of Model Evaluation

Every design choice has a cost, and Model Evaluation is no exception. When people talk about Model Evaluation, they usually mean measuring how well a machine learning model works on data it has not seen. It appears in almost every serious software project, whether or not developers notice it.

Reproducible experiments make it far easier to trust and improve a model.

On the positive side, Model Evaluation is well suited to comparing models and choosing thresholds. On the other hand, it is not free. Keep these risks in mind: using accuracy on imbalanced data; tuning on the test set; ignoring variance between runs. Whether the trade is worth it depends on how much accuracy and precision and recall matter to your project.

Three issues come up again and again: using accuracy on imbalanced data; tuning on the test set; and ignoring variance between runs. Knowing them in advance saves hours of debugging.

Keep an eye on this risk during code review: using accuracy on imbalanced data. Automate repetitive steps so that results are easy to reproduce.

None of this needs special hardware or expensive software. A laptop, curiosity and a few small experiments are enough to get comfortable with Model Evaluation.
