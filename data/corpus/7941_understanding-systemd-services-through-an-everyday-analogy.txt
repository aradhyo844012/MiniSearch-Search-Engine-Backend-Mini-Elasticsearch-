Understanding systemd Services Through an Everyday Analogy

Abstract ideas are easier to hold onto when you attach them to something familiar. So picture a building manager who opens each office in the right order every morning.

In that picture, unit files is the first rule you follow, targets is the next step, and service states and journal logs keep everything running smoothly.

Every analogy breaks down eventually. Real systemd Services is the init system and service manager used by most modern Linux distributions, and it involves details such as editing unit files without reloading the daemon that no everyday picture captures.

Use the analogy to remember the shape of the idea, and use documentation for systemctl to learn the exact behavior. The same picture helps when you explain starting services at boot to a friend.
