A Short History of HTML Semantics

Ideas behind HTML Semantics developed gradually as engineers ran into practical limits and shared what they learned. Many students first meet HTML Semantics in a classroom, but it matters just as much in production systems. The term refers to the practice of using HTML elements that describe the meaning of content.

Small improvements in markup and styling often have a large effect on usability. Web pages must work across many browsers, devices and network conditions.

To understand HTML Semantics, start with headings. Then move on to landmarks, and finally look at lists and tables and forms. Understanding landmarks first often makes lists and tables much easier to reason about.

A solid grasp of HTML Semantics also makes Browser Rendering easier to understand.

None of this needs special hardware or expensive software. A laptop, curiosity and a few small experiments are enough to get comfortable with HTML Semantics.
