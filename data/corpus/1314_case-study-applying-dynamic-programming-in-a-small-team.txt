Case Study: Applying Dynamic Programming in a Small Team

Imagine a small student team building a project that depends on Dynamic Programming. When people talk about Dynamic Programming, they usually mean a technique for solving problems by storing the answers to overlapping subproblems. It appears in almost every serious software project, whether or not developers notice it.

The team first spent a week learning overlapping subproblems and optimal substructure. Early on they hit a familiar problem: defining the state incorrectly. After fixing it, they used Python functools.lru_cache to check their work and adopted Dynamic Programming for computing edit distance. Looking back, they said the biggest lesson was to document decisions as they went.

Watch out for these problems: defining the state incorrectly; storing more state than needed; and ignoring base cases.

A solid grasp of Dynamic Programming also makes Big O Notation easier to understand.

Keep experimenting, keep notes, and revisit Dynamic Programming whenever a new project gives you a reason.
