Teaching Shaders to a Teammate

Explaining Shaders to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: a tiny painter that colors each pixel following your instructions. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Shaders is small programs that run on the graphics card to control how things are drawn. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving lighting effects, naming vertex shaders and fragment shaders as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain uniforms in Shaders back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, branches that slow the GPU, and suggest this habit: start from a minimal shader and add one effect at a time.
