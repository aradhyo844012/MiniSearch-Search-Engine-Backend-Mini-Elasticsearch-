Distributed Locks vs CQRS: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Distributed Locks or CQRS first. Both sit in the area of Distributed Systems, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Distributed Locks is mechanisms that ensure only one process at a time performs a critical action across machines, and it is closely tied to lease times and fencing tokens. CQRS, by contrast, is a pattern that separates the models used to change data from the models used to read it, and it is closely tied to commands and queries.

Choose Distributed Locks first if you care about running scheduled jobs once or protecting shared resources. Choose CQRS first if you care about high-read dashboards or complex domains.

Think of Distributed Locks as a single key to a shared meeting room that expires automatically, and of CQRS as a restaurant with one counter for orders and another for pickup. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Distributed Locks, avoid locks that never expire. With CQRS, avoid adding it to simple apps.

A good habit for Distributed Locks is to always use a timeout together with a fencing token. A good habit for CQRS is to start with a single model and split only when reads and writes truly diverge.
