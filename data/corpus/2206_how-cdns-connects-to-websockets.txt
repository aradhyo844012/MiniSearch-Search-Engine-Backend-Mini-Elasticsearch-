How CDNs Connects to WebSockets

CDNs and WebSockets are closely connected parts of Networking. Seeing how they fit together helps you learn both faster.

A project involving serving images and scripts may also involve chat applications. In such a project, edge servers and persistent connections often influence each other.

Start with CDNs: the term refers to networks of servers around the world that deliver static content from a location close to the user. Then look at WebSockets, which refers to a protocol that keeps a two-way connection open between a browser and a server.

A useful exercise is to take one example, such as speeding up global sites, and explain how messages would change the design.

Common problems are shared too. For CDNs, watch for this: forgetting to purge old files. For WebSockets, watch for this: unbounded message queues. Every web request crosses several layers of protocols before it reaches an application.

Spend a few hours on each topic, then combine them in one small project using Cloudflare or Socket.IO.
