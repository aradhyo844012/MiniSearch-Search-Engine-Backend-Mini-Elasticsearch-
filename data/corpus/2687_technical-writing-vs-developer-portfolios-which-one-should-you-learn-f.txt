Technical Writing vs Developer Portfolios: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Technical Writing or Developer Portfolios first. Both sit in the area of Developer Career and Tools, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Technical Writing is explaining technical ideas clearly in writing for a chosen audience, and it is closely tied to audience and structure. Developer Portfolios, by contrast, is a collection of projects and writing that shows what a developer can build, and it is closely tied to projects and README quality.

Choose Technical Writing first if you care about documentation or blog posts. Choose Developer Portfolios first if you care about applying for internships or showing skills to recruiters.

Think of Technical Writing as giving directions to a stranger, and of Developer Portfolios as a shop window displaying your best work. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Technical Writing, avoid assuming too much knowledge. With Developer Portfolios, avoid too many unfinished projects.

A good habit for Technical Writing is to state the main point in the first two sentences. A good habit for Developer Portfolios is to feature a few polished projects rather than many half-finished ones.
