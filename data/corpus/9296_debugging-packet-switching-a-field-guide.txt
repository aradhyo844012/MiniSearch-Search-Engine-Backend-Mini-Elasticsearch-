Debugging Packet Switching: A Field Guide

When something goes wrong around Packet Switching, resist the urge to guess. Work through the problem in order.

Step 1: reproduce the problem with the smallest possible example.
Step 2: list the usual suspects: packet loss, out-of-order delivery, and large packets being fragmented.
Step 3: revisit packets and headers, since misunderstandings there cause many bugs.
Step 4: use Wireshark or similar tools to look inside.
Step 5: write a test that fails because of the bug and passes after the fix.

Many bugs involving Packet Switching in work such as internet traffic come down to the first suspect on the list. Check the simple causes before suspecting anything exotic.

Finally, capture a few packets to see the headers for yourself. It prevents a surprising number of repeat problems.
