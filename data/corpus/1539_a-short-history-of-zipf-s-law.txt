A Short History of Zipf's Law

Ideas behind Zipf's Law developed gradually as engineers ran into practical limits and shared what they learned. When people talk about Zipf's Law, they usually mean the observation that a word's frequency is roughly inversely related to its rank in natural language. It appears in almost every serious software project, whether or not developers notice it.

Real query logs reveal what people actually look for, which is rarely what designers expect. Search quality depends on how text is prepared before it is ever indexed.

Users judge a search engine by whether the right result appears near the top.

Four ideas sit at the heart of Zipf's Law: rank and frequency, power laws, log-log plots, and long tails. Each one builds on the one before it. A good way to remember rank and frequency is to connect it with a concrete example from your own code.

If you are comfortable with Zipf's Law, explore TF-IDF next. The two often appear in the same project.

None of this needs special hardware or expensive software. A laptop, curiosity and a few small experiments are enough to get comfortable with Zipf's Law.
