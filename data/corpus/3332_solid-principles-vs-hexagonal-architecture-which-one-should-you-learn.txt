SOLID Principles vs Hexagonal Architecture: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study SOLID Principles or Hexagonal Architecture first. Both sit in the area of Software Design, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

SOLID Principles is five guidelines for designing object-oriented code that is easy to change, and it is closely tied to single responsibility and the open-closed principle. Hexagonal Architecture, by contrast, is a design that keeps core logic separate from outside systems through ports and adapters, and it is closely tied to ports and adapters.

Choose SOLID Principles first if you care about structuring classes or reviewing designs. Choose Hexagonal Architecture first if you care about testable business logic or swapping databases.

Think of SOLID Principles as house rules that keep a shared kitchen tidy, and of Hexagonal Architecture as a power strip that lets any plug connect to the same core. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With SOLID Principles, avoid applying them rigidly to tiny scripts. With Hexagonal Architecture, avoid too many layers for small apps.

A good habit for SOLID Principles is to apply them when a design starts to hurt, not before. A good habit for Hexagonal Architecture is to keep framework imports out of the core domain.
