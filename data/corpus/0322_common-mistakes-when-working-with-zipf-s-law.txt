Common Mistakes When Working with Zipf's Law

Almost everyone makes a few avoidable errors when first working with Zipf's Law. Put simply, Zipf's Law covers the observation that a word's frequency is roughly inversely related to its rank in natural language. Learning it well pays off across many areas of computing.

Three issues come up again and again: testing it on random text; reading too much into a perfect fit; and ignoring small samples. Knowing them in advance saves hours of debugging.

Consider the first problem: testing it on random text. A short test or a careful review comment usually catches it. The second problem is similar: reading too much into a perfect fit.
