The Trade-offs of Overfitting

Every design choice has a cost, and Overfitting is no exception. The term Overfitting refers to when a model learns the training data too closely and performs poorly on new data. This article explains the idea in plain language and points out where it fits in real projects.

On the positive side, Overfitting is well suited to diagnosing weak models and choosing simpler models. On the other hand, it is not free. Keep these risks in mind: evaluating only on training data; leaking test data; adding complexity without evidence. Whether the trade is worth it depends on how much training and validation error and model complexity matter to your project.

Start small by applying Overfitting to one case, such as diagnosing weak models, before rolling it out more widely. Read the official documentation for early stopping; it often explains model complexity better than blog posts do.

In short, careful study of Overfitting pays off. Spend time on training and validation error and model complexity, and the rest will follow.
