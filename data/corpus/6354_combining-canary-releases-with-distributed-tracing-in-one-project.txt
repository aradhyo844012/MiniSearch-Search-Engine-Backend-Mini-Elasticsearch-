Combining Canary Releases with Distributed Tracing in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Canary Releases and Distributed Tracing can work together in a single project.

Canary Releases is a rollout strategy that sends a small share of traffic to a new version before everyone gets it. Distributed Tracing is following a single request as it passes through many services.

Imagine a project that involves safer releases and finding slow services. Canary Releases brings traffic percentages, while Distributed Tracing brings spans.

A sensible plan is to start with Argo Rollouts for the Canary Releases part and OpenTelemetry for the Distributed Tracing part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: no clear success metrics on one side, and sampling too aggressively on the other.

Picture the whole as a canary in a coal mine warning miners before the danger spreads, working alongside a parcel tracking page that shows every stop on the route.

Two habits help: first, define rollback conditions before starting the rollout; second, propagate trace IDs through every outgoing call.
