Combining Offline-First Apps with Raspberry Pi in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Offline-First Apps and Raspberry Pi can work together in a single project.

Offline-First Apps is apps designed to work without a network and sync when a connection returns. Raspberry Pi is a small, low-cost computer popular for learning, prototypes and home projects.

Imagine a project that involves field service tools and home automation. Offline-First Apps brings local storage, while Raspberry Pi brings GPIO pins.

A sensible plan is to start with SQLite for the Offline-First Apps part and Raspberry Pi OS for the Raspberry Pi part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: unbounded local data on one side, and inadequate power supplies on the other.

Picture the whole as writing in a notebook and mailing the pages later, working alongside a pocket-sized workshop for experiments.

Two habits help: first, show users which changes have not synced yet; second, use a quality power supply and a reliable memory card.
