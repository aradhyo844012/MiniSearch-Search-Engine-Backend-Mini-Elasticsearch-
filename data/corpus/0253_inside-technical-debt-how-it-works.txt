Inside Technical Debt: How It Works

This article walks through what happens inside Technical Debt, step by step. If you build software for long enough, you will run into Technical Debt. The term refers to the future cost of shortcuts taken in code, design or documentation.

The vocabulary of Technical Debt is small. Learn shortcuts, interest, refactoring backlog, and code smells, and most documentation will start to make sense. A good way to remember shortcuts is to connect it with a concrete example from your own code.

First, shortcuts sets the stage for everything else. Next, interest builds on it. After that, refactoring backlog comes into play, and finally code smells ties the pieces together. Seeing the sequence once, with a tiny example, makes the whole topic easier to remember.

Teams succeed when they agree on a few simple practices and follow them consistently. Readable code saves more time than clever code over the long run.

A solid grasp of Technical Debt also makes Refactoring easier to understand.

In short, careful study of Technical Debt pays off. Spend time on shortcuts and interest, and the rest will follow.
