LSM Trees vs Time-Series Databases: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study LSM Trees or Time-Series Databases first. Both sit in the area of Storage Engines and Databases, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

LSM Trees is storage structures that buffer writes in memory and merge them into sorted files on disk, and it is closely tied to memtable and sorted string tables. Time-Series Databases, by contrast, is databases optimized for storing and querying data points ordered by time, and it is closely tied to timestamps and retention policies.

Choose LSM Trees first if you care about write-heavy databases or time-ordered data. Choose Time-Series Databases first if you care about server metrics or sensor readings.

Think of LSM Trees as a mail room that sorts incoming letters in batches rather than one by one, and of Time-Series Databases as a long logbook where every line starts with the time. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With LSM Trees, avoid read amplification. With Time-Series Databases, avoid high-cardinality tags.

A good habit for LSM Trees is to watch compaction metrics before blaming the application. A good habit for Time-Series Databases is to include a time range in every query.
