Debugging DHCP: A Field Guide

When something goes wrong around DHCP, resist the urge to guess. Work through the problem in order.

Step 1: reproduce the problem with the smallest possible example.
Step 2: list the usual suspects: rogue DHCP servers, exhausted address pools, and conflicting static addresses.
Step 3: revisit leases and address pools, since misunderstandings there cause many bugs.
Step 4: use dnsmasq or similar tools to look inside.
Step 5: write a test that fails because of the bug and passes after the fix.

Many bugs involving DHCP in work such as home and office networks come down to the first suspect on the list. Check the simple causes before suspecting anything exotic.

Finally, reserve fixed addresses for servers and printers. It prevents a surprising number of repeat problems.
