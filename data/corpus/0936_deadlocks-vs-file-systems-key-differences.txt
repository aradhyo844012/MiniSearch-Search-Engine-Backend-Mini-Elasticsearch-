Deadlocks vs File Systems: Key Differences

Deadlocks and File Systems both belong to the area of Operating Systems, but they solve different problems. This article compares them side by side so you can choose with confidence.

The term Deadlocks refers to situations where two or more tasks wait for each other forever. Its core ideas are mutual exclusion, hold and wait, and circular wait.

The term File Systems refers to the part of an operating system that organizes how data is stored and found on disk. Its core ideas are files and directories, inodes, and permissions.

The main difference is focus. Deadlocks puts the emphasis on mutual exclusion and hold and wait, while File Systems puts the emphasis on files and directories and inodes.

Reach for Deadlocks when your task involves database lock conflicts or multi-threaded programs. Reach for File Systems when your task involves storing documents or managing logs.

Both have risks. With Deadlocks, watch for this: acquiring locks in inconsistent order. With File Systems, watch for this: running out of inodes.

In practice you do not always have to choose. Many projects use Deadlocks alongside File Systems, with tools such as thread dumps and ext4. Understanding what the kernel does explains many surprising performance problems.
