Developer Portfolios vs Reading Documentation: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Developer Portfolios or Reading Documentation first. Both sit in the area of Developer Career and Tools, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Developer Portfolios is a collection of projects and writing that shows what a developer can build, and it is closely tied to projects and README quality. Reading Documentation, by contrast, is the skill of finding accurate answers in official guides and references, and it is closely tied to search and tutorials versus references.

Choose Developer Portfolios first if you care about applying for internships or showing skills to recruiters. Choose Reading Documentation first if you care about learning new libraries or solving errors.

Think of Developer Portfolios as a shop window displaying your best work, and of Reading Documentation as reading the manual before assembling the furniture. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Developer Portfolios, avoid too many unfinished projects. With Reading Documentation, avoid reading the wrong version.

A good habit for Developer Portfolios is to feature a few polished projects rather than many half-finished ones. A good habit for Reading Documentation is to check that the documentation version matches your installed version.
