Debugging Load Testing: A Field Guide

When something goes wrong around Load Testing, resist the urge to guess. Work through the problem in order.

Step 1: reproduce the problem with the smallest possible example.
Step 2: list the usual suspects: testing unrealistic traffic, ignoring warm-up, and using average latency only.
Step 3: revisit virtual users and throughput, since misunderstandings there cause many bugs.
Step 4: use k6 or similar tools to look inside.
Step 5: write a test that fails because of the bug and passes after the fix.

Many bugs involving Load Testing in work such as capacity planning come down to the first suspect on the list. Check the simple causes before suspecting anything exotic.

Finally, report p95 and p99 latency, not only averages. It prevents a surprising number of repeat problems.
