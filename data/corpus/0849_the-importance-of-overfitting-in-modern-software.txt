The Importance of Overfitting in Modern Software

It is easy to underestimate Overfitting until something goes wrong without it. Many students first meet Overfitting in a classroom, but it matters just as much in production systems. The term refers to when a model learns the training data too closely and performs poorly on new data.

Simple baselines are worth building first because they show what improvement is realistic. Reproducible experiments make it far easier to trust and improve a model.

People use Overfitting for diagnosing weak models, for choosing simpler models, and for tuning hyperparameters.

Beginners often stumble on three things. First: evaluating only on training data. Second: leaking test data. Third: adding complexity without evidence.

A solid grasp of Overfitting also makes Gradient Descent easier to understand.

None of this needs special hardware or expensive software. A laptop, curiosity and a few small experiments are enough to get comfortable with Overfitting.
