Combining Distributed Locks with Saga Pattern in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Distributed Locks and Saga Pattern can work together in a single project.

Distributed Locks is mechanisms that ensure only one process at a time performs a critical action across machines. Saga Pattern is a way of managing long business operations across services using a sequence of local steps and compensating actions.

Imagine a project that involves running scheduled jobs once and order processing. Distributed Locks brings lease times, while Saga Pattern brings local transactions.

A sensible plan is to start with Redis for the Distributed Locks part and Temporal for the Saga Pattern part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: clock drift on one side, and steps that are not idempotent on the other.

Picture the whole as a single key to a shared meeting room that expires automatically, working alongside booking a trip with separate hotel, flight and car reservations that can each be cancelled.

Two habits help: first, always use a timeout together with a fencing token; second, make every step and its compensation idempotent.
