Scaling Considerations for Computability and the Halting Problem

A design that works for a classroom project can behave very differently under real load. Here is how Computability and the Halting Problem changes as things grow.

Small scale: almost any approach works. Focus on understanding Turing machines and getting understanding the limits of tools right.

Medium scale: problems start to appear. Watch for assuming everything is solvable and confusing hard with undecidable, and measure with Turing machine simulators before changing anything.

Large scale: the halting problem and reductions move from background details to central design questions. Teams often revisit their choices around proofs of impossibility at this stage.

Before you scale, learn the diagonal argument step by step. It is cheaper to build that habit early than to fix a crisis later.
