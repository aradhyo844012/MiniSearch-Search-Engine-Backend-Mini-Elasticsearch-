Is Mocking and Stubbing Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Mocking and Stubbing, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Mocking and Stubbing is replacing real dependencies with controlled fakes during tests.

It is a good fit when your work involves isolating units or simulating failures, and when your team understands mocks.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn stubs, or when you cannot manage these risks: over-mocking, tests that mirror the implementation, and mocks that drift from reality.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether spies and fakes are part of the answer. If they are, Mocking and Stubbing is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with avoiding network calls and prefer simple fakes over deep mock chains.
