Debugging Retrieval-Augmented Generation: A Field Guide

When something goes wrong around Retrieval-Augmented Generation, resist the urge to guess. Work through the problem in order.

Step 1: reproduce the problem with the smallest possible example.
Step 2: list the usual suspects: poor chunking, irrelevant retrieved text, and no evaluation of the answers.
Step 3: revisit retrieval and chunking, since misunderstandings there cause many bugs.
Step 4: use LangChain or similar tools to look inside.
Step 5: write a test that fails because of the bug and passes after the fix.

Many bugs involving Retrieval-Augmented Generation in work such as company knowledge assistants come down to the first suspect on the list. Check the simple causes before suspecting anything exotic.

Finally, evaluate retrieval quality separately from answer quality. It prevents a surprising number of repeat problems.
