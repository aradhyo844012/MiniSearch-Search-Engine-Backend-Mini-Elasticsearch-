Teaching B-Trees on Disk to a Teammate

Explaining B-Trees on Disk to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: a filing cabinet where each drawer tells you which drawer to open next. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: B-Trees on Disk is balanced tree structures designed to read large blocks from disk efficiently. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving database indexes, naming pages and branching factor as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain node splits in B-Trees on Disk back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, random writes on fragmented pages, and suggest this habit: keep indexed keys short so more of them fit on a page.
