Combining Embeddings with Retrieval-Augmented Generation in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Embeddings and Retrieval-Augmented Generation can work together in a single project.

Embeddings is lists of numbers that represent words, images or items so that similar things sit close together. Retrieval-Augmented Generation is a technique that gives a language model relevant documents to read before it answers.

Imagine a project that involves semantic search and company knowledge assistants. Embeddings brings vectors, while Retrieval-Augmented Generation brings retrieval.

A sensible plan is to start with sentence-transformers for the Embeddings part and LangChain for the Retrieval-Augmented Generation part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: ignoring normalization on one side, and irrelevant retrieved text on the other.

Picture the whole as coordinates on a map where similar places are close together, working alongside an open-book exam where the student first looks up the right pages.

Two habits help: first, use the same embedding model for indexing and querying; second, evaluate retrieval quality separately from answer quality.
