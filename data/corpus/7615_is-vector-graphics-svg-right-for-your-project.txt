Is Vector Graphics (SVG) Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Vector Graphics (SVG), ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Vector Graphics (SVG) is images described by shapes and paths so they stay sharp at any size.

It is a good fit when your work involves logos or icons, and when your team understands paths.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn the viewBox, or when you cannot manage these risks: overly complex paths, a missing viewBox, and inconsistent stroke widths.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether fill and stroke and transforms are part of the answer. If they are, Vector Graphics (SVG) is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with charts and set a viewBox so the image scales cleanly.
