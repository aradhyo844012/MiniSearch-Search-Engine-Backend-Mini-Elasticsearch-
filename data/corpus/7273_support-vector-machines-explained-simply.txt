Support Vector Machines Explained Simply

Here is Support Vector Machines without the jargon: it is models that classify data by finding the boundary with the widest margin between classes.

Think of Support Vector Machines as drawing the widest road between two neighborhoods. Keep that picture in mind as you read on.

The vocabulary has four main pieces: margin, support vectors, kernels, and regularization. You do not need to master them all at once. Start with margin, then add support vectors.

People reach for Support Vector Machines when they work on text classification, small high-dimensional datasets, and image recognition baselines.

One practical tip is to scale features and try a linear kernel first.

Once Support Vector Machines feels comfortable, read about Transformers, which lives in the same neighborhood of ideas.
