Mistakes to Avoid with Fenwick Trees

Everyone learns Fenwick Trees partly through mistakes. Here are three worth avoiding.

Mistake one: using zero-based indexes by mistake. It usually appears when someone skips binary indexed trees and jumps straight to implementation. The fix is to slow down and review the basics.

Mistake two: forgetting that nodes store partial sums. It tends to be noticed late, often during testing of counting inversions. A short checklist during review catches it early.

Mistake three: using it for operations that cannot be reversed. It is easy to miss because everything seems to work at first. Keep an eye on point updates to spot it.

If you remember only one rule, make it this: start indexing from one to keep the bit tricks simple.
