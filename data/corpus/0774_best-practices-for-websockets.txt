Best Practices for WebSockets

The practices below come from patterns that tend to work well across many teams and projects. When people talk about WebSockets, they usually mean a protocol that keeps a two-way connection open between a browser and a server. It appears in almost every serious software project, whether or not developers notice it.

1. Keep notes on what worked. A short README explaining your decisions about WebSockets helps the next developer.
2. Ask a teammate to explain heartbeats back to you; teaching is a quick way to find gaps.
3. Keep an eye on this risk during code review: forgetting reconnection logic.
4. Measure before you optimize. Use Socket.IO or a simple timer to see where time is actually spent.
5. Write a few tests that show how WebSockets should behave. Tests double as documentation for your teammates.

In practice you can start with Socket.IO, then explore ws library or Python websockets as your needs grow.

If you are comfortable with WebSockets, explore DNS next. The two often appear in the same project.

With steady practice, WebSockets will stop feeling abstract and become a tool you reach for naturally.
