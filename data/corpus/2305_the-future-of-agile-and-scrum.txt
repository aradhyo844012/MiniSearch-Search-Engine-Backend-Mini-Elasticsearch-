The Future of Agile and Scrum

Predicting the future is risky, but the direction of Agile and Scrum is easy to describe in general terms. If you build software for long enough, you will run into Agile and Scrum. The term refers to iterative ways of planning and delivering software in short cycles.

Readable code saves more time than clever code over the long run.

Agile and Scrum and Technical Debt are closely related, so learning both gives a fuller picture of Software Engineering.

Expect tools such as Jira and Trello to keep improving, and expect newer ideas to keep building on sprints. The basics, though, tend to stay the same, so time spent on backlogs and stand-ups is rarely wasted. Anyone working on student team projects will keep meeting Agile and Scrum for years to come.

Read the official documentation for Trello; it often explains backlogs better than blog posts do. Automate repetitive steps so that results are easy to reproduce.

Keep experimenting, keep notes, and revisit Agile and Scrum whenever a new project gives you a reason.
