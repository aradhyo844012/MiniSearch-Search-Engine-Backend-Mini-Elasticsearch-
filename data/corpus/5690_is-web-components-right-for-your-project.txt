Is Web Components Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Web Components, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Web Components is browser-native custom elements with their own markup, style and behavior.

It is a good fit when your work involves design system components or widgets reused across frameworks, and when your team understands custom elements.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn shadow DOM, or when you cannot manage these risks: styling surprises across the shadow boundary, poor accessibility, and heavy polyfills.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether templates and slots are part of the answer. If they are, Web Components is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with embeddable tools and document the attributes and events like a public API.
