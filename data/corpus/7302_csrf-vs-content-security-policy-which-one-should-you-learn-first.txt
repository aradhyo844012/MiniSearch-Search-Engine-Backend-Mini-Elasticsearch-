CSRF vs Content Security Policy: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study CSRF or Content Security Policy first. Both sit in the area of Application Security, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

CSRF is an attack that tricks a logged-in user's browser into sending unwanted requests to a site, and it is closely tied to session cookies and anti-CSRF tokens. Content Security Policy, by contrast, is a browser feature that limits which scripts and resources a page may load, and it is closely tied to directives and nonces.

Choose CSRF first if you care about protecting forms or securing account settings. Choose Content Security Policy first if you care about reducing the impact of XSS or blocking unwanted scripts.

Think of CSRF as a forged letter that a bank clerk stamps because your ID is already on file, and of Content Security Policy as a guest list at the door of a building. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With CSRF, avoid using GET requests for state changes. With Content Security Policy, avoid allowing unsafe-inline.

A good habit for CSRF is to combine SameSite cookies with anti-CSRF tokens. A good habit for Content Security Policy is to start in report-only mode to learn what would break.
