A Short History of Big O Notation

Ideas behind Big O Notation developed gradually as engineers ran into practical limits and shared what they learned. Many students first meet Big O Notation in a classroom, but it matters just as much in production systems. The term refers to a way of describing how running time or memory grows as the input size grows.

Most performance problems in everyday programs come from doing unnecessary work on large inputs.

Algorithms are easiest to learn by tracing small examples on paper before writing any code. Interviewers and engineers alike value clear reasoning about time and space costs.

The vocabulary of Big O Notation is small. Learn upper bounds, constant, linear and logarithmic growth, worst case, and amortized cost, and most documentation will start to make sense. Sketching amortized cost on paper before coding helps many learners see how the pieces fit.

If you are comfortable with Big O Notation, explore Hash Tables next. The two often appear in the same project.

In short, careful study of Big O Notation pays off. Spend time on upper bounds and constant, linear and logarithmic growth, and the rest will follow.
