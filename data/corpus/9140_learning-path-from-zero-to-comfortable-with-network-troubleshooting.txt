Learning Path: From Zero to Comfortable with Network Troubleshooting

This path is for a learner who has never studied Network Troubleshooting before. Move at your own speed.

Stage 1, the basics: read a short definition (Network Troubleshooting is a step-by-step process for finding out why a network connection fails or is slow) and learn protocol layers and ping and traceroute.

Stage 2, hands-on: install ping, follow a tutorial, and build a small example involving fixing office connectivity.

Stage 3, depth: study DNS checks and packet captures, then deliberately explore this problem: changing several things at once.

Stage 4, application: apply Network Troubleshooting to diagnosing slow apps, compare traceroute with Wireshark, and write up your results.

Stage 5, review: teach Network Troubleshooting to someone else, and then move on to HTTP/2 and HTTP/3.
