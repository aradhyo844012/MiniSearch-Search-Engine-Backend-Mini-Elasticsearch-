A Short History of Public Key Cryptography

Ideas behind Public Key Cryptography developed gradually as engineers ran into practical limits and shared what they learned. Many students first meet Public Key Cryptography in a classroom, but it matters just as much in production systems. The term refers to encryption that uses a pair of keys, one public and one private.

Security is a habit applied throughout development, not a feature added at the end. Attackers usually choose the easiest weakness, not the most interesting one.

Four ideas sit at the heart of Public Key Cryptography: key pairs, digital signatures, certificates, and key exchange. Each one builds on the one before it. A good way to remember key pairs is to connect it with a concrete example from your own code.
