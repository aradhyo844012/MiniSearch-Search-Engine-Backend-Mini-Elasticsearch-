A Short History of Model Evaluation

Ideas behind Model Evaluation developed gradually as engineers ran into practical limits and shared what they learned. Put simply, Model Evaluation covers measuring how well a machine learning model works on data it has not seen. Learning it well pays off across many areas of computing.

A model is only as good as the data used to train and test it.

Four ideas sit at the heart of Model Evaluation: accuracy, precision and recall, confusion matrices, and validation sets. Each one builds on the one before it. Understanding precision and recall first often makes confusion matrices much easier to reason about.

Ask a teammate to explain confusion matrices back to you; teaching is a quick way to find gaps. Measure before you optimize. Use scikit-learn metrics or a simple timer to see where time is actually spent.

A solid grasp of Model Evaluation also makes Neural Networks easier to understand.

None of this needs special hardware or expensive software. A laptop, curiosity and a few small experiments are enough to get comfortable with Model Evaluation.
