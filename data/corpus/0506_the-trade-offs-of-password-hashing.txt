The Trade-offs of Password Hashing

Every design choice has a cost, and Password Hashing is no exception. Put simply, Password Hashing covers storing passwords as one-way hashes so the originals are never kept. Learning it well pays off across many areas of computing.

On the positive side, Password Hashing is well suited to user login systems and protecting data after a breach. On the other hand, it is not free. Keep these risks in mind: using fast hashes like plain SHA-256; reusing salts; storing passwords in plain text. Whether the trade is worth it depends on how much salts and slow hash functions matter to your project.
