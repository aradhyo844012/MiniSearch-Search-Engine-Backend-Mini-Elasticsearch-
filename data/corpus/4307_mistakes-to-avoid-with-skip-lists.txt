Mistakes to Avoid with Skip Lists

Everyone learns Skip Lists partly through mistakes. Here are three worth avoiding.

Mistake one: poor random number choices. It usually appears when someone skips multiple levels and jumps straight to implementation. The fix is to slow down and review the basics.

Mistake two: memory overhead. It tends to be noticed late, often during testing of ordered in-memory indexes. A short checklist during review catches it early.

Mistake three: harder debugging than arrays. It is easy to miss because everything seems to work at first. Keep an eye on expected logarithmic time to spot it.

If you remember only one rule, make it this: draw several levels by hand to see how a search moves down.
