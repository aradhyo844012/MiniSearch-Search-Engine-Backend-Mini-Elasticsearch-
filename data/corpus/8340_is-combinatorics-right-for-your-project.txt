Is Combinatorics Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Combinatorics, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Combinatorics is the art of counting arrangements and selections.

It is a good fit when your work involves analyzing algorithms or probability, and when your team understands permutations.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn combinations, or when you cannot manage these risks: double counting, mixing order and no-order cases, and ignoring repetition.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether the pigeonhole principle and inclusion-exclusion are part of the answer. If they are, Combinatorics is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with estimating password strength and test counting formulas on tiny cases first.
