Skills to Build Around Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Knowing the definition of Retrieval-Augmented Generation is only the start. These skills turn that knowledge into something useful.

Foundations: be comfortable with retrieval and chunking, and able to explain them without notes.

Hands-on practice: spend real time with LangChain on a Retrieval-Augmented Generation task, and try at least one alternative such as FAISS.

Judgment: learn to decide when Retrieval-Augmented Generation fits a problem such as company knowledge assistants, and when something simpler is better.

Communication: practice describing it with an everyday picture, for example an open-book exam where the student first looks up the right pages.

Habits: evaluate retrieval quality separately from answer quality, and write down what you learn after each project.
