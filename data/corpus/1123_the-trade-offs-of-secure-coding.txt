The Trade-offs of Secure Coding

Every design choice has a cost, and Secure Coding is no exception. Put simply, Secure Coding covers writing software in ways that avoid common vulnerabilities. Learning it well pays off across many areas of computing.

On the positive side, Secure Coding is well suited to reviewing pull requests and building web services. On the other hand, it is not free. Keep these risks in mind: trusting user input; committing secrets to repositories; ignoring outdated libraries. Whether the trade is worth it depends on how much input validation and least privilege matter to your project.
