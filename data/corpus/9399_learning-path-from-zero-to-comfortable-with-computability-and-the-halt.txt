Learning Path: From Zero to Comfortable with Computability and the Halting Problem

This path is for a learner who has never studied Computability and the Halting Problem before. Move at your own speed.

Stage 1, the basics: read a short definition (Computability and the Halting Problem is the study of what problems computers can and cannot solve) and learn Turing machines and decidability.

Stage 2, hands-on: install Turing machine simulators, follow a tutorial, and build a small example involving understanding the limits of tools.

Stage 3, depth: study the halting problem and reductions, then deliberately explore this problem: assuming everything is solvable.

Stage 4, application: apply Computability and the Halting Problem to proofs of impossibility, compare textbooks with proof sketches, and write up your results.

Stage 5, review: teach Computability and the Halting Problem to someone else, and then move on to Discrete Mathematics.
