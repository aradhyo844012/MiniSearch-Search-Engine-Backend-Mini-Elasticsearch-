Combining B-Trees on Disk with Graph Databases in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how B-Trees on Disk and Graph Databases can work together in a single project.

B-Trees on Disk is balanced tree structures designed to read large blocks from disk efficiently. Graph Databases is databases that store data as nodes and relationships and query connections directly.

Imagine a project that involves database indexes and social networks. B-Trees on Disk brings pages, while Graph Databases brings nodes.

A sensible plan is to start with PostgreSQL for the B-Trees on Disk part and Neo4j for the Graph Databases part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: oversized keys on one side, and unbounded traversals on the other.

Picture the whole as a filing cabinet where each drawer tells you which drawer to open next, working alongside a city map where intersections are records and roads are relationships.

Two habits help: first, keep indexed keys short so more of them fit on a page; second, limit traversal depth in every exploratory query.
