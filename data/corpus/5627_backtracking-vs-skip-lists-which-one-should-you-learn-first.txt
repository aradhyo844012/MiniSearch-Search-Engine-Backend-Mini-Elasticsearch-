Backtracking vs Skip Lists: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Backtracking or Skip Lists first. Both sit in the area of Algorithms in Practice, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Backtracking is a search technique that builds solutions step by step and undoes steps that lead to dead ends, and it is closely tied to decision tree and pruning. Skip Lists, by contrast, is layered linked lists that allow fast searching by skipping over many elements, and it is closely tied to multiple levels and random level assignment.

Choose Backtracking first if you care about solving Sudoku or generating permutations. Choose Skip Lists first if you care about ordered in-memory indexes or sorted sets in caches.

Think of Backtracking as walking a maze and returning to the last fork whenever you hit a wall, and of Skip Lists as an express train line layered over a local train line. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Backtracking, avoid forgetting to undo changes. With Skip Lists, avoid poor random number choices.

A good habit for Backtracking is to prune as early as possible because the savings multiply. A good habit for Skip Lists is to draw several levels by hand to see how a search moves down.
