Is Probability Basics Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Probability Basics, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Probability Basics is the study of how likely events are to happen.

It is a good fit when your work involves randomized algorithms or machine learning, and when your team understands events.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn conditional probability, or when you cannot manage these risks: confusing independent and exclusive events, ignoring base rates, and falling for the gambler's fallacy.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether independence and expected value are part of the answer. If they are, Probability Basics is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with risk estimates and simulate the problem in code to check your reasoning.
