Encryption at Rest in Real Projects

Reading about Encryption at Rest is useful, but the ideas make more sense in context. These three scenarios show how it appears in real projects.

Scenario one: protecting backups. Here, symmetric keys drives the main design choices, and the team needs to guard against storing keys next to the data.

Scenario two: meeting compliance rules. This time key management matters most, and tools such as LUKS help keep things manageable.

Scenario three: securing laptops. The key idea is disk encryption, and the biggest risk is no key rotation.

Across all three scenarios, Encryption at Rest stays the same idea: protecting stored data by encrypting it on disk or in a database. Only the details change.
