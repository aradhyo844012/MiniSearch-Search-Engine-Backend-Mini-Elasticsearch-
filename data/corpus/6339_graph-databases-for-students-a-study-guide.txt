Graph Databases for Students: A Study Guide

This study guide prepares you for exams, labs and interviews that cover Graph Databases.

Learning goals:
1. Explain Graph Databases in your own words.
2. Describe nodes, edges, properties, and traversal queries.
3. Give examples from social networks and recommendation engines.
4. Name three problems to watch for.

Start with the definition: Graph Databases is databases that store data as nodes and relationships and query connections directly. Then draw a city map where intersections are records and roads are relationships on paper and label the four terms from the goals above.

Self-test questions: Why is using them for simple tabular data a problem? How does edges relate to properties? When would you choose Neo4j or Cypher?

Study habit: limit traversal depth in every exploratory query.

When you feel ready, compare Graph Databases with B-Trees on Disk and write down three differences.
