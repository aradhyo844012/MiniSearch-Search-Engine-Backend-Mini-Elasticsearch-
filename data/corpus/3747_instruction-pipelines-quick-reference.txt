Instruction Pipelines Quick Reference

A one-screen reminder for Instruction Pipelines. Bookmark it and return when the details fade.

What it is: a technique where a processor overlaps the stages of several instructions.
Key terms: fetch, decode and execute stages, hazards, branch prediction, and stalls.
Typical uses: explaining CPU speed, understanding branch costs, and optimizing tight loops.
Risks: unpredictable branches, dependency chains, and assuming each instruction takes one step.
Tools: perf, Compiler Explorer, and published instruction tables.

Mental picture: an assembly line where several cars are in different stages at once.

Habit to build: avoid unpredictable branches in hot loops.
