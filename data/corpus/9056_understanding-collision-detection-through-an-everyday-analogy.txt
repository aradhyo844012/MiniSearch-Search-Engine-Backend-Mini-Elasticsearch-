Understanding Collision Detection Through an Everyday Analogy

Abstract ideas are easier to hold onto when you attach them to something familiar. So picture two people checking whether their personal space bubbles overlap.

In that picture, bounding boxes is the first rule you follow, broad and narrow phases is the next step, and spatial partitioning and collision response keep everything running smoothly.

Every analogy breaks down eventually. Real Collision Detection is the process of finding out when objects in a game touch or overlap, and it involves details such as tunneling through thin objects that no everyday picture captures.

Use the analogy to remember the shape of the idea, and use documentation for Box2D to learn the exact behavior. The same picture helps when you explain platformers to a friend.
