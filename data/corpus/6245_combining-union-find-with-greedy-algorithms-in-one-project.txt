Combining Union-Find with Greedy Algorithms in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Union-Find and Greedy Algorithms can work together in a single project.

Union-Find is a data structure that tracks which items belong to the same group and merges groups quickly. Greedy Algorithms is algorithms that build a solution by always taking the locally best choice.

Imagine a project that involves detecting connected components and interval scheduling. Union-Find brings parent pointers, while Greedy Algorithms brings greedy choice property.

A sensible plan is to start with Python lists for the Union-Find part and pen and paper proofs for the Greedy Algorithms part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: merging without checking roots on one side, and missing counterexamples on the other.

Picture the whole as a set of club rosters where two clubs can merge into one, working alongside picking the closest open parking spot at every junction and hoping it works out.

Two habits help: first, implement path compression and union by rank together; second, try to break your greedy idea with a small counterexample before trusting it.
