Debugging Minimum Spanning Trees: A Field Guide

When something goes wrong around Minimum Spanning Trees, resist the urge to guess. Work through the problem in order.

Step 1: reproduce the problem with the smallest possible example.
Step 2: list the usual suspects: using it on directed graphs, forgetting that the graph must be connected, and treating ties as errors.
Step 3: revisit weighted graphs and Kruskal's algorithm, since misunderstandings there cause many bugs.
Step 4: use NetworkX or similar tools to look inside.
Step 5: write a test that fails because of the bug and passes after the fix.

Many bugs involving Minimum Spanning Trees in work such as designing cable networks come down to the first suspect on the list. Check the simple causes before suspecting anything exotic.

Finally, sort the edges once and reuse the order when testing variations. It prevents a surprising number of repeat problems.
