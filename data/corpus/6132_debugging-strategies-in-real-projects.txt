Debugging Strategies in Real Projects

Reading about Debugging Strategies is useful, but the ideas make more sense in context. These three scenarios show how it appears in real projects.

Scenario one: fixing production issues. Here, reproducing the bug drives the main design choices, and the team needs to guard against changing many things at once.

Scenario two: learning unfamiliar code. This time isolating the cause matters most, and tools such as debuggers help keep things manageable.

Scenario three: reducing repeat bugs. The key idea is forming hypotheses, and the biggest risk is guessing without evidence.

Across all three scenarios, Debugging Strategies stays the same idea: systematic ways of finding and fixing the cause of unexpected program behavior. Only the details change.
