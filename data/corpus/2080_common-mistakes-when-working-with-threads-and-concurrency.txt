Common Mistakes When Working with Threads and Concurrency

Almost everyone makes a few avoidable errors when first working with Threads and Concurrency. When people talk about Threads and Concurrency, they usually mean ways of making progress on several tasks at once within a program. It appears in almost every serious software project, whether or not developers notice it.

Watch out for these problems: forgetting to protect shared data; creating too many threads; and mixing up concurrency and parallelism.

A solid grasp of Threads and Concurrency also makes Containers easier to understand.

Consider the first problem: forgetting to protect shared data. A short test or a careful review comment usually catches it. The second problem is similar: creating too many threads. The third problem is this: mixing up concurrency and parallelism. It tends to appear only once a project grows, so it is worth planning for early.

Write a few tests that show how Threads and Concurrency should behave. Tests double as documentation for your teammates. Measure before you optimize. Use Python threading or a simple timer to see where time is actually spent. Keep an eye on this risk during code review: forgetting to protect shared data.

With steady practice, Threads and Concurrency will stop feeling abstract and become a tool you reach for naturally.
