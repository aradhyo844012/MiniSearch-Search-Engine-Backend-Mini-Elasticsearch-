HTTP/2 and HTTP/3 for Students: A Study Guide

This study guide prepares you for exams, labs and interviews that cover HTTP/2 and HTTP/3.

Learning goals:
1. Explain HTTP/2 and HTTP/3 in your own words.
2. Describe multiplexing, header compression, QUIC, and server push.
3. Give examples from faster page loads and improving mobile performance.
4. Name three problems to watch for.

Start with the definition: HTTP/2 and HTTP/3 is newer versions of the web protocol that load pages faster over fewer connections. Then draw a multi-lane highway replacing a single-lane road on paper and label the four terms from the goals above.

Self-test questions: Why is assuming old tricks like sprite sheets still help a problem? How does header compression relate to QUIC? When would you choose curl with HTTP/3 support or Chrome DevTools?

Study habit worth keeping: keep a working HTTP/1.1 fallback.

When you feel ready, compare HTTP/2 and HTTP/3 with DHCP and write down three differences.
