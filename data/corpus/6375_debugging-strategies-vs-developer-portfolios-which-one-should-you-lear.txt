Debugging Strategies vs Developer Portfolios: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Debugging Strategies or Developer Portfolios first. Both sit in the area of Developer Career and Tools, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Debugging Strategies is systematic ways of finding and fixing the cause of unexpected program behavior, and it is closely tied to reproducing the bug and isolating the cause. Developer Portfolios, by contrast, is a collection of projects and writing that shows what a developer can build, and it is closely tied to projects and README quality.

Choose Debugging Strategies first if you care about fixing production issues or learning unfamiliar code. Choose Developer Portfolios first if you care about applying for internships or showing skills to recruiters.

Think of Debugging Strategies as a detective narrowing down suspects, and of Developer Portfolios as a shop window displaying your best work. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Debugging Strategies, avoid changing many things at once. With Developer Portfolios, avoid too many unfinished projects.

A good habit for Debugging Strategies is to change one thing at a time and record the result. A good habit for Developer Portfolios is to feature a few polished projects rather than many half-finished ones.
