Semantic Versioning Explained Simply

Here is Semantic Versioning without the jargon: it is a numbering scheme where major, minor and patch numbers signal how much changed.

Think of Semantic Versioning as the edition number on a textbook that tells you whether you must relearn anything. Keep that picture in mind as you read on.

The vocabulary has four main pieces: major, minor and patch numbers, breaking changes, pre-release tags, and version ranges. You do not need to master them all at once. Start with major, minor and patch numbers, then add breaking changes.

You will meet Semantic Versioning in work such as publishing libraries, declaring dependencies, and planning upgrades.

One practical tip is to write a changelog entry with every release.
