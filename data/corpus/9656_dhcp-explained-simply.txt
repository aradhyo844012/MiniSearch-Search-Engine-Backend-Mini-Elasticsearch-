DHCP Explained Simply

Here is DHCP without the jargon: it is a service that automatically gives devices IP addresses and network settings.

Think of DHCP as a hotel desk that hands out room numbers for a limited stay. Keep that picture in mind as you read on.

The vocabulary has four main pieces: leases, address pools, reservations, and discover and offer messages. You do not need to master them all at once. Start with leases, then add address pools.

You will meet DHCP in work such as home and office networks, guest Wi-Fi, and booting machines over a network.

One practical tip is to reserve fixed addresses for servers and printers.

Once DHCP feels comfortable, read about BGP and Internet Routing, which lives in the same neighborhood of ideas.
