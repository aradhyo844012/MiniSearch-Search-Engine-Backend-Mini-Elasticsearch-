Debugging Compilers and Interpreters: A Field Guide

When something goes wrong around Compilers and Interpreters, resist the urge to guess. Work through the problem in order.

Step 1: reproduce the problem with the smallest possible example.
Step 2: list the usual suspects: assuming interpreted means slow without measuring, ignoring compiler warnings, and confusing compile-time and run-time errors.
Step 3: revisit lexing and parsing, since misunderstandings there cause many bugs.
Step 4: use GCC or similar tools to look inside.
Step 5: write a test that fails because of the bug and passes after the fix.

Many bugs involving Compilers and Interpreters in work such as running Python and JavaScript come down to the first suspect on the list. Check the simple causes before suspecting anything exotic.

Finally, turn on compiler warnings and treat them seriously. It prevents a surprising number of repeat problems.
