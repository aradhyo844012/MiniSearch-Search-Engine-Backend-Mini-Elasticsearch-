Debugging SOLID Principles: A Field Guide

When something goes wrong around SOLID Principles, resist the urge to guess. Work through the problem in order.

Step 1: reproduce the problem with the smallest possible example.
Step 2: list the usual suspects: applying them rigidly to tiny scripts, creating too many tiny classes, and confusing interfaces with inheritance.
Step 3: revisit single responsibility and the open-closed principle, since misunderstandings there cause many bugs.
Step 4: use UML diagrams or similar tools to look inside.
Step 5: write a test that fails because of the bug and passes after the fix.

Many bugs involving SOLID Principles in work such as structuring classes come down to the first suspect on the list. Check the simple causes before suspecting anything exotic.

Finally, apply them when a design starts to hurt, not before. It prevents a surprising number of repeat problems.
