The Pros and Cons of Fenwick Trees

No technology is free of trade-offs, and Fenwick Trees is no exception. Here is an honest summary.

Advantages:
- Supports counting inversions
- Makes maintaining running totals easier
- Helps with building frequency tables

Drawbacks and risks:
- Risk: using zero-based indexes by mistake
- Risk: forgetting that nodes store partial sums
- Risk: using it for operations that cannot be reversed

Weigh the two lists against your own project. If the advantages match your needs, such as counting inversions, and you can manage the risks, Fenwick Trees is a sound choice. Whatever you decide, start indexing from one to keep the bit tricks simple.
