Inside Microservices: How It Works

This article walks through what happens inside Microservices, step by step. When people talk about Microservices, they usually mean an architecture that splits an application into small services that can be deployed independently. It appears in almost every serious software project, whether or not developers notice it.

The vocabulary of Microservices is small. Learn service boundaries, APIs, independent deployment, and distributed data, and most documentation will start to make sense. Sketching distributed data on paper before coding helps many learners see how the pieces fit.

Microservices and JSON are closely related, so learning both gives a fuller picture of Backend and APIs.

First, service boundaries sets the stage for everything else. Next, APIs builds on it. After that, independent deployment comes into play, and finally distributed data ties the pieces together. Seeing the sequence once, with a tiny example, makes the whole topic easier to remember.

Failures between services are normal, so retries and timeouts need careful design.

If you are comfortable with Microservices, explore JSON next. The two often appear in the same project.

In short, careful study of Microservices pays off. Spend time on service boundaries and APIs, and the rest will follow.
