The Trade-offs of Linear Regression

Every design choice has a cost, and Linear Regression is no exception. When people talk about Linear Regression, they usually mean a method for predicting a number by fitting a straight line or plane to data. It appears in almost every serious software project, whether or not developers notice it.

On the positive side, Linear Regression is well suited to predicting prices and estimating demand. On the other hand, it is not free. Keep these risks in mind: extrapolating far beyond the data; ignoring outliers; using correlated features carelessly. Whether the trade is worth it depends on how much coefficients and residuals matter to your project.

Beginners often stumble on three things. First: extrapolating far beyond the data. Second: ignoring outliers. Third: using correlated features carelessly.

Reproducible experiments make it far easier to trust and improve a model.

Read the official documentation for NumPy; it often explains residuals better than blog posts do. Measure before you optimize. Use scikit-learn or a simple timer to see where time is actually spent.

With steady practice, Linear Regression will stop feeling abstract and become a tool you reach for naturally.
