Combining Raft Consensus with Distributed Locks in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Raft Consensus and Distributed Locks can work together in a single project.

Raft Consensus is an algorithm that lets a group of servers agree on a shared log of operations. Distributed Locks is mechanisms that ensure only one process at a time performs a critical action across machines.

Imagine a project that involves configuration stores and running scheduled jobs once. Raft Consensus brings leader election, while Distributed Locks brings lease times.

A sensible plan is to start with etcd for the Raft Consensus part and Redis for the Distributed Locks part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: slow disks causing elections on one side, and clock drift on the other.

Picture the whole as a committee that elects a chair and only records decisions most members confirm, working alongside a single key to a shared meeting room that expires automatically.

Two habits help: first, use an odd number of nodes; second, always use a timeout together with a fencing token.
