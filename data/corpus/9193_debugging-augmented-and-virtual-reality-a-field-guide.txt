Debugging Augmented and Virtual Reality: A Field Guide

When something goes wrong around Augmented and Virtual Reality, resist the urge to guess. Work through the problem in order.

Step 1: reproduce the problem with the smallest possible example.
Step 2: list the usual suspects: motion sickness from low frame rates, heavy assets, and poor interface design in 3D.
Step 3: revisit head tracking and rendering at high frame rates, since misunderstandings there cause many bugs.
Step 4: use Unity XR or similar tools to look inside.
Step 5: write a test that fails because of the bug and passes after the fix.

Many bugs involving Augmented and Virtual Reality in work such as training simulations come down to the first suspect on the list. Check the simple causes before suspecting anything exotic.

Finally, keep frame rates high and test comfort with real users. It prevents a surprising number of repeat problems.
