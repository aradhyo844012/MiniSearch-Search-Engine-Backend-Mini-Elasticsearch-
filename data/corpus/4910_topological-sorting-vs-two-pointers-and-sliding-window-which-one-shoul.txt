Topological Sorting vs Two Pointers and Sliding Window: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Topological Sorting or Two Pointers and Sliding Window first. Both sit in the area of Algorithms in Practice, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Topological Sorting is an ordering of tasks in a directed graph so that every task comes after the tasks it depends on, and it is closely tied to directed acyclic graphs and in-degree counts. Two Pointers and Sliding Window, by contrast, is techniques that scan arrays with two moving positions to avoid nested loops, and it is closely tied to left and right pointers and window boundaries.

Choose Topological Sorting first if you care about build systems or course prerequisite planning. Choose Two Pointers and Sliding Window first if you care about finding pairs with a given sum or longest substring problems.

Think of Topological Sorting as a recipe that must list every step after the steps it depends on, and of Two Pointers and Sliding Window as a small frame that slides along a line of text. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Topological Sorting, avoid ignoring cycles. With Two Pointers and Sliding Window, avoid moving the wrong pointer.

A good habit for Topological Sorting is to check for cycles first so you can report a clear error. A good habit for Two Pointers and Sliding Window is to write down the window invariant before coding the loop.
