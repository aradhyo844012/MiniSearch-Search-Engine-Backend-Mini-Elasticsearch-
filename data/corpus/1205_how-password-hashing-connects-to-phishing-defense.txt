How Password Hashing Connects to Phishing Defense

Password Hashing and Phishing Defense are closely connected parts of Security. Seeing how they fit together helps you learn both faster.

A project involving user login systems may also involve protecting employees. In such a project, salts and suspicious links often influence each other.

Start with Password Hashing: the term refers to storing passwords as one-way hashes so the originals are never kept. Then look at Phishing Defense, which refers to recognizing and reducing attacks that trick people into revealing information.

A useful exercise is to take one example, such as protecting data after a breach, and explain how sender verification would change the design.

Common problems are shared too. For Password Hashing, watch for this: reusing salts. For Phishing Defense, watch for this: reusing passwords. Never trust input that comes from outside your own code.

Spend a few hours on each topic, then combine them in one small project using bcrypt or SPF.
