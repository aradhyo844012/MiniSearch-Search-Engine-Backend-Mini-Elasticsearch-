Is Floating-Point Arithmetic Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Floating-Point Arithmetic, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Floating-Point Arithmetic is the way computers approximate real numbers with a fixed number of bits.

It is a good fit when your work involves scientific computing or graphics, and when your team understands mantissa and exponent.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn rounding, or when you cannot manage these risks: comparing floats with equality, accumulated rounding errors, and using floats for money.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether precision and special values are part of the answer. If they are, Floating-Point Arithmetic is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with financial calculations that need care and compare floats with a tolerance and use decimals for money.
