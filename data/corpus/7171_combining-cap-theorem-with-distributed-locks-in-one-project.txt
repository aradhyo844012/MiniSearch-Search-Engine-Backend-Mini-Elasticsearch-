Combining CAP Theorem with Distributed Locks in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how CAP Theorem and Distributed Locks can work together in a single project.

CAP Theorem is a result stating that a distributed system cannot guarantee consistency, availability and partition tolerance all at once. Distributed Locks is mechanisms that ensure only one process at a time performs a critical action across machines.

Imagine a project that involves choosing database types and running scheduled jobs once. CAP Theorem brings consistency, while Distributed Locks brings lease times.

A sensible plan is to start with Apache Cassandra for the CAP Theorem part and Redis for the Distributed Locks part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: ignoring latency trade-offs in normal operation on one side, and clock drift on the other.

Picture the whole as a phone tree that must choose between giving old information or staying silent when a line is cut, working alongside a single key to a shared meeting room that expires automatically.

Two habits help: first, ask what your users prefer during a failure: stale answers or errors; second, always use a timeout together with a fencing token.
