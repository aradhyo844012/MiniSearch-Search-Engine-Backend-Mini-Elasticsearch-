Assembly Language Basics for Students: A Study Guide

This study guide prepares you for exams, labs and interviews that cover Assembly Language Basics.

Learning goals:
1. Explain Assembly Language Basics in your own words.
2. Describe registers, instructions, memory addressing, and the stack.
3. Give examples from understanding compiled code and debugging crashes.
4. Name three problems to watch for.

Start with the definition: Assembly Language Basics is low-level instructions that map closely to what a processor actually executes. Then draw the individual muscle movements behind a simple action on paper and label the four terms from the goals above.

Self-test questions: Why is wrong calling conventions a problem? How does instructions relate to memory addressing? When would you choose GDB or objdump?

Study habit: read the assembly of a tiny C function to see what the compiler does.

When you feel ready, compare Assembly Language Basics with Boolean Logic and Gates and write down three differences.
