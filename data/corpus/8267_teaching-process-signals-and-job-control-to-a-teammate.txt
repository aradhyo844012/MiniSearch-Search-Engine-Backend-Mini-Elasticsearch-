Teaching Process Signals and Job Control to a Teammate

Explaining Process Signals and Job Control to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: a traffic officer's hand signals for running programs. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Process Signals and Job Control is messages and shell features used to stop, pause and resume running programs. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving stopping runaway programs, naming SIGTERM and SIGKILL and foreground and background jobs as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain kill and trap in Process Signals and Job Control back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, using kill -9 first, and suggest this habit: try SIGTERM before SIGKILL so programs can clean up.
