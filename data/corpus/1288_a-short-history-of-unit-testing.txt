A Short History of Unit Testing

Ideas behind Unit Testing developed gradually as engineers ran into practical limits and shared what they learned. Many students first meet Unit Testing in a classroom, but it matters just as much in production systems. The term refers to writing small automated tests that check one piece of code at a time.

Small, frequent feedback loops catch problems while they are still cheap to fix.

The vocabulary of Unit Testing is small. Learn test cases, assertions, fixtures, and mocking, and most documentation will start to make sense. Sketching mocking on paper before coding helps many learners see how the pieces fit.

Unit Testing and Agile and Scrum are closely related, so learning both gives a fuller picture of Software Engineering.

A solid grasp of Unit Testing also makes Agile and Scrum easier to understand.

None of this needs special hardware or expensive software. A laptop, curiosity and a few small experiments are enough to get comfortable with Unit Testing.
