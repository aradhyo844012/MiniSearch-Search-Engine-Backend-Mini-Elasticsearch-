Why NAT Matters for Developers

Developers who understand NAT make better decisions, because NAT is a technique that lets many devices share one public IP address by rewriting addresses.

Three situations show why it matters. First, home routers. Second, sharing limited IPv4 addresses. Third, hiding internal networks. In each case, a solid grasp of private addresses and translation tables separates a quick fix from a lasting solution.

Ignoring it has a cost. Teams run into these problems: breaking peer-to-peer connections, exhausted translation tables, and misconfigured port forwarding.

You will not need to build everything yourself; iptables, router admin pages, and conntrack cover most needs.
