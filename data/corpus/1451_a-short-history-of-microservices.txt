A Short History of Microservices

Ideas behind Microservices developed gradually as engineers ran into practical limits and shared what they learned. Many students first meet Microservices in a classroom, but it matters just as much in production systems. The term refers to an architecture that splits an application into small services that can be deployed independently.

If you are comfortable with Microservices, explore JWT next. The two often appear in the same project.

Failures between services are normal, so retries and timeouts need careful design.

The vocabulary of Microservices is small. Learn service boundaries, APIs, independent deployment, and distributed data, and most documentation will start to make sense. A good way to remember service boundaries is to connect it with a concrete example from your own code.
