A Short History of Natural Language Processing

Ideas behind Natural Language Processing developed gradually as engineers ran into practical limits and shared what they learned. Many students first meet Natural Language Processing in a classroom, but it matters just as much in production systems. The term refers to the field of teaching computers to work with human language.

Simple baselines are worth building first because they show what improvement is realistic. Reproducible experiments make it far easier to trust and improve a model.

To understand Natural Language Processing, start with tokenization. Then move on to embeddings, and finally look at language models and evaluation metrics. Sketching evaluation metrics on paper before coding helps many learners see how the pieces fit.

Natural Language Processing and Feature Engineering are closely related, so learning both gives a fuller picture of Machine Learning.

None of this needs special hardware or expensive software. A laptop, curiosity and a few small experiments are enough to get comfortable with Natural Language Processing.
