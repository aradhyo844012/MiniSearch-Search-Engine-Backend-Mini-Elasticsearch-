Combining Domain-Driven Design with Hexagonal Architecture in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Domain-Driven Design and Hexagonal Architecture can work together in a single project.

Domain-Driven Design is an approach to software that models the code around the real business domain. Hexagonal Architecture is a design that keeps core logic separate from outside systems through ports and adapters.

Imagine a project that involves complex business systems and testable business logic. Domain-Driven Design brings ubiquitous language, while Hexagonal Architecture brings ports.

A sensible plan is to start with event storming for the Domain-Driven Design part and Spring for the Hexagonal Architecture part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: models that never match the business on one side, and leaking framework types into the core on the other.

Picture the whole as writing software in the same vocabulary the business speaks, working alongside a power strip that lets any plug connect to the same core.

Two habits help: first, agree on shared terms with domain experts before drawing classes; second, keep framework imports out of the core domain.
