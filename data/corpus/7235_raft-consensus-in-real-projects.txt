Raft Consensus in Real Projects

Reading about Raft Consensus is useful, but the ideas make more sense in context. These three scenarios show how it appears in real projects.

Scenario one: configuration stores. Here, leader election drives the main design choices, and the team needs to guard against even-sized clusters.

Scenario two: distributed locks. This time log replication matters most, and tools such as etcd help keep things manageable.

Scenario three: replicated state machines. The key idea is terms, and the biggest risk is slow disks causing elections.

Across all three scenarios, Raft Consensus stays the same idea: an algorithm that lets a group of servers agree on a shared log of operations. Only the details change.
