SOLID Principles in 10 Minutes

You have ten minutes, so here is the fast route through SOLID Principles.

Minutes 1 and 2, the definition: SOLID Principles is five guidelines for designing object-oriented code that is easy to change.

Minutes 3 and 4, the vocabulary: learn single responsibility, the open-closed principle, Liskov substitution, and dependency inversion.

Minutes 5 and 6, the picture: imagine house rules that keep a shared kitchen tidy. That is the intuition.

Minutes 7 and 8, the use cases: skim through structuring classes, reviewing designs, and reducing coupling.

Minutes 9 and 10, the traps and the tools: watch for applying them rigidly to tiny scripts and creating too many tiny classes, then try UML diagrams for a first experiment.

If you have a spare minute, remember to apply them when a design starts to hurt, not before.
