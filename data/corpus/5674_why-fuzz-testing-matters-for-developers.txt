Why Fuzz Testing Matters for Developers

Developers who understand Fuzz Testing make better decisions, because Fuzz Testing is feeding programs random or malformed input to uncover crashes and security bugs.

Three situations show why it matters. First, testing parsers. Second, finding memory bugs. Third, security research. In each case, a solid grasp of input generation and coverage guidance separates a quick fix from a lasting solution.

Ignoring it has a cost. Teams run into these problems: running without sanitizers, crashes that cannot be reproduced, and ignoring slow inputs.

You will not need to build everything yourself; AFL++, libFuzzer, and Atheris cover most needs.
