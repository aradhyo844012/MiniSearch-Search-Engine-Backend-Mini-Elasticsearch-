Convolutional Neural Networks vs Retrieval-Augmented Generation: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Convolutional Neural Networks or Retrieval-Augmented Generation first. Both sit in the area of AI and Machine Learning, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Convolutional Neural Networks is neural networks that scan images with small filters to detect patterns, and it is closely tied to filters and pooling. Retrieval-Augmented Generation, by contrast, is a technique that gives a language model relevant documents to read before it answers, and it is closely tied to retrieval and chunking.

Choose Convolutional Neural Networks first if you care about image classification or medical image analysis. Choose Retrieval-Augmented Generation first if you care about company knowledge assistants or question answering over documents.

Think of Convolutional Neural Networks as a magnifying glass sweeping across a photo looking for edges and shapes, and of Retrieval-Augmented Generation as an open-book exam where the student first looks up the right pages. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Convolutional Neural Networks, avoid too little training data. With Retrieval-Augmented Generation, avoid poor chunking.

A good habit for Convolutional Neural Networks is to start from a pretrained model. A good habit for Retrieval-Augmented Generation is to evaluate retrieval quality separately from answer quality.
