Is Edge Computing Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Edge Computing, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Edge Computing is processing data close to where it is produced instead of in a distant data center.

It is a good fit when your work involves factory sensors or video analytics, and when your team understands edge nodes.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn latency, or when you cannot manage these risks: hard-to-update devices, inconsistent hardware, and security at many sites.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether bandwidth savings and synchronization are part of the answer. If they are, Edge Computing is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with smart cities and decide which data must stay local before designing the system.
