Dijkstra's Algorithm vs Backtracking: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Dijkstra's Algorithm or Backtracking first. Both sit in the area of Algorithms in Practice, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Dijkstra's Algorithm is a method for finding the shortest path from one node to all others in a graph with non-negative weights, and it is closely tied to priority queue and distance table. Backtracking, by contrast, is a search technique that builds solutions step by step and undoes steps that lead to dead ends, and it is closely tied to decision tree and pruning.

Choose Dijkstra's Algorithm first if you care about route planning or network routing. Choose Backtracking first if you care about solving Sudoku or generating permutations.

Think of Dijkstra's Algorithm as a GPS that keeps expanding to the nearest unvisited intersection, and of Backtracking as walking a maze and returning to the last fork whenever you hit a wall. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Dijkstra's Algorithm, avoid using it with negative edge weights. With Backtracking, avoid forgetting to undo changes.

A good habit for Dijkstra's Algorithm is to stop as soon as the destination is popped from the queue. A good habit for Backtracking is to prune as early as possible because the savings multiply.
