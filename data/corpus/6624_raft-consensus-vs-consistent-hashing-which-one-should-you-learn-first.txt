Raft Consensus vs Consistent Hashing: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Raft Consensus or Consistent Hashing first. Both sit in the area of Distributed Systems, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Raft Consensus is an algorithm that lets a group of servers agree on a shared log of operations, and it is closely tied to leader election and log replication. Consistent Hashing, by contrast, is a way of mapping keys to servers so that adding or removing servers moves very few keys, and it is closely tied to hash ring and virtual nodes.

Choose Raft Consensus first if you care about configuration stores or distributed locks. Choose Consistent Hashing first if you care about distributed caches or sharded databases.

Think of Raft Consensus as a committee that elects a chair and only records decisions most members confirm, and of Consistent Hashing as seats around a round table where a new guest only displaces their neighbor. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Raft Consensus, avoid even-sized clusters. With Consistent Hashing, avoid too few virtual nodes.

A good habit for Raft Consensus is to use an odd number of nodes. A good habit for Consistent Hashing is to use many virtual nodes per server to even out the load.
