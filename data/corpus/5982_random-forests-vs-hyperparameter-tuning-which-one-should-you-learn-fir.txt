Random Forests vs Hyperparameter Tuning: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Random Forests or Hyperparameter Tuning first. Both sit in the area of AI and Machine Learning, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Random Forests is ensembles of many decision trees whose predictions are combined, and it is closely tied to bagging and feature randomness. Hyperparameter Tuning, by contrast, is the process of searching for the best settings that control how a model learns, and it is closely tied to search spaces and grid search.

Choose Random Forests first if you care about tabular predictions or feature importance ranking. Choose Hyperparameter Tuning first if you care about improving model accuracy or comparing algorithms.

Think of Random Forests as asking many different experts and taking the majority view, and of Hyperparameter Tuning as adjusting the knobs on a radio until the signal is clear. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Random Forests, avoid treating importance scores as causes. With Hyperparameter Tuning, avoid tuning on the test set.

A good habit for Random Forests is to tune the number of trees only after the basics work. A good habit for Hyperparameter Tuning is to try random search before anything fancier.
