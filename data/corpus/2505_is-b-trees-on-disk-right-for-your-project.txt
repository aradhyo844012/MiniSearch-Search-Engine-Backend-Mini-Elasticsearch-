Is B-Trees on Disk Right for Your Project?

Before adopting B-Trees on Disk, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that B-Trees on Disk is balanced tree structures designed to read large blocks from disk efficiently.

It is a good fit when your work involves database indexes or file system directories, and when your team understands pages.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn branching factor, or when you cannot manage these risks: random writes on fragmented pages, oversized keys, and ignoring page fill factor.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether node splits and sorted keys are part of the answer. If they are, B-Trees on Disk is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with range scans and keep indexed keys short so more of them fit on a page.
