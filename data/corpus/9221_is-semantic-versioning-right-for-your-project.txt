Is Semantic Versioning Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Semantic Versioning, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Semantic Versioning is a numbering scheme where major, minor and patch numbers signal how much changed.

It is a good fit when your work involves publishing libraries or declaring dependencies, and when your team understands major, minor and patch numbers.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn breaking changes, or when you cannot manage these risks: breaking changes in minor releases, overly loose ranges, and forgetting to document changes.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether pre-release tags and version ranges are part of the answer. If they are, Semantic Versioning is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with planning upgrades and write a changelog entry with every release.
