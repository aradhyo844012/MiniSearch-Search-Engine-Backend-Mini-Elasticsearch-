Is systemd Services Right for Your Project?

Before adopting systemd Services, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that systemd Services is the init system and service manager used by most modern Linux distributions.

It is a good fit when your work involves starting services at boot or restarting crashed programs, and when your team understands unit files.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn targets, or when you cannot manage these risks: editing unit files without reloading the daemon, missing restart policies, and ignoring dependency ordering.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether service states and journal logs are part of the answer. If they are, systemd Services is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with managing dependencies between services and run systemctl daemon-reload after every unit file change.
