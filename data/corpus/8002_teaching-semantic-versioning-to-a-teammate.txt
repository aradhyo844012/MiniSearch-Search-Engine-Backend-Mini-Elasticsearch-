Teaching Semantic Versioning to a Teammate

Explaining Semantic Versioning to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: the edition number on a textbook that tells you whether you must relearn anything. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Semantic Versioning is a numbering scheme where major, minor and patch numbers signal how much changed. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving publishing libraries, naming major, minor and patch numbers and breaking changes as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain pre-release tags in Semantic Versioning back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, breaking changes in minor releases, and suggest this habit: write a changelog entry with every release.
