Mistakes to Avoid with Garbage Collection

Everyone learns Garbage Collection partly through mistakes. Here are three worth avoiding.

Mistake one: creating huge numbers of short-lived objects. It usually appears when someone skips reachability and jumps straight to implementation. The fix is to slow down and review the basics.

Mistake two: holding references longer than needed. It tends to be noticed late, often during testing of managed languages like Java and Python. A short checklist during review catches it early.

Mistake three: ignoring pause times in latency-sensitive code. It is easy to miss because everything seems to work at first. Keep an eye on pause times to spot it.

If you remember only one rule, make it this: measure allocation rates before tuning collector settings.
