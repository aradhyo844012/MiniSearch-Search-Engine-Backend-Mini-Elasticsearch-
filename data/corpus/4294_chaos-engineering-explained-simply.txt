Chaos Engineering Explained Simply

Here is Chaos Engineering without the jargon: it is deliberately causing failures in a system to learn how it responds.

Think of Chaos Engineering as a fire drill where you secretly block one exit. Keep that picture in mind as you read on.

The vocabulary has four main pieces: steady state, hypotheses, blast radius, and fault injection. You do not need to master them all at once. Start with steady state, then add hypotheses.

You will meet Chaos Engineering in work such as testing resilience, validating failover, and preparing teams for incidents.

One practical tip is to start with small experiments in a test environment.

After Chaos Engineering, a natural next topic is End-to-End Testing.
