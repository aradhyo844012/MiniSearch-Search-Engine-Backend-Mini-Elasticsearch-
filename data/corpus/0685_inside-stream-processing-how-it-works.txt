Inside Stream Processing: How It Works

This article walks through what happens inside Stream Processing, step by step. If you build software for long enough, you will run into Stream Processing. The term refers to handling data continuously as it arrives rather than in large batches.

The vocabulary of Stream Processing is small. Learn events, windows, offsets, and state, and most documentation will start to make sense. A good way to remember events is to connect it with a concrete example from your own code.

First, events sets the stage for everything else. Next, windows builds on it. After that, offsets comes into play, and finally state ties the pieces together. Seeing the sequence once, with a tiny example, makes the whole topic easier to remember.

Writing down assumptions about the data prevents confusing surprises later. Data work is mostly preparation, and cleaning often takes longer than analysis.

If you are comfortable with Stream Processing, explore ETL Pipelines next. The two often appear in the same project.

With steady practice, Stream Processing will stop feeling abstract and become a tool you reach for naturally.
