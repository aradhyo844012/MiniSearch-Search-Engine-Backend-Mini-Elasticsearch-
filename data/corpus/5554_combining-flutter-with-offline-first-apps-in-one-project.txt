Combining Flutter with Offline-First Apps in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Flutter and Offline-First Apps can work together in a single project.

Flutter is a toolkit for building natively compiled apps from a single Dart codebase. Offline-First Apps is apps designed to work without a network and sync when a connection returns.

Imagine a project that involves cross-platform mobile apps and field service tools. Flutter brings widgets, while Offline-First Apps brings local storage.

A sensible plan is to start with Dart for the Flutter part and SQLite for the Offline-First Apps part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: poor state organization on one side, and unbounded local data on the other.

Picture the whole as a box of customizable building blocks drawn by its own engine, working alongside writing in a notebook and mailing the pages later.

Two habits help: first, split big widgets into small reusable ones; second, show users which changes have not synced yet.
