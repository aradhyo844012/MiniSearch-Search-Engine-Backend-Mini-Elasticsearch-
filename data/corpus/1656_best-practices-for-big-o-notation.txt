Best Practices for Big O Notation

The practices below come from patterns that tend to work well across many teams and projects. When people talk about Big O Notation, they usually mean a way of describing how running time or memory grows as the input size grows. It appears in almost every serious software project, whether or not developers notice it.

1. Measure before you optimize. Use Python timeit or a simple timer to see where time is actually spent.
2. Start small by applying Big O Notation to one case, such as comparing algorithms, before rolling it out more widely.
3. Automate repetitive steps so that results are easy to reproduce.
4. Keep an eye on this risk during code review: ignoring constant factors entirely.
5. Read the official documentation for big-O cheat sheets; it often explains constant, linear and logarithmic growth better than blog posts do.

Big O Notation and Bloom Filters are closely related, so learning both gives a fuller picture of Data Structures and Algorithms.

In practice you can start with Python timeit, then explore big-O cheat sheets or profilers as your needs grow.

A solid grasp of Big O Notation also makes Bloom Filters easier to understand.

In short, careful study of Big O Notation pays off. Spend time on upper bounds and constant, linear and logarithmic growth, and the rest will follow.
