Debugging Computability and the Halting Problem: A Field Guide

When something goes wrong around Computability and the Halting Problem, resist the urge to guess. Work through the problem in order.

Step 1: reproduce the problem with the smallest possible example.
Step 2: list the usual suspects: assuming everything is solvable, confusing hard with undecidable, and hand-wavy proofs.
Step 3: revisit Turing machines and decidability, since misunderstandings there cause many bugs.
Step 4: use Turing machine simulators or similar tools to look inside.
Step 5: write a test that fails because of the bug and passes after the fix.

Many bugs involving Computability and the Halting Problem in work such as understanding the limits of tools come down to the first suspect on the list. Check the simple causes before suspecting anything exotic.

Finally, learn the diagonal argument step by step. It prevents a surprising number of repeat problems.
