Teaching JavaScript Closures to a Teammate

Explaining JavaScript Closures to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: a backpack a function carries with everything it needs from home. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: JavaScript Closures is functions that remember the variables from the place where they were created. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving keeping private state in modules, naming lexical scope and captured variables as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain function factories in JavaScript Closures back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, loop variables captured by mistake, and suggest this habit: use let instead of var inside loops.
