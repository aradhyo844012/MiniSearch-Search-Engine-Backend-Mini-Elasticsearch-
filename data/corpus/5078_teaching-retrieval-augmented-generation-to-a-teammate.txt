Teaching Retrieval-Augmented Generation to a Teammate

Explaining Retrieval-Augmented Generation to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: an open-book exam where the student first looks up the right pages. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Retrieval-Augmented Generation is a technique that gives a language model relevant documents to read before it answers. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving company knowledge assistants, naming retrieval and chunking as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain prompts with context in Retrieval-Augmented Generation back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, poor chunking, and suggest this habit: evaluate retrieval quality separately from answer quality.
