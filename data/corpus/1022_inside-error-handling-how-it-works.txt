Inside Error Handling: How It Works

This article walks through what happens inside Error Handling, step by step. Many students first meet Error Handling in a classroom, but it matters just as much in production systems. The term refers to planning for failures so that programs respond clearly instead of crashing.

Four ideas sit at the heart of Error Handling: exceptions, validation, logging, and retries. Each one builds on the one before it. A good way to remember exceptions is to connect it with a concrete example from your own code.

First, exceptions sets the stage for everything else. Next, validation builds on it. After that, logging comes into play, and finally retries ties the pieces together. Seeing the sequence once, with a tiny example, makes the whole topic easier to remember.
