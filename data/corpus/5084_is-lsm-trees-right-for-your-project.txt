Is LSM Trees Right for Your Project?

Before adopting LSM Trees, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that LSM Trees is storage structures that buffer writes in memory and merge them into sorted files on disk.

It is a good fit when your work involves write-heavy databases or time-ordered data, and when your team understands memtable.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn sorted string tables, or when you cannot manage these risks: read amplification, compaction stalls, and poor bloom filter tuning.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether compaction and write amplification are part of the answer. If they are, LSM Trees is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with key-value stores and watch compaction metrics before blaming the application.
