Instruction Pipelines Explained Simply

Here is Instruction Pipelines without the jargon: it is a technique where a processor overlaps the stages of several instructions.

Think of Instruction Pipelines as an assembly line where several cars are in different stages at once. Keep that picture in mind as you read on.

The vocabulary has four main pieces: fetch, decode and execute stages, hazards, branch prediction, and stalls. You do not need to master them all at once. Start with fetch, decode and execute stages, then add hazards.

You will meet Instruction Pipelines in work such as explaining CPU speed, understanding branch costs, and optimizing tight loops.

One practical tip is to avoid unpredictable branches in hot loops.

Once Instruction Pipelines feels comfortable, read about GPUs and Parallelism, which lives in the same neighborhood of ideas.
