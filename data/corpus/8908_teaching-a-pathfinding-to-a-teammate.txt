Teaching A* Pathfinding to a Teammate

Explaining A* Pathfinding to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: a hiker who prefers trails that seem to head toward the summit. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: A* Pathfinding is a search algorithm that finds short routes by combining distance traveled with an estimate of distance remaining. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving game characters, naming open and closed sets and cost so far as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain heuristics in A* Pathfinding back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, overestimating the heuristic, and suggest this habit: choose an admissible heuristic so paths stay optimal.
