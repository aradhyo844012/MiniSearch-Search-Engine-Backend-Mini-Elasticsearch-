Teaching Pair Programming to a Teammate

Explaining Pair Programming to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: a pilot and co-pilot who check each other's decisions. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Pair Programming is two developers working together at one computer on the same code. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving teaching new teammates, naming driver and navigator and rotation as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain communication in Pair Programming back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, one person dominating, and suggest this habit: swap roles every twenty to thirty minutes.
