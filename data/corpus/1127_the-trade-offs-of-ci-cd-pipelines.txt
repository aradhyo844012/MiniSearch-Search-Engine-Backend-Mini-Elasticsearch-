The Trade-offs of CI/CD Pipelines

Every design choice has a cost, and CI/CD Pipelines is no exception. When people talk about CI/CD Pipelines, they usually mean automated workflows that build, test and deliver code whenever it changes. It appears in almost every serious software project, whether or not developers notice it.

On the positive side, CI/CD Pipelines is well suited to running tests on every push and shipping releases safely. On the other hand, it is not free. Keep these risks in mind: slow pipelines; flaky tests; secrets in logs. Whether the trade is worth it depends on how much build stage and test stage matter to your project.

Three issues come up again and again: slow pipelines; flaky tests; and secrets in logs. Knowing them in advance saves hours of debugging.

Keep notes on what worked. A short README explaining your decisions about CI/CD Pipelines helps the next developer. Ask a teammate to explain artifacts back to you; teaching is a quick way to find gaps.

Start small by applying CI/CD Pipelines to one case, such as running tests on every push, before rolling it out more widely. Ask a teammate to explain artifacts back to you; teaching is a quick way to find gaps.

None of this needs special hardware or expensive software. A laptop, curiosity and a few small experiments are enough to get comfortable with CI/CD Pipelines.
