The Trade-offs of TCP and UDP

Every design choice has a cost, and TCP and UDP is no exception. When people talk about TCP and UDP, they usually mean the two main transport protocols, one reliable and ordered and the other fast and connectionless. It appears in almost every serious software project, whether or not developers notice it.

On the positive side, TCP and UDP is well suited to web traffic and video calls. On the other hand, it is not free. Keep these risks in mind: assuming UDP guarantees delivery; ignoring timeouts; opening too many connections. Whether the trade is worth it depends on how much handshakes and acknowledgements matter to your project.

Latency is limited by distance and by the number of round trips a request needs. Simple command-line tools can reveal a lot about what is happening on the wire.

Three issues come up again and again: assuming UDP guarantees delivery; ignoring timeouts; and opening too many connections. Knowing them in advance saves hours of debugging.

Start small by applying TCP and UDP to one case, such as web traffic, before rolling it out more widely. Keep an eye on this risk during code review: assuming UDP guarantees delivery. Practice handshakes with small exercises until it feels natural.

In short, careful study of TCP and UDP pays off. Spend time on handshakes and acknowledgements, and the rest will follow.
