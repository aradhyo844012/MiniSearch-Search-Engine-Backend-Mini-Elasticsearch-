NAT for Students: A Study Guide

This study guide prepares you for exams, labs and interviews that cover NAT.

Learning goals:
1. Explain NAT in your own words.
2. Describe private addresses, translation tables, port mapping, and port forwarding.
3. Give examples from home routers and sharing limited IPv4 addresses.
4. Name three problems to watch for.

Start with the definition: NAT is a technique that lets many devices share one public IP address by rewriting addresses. Then draw a receptionist who forwards calls for many desks through one phone number on paper and label the four terms from the goals above.

Self-test questions: Why is breaking peer-to-peer connections a problem? How does translation tables relate to port mapping? When would you choose iptables or router admin pages?

Study habit worth keeping: document every port forward you create.

When you feel ready, compare NAT with VPNs and write down three differences.
