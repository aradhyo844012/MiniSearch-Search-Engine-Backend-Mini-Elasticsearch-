Is Garbage Collection Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Garbage Collection, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Garbage Collection is automatic memory management that finds and frees objects a program no longer uses.

It is a good fit when your work involves managed languages like Java and Python or reducing memory bugs, and when your team understands reachability.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn generations, or when you cannot manage these risks: creating huge numbers of short-lived objects, holding references longer than needed, and ignoring pause times in latency-sensitive code.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether mark and sweep and pause times are part of the answer. If they are, Garbage Collection is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with long-running services and measure allocation rates before tuning collector settings.
