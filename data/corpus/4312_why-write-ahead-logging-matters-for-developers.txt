Why Write-Ahead Logging Matters for Developers

Developers who understand Write-Ahead Logging make better decisions, because Write-Ahead Logging is a technique that records changes in a log before they are applied to the main data.

Three situations show why it matters. First, surviving crashes. Second, feeding replication streams. Third, point-in-time recovery. In each case, a solid grasp of log records and checkpoints separates a quick fix from a lasting solution.

Ignoring it has a cost. Teams run into these problems: disabling fsync for speed, log files filling the disk, and forgetting to archive logs.

Good tools exist, including PostgreSQL WAL, SQLite journal, and Apache Kafka, so you rarely have to start from nothing.
