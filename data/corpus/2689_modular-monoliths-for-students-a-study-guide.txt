Modular Monoliths for Students: A Study Guide

This study guide prepares you for exams, labs and interviews that cover Modular Monoliths.

Learning goals:
1. Explain Modular Monoliths in your own words.
2. Describe module boundaries, public interfaces, shared database rules, and dependency checks.
3. Give examples from small teams and early-stage products.
4. Name three problems to watch for.

Start with the definition: Modular Monoliths is single deployable applications built from well-separated internal modules. Then draw an apartment building with separate flats under one roof on paper and label the four terms from the goals above.

Self-test questions: Why is modules reaching into each other's tables a problem? How does public interfaces relate to shared database rules? When would you choose ArchUnit or import-linter?

Study habit worth keeping: enforce boundaries with automated checks.

When you feel ready, compare Modular Monoliths with Hexagonal Architecture and write down three differences.
