Web Components for Students: A Study Guide

This study guide prepares you for exams, labs and interviews that cover Web Components.

Learning goals:
1. Explain Web Components in your own words.
2. Describe custom elements, shadow DOM, templates, and slots.
3. Give examples from design system components and widgets reused across frameworks.
4. Name three problems to watch for.

Start with the definition: Web Components is browser-native custom elements with their own markup, style and behavior. Then draw a Lego brick in its own sealed packaging on paper and label the four terms from the goals above.

Self-test questions: Why is styling surprises across the shadow boundary a problem? How does shadow DOM relate to templates? When would you choose Lit or Stencil?

Study habit worth keeping: document the attributes and events like a public API.

When you feel ready, compare Web Components with Service Workers and write down three differences.
