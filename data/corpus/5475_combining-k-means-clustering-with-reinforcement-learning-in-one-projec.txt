Combining K-Means Clustering with Reinforcement Learning in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how K-Means Clustering and Reinforcement Learning can work together in a single project.

K-Means Clustering is an algorithm that groups data points into a chosen number of clusters around central points. Reinforcement Learning is a way of training agents to choose actions by rewarding good outcomes.

Imagine a project that involves customer segmentation and game playing. K-Means Clustering brings centroids, while Reinforcement Learning brings agent.

A sensible plan is to start with scikit-learn for the K-Means Clustering part and Gymnasium for the Reinforcement Learning part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: unscaled features on one side, and slow training on the other.

Picture the whole as dividing students into study groups around the most central member, working alongside training a dog with treats for good behavior.

Two habits help: first, scale features before clustering; second, start with simple environments.
