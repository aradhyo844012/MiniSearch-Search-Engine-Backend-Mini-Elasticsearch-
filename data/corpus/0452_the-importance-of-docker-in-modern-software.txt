The Importance of Docker in Modern Software

It is easy to underestimate Docker until something goes wrong without it. Put simply, Docker covers a platform for building and running applications in containers. Learning it well pays off across many areas of computing.

A system that cannot be observed cannot be reliably operated. Small, frequent releases are usually safer than rare, large ones.

People use Docker for reproducible development setups, for shipping applications, and for CI jobs.

Three issues come up again and again: huge images; secrets baked into images; and running as root. Knowing them in advance saves hours of debugging.

If you are comfortable with Docker, explore Infrastructure as Code next. The two often appear in the same project.

In short, careful study of Docker pays off. Spend time on Dockerfile and image layers, and the rest will follow.
