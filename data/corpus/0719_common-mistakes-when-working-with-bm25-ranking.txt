Common Mistakes When Working with BM25 Ranking

Almost everyone makes a few avoidable errors when first working with BM25 Ranking. The term BM25 Ranking refers to a ranking function that improves on plain term frequency with saturation and length normalization. This article explains the idea in plain language and points out where it fits in real projects.

Beginners often stumble on three things. First: untuned parameters. Second: ignoring field boundaries. Third: comparing scores across different queries.

Consider the first problem: untuned parameters. A short test or a careful review comment usually catches it. The second problem is similar: ignoring field boundaries. The third problem is this: comparing scores across different queries. It tends to appear only once a project grows, so it is worth planning for early.

Automate repetitive steps so that results are easy to reproduce. Ask a teammate to explain k1 and b parameters back to you; teaching is a quick way to find gaps. Write a few tests that show how BM25 Ranking should behave. Tests double as documentation for your teammates.

In short, careful study of BM25 Ranking pays off. Spend time on term saturation and document length normalization, and the rest will follow.
