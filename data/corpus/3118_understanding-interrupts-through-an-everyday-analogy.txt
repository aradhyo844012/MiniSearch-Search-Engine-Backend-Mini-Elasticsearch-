Understanding Interrupts Through an Everyday Analogy

Abstract ideas are easier to hold onto when you attach them to something familiar. So picture a phone call interrupting your reading.

In that picture, interrupt handlers is the first rule you follow, interrupt vectors is the next step, and priorities and context saving keep everything running smoothly.

Every analogy breaks down eventually. Real Interrupts is signals that make a processor pause its work to handle an urgent event, and it involves details such as long handlers that no everyday picture captures.

Use the analogy to remember the shape of the idea, and use documentation for Linux /proc/interrupts to learn the exact behavior. The same picture helps when you explain keyboard and mouse input to a friend.
