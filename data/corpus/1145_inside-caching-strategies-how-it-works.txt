Inside Caching Strategies: How It Works

This article walks through what happens inside Caching Strategies, step by step. If you build software for long enough, you will run into Caching Strategies. The term refers to approaches for storing computed results so they can be reused instead of recalculated.

Four ideas sit at the heart of Caching Strategies: cache-aside, write-through, time to live, and invalidation. Each one builds on the one before it. A good way to remember cache-aside is to connect it with a concrete example from your own code.

First, cache-aside sets the stage for everything else. Next, write-through builds on it. After that, time to live comes into play, and finally invalidation ties the pieces together. Seeing the sequence once, with a tiny example, makes the whole topic easier to remember.

A backend turns requests from many clients into reliable, consistent responses. Clear contracts between services make systems easier to change and test.

Keep an eye on this risk during code review: stale data. Read the official documentation for Memcached; it often explains write-through better than blog posts do. Ask a teammate to explain time to live back to you; teaching is a quick way to find gaps.
