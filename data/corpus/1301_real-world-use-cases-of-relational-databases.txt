Real-World Use Cases of Relational Databases

The best way to appreciate Relational Databases is to see where it is actually used. The term Relational Databases refers to systems that store data in tables with rows, columns and defined relationships. This article explains the idea in plain language and points out where it fits in real projects.

People use Relational Databases for storing orders and customers, for reporting and analytics, and for content management.

Consider storing orders and customers. In a case like this, Relational Databases gives the work a clear structure, and understanding tables and rows makes the design easier to explain. A different example is reporting and analytics, where primary keys often decides how well the solution scales.

Backups are only useful if restoring from them has been tested.

Popular tools and libraries include PostgreSQL, MySQL, and SQLite.

None of this needs special hardware or expensive software. A laptop, curiosity and a few small experiments are enough to get comfortable with Relational Databases.
