Best Practices for Linear Regression

The practices below come from patterns that tend to work well across many teams and projects. When people talk about Linear Regression, they usually mean a method for predicting a number by fitting a straight line or plane to data. It appears in almost every serious software project, whether or not developers notice it.

1. Automate repetitive steps so that results are easy to reproduce.
2. Start small by applying Linear Regression to one case, such as predicting prices, before rolling it out more widely.
3. Ask a teammate to explain least squares back to you; teaching is a quick way to find gaps.
4. Keep notes on what worked. A short README explaining your decisions about Linear Regression helps the next developer.
5. Read the official documentation for NumPy; it often explains residuals better than blog posts do.

In practice you can start with scikit-learn, then explore NumPy or statsmodels as your needs grow.

If you are comfortable with Linear Regression, explore Decision Trees next. The two often appear in the same project.

Keep experimenting, keep notes, and revisit Linear Regression whenever a new project gives you a reason.
