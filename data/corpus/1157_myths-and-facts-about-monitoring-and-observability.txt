Myths and Facts About Monitoring and Observability

A lot of folklore has built up around Monitoring and Observability. Some of it is true, and some of it is not. Many students first meet Monitoring and Observability in a classroom, but it matters just as much in production systems. The term refers to collecting metrics, logs and traces so you can understand how a system behaves.

Automation turns risky manual steps into repeatable and reviewable processes. A system that cannot be observed cannot be reliably operated.

Myth: Monitoring and Observability is only for experts.
Fact: beginners can use it effectively once they understand metrics and logs.

Myth: Monitoring and Observability is only useful in very large systems.
Fact: small projects such as detecting outages benefit as well.

Myth: one tool does everything.
Fact: Prometheus, Grafana, and OpenTelemetry make different trade-offs, so the right choice depends on the project.

Myth: mistakes with Monitoring and Observability are always obvious.
Fact: subtle problems happen, for example noisy alerts.

With steady practice, Monitoring and Observability will stop feeling abstract and become a tool you reach for naturally.
