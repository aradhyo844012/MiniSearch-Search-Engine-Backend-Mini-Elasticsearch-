CQRS vs Saga Pattern: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study CQRS or Saga Pattern first. Both sit in the area of Distributed Systems, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

CQRS is a pattern that separates the models used to change data from the models used to read it, and it is closely tied to commands and queries. Saga Pattern, by contrast, is a way of managing long business operations across services using a sequence of local steps and compensating actions, and it is closely tied to local transactions and compensating actions.

Choose CQRS first if you care about high-read dashboards or complex domains. Choose Saga Pattern first if you care about order processing or travel bookings.

Think of CQRS as a restaurant with one counter for orders and another for pickup, and of Saga Pattern as booking a trip with separate hotel, flight and car reservations that can each be cancelled. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With CQRS, avoid adding it to simple apps. With Saga Pattern, avoid missing compensations.

A good habit for CQRS is to start with a single model and split only when reads and writes truly diverge. A good habit for Saga Pattern is to make every step and its compensation idempotent.
