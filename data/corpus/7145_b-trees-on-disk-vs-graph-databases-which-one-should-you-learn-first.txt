B-Trees on Disk vs Graph Databases: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study B-Trees on Disk or Graph Databases first. Both sit in the area of Storage Engines and Databases, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

B-Trees on Disk is balanced tree structures designed to read large blocks from disk efficiently, and it is closely tied to pages and branching factor. Graph Databases, by contrast, is databases that store data as nodes and relationships and query connections directly, and it is closely tied to nodes and edges.

Choose B-Trees on Disk first if you care about database indexes or file system directories. Choose Graph Databases first if you care about social networks or recommendation engines.

Think of B-Trees on Disk as a filing cabinet where each drawer tells you which drawer to open next, and of Graph Databases as a city map where intersections are records and roads are relationships. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With B-Trees on Disk, avoid random writes on fragmented pages. With Graph Databases, avoid using them for simple tabular data.

A good habit for B-Trees on Disk is to keep indexed keys short so more of them fit on a page. A good habit for Graph Databases is to limit traversal depth in every exploratory query.
