The Trade-offs of Docker

Every design choice has a cost, and Docker is no exception. Many students first meet Docker in a classroom, but it matters just as much in production systems. The term refers to a platform for building and running applications in containers.

On the positive side, Docker is well suited to reproducible development setups and shipping applications. On the other hand, it is not free. Keep these risks in mind: huge images; secrets baked into images; running as root. Whether the trade is worth it depends on how much Dockerfile and image layers matter to your project.

Practice Dockerfile with small exercises until it feels natural. Measure before you optimize. Use Docker Compose or a simple timer to see where time is actually spent. Keep notes on what worked. A short README explaining your decisions about Docker helps the next developer.

Keep an eye on this risk during code review: huge images. Ask a teammate to explain volumes back to you; teaching is a quick way to find gaps.

None of this needs special hardware or expensive software. A laptop, curiosity and a few small experiments are enough to get comfortable with Docker.
