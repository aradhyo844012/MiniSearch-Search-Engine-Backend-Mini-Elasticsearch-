Is Consistent Hashing Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Consistent Hashing, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Consistent Hashing is a way of mapping keys to servers so that adding or removing servers moves very few keys.

It is a good fit when your work involves distributed caches or sharded databases, and when your team understands hash ring.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn virtual nodes, or when you cannot manage these risks: too few virtual nodes, uneven load, and hot keys.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether key placement and rebalancing are part of the answer. If they are, Consistent Hashing is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with load balancing and use many virtual nodes per server to even out the load.
