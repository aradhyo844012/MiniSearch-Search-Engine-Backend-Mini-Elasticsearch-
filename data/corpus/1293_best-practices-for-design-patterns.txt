Best Practices for Design Patterns

The practices below come from patterns that tend to work well across many teams and projects. Many students first meet Design Patterns in a classroom, but it matters just as much in production systems. The term refers to reusable solutions to common problems in software design.

1. Read the official documentation for refactoring guides; it often explains factory better than blog posts do.
2. Write a few tests that show how Design Patterns should behave. Tests double as documentation for your teammates.
3. Ask a teammate to explain observer back to you; teaching is a quick way to find gaps.
4. Practice singleton with small exercises until it feels natural.
5. Measure before you optimize. Use UML diagrams or a simple timer to see where time is actually spent.

Popular tools and libraries include UML diagrams, refactoring guides, and code examples.

Keep an eye on this risk during code review: forcing patterns where they are unnecessary. Practice singleton with small exercises until it feels natural.

A solid grasp of Design Patterns also makes Clean Code easier to understand.

In short, careful study of Design Patterns pays off. Spend time on singleton and factory, and the rest will follow.
