A Beginner's Guide to Large Language Models

This guide is written for readers who are new to Large Language Models and want a clear starting point. Many students first meet Large Language Models in a classroom, but it matters just as much in production systems. The term refers to very large neural networks trained on text that can generate and analyze language.

A model is only as good as the data used to train and test it.

The vocabulary of Large Language Models is small. Learn tokens, attention, pretraining, and prompts, and most documentation will start to make sense. Understanding attention first often makes pretraining much easier to reason about.

You will see Large Language Models whenever people work on drafting text or answering questions. It also helps with assisting with code.

Popular tools and libraries include Hugging Face, API playgrounds, and evaluation harnesses.

Three issues come up again and again: trusting outputs without checking; vague prompts; and sending sensitive data carelessly. Knowing them in advance saves hours of debugging.
