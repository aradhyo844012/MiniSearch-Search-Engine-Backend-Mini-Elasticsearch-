Combining Backtracking with Skip Lists in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Backtracking and Skip Lists can work together in a single project.

Backtracking is a search technique that builds solutions step by step and undoes steps that lead to dead ends. Skip Lists is layered linked lists that allow fast searching by skipping over many elements.

Imagine a project that involves solving Sudoku and ordered in-memory indexes. Backtracking brings decision tree, while Skip Lists brings multiple levels.

A sensible plan is to start with Python recursion for the Backtracking part and Redis sorted sets for the Skip Lists part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: missing pruning rules on one side, and memory overhead on the other.

Picture the whole as walking a maze and returning to the last fork whenever you hit a wall, working alongside an express train line layered over a local train line.

Two habits help: first, prune as early as possible because the savings multiply; second, draw several levels by hand to see how a search moves down.
