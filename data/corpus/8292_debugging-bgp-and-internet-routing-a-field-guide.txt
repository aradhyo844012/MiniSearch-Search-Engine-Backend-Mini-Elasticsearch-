Debugging BGP and Internet Routing: A Field Guide

When something goes wrong around BGP and Internet Routing, resist the urge to guess. Work through the problem in order.

Step 1: reproduce the problem with the smallest possible example.
Step 2: list the usual suspects: route leaks, hijacked prefixes, and misconfigured filters.
Step 3: revisit autonomous systems and route announcements, since misunderstandings there cause many bugs.
Step 4: use bgpq4 or similar tools to look inside.
Step 5: write a test that fails because of the bug and passes after the fix.

Many bugs involving BGP and Internet Routing in work such as connecting ISPs come down to the first suspect on the list. Check the simple causes before suspecting anything exotic.

Finally, filter route announcements strictly. It prevents a surprising number of repeat problems.
