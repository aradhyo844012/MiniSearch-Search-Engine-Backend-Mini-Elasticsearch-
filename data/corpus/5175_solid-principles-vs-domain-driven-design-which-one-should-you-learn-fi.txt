SOLID Principles vs Domain-Driven Design: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study SOLID Principles or Domain-Driven Design first. Both sit in the area of Software Design, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

SOLID Principles is five guidelines for designing object-oriented code that is easy to change, and it is closely tied to single responsibility and the open-closed principle. Domain-Driven Design, by contrast, is an approach to software that models the code around the real business domain, and it is closely tied to ubiquitous language and bounded contexts.

Choose SOLID Principles first if you care about structuring classes or reviewing designs. Choose Domain-Driven Design first if you care about complex business systems or aligning developers with domain experts.

Think of SOLID Principles as house rules that keep a shared kitchen tidy, and of Domain-Driven Design as writing software in the same vocabulary the business speaks. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With SOLID Principles, avoid applying them rigidly to tiny scripts. With Domain-Driven Design, avoid using it for simple CRUD apps.

A good habit for SOLID Principles is to apply them when a design starts to hurt, not before. A good habit for Domain-Driven Design is to agree on shared terms with domain experts before drawing classes.
