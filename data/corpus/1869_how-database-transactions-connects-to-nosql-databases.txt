How Database Transactions Connects to NoSQL Databases

Database Transactions and NoSQL Databases are closely connected parts of Databases. Seeing how they fit together helps you learn both faster.

A project involving transferring money between accounts may also involve flexible schemas. In such a project, atomicity and document stores often influence each other.

Start with Database Transactions: the term refers to groups of operations that succeed or fail together. Then look at NoSQL Databases, which refers to a broad family of databases that do not use the traditional relational table model.

A useful exercise is to take one example, such as placing orders, and explain how key-value stores would change the design.

Common problems are shared too. For Database Transactions, watch for this: ignoring isolation levels. For NoSQL Databases, watch for this: weak consistency surprises. Most slow pages in data-driven applications trace back to a handful of slow queries.

Spend a few hours on each topic, then combine them in one small project using PostgreSQL or MongoDB.
