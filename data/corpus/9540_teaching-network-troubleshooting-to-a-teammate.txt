Teaching Network Troubleshooting to a Teammate

Explaining Network Troubleshooting to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: a doctor who checks the heart, lungs and blood pressure in order. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Network Troubleshooting is a step-by-step process for finding out why a network connection fails or is slow. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving fixing office connectivity, naming protocol layers and ping and traceroute as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain DNS checks in Network Troubleshooting back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, changing several things at once, and suggest this habit: work from the bottom layer upward.
