Teaching Debugging Strategies to a Teammate

Explaining Debugging Strategies to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: a detective narrowing down suspects. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Debugging Strategies is systematic ways of finding and fixing the cause of unexpected program behavior. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving fixing production issues, naming reproducing the bug and isolating the cause as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain forming hypotheses in Debugging Strategies back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, changing many things at once, and suggest this habit: change one thing at a time and record the result.
