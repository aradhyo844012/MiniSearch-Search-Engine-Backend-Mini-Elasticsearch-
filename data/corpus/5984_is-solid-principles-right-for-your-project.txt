Is SOLID Principles Right for Your Project?

Before adopting SOLID Principles, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that SOLID Principles is five guidelines for designing object-oriented code that is easy to change.

It is a good fit when your work involves structuring classes or reviewing designs, and when your team understands single responsibility.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn the open-closed principle, or when you cannot manage these risks: applying them rigidly to tiny scripts, creating too many tiny classes, and confusing interfaces with inheritance.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether Liskov substitution and dependency inversion are part of the answer. If they are, SOLID Principles is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with reducing coupling and apply them when a design starts to hurt, not before.
