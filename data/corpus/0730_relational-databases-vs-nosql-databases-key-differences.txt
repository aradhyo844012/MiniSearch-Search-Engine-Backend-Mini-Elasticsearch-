Relational Databases vs NoSQL Databases: Key Differences

Relational Databases and NoSQL Databases both belong to the area of Databases, but they solve different problems. This article compares them side by side so you can choose with confidence.

The term Relational Databases refers to systems that store data in tables with rows, columns and defined relationships. Its core ideas are tables and rows, primary keys, and foreign keys.

The term NoSQL Databases refers to a broad family of databases that do not use the traditional relational table model. Its core ideas are document stores, key-value stores, and column families.

The main difference is focus. Relational Databases puts the emphasis on tables and rows and primary keys, while NoSQL Databases puts the emphasis on document stores and key-value stores.

Reach for Relational Databases when your task involves storing orders and customers or reporting and analytics. Reach for NoSQL Databases when your task involves flexible schemas or high write throughput.

Both have risks. With Relational Databases, watch for this: skipping constraints. With NoSQL Databases, watch for this: assuming no schema means no design.

In practice you do not always have to choose. Many projects use Relational Databases alongside NoSQL Databases, with tools such as PostgreSQL and MongoDB. Most slow pages in data-driven applications trace back to a handful of slow queries.
