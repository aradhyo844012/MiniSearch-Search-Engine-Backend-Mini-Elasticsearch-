Chaos Engineering in 10 Minutes

You have ten minutes, so here is the fast route through Chaos Engineering.

Minutes 1 and 2, the definition: Chaos Engineering is deliberately causing failures in a system to learn how it responds.

Minutes 3 and 4, the vocabulary: learn steady state, hypotheses, blast radius, and fault injection.

Minutes 5 and 6, the picture: imagine a fire drill where you secretly block one exit. That is the intuition.

Minutes 7 and 8, the use cases: skim through testing resilience, validating failover, and preparing teams for incidents.

Minutes 9 and 10, the traps and the tools: watch for experimenting without safeguards and having no steady-state metrics, then try Chaos Monkey for a first experiment.

If you have a spare minute, remember to start with small experiments in a test environment.
