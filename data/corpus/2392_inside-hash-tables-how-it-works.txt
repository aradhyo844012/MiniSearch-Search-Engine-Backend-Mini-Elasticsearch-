Inside Hash Tables: How It Works

This article walks through what happens inside Hash Tables, step by step. When people talk about Hash Tables, they usually mean a way of storing key-value pairs so that lookups take roughly constant time. It appears in almost every serious software project, whether or not developers notice it.

Four ideas sit at the heart of Hash Tables: hash function, buckets, collision handling, and load factor. Each one builds on the one before it. A good way to remember hash function is to connect it with a concrete example from your own code.

First, hash function sets the stage for everything else. Next, buckets builds on it. After that, collision handling comes into play, and finally load factor ties the pieces together. Seeing the sequence once, with a tiny example, makes the whole topic easier to remember.

Most performance problems in everyday programs come from doing unnecessary work on large inputs. Interviewers and engineers alike value clear reasoning about time and space costs.
