Teaching Interrupts to a Teammate

Explaining Interrupts to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: a phone call interrupting your reading. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Interrupts is signals that make a processor pause its work to handle an urgent event. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving keyboard and mouse input, naming interrupt handlers and interrupt vectors as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain priorities in Interrupts back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, long handlers, and suggest this habit: keep interrupt handlers short and defer the heavy work.
