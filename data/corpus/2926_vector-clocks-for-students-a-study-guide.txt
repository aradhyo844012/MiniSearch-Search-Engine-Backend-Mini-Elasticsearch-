Vector Clocks for Students: A Study Guide

This study guide prepares you for exams, labs and interviews that cover Vector Clocks.

Learning goals:
1. Explain Vector Clocks in your own words.
2. Describe logical time, per-process counters, the happened-before relation, and conflict detection.
3. Give examples from detecting concurrent edits and replicated databases.
4. Name three problems to watch for.

Start with the definition: Vector Clocks is counters that let distributed processes track the order of events without a shared clock. Then draw each person in a group chat keeping a tally of messages seen from everyone on paper and label the four terms from the goals above.

Self-test questions: Why is clocks that grow with many nodes a problem? How does per-process counters relate to the happened-before relation? When would you choose Riak or the Amazon Dynamo paper?

Study habit worth keeping: compare clocks entry by entry to detect true concurrency.

When you feel ready, compare Vector Clocks with CAP Theorem and write down three differences.
