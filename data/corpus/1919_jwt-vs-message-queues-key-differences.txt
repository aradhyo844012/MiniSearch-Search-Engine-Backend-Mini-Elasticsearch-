JWT vs Message Queues: Key Differences

JWT and Message Queues both belong to the area of Backend and APIs, but they solve different problems. This article compares them side by side so you can choose with confidence.

The term JWT refers to a compact token format that carries signed claims between a client and a server. Its core ideas are header, payload and signature, claims, and expiry.

The term Message Queues refers to systems that let services send work to each other asynchronously through a buffer. Its core ideas are producers, consumers, and acknowledgements.

The main difference is focus. JWT puts the emphasis on header, payload and signature and claims, while Message Queues puts the emphasis on producers and consumers.

Reach for JWT when your task involves stateless API authentication or service-to-service calls. Reach for Message Queues when your task involves sending emails or processing uploads.

Both have risks. With JWT, watch for this: storing tokens in insecure places. With Message Queues, watch for this: losing messages without acknowledgements.

In practice you do not always have to choose. Many projects use JWT alongside Message Queues, with tools such as jwt.io and RabbitMQ. Clear contracts between services make systems easier to change and test.
