Inside Docker: How It Works

This article walks through what happens inside Docker, step by step. Many students first meet Docker in a classroom, but it matters just as much in production systems. The term refers to a platform for building and running applications in containers.

To understand Docker, start with Dockerfile. Then move on to image layers, and finally look at volumes and networks. A good way to remember Dockerfile is to connect it with a concrete example from your own code.

First, Dockerfile sets the stage for everything else. Next, image layers builds on it. After that, volumes comes into play, and finally networks ties the pieces together. Seeing the sequence once, with a tiny example, makes the whole topic easier to remember.

A system that cannot be observed cannot be reliably operated.

A solid grasp of Docker also makes Cloud Storage easier to understand.

In short, careful study of Docker pays off. Spend time on Dockerfile and image layers, and the rest will follow.
