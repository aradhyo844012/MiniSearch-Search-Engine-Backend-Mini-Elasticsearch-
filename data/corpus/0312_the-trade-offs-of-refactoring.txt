The Trade-offs of Refactoring

Every design choice has a cost, and Refactoring is no exception. Put simply, Refactoring covers improving the structure of existing code without changing its behavior. Learning it well pays off across many areas of computing.

On the positive side, Refactoring is well suited to reducing duplication and preparing for new features. On the other hand, it is not free. Keep these risks in mind: refactoring without tests; mixing refactoring with new features; rewriting instead of improving. Whether the trade is worth it depends on how much small steps and tests as a safety net matter to your project.

Read the official documentation for pytest; it often explains tests as a safety net better than blog posts do. Practice small steps with small exercises until it feels natural.

Watch out for these problems: refactoring without tests; mixing refactoring with new features; and rewriting instead of improving.
