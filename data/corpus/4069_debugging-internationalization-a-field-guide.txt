Debugging Internationalization: A Field Guide

When something goes wrong around Internationalization, resist the urge to guess. Work through the problem in order.

Step 1: reproduce the problem with the smallest possible example.
Step 2: list the usual suspects: hard-coded strings, text that overflows layouts, and assuming left-to-right text.
Step 3: revisit locale detection and translation files, since misunderstandings there cause many bugs.
Step 4: use i18next or similar tools to look inside.
Step 5: write a test that fails because of the bug and passes after the fix.

Many bugs involving Internationalization in work such as global products come down to the first suspect on the list. Check the simple causes before suspecting anything exotic.

Finally, move every user-facing string into translation files from the first day. It prevents a surprising number of repeat problems.
