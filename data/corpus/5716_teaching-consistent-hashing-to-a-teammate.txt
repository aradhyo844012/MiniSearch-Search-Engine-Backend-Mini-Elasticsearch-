Teaching Consistent Hashing to a Teammate

Explaining Consistent Hashing to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: seats around a round table where a new guest only displaces their neighbor. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Consistent Hashing is a way of mapping keys to servers so that adding or removing servers moves very few keys. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving distributed caches, naming hash ring and virtual nodes as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain key placement in Consistent Hashing back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, too few virtual nodes, and suggest this habit: use many virtual nodes per server to even out the load.
