Combining SOLID Principles with Modular Monoliths in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how SOLID Principles and Modular Monoliths can work together in a single project.

SOLID Principles is five guidelines for designing object-oriented code that is easy to change. Modular Monoliths is single deployable applications built from well-separated internal modules.

Imagine a project that involves structuring classes and small teams. SOLID Principles brings single responsibility, while Modular Monoliths brings module boundaries.

A sensible plan is to start with UML diagrams for the SOLID Principles part and ArchUnit for the Modular Monoliths part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: creating too many tiny classes on one side, and no enforced boundaries on the other.

Picture the whole as house rules that keep a shared kitchen tidy, working alongside an apartment building with separate flats under one roof.

Two habits help: first, apply them when a design starts to hurt, not before; second, enforce boundaries with automated checks.
