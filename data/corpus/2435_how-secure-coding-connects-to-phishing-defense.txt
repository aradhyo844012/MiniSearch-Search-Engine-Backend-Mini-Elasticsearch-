How Secure Coding Connects to Phishing Defense

Secure Coding and Phishing Defense are closely connected parts of Security. Seeing how they fit together helps you learn both faster.

A project involving reviewing pull requests may also involve protecting employees. In such a project, input validation and suspicious links often influence each other.

Start with Secure Coding: the term refers to writing software in ways that avoid common vulnerabilities. Then look at Phishing Defense, which refers to recognizing and reducing attacks that trick people into revealing information.

A useful exercise is to take one example, such as building web services, and explain how sender verification would change the design.

Common problems are shared too. For Secure Coding, watch for this: committing secrets to repositories. For Phishing Defense, watch for this: reusing passwords. Attackers usually choose the easiest weakness, not the most interesting one.

Spend a few hours on each topic, then combine them in one small project using OWASP Top 10 or SPF.
