Best Practices for Data Cleaning

The practices below come from patterns that tend to work well across many teams and projects. When people talk about Data Cleaning, they usually mean finding and fixing errors, gaps and inconsistencies in raw data. It appears in almost every serious software project, whether or not developers notice it.

1. Keep notes on what worked. A short README explaining your decisions about Data Cleaning helps the next developer.
2. Measure before you optimize. Use pandas or a simple timer to see where time is actually spent.
3. Practice missing values with small exercises until it feels natural.
4. Read the official documentation for OpenRefine; it often explains duplicates better than blog posts do.
5. Write a few tests that show how Data Cleaning should behave. Tests double as documentation for your teammates.

A chart is only useful if the reader can see the point within a few seconds.

In practice you can start with pandas, then explore OpenRefine or regular expressions as your needs grow.

A solid grasp of Data Cleaning also makes ETL Pipelines easier to understand.

None of this needs special hardware or expensive software. A laptop, curiosity and a few small experiments are enough to get comfortable with Data Cleaning.
