Is Data Anonymization Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Data Anonymization, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Data Anonymization is removing or altering personal details in data so people cannot be identified.

It is a good fit when your work involves sharing datasets with researchers or testing with production-like data, and when your team understands masking.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn generalization, or when you cannot manage these risks: re-identification through combined fields, removing too little, and destroying usefulness by removing too much.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether k-anonymity and differential privacy are part of the answer. If they are, Data Anonymization is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with meeting privacy rules and test whether combining fields could identify a person.
