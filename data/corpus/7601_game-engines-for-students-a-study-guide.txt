Game Engines for Students: A Study Guide

This study guide prepares you for exams, labs and interviews that cover Game Engines.

Learning goals:
1. Explain Game Engines in your own words.
2. Describe scenes, assets, scripting, and build pipelines.
3. Give examples from indie games and simulations.
4. Name three problems to watch for.

Start with the definition: Game Engines is software frameworks that provide graphics, physics, input and tools for building games. Then draw a fully equipped workshop with the tools for building a game on paper and label the four terms from the goals above.

Self-test questions: Why is choosing an engine by hype a problem? How does assets relate to scripting? When would you choose Unity or Godot?

Study habit worth keeping: build a tiny prototype in two engines before choosing.

When you feel ready, compare Game Engines with Shaders and write down three differences.
