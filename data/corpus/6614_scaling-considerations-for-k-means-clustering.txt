Scaling Considerations for K-Means Clustering

A design that works for a classroom project can behave very differently under real load. Here is how K-Means Clustering changes as things grow.

Small scale: almost any approach works. Focus on understanding centroids and getting customer segmentation right.

Medium scale: problems start to appear. Watch for choosing k blindly and unscaled features, and measure with scikit-learn before changing anything.

Large scale: choosing k and iterations move from background details to central design questions. Teams often revisit their choices around image compression at this stage.

Before you scale, scale features before clustering. It is cheaper to build that habit early than to fix a crisis later.
