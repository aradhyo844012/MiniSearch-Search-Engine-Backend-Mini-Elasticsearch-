Combining Interrupts with Compilers and Interpreters in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Interrupts and Compilers and Interpreters can work together in a single project.

Interrupts is signals that make a processor pause its work to handle an urgent event. Compilers and Interpreters is programs that translate or run source code written in a high-level language.

Imagine a project that involves keyboard and mouse input and running Python and JavaScript. Interrupts brings interrupt handlers, while Compilers and Interpreters brings lexing.

A sensible plan is to start with Linux /proc/interrupts for the Interrupts part and GCC for the Compilers and Interpreters part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: race conditions with shared data on one side, and ignoring compiler warnings on the other.

Picture the whole as a phone call interrupting your reading, working alongside a translator who either translates a whole book first or interprets sentence by sentence.

Two habits help: first, keep interrupt handlers short and defer the heavy work; second, turn on compiler warnings and treat them seriously.
