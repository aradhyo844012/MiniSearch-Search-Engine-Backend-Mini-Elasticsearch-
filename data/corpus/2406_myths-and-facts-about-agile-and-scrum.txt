Myths and Facts About Agile and Scrum

A lot of folklore has built up around Agile and Scrum. Some of it is true, and some of it is not. Many students first meet Agile and Scrum in a classroom, but it matters just as much in production systems. The term refers to iterative ways of planning and delivering software in short cycles.

Myth: Agile and Scrum is only for experts.
Fact: beginners can use it effectively once they understand sprints and backlogs.

Myth: Agile and Scrum is only useful in very large systems.
Fact: small projects such as student team projects benefit as well.

Myth: one tool does everything.
Fact: Jira, Trello, and GitHub Projects make different trade-offs, so the right choice depends on the project.

Myth: mistakes with Agile and Scrum are always obvious.
Fact: subtle problems happen, for example skipping retrospectives.

A solid grasp of Agile and Scrum also makes Version Control Workflow easier to understand.

None of this needs special hardware or expensive software. A laptop, curiosity and a few small experiments are enough to get comfortable with Agile and Scrum.
