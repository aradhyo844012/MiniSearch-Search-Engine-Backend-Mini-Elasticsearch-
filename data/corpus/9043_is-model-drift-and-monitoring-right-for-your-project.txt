Is Model Drift and Monitoring Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Model Drift and Monitoring, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Model Drift and Monitoring is watching deployed models for changes in data or performance over time.

It is a good fit when your work involves catching degrading models or scheduling retraining, and when your team understands data drift.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn concept drift, or when you cannot manage these risks: monitoring only system health, having no ground truth labels, and alert fatigue.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether performance metrics and alerts are part of the answer. If they are, Model Drift and Monitoring is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with keeping predictions trustworthy and monitor input data as well as accuracy.
