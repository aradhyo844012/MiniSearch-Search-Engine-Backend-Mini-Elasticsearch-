Bash Scripting for Students: A Study Guide

This study guide prepares you for exams, labs and interviews that cover Bash Scripting.

Learning goals:
1. Explain Bash Scripting in your own words.
2. Describe variables, conditionals and loops, exit codes, and functions.
3. Give examples from automating deployments and processing text files.
4. Name three problems to watch for.

Start with the definition: Bash Scripting is writing shell scripts that automate tasks by chaining commands, variables and control flow. Then draw a recipe card that the computer follows without help on paper and label the four terms from the goals above.

Self-test questions: Why is unquoted variables a problem? How does conditionals and loops relate to exit codes? When would you choose Bash or ShellCheck?

Study habit worth keeping: start scripts with set -euo pipefail and quote every variable.

When you feel ready, compare Bash Scripting with Package Managers and write down three differences.
