Teaching Compilers and Interpreters to a Teammate

Explaining Compilers and Interpreters to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: a translator who either translates a whole book first or interprets sentence by sentence. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Compilers and Interpreters is programs that translate or run source code written in a high-level language. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving running Python and JavaScript, naming lexing and parsing as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain code generation in Compilers and Interpreters back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, assuming interpreted means slow without measuring, and suggest this habit: turn on compiler warnings and treat them seriously.
