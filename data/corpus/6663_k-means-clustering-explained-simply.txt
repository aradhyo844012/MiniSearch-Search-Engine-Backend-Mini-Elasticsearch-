K-Means Clustering Explained Simply

Here is K-Means Clustering without the jargon: it is an algorithm that groups data points into a chosen number of clusters around central points.

Think of K-Means Clustering as dividing students into study groups around the most central member. Keep that picture in mind as you read on.

The vocabulary has four main pieces: centroids, distance measures, choosing k, and iterations. You do not need to master them all at once. Start with centroids, then add distance measures.

You will meet K-Means Clustering in work such as customer segmentation, image compression, and grouping documents.

One practical tip is to scale features before clustering.

After K-Means Clustering, a natural next topic is Support Vector Machines.
