Site Reliability: Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What is Site Reliability?
A: The term Site Reliability refers to the discipline of keeping services dependable using engineering practices.

Q: Do I need special hardware to practice?
A: No. A normal laptop and a few small experiments are enough to get comfortable with Site Reliability.

Q: Which tools help?
A: Popular choices include PagerDuty, Prometheus, and runbooks.

Q: What should a beginner learn first?
A: Start with service level objectives and error budgets, then move on to incident response and postmortems.

Q: Why do developers use Site Reliability?
A: Common uses include running production services, reducing outages, and planning on-call rotations.
