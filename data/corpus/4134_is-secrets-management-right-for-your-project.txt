Is Secrets Management Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Secrets Management, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Secrets Management is storing and distributing passwords, keys and tokens securely.

It is a good fit when your work involves database credentials or API keys, and when your team understands vaults.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn rotation, or when you cannot manage these risks: secrets in source control, long-lived shared credentials, and no rotation plan.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether access policies and audit logs are part of the answer. If they are, Secrets Management is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with TLS certificates and rotate secrets on a schedule and after every suspected leak.
