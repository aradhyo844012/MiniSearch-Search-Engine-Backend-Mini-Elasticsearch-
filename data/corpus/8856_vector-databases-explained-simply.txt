Vector Databases Explained Simply

Here is Vector Databases without the jargon: it is databases built to store embeddings and find the most similar ones quickly.

Think of Vector Databases as a map where similar ideas sit near each other. Keep that picture in mind as you read on.

The vocabulary has four main pieces: embeddings, approximate nearest neighbors, distance metrics, and metadata filters. You do not need to master them all at once. Start with embeddings, then add approximate nearest neighbors.

You will meet Vector Databases in work such as semantic search, recommendations, and retrieval for AI assistants.

One practical tip is to measure recall on a small labeled test set.

Once Vector Databases feels comfortable, read about Views and Materialized Views, which lives in the same neighborhood of ideas.
