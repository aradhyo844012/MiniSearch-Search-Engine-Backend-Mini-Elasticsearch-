Teaching Computability and the Halting Problem to a Teammate

Explaining Computability and the Halting Problem to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: asking a machine to predict whether any program will ever finish. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Computability and the Halting Problem is the study of what problems computers can and cannot solve. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving understanding the limits of tools, naming Turing machines and decidability as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain the halting problem in Computability and the Halting Problem back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, assuming everything is solvable, and suggest this habit: learn the diagonal argument step by step.
