CAP Theorem Quick Reference

A one-screen reminder for CAP Theorem. Bookmark it and return when the details fade.

What it is: a result stating that a distributed system cannot guarantee consistency, availability and partition tolerance all at once.
Key terms: consistency, availability, partition tolerance, and trade-offs.
Typical uses: choosing database types, designing multi-region services, and explaining system behavior in interviews.
Risks: treating it as a three-way menu, ignoring latency trade-offs in normal operation, and assuming partitions never happen.
Tools: Apache Cassandra, etcd, and ZooKeeper.

Mental picture: a phone tree that must choose between giving old information or staying silent when a line is cut.

Habit to build: ask what your users prefer during a failure: stale answers or errors.

See also: Idempotency, which sits close to CAP Theorem in the area of Distributed Systems.
