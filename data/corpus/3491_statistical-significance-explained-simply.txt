Statistical Significance Explained Simply

If Statistical Significance sounds intimidating, relax. It is a measure of how unlikely a result would be if there were no real effect, and the idea is simpler than the name suggests.

Think of Statistical Significance as asking how surprised you should be if a coin were actually fair. Keep that picture in mind as you read on.

The vocabulary has four main pieces: p-values, the null hypothesis, confidence intervals, and effect size. You do not need to master them all at once. Start with p-values, then add the null hypothesis.

People reach for Statistical Significance when they work on judging experiment results, comparing model performance, and reporting research findings.

One practical tip is to always report the effect size alongside the p-value.
