Skills to Build Around WebAssembly

Knowing the definition of WebAssembly is only the start. These skills turn that knowledge into something useful.

Foundations: be comfortable with modules and linear memory, and able to explain them without notes.

Hands-on practice: spend real time with Emscripten on a WebAssembly task, and try at least one alternative such as wasm-pack.

Judgment: learn to decide when WebAssembly fits a problem such as running heavy computation in the browser, and when something simpler is better.

Communication: practice describing it with an everyday picture, for example a fast engine installed in the corner of a web page.

Habits: measure before moving code to WebAssembly, and write down what you learn after each project.
