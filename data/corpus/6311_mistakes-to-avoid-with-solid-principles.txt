Mistakes to Avoid with SOLID Principles

Everyone learns SOLID Principles partly through mistakes. Here are three worth avoiding.

Mistake one: applying them rigidly to tiny scripts. It usually appears when someone skips single responsibility and jumps straight to implementation. The fix is to slow down and review the basics.

Mistake two: creating too many tiny classes. It tends to be noticed late, often during testing of structuring classes. A short checklist during review catches it early.

Mistake three: confusing interfaces with inheritance. It is easy to miss because everything seems to work at first. Keep an eye on dependency inversion to spot it.

If you remember only one rule, make it this: apply them when a design starts to hurt, not before.
