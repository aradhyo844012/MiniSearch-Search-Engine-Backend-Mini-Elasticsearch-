Combining Mocking and Stubbing with Chaos Engineering in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Mocking and Stubbing and Chaos Engineering can work together in a single project.

Mocking and Stubbing is replacing real dependencies with controlled fakes during tests. Chaos Engineering is deliberately causing failures in a system to learn how it responds.

Imagine a project that involves isolating units and testing resilience. Mocking and Stubbing brings mocks, while Chaos Engineering brings steady state.

A sensible plan is to start with unittest.mock for the Mocking and Stubbing part and Chaos Monkey for the Chaos Engineering part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: tests that mirror the implementation on one side, and having no steady-state metrics on the other.

Picture the whole as a crash test dummy standing in for a real passenger, working alongside a fire drill where you secretly block one exit.

Two habits help: first, prefer simple fakes over deep mock chains; second, start with small experiments in a test environment.
