Best Practices for Natural Language Processing

The practices below come from patterns that tend to work well across many teams and projects. When people talk about Natural Language Processing, they usually mean the field of teaching computers to work with human language. It appears in almost every serious software project, whether or not developers notice it.

1. Start small by applying Natural Language Processing to one case, such as search engines, before rolling it out more widely.
2. Read the official documentation for NLTK; it often explains embeddings better than blog posts do.
3. Measure before you optimize. Use spaCy or a simple timer to see where time is actually spent.
4. Write a few tests that show how Natural Language Processing should behave. Tests double as documentation for your teammates.
5. Automate repetitive steps so that results are easy to reproduce.

Popular tools and libraries include spaCy, NLTK, and Hugging Face.

If you are comfortable with Natural Language Processing, explore Gradient Descent next. The two often appear in the same project.

A solid grasp of Natural Language Processing also makes Gradient Descent easier to understand.

With steady practice, Natural Language Processing will stop feeling abstract and become a tool you reach for naturally.
