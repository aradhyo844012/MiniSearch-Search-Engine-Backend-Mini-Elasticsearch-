Why Network Troubleshooting Matters for Developers

Developers who understand Network Troubleshooting make better decisions, because Network Troubleshooting is a step-by-step process for finding out why a network connection fails or is slow.

Three situations show why it matters. First, fixing office connectivity. Second, diagnosing slow apps. Third, verifying firewall changes. In each case, a solid grasp of protocol layers and ping and traceroute separates a quick fix from a lasting solution.

Ignoring it has a cost. Teams run into these problems: changing several things at once, blaming the application too early, and ignoring DNS.

Good tools exist, including ping, traceroute, and Wireshark, so you rarely have to start from nothing.
