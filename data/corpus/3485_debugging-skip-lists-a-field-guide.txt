Debugging Skip Lists: A Field Guide

When something goes wrong around Skip Lists, resist the urge to guess. Work through the problem in order.

Step 1: reproduce the problem with the smallest possible example.
Step 2: list the usual suspects: poor random number choices, memory overhead, and harder debugging than arrays.
Step 3: revisit multiple levels and random level assignment, since misunderstandings there cause many bugs.
Step 4: use Redis sorted sets or similar tools to look inside.
Step 5: write a test that fails because of the bug and passes after the fix.

Many bugs involving Skip Lists in work such as ordered in-memory indexes come down to the first suspect on the list. Check the simple causes before suspecting anything exotic.

Finally, draw several levels by hand to see how a search moves down. It prevents a surprising number of repeat problems.
