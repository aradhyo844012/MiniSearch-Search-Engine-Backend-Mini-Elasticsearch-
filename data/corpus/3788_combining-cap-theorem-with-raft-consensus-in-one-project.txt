Combining CAP Theorem with Raft Consensus in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how CAP Theorem and Raft Consensus can work together in a single project.

CAP Theorem is a result stating that a distributed system cannot guarantee consistency, availability and partition tolerance all at once. Raft Consensus is an algorithm that lets a group of servers agree on a shared log of operations.

Imagine a project that involves choosing database types and configuration stores. CAP Theorem brings consistency, while Raft Consensus brings leader election.

A sensible plan is to start with Apache Cassandra for the CAP Theorem part and etcd for the Raft Consensus part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: ignoring latency trade-offs in normal operation on one side, and slow disks causing elections on the other.

Picture the whole as a phone tree that must choose between giving old information or staying silent when a line is cut, working alongside a committee that elects a chair and only records decisions most members confirm.

Two habits help: first, ask what your users prefer during a failure: stale answers or errors; second, use an odd number of nodes.
