DNS vs WebSockets: Key Differences

DNS and WebSockets both belong to the area of Networking, but they solve different problems. This article compares them side by side so you can choose with confidence.

The term DNS refers to the system that translates human-friendly domain names into IP addresses. Its core ideas are resolvers, records, and caching and TTL.

The term WebSockets refers to a protocol that keeps a two-way connection open between a browser and a server. Its core ideas are persistent connections, messages, and heartbeats.

The main difference is focus. DNS puts the emphasis on resolvers and records, while WebSockets puts the emphasis on persistent connections and messages.

Reach for DNS when your task involves mapping domains to servers or email routing. Reach for WebSockets when your task involves chat applications or live dashboards.

Both have risks. With DNS, watch for this: forgetting TTLs when migrating. With WebSockets, watch for this: forgetting reconnection logic.

In practice you do not always have to choose. Many projects use DNS alongside WebSockets, with tools such as dig and Socket.IO. Latency is limited by distance and by the number of round trips a request needs.
