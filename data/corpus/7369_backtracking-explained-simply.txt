Backtracking Explained Simply

If Backtracking sounds intimidating, relax. It is a search technique that builds solutions step by step and undoes steps that lead to dead ends, and the idea is simpler than the name suggests.

Think of Backtracking as walking a maze and returning to the last fork whenever you hit a wall. Keep that picture in mind as you read on.

The vocabulary has four main pieces: decision tree, pruning, state restoration, and constraint checking. You do not need to master them all at once. Start with decision tree, then add pruning.

People reach for Backtracking when they work on solving Sudoku, generating permutations, and N-Queens puzzles.

One practical tip is to prune as early as possible because the savings multiply.
