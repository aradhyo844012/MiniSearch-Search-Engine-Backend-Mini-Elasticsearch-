Interrupts vs Compilers and Interpreters: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Interrupts or Compilers and Interpreters first. Both sit in the area of Computer Systems, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Interrupts is signals that make a processor pause its work to handle an urgent event, and it is closely tied to interrupt handlers and interrupt vectors. Compilers and Interpreters, by contrast, is programs that translate or run source code written in a high-level language, and it is closely tied to lexing and parsing.

Choose Interrupts first if you care about keyboard and mouse input or timers. Choose Compilers and Interpreters first if you care about running Python and JavaScript or building native programs.

Think of Interrupts as a phone call interrupting your reading, and of Compilers and Interpreters as a translator who either translates a whole book first or interprets sentence by sentence. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Interrupts, avoid long handlers. With Compilers and Interpreters, avoid assuming interpreted means slow without measuring.

A good habit for Interrupts is to keep interrupt handlers short and defer the heavy work. A good habit for Compilers and Interpreters is to turn on compiler warnings and treat them seriously.
