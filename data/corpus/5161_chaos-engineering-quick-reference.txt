Chaos Engineering Quick Reference

A one-screen reminder for Chaos Engineering. Bookmark it and return when the details fade.

What it is: deliberately causing failures in a system to learn how it responds.
Key terms: steady state, hypotheses, blast radius, and fault injection.
Typical uses: testing resilience, validating failover, and preparing teams for incidents.
Risks: experimenting without safeguards, having no steady-state metrics, and running first in production.
Tools: Chaos Monkey, Gremlin, and LitmusChaos.

Mental picture: a fire drill where you secretly block one exit.

Habit to build: start with small experiments in a test environment.

See also: Static Analysis, which sits close to Chaos Engineering in the area of Testing and Quality.
