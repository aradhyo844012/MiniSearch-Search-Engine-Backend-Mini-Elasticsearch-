Combining Penetration Testing with Single Sign-On in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Penetration Testing and Single Sign-On can work together in a single project.

Penetration Testing is authorized attempts to find and exploit weaknesses in a system before real attackers do. Single Sign-On is letting users log in once and access several applications.

Imagine a project that involves pre-launch security checks and company intranets. Penetration Testing brings scoping, while Single Sign-On brings identity provider.

A sensible plan is to start with OWASP ZAP for the Penetration Testing part and Okta for the Single Sign-On part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: ignoring the findings afterward on one side, and misconfigured redirects on the other.

Picture the whole as hiring a locksmith to test your doors, working alongside a festival wristband that opens every gate.

Two habits help: first, get written authorization before any test; second, plan for identity provider outages.
