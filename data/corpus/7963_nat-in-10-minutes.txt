NAT in 10 Minutes

You have ten minutes, so here is the fast route through NAT.

Minutes 1 and 2, the definition: NAT is a technique that lets many devices share one public IP address by rewriting addresses.

Minutes 3 and 4, the vocabulary: learn private addresses, translation tables, port mapping, and port forwarding.

Minutes 5 and 6, the picture: imagine a receptionist who forwards calls for many desks through one phone number. That is the intuition.

Minutes 7 and 8, the use cases: skim through home routers, sharing limited IPv4 addresses, and hiding internal networks.

Minutes 9 and 10, the traps and the tools: watch for breaking peer-to-peer connections and exhausted translation tables, then try iptables for a first experiment.

If you have a spare minute, remember to document every port forward you create.
