Combining Consistent Hashing with Saga Pattern in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Consistent Hashing and Saga Pattern can work together in a single project.

Consistent Hashing is a way of mapping keys to servers so that adding or removing servers moves very few keys. Saga Pattern is a way of managing long business operations across services using a sequence of local steps and compensating actions.

Imagine a project that involves distributed caches and order processing. Consistent Hashing brings hash ring, while Saga Pattern brings local transactions.

A sensible plan is to start with Redis Cluster for the Consistent Hashing part and Temporal for the Saga Pattern part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: uneven load on one side, and steps that are not idempotent on the other.

Picture the whole as seats around a round table where a new guest only displaces their neighbor, working alongside booking a trip with separate hotel, flight and car reservations that can each be cancelled.

Two habits help: first, use many virtual nodes per server to even out the load; second, make every step and its compensation idempotent.
