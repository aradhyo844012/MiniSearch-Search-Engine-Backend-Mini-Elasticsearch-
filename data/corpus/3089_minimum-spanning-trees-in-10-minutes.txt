Minimum Spanning Trees in 10 Minutes

You have ten minutes, so here is the fast route through Minimum Spanning Trees.

Minutes 1 and 2, the definition: Minimum Spanning Trees is a set of edges that connects every node in a graph with the smallest possible total weight.

Minutes 3 and 4, the vocabulary: learn weighted graphs, Kruskal's algorithm, Prim's algorithm, and the cut property.

Minutes 5 and 6, the picture: imagine laying the cheapest set of roads that still links every town. That is the intuition.

Minutes 7 and 8, the use cases: skim through designing cable networks, clustering data, and reducing infrastructure cost.

Minutes 9 and 10, the traps and the tools: watch for using it on directed graphs and forgetting that the graph must be connected, then try NetworkX for a first experiment.

If you have a spare minute, remember to sort the edges once and reuse the order when testing variations.
