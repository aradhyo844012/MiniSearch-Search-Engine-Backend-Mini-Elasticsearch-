Debugging HTTP/2 and HTTP/3: A Field Guide

When something goes wrong around HTTP/2 and HTTP/3, resist the urge to guess. Work through the problem in order.

Step 1: reproduce the problem with the smallest possible example.
Step 2: list the usual suspects: assuming old tricks like sprite sheets still help, middleboxes blocking UDP, and misconfigured fallbacks.
Step 3: revisit multiplexing and header compression, since misunderstandings there cause many bugs.
Step 4: use curl with HTTP/3 support or similar tools to look inside.
Step 5: write a test that fails because of the bug and passes after the fix.

Many bugs involving HTTP/2 and HTTP/3 in work such as faster page loads come down to the first suspect on the list. Check the simple causes before suspecting anything exotic.

Finally, keep a working HTTP/1.1 fallback. It prevents a surprising number of repeat problems.
