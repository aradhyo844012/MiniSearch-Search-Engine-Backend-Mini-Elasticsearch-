NoSQL Databases: Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What is NoSQL Databases?
A: The term NoSQL Databases refers to a broad family of databases that do not use the traditional relational table model.

Q: How does it relate to Database Indexing?
A: NoSQL Databases and Database Indexing often appear in the same project, and both belong to the area of Databases.

Q: Why do developers use NoSQL Databases?
A: Common uses include flexible schemas, high write throughput, and caching and sessions.

Q: What should a beginner learn first?
A: Start with document stores and key-value stores, then move on to column families and graph databases.

Q: How long does it take to learn?
A: Most people grasp the basics in a few weeks of steady practice, then deepen their understanding by building projects.

Q: What mistakes are most common?
A: Watch for these: assuming no schema means no design; weak consistency surprises; and expensive cross-collection queries.

Q: Do I need special hardware to practice?
A: No. A normal laptop and a few small experiments are enough to get comfortable with NoSQL Databases.
