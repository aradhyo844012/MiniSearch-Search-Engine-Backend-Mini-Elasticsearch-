Teaching Collision Detection to a Teammate

Explaining Collision Detection to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: two people checking whether their personal space bubbles overlap. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Collision Detection is the process of finding out when objects in a game touch or overlap. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving platformers, naming bounding boxes and broad and narrow phases as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain spatial partitioning in Collision Detection back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, tunneling through thin objects, and suggest this habit: use simple shapes first and refine only when needed.
