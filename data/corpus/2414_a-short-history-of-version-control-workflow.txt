A Short History of Version Control Workflow

Ideas behind Version Control Workflow developed gradually as engineers ran into practical limits and shared what they learned. If you build software for long enough, you will run into Version Control Workflow. The term refers to the habits and rules a team uses to track and combine changes to code.

Version Control Workflow and Code Review are closely related, so learning both gives a fuller picture of Software Engineering.

Teams succeed when they agree on a few simple practices and follow them consistently. Software spends most of its life being changed, not written for the first time.

Four ideas sit at the heart of Version Control Workflow: commits, branches, tags, and release process. Each one builds on the one before it. A good way to remember commits is to connect it with a concrete example from your own code.

None of this needs special hardware or expensive software. A laptop, curiosity and a few small experiments are enough to get comfortable with Version Control Workflow.
