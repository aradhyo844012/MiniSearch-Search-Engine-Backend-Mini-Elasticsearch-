Assembly Language Basics vs GPUs and Parallelism: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Assembly Language Basics or GPUs and Parallelism first. Both sit in the area of Computer Systems, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Assembly Language Basics is low-level instructions that map closely to what a processor actually executes, and it is closely tied to registers and instructions. GPUs and Parallelism, by contrast, is processors with thousands of small cores that handle many similar calculations at once, and it is closely tied to threads and blocks and kernels.

Choose Assembly Language Basics first if you care about understanding compiled code or debugging crashes. Choose GPUs and Parallelism first if you care about training neural networks or rendering graphics.

Think of Assembly Language Basics as the individual muscle movements behind a simple action, and of GPUs and Parallelism as a stadium of workers each doing one small identical task. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Assembly Language Basics, avoid wrong calling conventions. With GPUs and Parallelism, avoid slow data transfers.

A good habit for Assembly Language Basics is to read the assembly of a tiny C function to see what the compiler does. A good habit for GPUs and Parallelism is to minimize copies between CPU and GPU memory.
