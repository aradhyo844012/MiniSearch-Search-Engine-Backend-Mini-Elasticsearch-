CPU Caches vs Instruction Pipelines: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study CPU Caches or Instruction Pipelines first. Both sit in the area of Computer Systems, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

CPU Caches is small, fast memory close to the processor that stores recently used data, and it is closely tied to cache lines and hit and miss rates. Instruction Pipelines, by contrast, is a technique where a processor overlaps the stages of several instructions, and it is closely tied to fetch, decode and execute stages and hazards.

Choose CPU Caches first if you care about speeding up loops or explaining performance differences. Choose Instruction Pipelines first if you care about explaining CPU speed or understanding branch costs.

Think of CPU Caches as keeping your most-used books on your desk instead of in the library, and of Instruction Pipelines as an assembly line where several cars are in different stages at once. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With CPU Caches, avoid false sharing. With Instruction Pipelines, avoid unpredictable branches.

A good habit for CPU Caches is to access arrays in the order they are stored. A good habit for Instruction Pipelines is to avoid unpredictable branches in hot loops.
