Why LSM Trees Matters for Developers

Developers who understand LSM Trees make better decisions, because LSM Trees is storage structures that buffer writes in memory and merge them into sorted files on disk.

Three situations show why it matters. First, write-heavy databases. Second, time-ordered data. Third, key-value stores. In each case, a solid grasp of memtable and sorted string tables separates a quick fix from a lasting solution.

Ignoring it has a cost. Teams run into these problems: read amplification, compaction stalls, and poor bloom filter tuning.

You will not need to build everything yourself; RocksDB, LevelDB, and Apache Cassandra cover most needs.
