Is Augmented and Virtual Reality Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Augmented and Virtual Reality, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Augmented and Virtual Reality is technologies that overlay digital content on the real world or immerse users in a virtual one.

It is a good fit when your work involves training simulations or games, and when your team understands head tracking.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn rendering at high frame rates, or when you cannot manage these risks: motion sickness from low frame rates, heavy assets, and poor interface design in 3D.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether spatial mapping and input methods are part of the answer. If they are, Augmented and Virtual Reality is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with remote collaboration and keep frame rates high and test comfort with real users.
