B-Trees on Disk for Students: A Study Guide

This study guide prepares you for exams, labs and interviews that cover B-Trees on Disk.

Learning goals:
1. Explain B-Trees on Disk in your own words.
2. Describe pages, branching factor, node splits, and sorted keys.
3. Give examples from database indexes and file system directories.
4. Name three problems to watch for.

Start with the definition: B-Trees on Disk is balanced tree structures designed to read large blocks from disk efficiently. Then draw a filing cabinet where each drawer tells you which drawer to open next on paper and label the four terms from the goals above.

Self-test questions: Why is random writes on fragmented pages a problem? How does branching factor relate to node splits? When would you choose PostgreSQL or SQLite?

Study habit worth keeping: keep indexed keys short so more of them fit on a page.

When you feel ready, compare B-Trees on Disk with Time-Series Databases and write down three differences.
