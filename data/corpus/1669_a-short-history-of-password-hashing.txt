A Short History of Password Hashing

Ideas behind Password Hashing developed gradually as engineers ran into practical limits and shared what they learned. If you build software for long enough, you will run into Password Hashing. The term refers to storing passwords as one-way hashes so the originals are never kept.

Keeping dependencies updated removes a large share of known risks. Security is a habit applied throughout development, not a feature added at the end.

Never trust input that comes from outside your own code. Keeping dependencies updated removes a large share of known risks.

The vocabulary of Password Hashing is small. Learn salts, slow hash functions, work factors, and verification, and most documentation will start to make sense. Understanding slow hash functions first often makes work factors much easier to reason about.

Password Hashing and SQL Injection are closely related, so learning both gives a fuller picture of Security.

None of this needs special hardware or expensive software. A laptop, curiosity and a few small experiments are enough to get comfortable with Password Hashing.
