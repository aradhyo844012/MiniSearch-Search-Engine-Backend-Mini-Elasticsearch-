Teaching Random Forests to a Teammate

Explaining Random Forests to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: asking many different experts and taking the majority view. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Random Forests is ensembles of many decision trees whose predictions are combined. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving tabular predictions, naming bagging and feature randomness as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain voting in Random Forests back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, treating importance scores as causes, and suggest this habit: tune the number of trees only after the basics work.
