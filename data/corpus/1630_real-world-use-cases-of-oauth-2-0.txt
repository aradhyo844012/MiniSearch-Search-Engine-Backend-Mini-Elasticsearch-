Real-World Use Cases of OAuth 2.0

The best way to appreciate OAuth 2.0 is to see where it is actually used. When people talk about OAuth 2.0, they usually mean a framework that lets applications access resources on a user's behalf without sharing passwords. It appears in almost every serious software project, whether or not developers notice it.

People use OAuth 2.0 for sign in with another account, for API access for third-party apps, and for mobile logins.

Consider sign in with another account. In a case like this, OAuth 2.0 gives the work a clear structure, and understanding authorization server makes the design easier to explain. A different example is API access for third-party apps, where access tokens often decides how well the solution scales.

Attackers usually choose the easiest weakness, not the most interesting one. Never trust input that comes from outside your own code.

In practice you can start with Auth0, then explore Keycloak or authlib as your needs grow.

Keep experimenting, keep notes, and revisit OAuth 2.0 whenever a new project gives you a reason.
