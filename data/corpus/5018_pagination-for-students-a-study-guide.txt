Pagination for Students: A Study Guide

This study guide prepares you for exams, labs and interviews that cover Pagination.

Learning goals:
1. Explain Pagination in your own words.
2. Describe offset pagination, cursor pagination, page size, and stable ordering.
3. Give examples from search results and activity feeds.
4. Name three problems to watch for.

Start with the definition: Pagination is splitting large result sets into smaller pages that clients request one at a time. Then draw reading a long book one chapter at a time with a bookmark on paper and label the four terms from the goals above.

Self-test questions: Why is offsets getting slow on huge tables a problem? How does cursor pagination relate to page size? When would you choose SQL LIMIT and OFFSET or keyset queries?

Study habit: prefer cursors for large or fast-changing data.

When you feel ready, compare Pagination with Dependency Injection and write down three differences.
