CPU Caches vs GPUs and Parallelism: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study CPU Caches or GPUs and Parallelism first. Both sit in the area of Computer Systems, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

CPU Caches is small, fast memory close to the processor that stores recently used data, and it is closely tied to cache lines and hit and miss rates. GPUs and Parallelism, by contrast, is processors with thousands of small cores that handle many similar calculations at once, and it is closely tied to threads and blocks and kernels.

Choose CPU Caches first if you care about speeding up loops or explaining performance differences. Choose GPUs and Parallelism first if you care about training neural networks or rendering graphics.

Think of CPU Caches as keeping your most-used books on your desk instead of in the library, and of GPUs and Parallelism as a stadium of workers each doing one small identical task. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With CPU Caches, avoid false sharing. With GPUs and Parallelism, avoid slow data transfers.

A good habit for CPU Caches is to access arrays in the order they are stored. A good habit for GPUs and Parallelism is to minimize copies between CPU and GPU memory.
