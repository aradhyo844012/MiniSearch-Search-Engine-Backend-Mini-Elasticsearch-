Is K-Means Clustering Right for Your Project?

Before adopting K-Means Clustering, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that K-Means Clustering is an algorithm that groups data points into a chosen number of clusters around central points.

It is a good fit when your work involves customer segmentation or image compression, and when your team understands centroids.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn distance measures, or when you cannot manage these risks: choosing k blindly, unscaled features, and sensitivity to starting points.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether choosing k and iterations are part of the answer. If they are, K-Means Clustering is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with grouping documents and scale features before clustering.
