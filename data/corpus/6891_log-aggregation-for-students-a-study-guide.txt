Log Aggregation for Students: A Study Guide

This study guide prepares you for exams, labs and interviews that cover Log Aggregation.

Learning goals:
1. Explain Log Aggregation in your own words.
2. Describe shippers, indexing, retention, and search queries.
3. Give examples from troubleshooting across services and security investigations.
4. Name three problems to watch for.

Start with the definition: Log Aggregation is collecting logs from many machines into one searchable place. Then draw gathering notes from every desk into a single searchable binder on paper and label the four terms from the goals above.

Self-test questions: Why is unbounded storage growth a problem? How does indexing relate to retention? When would you choose Elasticsearch or Fluent Bit?

Study habit: use structured JSON logs so that fields are searchable.

When you feel ready, compare Log Aggregation with Distributed Tracing and write down three differences.
