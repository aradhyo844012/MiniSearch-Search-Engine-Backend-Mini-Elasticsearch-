Is High Availability Design Right for Your Project?

Before adopting High Availability Design, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that High Availability Design is designing systems so they keep working when parts fail.

It is a good fit when your work involves online stores or banking systems, and when your team understands redundancy.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn failover, or when you cannot manage these risks: hidden single points of failure, untested failover, and correlated failures.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether health checks and no single point of failure are part of the answer. If they are, High Availability Design is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with healthcare software and list every component and ask what happens if it dies.
