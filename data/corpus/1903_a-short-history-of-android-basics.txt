A Short History of Android Basics

Ideas behind Android Basics developed gradually as engineers ran into practical limits and shared what they learned. Many students first meet Android Basics in a classroom, but it matters just as much in production systems. The term refers to the core building blocks used to create apps for Android phones.

Network connections on phones are unreliable, so apps should handle offline states gracefully. Phones have limited memory, battery and screen space compared with desktops.

Four ideas sit at the heart of Android Basics: activities, layouts, intents, and lifecycle. Each one builds on the one before it. Understanding layouts first often makes intents much easier to reason about.

A solid grasp of Android Basics also makes Mobile App Performance easier to understand.

In short, careful study of Android Basics pays off. Spend time on activities and layouts, and the rest will follow.
