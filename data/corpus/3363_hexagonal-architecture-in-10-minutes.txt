Hexagonal Architecture in 10 Minutes

You have ten minutes, so here is the fast route through Hexagonal Architecture.

Minutes 1 and 2, the definition: Hexagonal Architecture is a design that keeps core logic separate from outside systems through ports and adapters.

Minutes 3 and 4, the vocabulary: learn ports, adapters, the core domain, and dependency direction.

Minutes 5 and 6, the picture: imagine a power strip that lets any plug connect to the same core. That is the intuition.

Minutes 7 and 8, the use cases: skim through testable business logic, swapping databases, and supporting several interfaces.

Minutes 9 and 10, the traps and the tools: watch for too many layers for small apps and leaking framework types into the core, then try Spring for a first experiment.

If you have a spare minute, remember to keep framework imports out of the core domain.
