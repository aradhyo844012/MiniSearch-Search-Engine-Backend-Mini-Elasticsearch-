Mistakes to Avoid with Statistical Significance

Everyone learns Statistical Significance partly through mistakes. Here are three worth avoiding.

Mistake one: mistaking significance for importance. It usually appears when someone skips p-values and jumps straight to implementation. The fix is to slow down and review the basics.

Mistake two: p-hacking. It tends to be noticed late, often during testing of judging experiment results. A short checklist during review catches it early.

Mistake three: ignoring sample size. It is easy to miss because everything seems to work at first. Keep an eye on effect size to spot it.

If you remember only one rule, make it this: always report the effect size alongside the p-value.
