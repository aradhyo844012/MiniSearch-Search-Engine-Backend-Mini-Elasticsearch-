How to Learn Password Hashing in 30 Days

You do not need months to become comfortable with Password Hashing. A focused plan of thirty days is enough to build a solid base. When people talk about Password Hashing, they usually mean storing passwords as one-way hashes so the originals are never kept. It appears in almost every serious software project, whether or not developers notice it.

Week 1: learn salts and slow hash functions, and write a one-page summary.
Week 2: practice with bcrypt on a small task such as user login systems.
Week 3: build a mini project around protecting data after a breach.
Week 4: review common mistakes such as using fast hashes like plain SHA-256, and explain Password Hashing to a friend.

In practice you can start with bcrypt, then explore Argon2 or scrypt as your needs grow.

With steady practice, Password Hashing will stop feeling abstract and become a tool you reach for naturally.
