Computability and the Halting Problem in 10 Minutes

You have ten minutes, so here is the fast route through Computability and the Halting Problem.

Minutes 1 and 2, the definition: Computability and the Halting Problem is the study of what problems computers can and cannot solve.

Minutes 3 and 4, the vocabulary: learn Turing machines, decidability, the halting problem, and reductions.

Minutes 5 and 6, the picture: imagine asking a machine to predict whether any program will ever finish. That is the intuition.

Minutes 7 and 8, the use cases: skim through understanding the limits of tools, proofs of impossibility, and theory courses.

Minutes 9 and 10, the traps and the tools: watch for assuming everything is solvable and confusing hard with undecidable, then try Turing machine simulators for a first experiment.

If you have a spare minute, remember to learn the diagonal argument step by step.
