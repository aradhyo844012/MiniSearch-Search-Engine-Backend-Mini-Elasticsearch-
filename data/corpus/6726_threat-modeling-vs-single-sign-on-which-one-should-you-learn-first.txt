Threat Modeling vs Single Sign-On: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Threat Modeling or Single Sign-On first. Both sit in the area of Application Security, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Threat Modeling is a structured way of identifying what could go wrong in a system and how to prevent it, and it is closely tied to assets and attackers. Single Sign-On, by contrast, is letting users log in once and access several applications, and it is closely tied to identity provider and SAML.

Choose Threat Modeling first if you care about designing new features or reviewing architecture. Choose Single Sign-On first if you care about company intranets or reducing password fatigue.

Think of Threat Modeling as walking around a house asking where a burglar might get in, and of Single Sign-On as a festival wristband that opens every gate. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Threat Modeling, avoid doing it once and never again. With Single Sign-On, avoid a single point of failure.

A good habit for Threat Modeling is to draw the data flow diagram first. A good habit for Single Sign-On is to plan for identity provider outages.
