Case Study: Applying Relational Databases in a Small Team

Imagine a small student team building a project that depends on Relational Databases. Put simply, Relational Databases covers systems that store data in tables with rows, columns and defined relationships. Learning it well pays off across many areas of computing.

The team first spent a week learning tables and rows and primary keys. Early on they hit a familiar problem: skipping constraints. After fixing it, they used PostgreSQL to check their work and adopted Relational Databases for storing orders and customers. Looking back, they said the biggest lesson was to keep the design simple.

Watch out for these problems: skipping constraints; storing lists in a single column; and missing indexes on filtered columns.

Data that must survive restarts and crashes belongs in a database rather than in memory. Good schema design is hard to change once real data and users depend on it.

Keep experimenting, keep notes, and revisit Relational Databases whenever a new project gives you a reason.
