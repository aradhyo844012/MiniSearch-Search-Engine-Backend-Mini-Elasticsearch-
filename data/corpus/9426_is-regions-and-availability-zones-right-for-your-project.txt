Is Regions and Availability Zones Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Regions and Availability Zones, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Regions and Availability Zones is the geographic locations and isolated data centers a cloud provider uses to host resources.

It is a good fit when your work involves placing services near users or surviving data center failures, and when your team understands regions.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn availability zones, or when you cannot manage these risks: putting everything in one zone, cross-region transfer fees, and forgetting data residency rules.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether latency and data residency are part of the answer. If they are, Regions and Availability Zones is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with meeting data laws and spread critical services across at least two zones.
