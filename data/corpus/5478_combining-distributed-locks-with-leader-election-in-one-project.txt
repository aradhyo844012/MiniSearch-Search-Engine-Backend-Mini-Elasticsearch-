Combining Distributed Locks with Leader Election in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Distributed Locks and Leader Election can work together in a single project.

Distributed Locks is mechanisms that ensure only one process at a time performs a critical action across machines. Leader Election is a process by which nodes in a cluster choose one node to coordinate the work.

Imagine a project that involves running scheduled jobs once and database primaries. Distributed Locks brings lease times, while Leader Election brings heartbeats.

A sensible plan is to start with Redis for the Distributed Locks part and ZooKeeper for the Leader Election part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: clock drift on one side, and flapping leaders on the other.

Picture the whole as a single key to a shared meeting room that expires automatically, working alongside a classroom picking a monitor when the teacher leaves.

Two habits help: first, always use a timeout together with a fencing token; second, tune heartbeat intervals against real network delays.
