Database Replication: Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What is Database Replication?
A: The term Database Replication refers to copying data from one database server to others to improve availability and read capacity.

Q: What mistakes are most common?
A: Watch for these: reading stale data from replicas; untested failover plans; and split-brain situations.

Q: How long does it take to learn?
A: Most people grasp the basics in a few weeks of steady practice, then deepen their understanding by building projects.

Q: Why do developers use Database Replication?
A: Common uses include surviving server failures, scaling read traffic, and running reports without slowing production.

Q: How does it relate to Redis Caching?
A: Database Replication and Redis Caching often appear in the same project, and both belong to the area of Databases.

Q: Which tools help?
A: Popular choices include PostgreSQL streaming replication, MySQL replication, and Patroni.

Q: Do I need special hardware to practice?
A: No. A normal laptop and a few small experiments are enough to get comfortable with Database Replication.
