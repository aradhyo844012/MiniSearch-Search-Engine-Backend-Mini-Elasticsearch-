Debugging Interrupts: A Field Guide

When something goes wrong around Interrupts, resist the urge to guess. Work through the problem in order.

Step 1: reproduce the problem with the smallest possible example.
Step 2: list the usual suspects: long handlers, race conditions with shared data, and lost interrupts.
Step 3: revisit interrupt handlers and interrupt vectors, since misunderstandings there cause many bugs.
Step 4: use Linux /proc/interrupts or similar tools to look inside.
Step 5: write a test that fails because of the bug and passes after the fix.

Many bugs involving Interrupts in work such as keyboard and mouse input come down to the first suspect on the list. Check the simple causes before suspecting anything exotic.

Finally, keep interrupt handlers short and defer the heavy work. It prevents a surprising number of repeat problems.
