Debugging CAP Theorem: A Field Guide

When something goes wrong around CAP Theorem, resist the urge to guess. Work through the problem in order.

Step 1: reproduce the problem with the smallest possible example.
Step 2: list the usual suspects: treating it as a three-way menu, ignoring latency trade-offs in normal operation, and assuming partitions never happen.
Step 3: revisit consistency and availability, since misunderstandings there cause many bugs.
Step 4: use Apache Cassandra or similar tools to look inside.
Step 5: write a test that fails because of the bug and passes after the fix.

Many bugs involving CAP Theorem in work such as choosing database types come down to the first suspect on the list. Check the simple causes before suspecting anything exotic.

Finally, ask what your users prefer during a failure: stale answers or errors. It prevents a surprising number of repeat problems.
