Is GPUs and Parallelism Right for Your Project?

Before adopting GPUs and Parallelism, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that GPUs and Parallelism is processors with thousands of small cores that handle many similar calculations at once.

It is a good fit when your work involves training neural networks or rendering graphics, and when your team understands threads and blocks.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn kernels, or when you cannot manage these risks: slow data transfers, branch-heavy code, and launching tiny workloads.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether memory transfers and SIMD are part of the answer. If they are, GPUs and Parallelism is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with scientific simulations and minimize copies between CPU and GPU memory.
