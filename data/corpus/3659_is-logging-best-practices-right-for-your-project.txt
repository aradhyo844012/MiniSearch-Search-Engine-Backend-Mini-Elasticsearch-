Is Logging Best Practices Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Logging Best Practices, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Logging Best Practices is recording useful, structured information about what an application is doing.

It is a good fit when your work involves debugging production issues or auditing actions, and when your team understands log levels.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn structured logs, or when you cannot manage these risks: logging sensitive data, too much noise, and missing context.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether correlation IDs and log rotation are part of the answer. If they are, Logging Best Practices is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with measuring usage and include a request ID in every log line.
