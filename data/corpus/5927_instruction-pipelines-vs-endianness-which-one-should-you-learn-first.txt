Instruction Pipelines vs Endianness: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Instruction Pipelines or Endianness first. Both sit in the area of Computer Systems, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Instruction Pipelines is a technique where a processor overlaps the stages of several instructions, and it is closely tied to fetch, decode and execute stages and hazards. Endianness, by contrast, is the order in which a computer stores the bytes of a multi-byte number, and it is closely tied to big-endian and little-endian.

Choose Instruction Pipelines first if you care about explaining CPU speed or understanding branch costs. Choose Endianness first if you care about network protocols or binary file formats.

Think of Instruction Pipelines as an assembly line where several cars are in different stages at once, and of Endianness as writing a date as day-month-year or as year-month-day. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Instruction Pipelines, avoid unpredictable branches. With Endianness, avoid mixing byte orders.

A good habit for Instruction Pipelines is to avoid unpredictable branches in hot loops. A good habit for Endianness is to always specify byte order in file and protocol formats.
