Combining K-Means Clustering with Retrieval-Augmented Generation in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how K-Means Clustering and Retrieval-Augmented Generation can work together in a single project.

K-Means Clustering is an algorithm that groups data points into a chosen number of clusters around central points. Retrieval-Augmented Generation is a technique that gives a language model relevant documents to read before it answers.

Imagine a project that involves customer segmentation and company knowledge assistants. K-Means Clustering brings centroids, while Retrieval-Augmented Generation brings retrieval.

A sensible plan is to start with scikit-learn for the K-Means Clustering part and LangChain for the Retrieval-Augmented Generation part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: unscaled features on one side, and irrelevant retrieved text on the other.

Picture the whole as dividing students into study groups around the most central member, working alongside an open-book exam where the student first looks up the right pages.

Two habits help: first, scale features before clustering; second, evaluate retrieval quality separately from answer quality.
