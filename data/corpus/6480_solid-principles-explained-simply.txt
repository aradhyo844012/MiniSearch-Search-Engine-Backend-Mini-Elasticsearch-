SOLID Principles Explained Simply

Here is SOLID Principles without the jargon: it is five guidelines for designing object-oriented code that is easy to change.

Think of SOLID Principles as house rules that keep a shared kitchen tidy. Keep that picture in mind as you read on.

The vocabulary has four main pieces: single responsibility, the open-closed principle, Liskov substitution, and dependency inversion. You do not need to master them all at once. Start with single responsibility, then add the open-closed principle.

People reach for SOLID Principles when they work on structuring classes, reviewing designs, and reducing coupling.

One practical tip is to apply them when a design starts to hurt, not before.

Once SOLID Principles feels comfortable, read about Event-Driven Architecture, which lives in the same neighborhood of ideas.
