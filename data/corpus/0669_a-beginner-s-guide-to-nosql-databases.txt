A Beginner's Guide to NoSQL Databases

This guide is written for readers who are new to NoSQL Databases and want a clear starting point. When people talk about NoSQL Databases, they usually mean a broad family of databases that do not use the traditional relational table model. It appears in almost every serious software project, whether or not developers notice it.

NoSQL Databases and Database Indexing are closely related, so learning both gives a fuller picture of Databases.

Good schema design is hard to change once real data and users depend on it. Data that must survive restarts and crashes belongs in a database rather than in memory.

The vocabulary of NoSQL Databases is small. Learn document stores, key-value stores, column families, and graph databases, and most documentation will start to make sense. A good way to remember document stores is to connect it with a concrete example from your own code.

You will see NoSQL Databases whenever people work on flexible schemas or high write throughput. It also helps with caching and sessions.

In practice you can start with MongoDB, then explore Cassandra or Redis as your needs grow.

Watch out for these problems: assuming no schema means no design; weak consistency surprises; and expensive cross-collection queries.

Measure before you optimize. Use MongoDB or a simple timer to see where time is actually spent. Practice document stores with small exercises until it feels natural. Automate repetitive steps so that results are easy to reproduce.

In short, careful study of NoSQL Databases pays off. Spend time on document stores and key-value stores, and the rest will follow.
