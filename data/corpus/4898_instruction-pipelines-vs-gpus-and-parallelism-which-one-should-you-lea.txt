Instruction Pipelines vs GPUs and Parallelism: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Instruction Pipelines or GPUs and Parallelism first. Both sit in the area of Computer Systems, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Instruction Pipelines is a technique where a processor overlaps the stages of several instructions, and it is closely tied to fetch, decode and execute stages and hazards. GPUs and Parallelism, by contrast, is processors with thousands of small cores that handle many similar calculations at once, and it is closely tied to threads and blocks and kernels.

Choose Instruction Pipelines first if you care about explaining CPU speed or understanding branch costs. Choose GPUs and Parallelism first if you care about training neural networks or rendering graphics.

Think of Instruction Pipelines as an assembly line where several cars are in different stages at once, and of GPUs and Parallelism as a stadium of workers each doing one small identical task. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Instruction Pipelines, avoid unpredictable branches. With GPUs and Parallelism, avoid slow data transfers.

A good habit for Instruction Pipelines is to avoid unpredictable branches in hot loops. A good habit for GPUs and Parallelism is to minimize copies between CPU and GPU memory.
