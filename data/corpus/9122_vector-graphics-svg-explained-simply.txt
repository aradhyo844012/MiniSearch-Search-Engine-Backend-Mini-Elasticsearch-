Vector Graphics (SVG) Explained Simply

If Vector Graphics (SVG) sounds intimidating, relax. It is images described by shapes and paths so they stay sharp at any size, and the idea is simpler than the name suggests.

Think of Vector Graphics (SVG) as a recipe for drawing a picture rather than a photo of it. Keep that picture in mind as you read on.

The vocabulary has four main pieces: paths, the viewBox, fill and stroke, and transforms. You do not need to master them all at once. Start with paths, then add the viewBox.

People reach for Vector Graphics (SVG) when they work on logos, icons, and charts.

One practical tip is to set a viewBox so the image scales cleanly.

Once Vector Graphics (SVG) feels comfortable, read about Computer Vision Basics, which lives in the same neighborhood of ideas.
