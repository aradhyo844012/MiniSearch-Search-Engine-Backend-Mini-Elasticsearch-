Why Connection Pooling Matters for Developers

Developers who understand Connection Pooling make better decisions, because Connection Pooling is reusing a limited set of open database connections instead of opening a new one for every request.

Three situations show why it matters. First, web backends. Second, serverless functions that call databases. Third, high-traffic APIs. In each case, a solid grasp of pool size and idle connections separates a quick fix from a lasting solution.

Ignoring it has a cost. Teams run into these problems: pools larger than the database allows, leaked connections, and long transactions that hold connections.

Good tools exist, including PgBouncer, HikariCP, and SQLAlchemy pools, so you rarely have to start from nothing.
