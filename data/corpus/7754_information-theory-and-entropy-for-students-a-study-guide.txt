Information Theory and Entropy for Students: A Study Guide

This study guide prepares you for exams, labs and interviews that cover Information Theory and Entropy.

Learning goals:
1. Explain Information Theory and Entropy in your own words.
2. Describe bits, entropy, compression limits, and noise.
3. Give examples from data compression and error-correcting codes.
4. Name three problems to watch for.

Start with the definition: Information Theory and Entropy is the mathematics of measuring and compressing information. Then draw measuring how surprised you are by each new message on paper and label the four terms from the goals above.

Self-test questions: Why is confusing information with meaning a problem? How does entropy relate to compression limits? When would you choose the Python math module or NumPy?

Study habit: compute the entropy of a short text by hand.

When you feel ready, compare Information Theory and Entropy with Computability and the Halting Problem and write down three differences.
