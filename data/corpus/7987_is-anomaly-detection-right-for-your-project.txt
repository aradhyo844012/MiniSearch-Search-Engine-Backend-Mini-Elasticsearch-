Is Anomaly Detection Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Anomaly Detection, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Anomaly Detection is finding data points or events that differ sharply from normal behavior.

It is a good fit when your work involves fraud detection or server monitoring, and when your team understands baselines.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn thresholds, or when you cannot manage these risks: too many false alarms, shifting baselines, and labeling normal data incorrectly.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether isolation methods and false positives are part of the answer. If they are, Anomaly Detection is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with manufacturing quality and tune alerts using real examples of past incidents.
