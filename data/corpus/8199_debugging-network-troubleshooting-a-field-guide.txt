Debugging Network Troubleshooting: A Field Guide

When something goes wrong around Network Troubleshooting, resist the urge to guess. Work through the problem in order.

Step 1: reproduce the problem with the smallest possible example.
Step 2: list the usual suspects: changing several things at once, blaming the application too early, and ignoring DNS.
Step 3: revisit protocol layers and ping and traceroute, since misunderstandings there cause many bugs.
Step 4: use ping or similar tools to look inside.
Step 5: write a test that fails because of the bug and passes after the fix.

Many bugs involving Network Troubleshooting in work such as fixing office connectivity come down to the first suspect on the list. Check the simple causes before suspecting anything exotic.

Finally, work from the bottom layer upward. It prevents a surprising number of repeat problems.
