CSRF vs Threat Modeling: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study CSRF or Threat Modeling first. Both sit in the area of Application Security, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

CSRF is an attack that tricks a logged-in user's browser into sending unwanted requests to a site, and it is closely tied to session cookies and anti-CSRF tokens. Threat Modeling, by contrast, is a structured way of identifying what could go wrong in a system and how to prevent it, and it is closely tied to assets and attackers.

Choose CSRF first if you care about protecting forms or securing account settings. Choose Threat Modeling first if you care about designing new features or reviewing architecture.

Think of CSRF as a forged letter that a bank clerk stamps because your ID is already on file, and of Threat Modeling as walking around a house asking where a burglar might get in. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With CSRF, avoid using GET requests for state changes. With Threat Modeling, avoid doing it once and never again.

A good habit for CSRF is to combine SameSite cookies with anti-CSRF tokens. A good habit for Threat Modeling is to draw the data flow diagram first.
