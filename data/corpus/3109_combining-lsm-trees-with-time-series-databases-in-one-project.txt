Combining LSM Trees with Time-Series Databases in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how LSM Trees and Time-Series Databases can work together in a single project.

LSM Trees is storage structures that buffer writes in memory and merge them into sorted files on disk. Time-Series Databases is databases optimized for storing and querying data points ordered by time.

Imagine a project that involves write-heavy databases and server metrics. LSM Trees brings memtable, while Time-Series Databases brings timestamps.

A sensible plan is to start with RocksDB for the LSM Trees part and InfluxDB for the Time-Series Databases part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: compaction stalls on one side, and keeping raw data forever on the other.

Picture the whole as a mail room that sorts incoming letters in batches rather than one by one, working alongside a long logbook where every line starts with the time.

Two habits help: first, watch compaction metrics before blaming the application; second, include a time range in every query.
