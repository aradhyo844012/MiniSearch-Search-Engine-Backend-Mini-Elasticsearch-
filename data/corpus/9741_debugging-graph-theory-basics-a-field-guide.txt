Debugging Graph Theory Basics: A Field Guide

When something goes wrong around Graph Theory Basics, resist the urge to guess. Work through the problem in order.

Step 1: reproduce the problem with the smallest possible example.
Step 2: list the usual suspects: mixing directed and undirected graphs, ignoring disconnected parts, and choosing the wrong representation for dense graphs.
Step 3: revisit vertices and edges and degree, since misunderstandings there cause many bugs.
Step 4: use NetworkX or similar tools to look inside.
Step 5: write a test that fails because of the bug and passes after the fix.

Many bugs involving Graph Theory Basics in work such as social networks come down to the first suspect on the list. Check the simple causes before suspecting anything exotic.

Finally, draw a small example before writing code. It prevents a surprising number of repeat problems.
