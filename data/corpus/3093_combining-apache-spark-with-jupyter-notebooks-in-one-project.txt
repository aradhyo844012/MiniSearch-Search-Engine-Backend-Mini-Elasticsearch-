Combining Apache Spark with Jupyter Notebooks in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Apache Spark and Jupyter Notebooks can work together in a single project.

Apache Spark is a distributed engine for processing large datasets across many machines. Jupyter Notebooks is interactive documents that combine code, results and notes.

Imagine a project that involves large ETL jobs and exploring data. Apache Spark brings DataFrames, while Jupyter Notebooks brings cells.

A sensible plan is to start with PySpark for the Apache Spark part and JupyterLab for the Jupyter Notebooks part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: too many small partitions on one side, and hidden state on the other.

Picture the whole as a team of workers each processing a share of a huge pile of papers, working alongside a lab notebook that can run its own experiments.

Two habits help: first, avoid collecting large results onto a single machine; second, restart the kernel and run all cells before sharing.
