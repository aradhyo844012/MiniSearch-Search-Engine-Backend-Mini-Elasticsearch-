Is Subqueries and CTEs Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Subqueries and CTEs, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Subqueries and CTEs is queries nested inside other queries or named as temporary result sets.

It is a good fit when your work involves breaking complex reports into steps or filtering with computed results, and when your team understands subqueries.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn WITH clauses, or when you cannot manage these risks: correlated subqueries that run slowly, deep nesting, and assuming CTEs are always materialized.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether correlated queries and readability are part of the answer. If they are, Subqueries and CTEs is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with walking recursive hierarchies and name each CTE after what it returns.
