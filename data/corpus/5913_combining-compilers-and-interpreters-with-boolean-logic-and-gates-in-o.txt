Combining Compilers and Interpreters with Boolean Logic and Gates in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Compilers and Interpreters and Boolean Logic and Gates can work together in a single project.

Compilers and Interpreters is programs that translate or run source code written in a high-level language. Boolean Logic and Gates is the basic true-or-false operations that digital circuits and program conditions are built from.

Imagine a project that involves running Python and JavaScript and writing conditions. Compilers and Interpreters brings lexing, while Boolean Logic and Gates brings AND, OR and NOT.

A sensible plan is to start with GCC for the Compilers and Interpreters part and Logisim for the Boolean Logic and Gates part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: ignoring compiler warnings on one side, and forgetting short-circuit behavior on the other.

Picture the whole as a translator who either translates a whole book first or interprets sentence by sentence, working alongside light switches wired in series or in parallel.

Two habits help: first, turn on compiler warnings and treat them seriously; second, write a truth table whenever a condition feels confusing.
