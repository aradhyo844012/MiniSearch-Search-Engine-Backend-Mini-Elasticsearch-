Database Constraints in Real Projects

Reading about Database Constraints is useful, but the ideas make more sense in context. These three scenarios show how it appears in real projects.

Scenario one: preventing bad data. Here, primary keys drives the main design choices, and the team needs to guard against skipping constraints for speed.

Scenario two: protecting relationships. This time foreign keys matters most, and tools such as PostgreSQL help keep things manageable.

Scenario three: documenting business rules. The key idea is unique constraints, and the biggest risk is missing indexes on foreign keys.

Across all three scenarios, Database Constraints stays the same idea: rules the database enforces to keep stored data valid. Only the details change.
