CSRF vs Hashing vs Encryption: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study CSRF or Hashing vs Encryption first. Both sit in the area of Application Security, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

CSRF is an attack that tricks a logged-in user's browser into sending unwanted requests to a site, and it is closely tied to session cookies and anti-CSRF tokens. Hashing vs Encryption, by contrast, is the difference between one-way digests and reversible protection of data, and it is closely tied to one-way functions and keys.

Choose CSRF first if you care about protecting forms or securing account settings. Choose Hashing vs Encryption first if you care about storing passwords or verifying file integrity.

Think of CSRF as a forged letter that a bank clerk stamps because your ID is already on file, and of Hashing vs Encryption as a paper shredder compared with a locked suitcase. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With CSRF, avoid using GET requests for state changes. With Hashing vs Encryption, avoid encrypting passwords instead of hashing them.

A good habit for CSRF is to combine SameSite cookies with anti-CSRF tokens. A good habit for Hashing vs Encryption is to ask whether you will ever need the original value back.
