The Trade-offs of Public Key Cryptography

Every design choice has a cost, and Public Key Cryptography is no exception. Put simply, Public Key Cryptography covers encryption that uses a pair of keys, one public and one private. Learning it well pays off across many areas of computing.

On the positive side, Public Key Cryptography is well suited to HTTPS and signing software. On the other hand, it is not free. Keep these risks in mind: losing private keys; trusting unverified keys; using keys that are too short. Whether the trade is worth it depends on how much key pairs and digital signatures matter to your project.

A solid grasp of Public Key Cryptography also makes Cross-Site Scripting easier to understand.

Keep experimenting, keep notes, and revisit Public Key Cryptography whenever a new project gives you a reason.
