How to Learn Graph Traversal in 30 Days

You do not need months to become comfortable with Graph Traversal. A focused plan of thirty days is enough to build a solid base. The term Graph Traversal refers to systematic ways of visiting every node in a graph, usually breadth-first or depth-first. This article explains the idea in plain language and points out where it fits in real projects.

If you are comfortable with Graph Traversal, explore Binary Search next. The two often appear in the same project.

Week 1: learn adjacency lists and breadth-first search, and write a one-page summary.
Week 2: practice with NetworkX on a small task such as finding shortest paths in unweighted graphs.
Week 3: build a mini project around detecting cycles.
Week 4: review common mistakes such as forgetting to mark nodes as visited, and explain Graph Traversal to a friend.

Popular tools and libraries include NetworkX, Python collections.deque, and Graphviz.

None of this needs special hardware or expensive software. A laptop, curiosity and a few small experiments are enough to get comfortable with Graph Traversal.
