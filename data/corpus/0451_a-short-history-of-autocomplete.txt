A Short History of Autocomplete

Ideas behind Autocomplete developed gradually as engineers ran into practical limits and shared what they learned. When people talk about Autocomplete, they usually mean suggesting likely completions while a person types a query. It appears in almost every serious software project, whether or not developers notice it.

Real query logs reveal what people actually look for, which is rarely what designers expect.

The vocabulary of Autocomplete is small. Learn prefixes, ranking suggestions, latency, and query logs, and most documentation will start to make sense. Sketching query logs on paper before coding helps many learners see how the pieces fit.

A solid grasp of Autocomplete also makes Inverted Index easier to understand.

None of this needs special hardware or expensive software. A laptop, curiosity and a few small experiments are enough to get comfortable with Autocomplete.
