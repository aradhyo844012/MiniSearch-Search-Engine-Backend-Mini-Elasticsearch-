Debugging Strategies for Students: A Study Guide

This study guide prepares you for exams, labs and interviews that cover Debugging Strategies.

Learning goals:
1. Explain Debugging Strategies in your own words.
2. Describe reproducing the bug, isolating the cause, forming hypotheses, and binary search through code.
3. Give examples from fixing production issues and learning unfamiliar code.
4. Name three problems to watch for.

Start with the definition: Debugging Strategies is systematic ways of finding and fixing the cause of unexpected program behavior. Then draw a detective narrowing down suspects on paper and label the four terms from the goals above.

Self-test questions: Why is changing many things at once a problem? How does isolating the cause relate to forming hypotheses? When would you choose debuggers or logging?

Study habit: change one thing at a time and record the result.

When you feel ready, compare Debugging Strategies with Code Editors and IDEs and write down three differences.
