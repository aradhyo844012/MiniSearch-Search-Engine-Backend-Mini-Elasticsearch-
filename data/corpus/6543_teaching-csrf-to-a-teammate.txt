Teaching CSRF to a Teammate

Explaining CSRF to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: a forged letter that a bank clerk stamps because your ID is already on file. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: CSRF is an attack that tricks a logged-in user's browser into sending unwanted requests to a site. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving protecting forms, naming session cookies and anti-CSRF tokens as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain SameSite cookies in CSRF back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, using GET requests for state changes, and suggest this habit: combine SameSite cookies with anti-CSRF tokens.
