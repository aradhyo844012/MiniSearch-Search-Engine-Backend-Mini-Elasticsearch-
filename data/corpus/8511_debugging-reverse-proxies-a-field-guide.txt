Debugging Reverse Proxies: A Field Guide

When something goes wrong around Reverse Proxies, resist the urge to guess. Work through the problem in order.

Step 1: reproduce the problem with the smallest possible example.
Step 2: list the usual suspects: losing the client IP address, wrong timeouts, and exposing internal paths.
Step 3: revisit upstream servers and proxy headers, since misunderstandings there cause many bugs.
Step 4: use NGINX or similar tools to look inside.
Step 5: write a test that fails because of the bug and passes after the fix.

Many bugs involving Reverse Proxies in work such as hiding backend servers come down to the first suspect on the list. Check the simple causes before suspecting anything exotic.

Finally, forward the real client IP in a trusted header. It prevents a surprising number of repeat problems.
