Relational Databases vs SQL Joins: Key Differences

Relational Databases and SQL Joins both belong to the area of Databases, but they solve different problems. This article compares them side by side so you can choose with confidence.

The term Relational Databases refers to systems that store data in tables with rows, columns and defined relationships. Its core ideas are tables and rows, primary keys, and foreign keys.

The term SQL Joins refers to operations that combine rows from two or more tables based on related columns. Its core ideas are inner join, left join, and join conditions.

The main difference is focus. Relational Databases puts the emphasis on tables and rows and primary keys, while SQL Joins puts the emphasis on inner join and left join.

Reach for Relational Databases when your task involves storing orders and customers or reporting and analytics. Reach for SQL Joins when your task involves linking customers to orders or building reports.

Both have risks. With Relational Databases, watch for this: skipping constraints. With SQL Joins, watch for this: forgetting the join condition.

In practice you do not always have to choose. Many projects use Relational Databases alongside SQL Joins, with tools such as PostgreSQL and PostgreSQL. Most slow pages in data-driven applications trace back to a handful of slow queries.
