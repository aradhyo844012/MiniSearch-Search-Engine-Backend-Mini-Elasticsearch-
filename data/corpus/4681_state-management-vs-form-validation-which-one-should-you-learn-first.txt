State Management vs Form Validation: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study State Management or Form Validation first. Both sit in the area of Frontend Engineering, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

State Management is the practice of organizing where an application keeps and updates its data, and it is closely tied to single source of truth and actions. Form Validation, by contrast, is checking user input for correctness before it is accepted, and it is closely tied to client-side checks and server-side checks.

Choose State Management first if you care about large single-page apps or shared shopping carts. Choose Form Validation first if you care about sign-up forms or payment pages.

Think of State Management as a shared whiteboard that everyone updates through the same rules, and of Form Validation as a proofreader checking a form before it is submitted. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With State Management, avoid putting everything in global state. With Form Validation, avoid validating only on the client.

A good habit for State Management is to keep state as close as possible to where it is used. A good habit for Form Validation is to validate on the server even when the browser checks too.
