The Trade-offs of Database Indexing

Every design choice has a cost, and Database Indexing is no exception. Many students first meet Database Indexing in a classroom, but it matters just as much in production systems. The term refers to auxiliary structures that let a database find rows without scanning a whole table.

On the positive side, Database Indexing is well suited to speeding up lookups and supporting sorted queries. On the other hand, it is not free. Keep these risks in mind: indexing every column; ignoring write overhead; wrong column order in composite indexes. Whether the trade is worth it depends on how much B-tree indexes and selectivity matter to your project.

Three issues come up again and again: indexing every column; ignoring write overhead; and wrong column order in composite indexes. Knowing them in advance saves hours of debugging.
