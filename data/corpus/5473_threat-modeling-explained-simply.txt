Threat Modeling Explained Simply

If Threat Modeling sounds intimidating, relax. It is a structured way of identifying what could go wrong in a system and how to prevent it, and the idea is simpler than the name suggests.

Think of Threat Modeling as walking around a house asking where a burglar might get in. Keep that picture in mind as you read on.

The vocabulary has four main pieces: assets, attackers, trust boundaries, and mitigations. You do not need to master them all at once. Start with assets, then add attackers.

People reach for Threat Modeling when they work on designing new features, reviewing architecture, and prioritizing security work.

One practical tip is to draw the data flow diagram first.

After Threat Modeling, a natural next topic is Dependency Vulnerabilities.
