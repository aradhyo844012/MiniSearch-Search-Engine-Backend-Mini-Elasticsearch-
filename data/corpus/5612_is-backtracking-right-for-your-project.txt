Is Backtracking Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Backtracking, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Backtracking is a search technique that builds solutions step by step and undoes steps that lead to dead ends.

It is a good fit when your work involves solving Sudoku or generating permutations, and when your team understands decision tree.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn pruning, or when you cannot manage these risks: forgetting to undo changes, missing pruning rules, and copying large state at every step.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether state restoration and constraint checking are part of the answer. If they are, Backtracking is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with N-Queens puzzles and prune as early as possible because the savings multiply.
