Combining Debugging Strategies with System Design Interviews in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Debugging Strategies and System Design Interviews can work together in a single project.

Debugging Strategies is systematic ways of finding and fixing the cause of unexpected program behavior. System Design Interviews is discussions in which candidates design a large software system and explain their choices.

Imagine a project that involves fixing production issues and preparing for senior roles. Debugging Strategies brings reproducing the bug, while System Design Interviews brings requirements.

A sensible plan is to start with debuggers for the Debugging Strategies part and whiteboard sketches for the System Design Interviews part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: guessing without evidence on one side, and ignoring trade-offs on the other.

Picture the whole as a detective narrowing down suspects, working alongside sketching the blueprint of a city while explaining why each road exists.

Two habits help: first, change one thing at a time and record the result; second, clarify requirements and scale before drawing boxes.
