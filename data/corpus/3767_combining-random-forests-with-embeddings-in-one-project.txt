Combining Random Forests with Embeddings in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Random Forests and Embeddings can work together in a single project.

Random Forests is ensembles of many decision trees whose predictions are combined. Embeddings is lists of numbers that represent words, images or items so that similar things sit close together.

Imagine a project that involves tabular predictions and semantic search. Random Forests brings bagging, while Embeddings brings vectors.

A sensible plan is to start with scikit-learn for the Random Forests part and sentence-transformers for the Embeddings part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: very large forests being slow on one side, and ignoring normalization on the other.

Picture the whole as asking many different experts and taking the majority view, working alongside coordinates on a map where similar places are close together.

Two habits help: first, tune the number of trees only after the basics work; second, use the same embedding model for indexing and querying.
