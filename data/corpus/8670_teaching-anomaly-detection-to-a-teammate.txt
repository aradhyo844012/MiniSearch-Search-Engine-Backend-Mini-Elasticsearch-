Teaching Anomaly Detection to a Teammate

Explaining Anomaly Detection to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: a security guard who notices the one person acting differently. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Anomaly Detection is finding data points or events that differ sharply from normal behavior. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving fraud detection, naming baselines and thresholds as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain isolation methods in Anomaly Detection back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, too many false alarms, and suggest this habit: tune alerts using real examples of past incidents.
