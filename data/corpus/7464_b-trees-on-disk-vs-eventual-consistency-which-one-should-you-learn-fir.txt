B-Trees on Disk vs Eventual Consistency: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study B-Trees on Disk or Eventual Consistency first. Both sit in the area of Storage Engines and Databases, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

B-Trees on Disk is balanced tree structures designed to read large blocks from disk efficiently, and it is closely tied to pages and branching factor. Eventual Consistency, by contrast, is a guarantee that replicas will agree on the same value if updates stop for long enough, and it is closely tied to replication delay and conflict resolution.

Choose B-Trees on Disk first if you care about database indexes or file system directories. Choose Eventual Consistency first if you care about global social feeds or shopping cart services.

Think of B-Trees on Disk as a filing cabinet where each drawer tells you which drawer to open next, and of Eventual Consistency as news spreading through a village until everyone has heard it. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With B-Trees on Disk, avoid random writes on fragmented pages. With Eventual Consistency, avoid assuming reads are always fresh.

A good habit for B-Trees on Disk is to keep indexed keys short so more of them fit on a page. A good habit for Eventual Consistency is to decide up front how your app handles stale reads.
