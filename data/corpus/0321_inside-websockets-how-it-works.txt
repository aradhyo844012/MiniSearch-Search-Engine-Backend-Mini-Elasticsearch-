Inside WebSockets: How It Works

This article walks through what happens inside WebSockets, step by step. Many students first meet WebSockets in a classroom, but it matters just as much in production systems. The term refers to a protocol that keeps a two-way connection open between a browser and a server.

To understand WebSockets, start with persistent connections. Then move on to messages, and finally look at heartbeats and reconnection. Sketching reconnection on paper before coding helps many learners see how the pieces fit.

First, persistent connections sets the stage for everything else. Next, messages builds on it. After that, heartbeats comes into play, and finally reconnection ties the pieces together. Seeing the sequence once, with a tiny example, makes the whole topic easier to remember.

Latency is limited by distance and by the number of round trips a request needs.

WebSockets and TCP and UDP are closely related, so learning both gives a fuller picture of Networking.

Keep experimenting, keep notes, and revisit WebSockets whenever a new project gives you a reason.
