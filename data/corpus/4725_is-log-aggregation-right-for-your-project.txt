Is Log Aggregation Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Log Aggregation, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Log Aggregation is collecting logs from many machines into one searchable place.

It is a good fit when your work involves troubleshooting across services or security investigations, and when your team understands shippers.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn indexing, or when you cannot manage these risks: unbounded storage growth, unstructured logs, and missing timestamps.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether retention and search queries are part of the answer. If they are, Log Aggregation is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with usage analysis and use structured JSON logs so that fields are searchable.
