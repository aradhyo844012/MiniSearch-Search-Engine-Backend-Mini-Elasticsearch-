Why Environment Variables Matters for Developers

Developers who understand Environment Variables make better decisions, because Environment Variables is named values the operating system gives to programs to configure their behavior.

Three situations show why it matters. First, storing configuration. Second, selecting program paths. Third, passing settings to containers. In each case, a solid grasp of variable names and scope separates a quick fix from a lasting solution.

Ignoring it has a cost. Teams run into these problems: committing secrets in dotfiles, variables not visible to child processes, and typos in names.

Good tools exist, including env, printenv, and dotenv files, so you rarely have to start from nothing.
