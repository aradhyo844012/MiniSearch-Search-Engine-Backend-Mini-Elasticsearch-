Combining CAP Theorem with Saga Pattern in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how CAP Theorem and Saga Pattern can work together in a single project.

CAP Theorem is a result stating that a distributed system cannot guarantee consistency, availability and partition tolerance all at once. Saga Pattern is a way of managing long business operations across services using a sequence of local steps and compensating actions.

Imagine a project that involves choosing database types and order processing. CAP Theorem brings consistency, while Saga Pattern brings local transactions.

A sensible plan is to start with Apache Cassandra for the CAP Theorem part and Temporal for the Saga Pattern part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: ignoring latency trade-offs in normal operation on one side, and steps that are not idempotent on the other.

Picture the whole as a phone tree that must choose between giving old information or staying silent when a line is cut, working alongside booking a trip with separate hotel, flight and car reservations that can each be cancelled.

Two habits help: first, ask what your users prefer during a failure: stale answers or errors; second, make every step and its compensation idempotent.
