Combining Instruction Pipelines with Assembly Language Basics in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Instruction Pipelines and Assembly Language Basics can work together in a single project.

Instruction Pipelines is a technique where a processor overlaps the stages of several instructions. Assembly Language Basics is low-level instructions that map closely to what a processor actually executes.

Imagine a project that involves explaining CPU speed and understanding compiled code. Instruction Pipelines brings fetch, decode and execute stages, while Assembly Language Basics brings registers.

A sensible plan is to start with perf for the Instruction Pipelines part and GDB for the Assembly Language Basics part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: dependency chains on one side, and stack corruption on the other.

Picture the whole as an assembly line where several cars are in different stages at once, working alongside the individual muscle movements behind a simple action.

Two habits help: first, avoid unpredictable branches in hot loops; second, read the assembly of a tiny C function to see what the compiler does.
