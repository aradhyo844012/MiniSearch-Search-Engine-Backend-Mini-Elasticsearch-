Combining Greedy Algorithms with Backtracking in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Greedy Algorithms and Backtracking can work together in a single project.

Greedy Algorithms is algorithms that build a solution by always taking the locally best choice. Backtracking is a search technique that builds solutions step by step and undoes steps that lead to dead ends.

Imagine a project that involves interval scheduling and solving Sudoku. Greedy Algorithms brings greedy choice property, while Backtracking brings decision tree.

A sensible plan is to start with pen and paper proofs for the Greedy Algorithms part and Python recursion for the Backtracking part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: missing counterexamples on one side, and missing pruning rules on the other.

Picture the whole as picking the closest open parking spot at every junction and hoping it works out, working alongside walking a maze and returning to the last fork whenever you hit a wall.

Two habits help: first, try to break your greedy idea with a small counterexample before trusting it; second, prune as early as possible because the savings multiply.
