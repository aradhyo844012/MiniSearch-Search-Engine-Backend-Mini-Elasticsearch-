Feature Flags vs Autoscaling: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Feature Flags or Autoscaling first. Both sit in the area of DevOps and Cloud, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Feature Flags is switches in configuration that turn features on or off without redeploying code, and it is closely tied to flag evaluation and targeting rules. Autoscaling, by contrast, is automatically adding or removing computing resources as demand changes, and it is closely tied to scaling metrics and cooldown periods.

Choose Feature Flags first if you care about dark launches or A/B tests. Choose Autoscaling first if you care about handling traffic spikes or saving money overnight.

Think of Feature Flags as light switches wired to every room of a house, and of Autoscaling as a restaurant opening extra tables when a crowd arrives. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Feature Flags, avoid never removing old flags. With Autoscaling, avoid scaling too slowly.

A good habit for Feature Flags is to give every flag an owner and a removal date. A good habit for Autoscaling is to load test the system to find realistic thresholds.
