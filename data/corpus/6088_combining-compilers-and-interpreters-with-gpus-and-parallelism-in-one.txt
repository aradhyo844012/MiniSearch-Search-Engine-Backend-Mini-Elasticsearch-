Combining Compilers and Interpreters with GPUs and Parallelism in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Compilers and Interpreters and GPUs and Parallelism can work together in a single project.

Compilers and Interpreters is programs that translate or run source code written in a high-level language. GPUs and Parallelism is processors with thousands of small cores that handle many similar calculations at once.

Imagine a project that involves running Python and JavaScript and training neural networks. Compilers and Interpreters brings lexing, while GPUs and Parallelism brings threads and blocks.

A sensible plan is to start with GCC for the Compilers and Interpreters part and CUDA for the GPUs and Parallelism part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: ignoring compiler warnings on one side, and branch-heavy code on the other.

Picture the whole as a translator who either translates a whole book first or interprets sentence by sentence, working alongside a stadium of workers each doing one small identical task.

Two habits help: first, turn on compiler warnings and treat them seriously; second, minimize copies between CPU and GPU memory.
