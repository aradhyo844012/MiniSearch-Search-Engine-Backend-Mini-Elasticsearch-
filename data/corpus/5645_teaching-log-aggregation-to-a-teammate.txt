Teaching Log Aggregation to a Teammate

Explaining Log Aggregation to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: gathering notes from every desk into a single searchable binder. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Log Aggregation is collecting logs from many machines into one searchable place. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving troubleshooting across services, naming shippers and indexing as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain retention in Log Aggregation back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, unbounded storage growth, and suggest this habit: use structured JSON logs so that fields are searchable.
