Combining Saga Pattern with Vector Clocks in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Saga Pattern and Vector Clocks can work together in a single project.

Saga Pattern is a way of managing long business operations across services using a sequence of local steps and compensating actions. Vector Clocks is counters that let distributed processes track the order of events without a shared clock.

Imagine a project that involves order processing and detecting concurrent edits. Saga Pattern brings local transactions, while Vector Clocks brings logical time.

A sensible plan is to start with Temporal for the Saga Pattern part and Riak for the Vector Clocks part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: steps that are not idempotent on one side, and confusing them with wall-clock time on the other.

Picture the whole as booking a trip with separate hotel, flight and car reservations that can each be cancelled, working alongside each person in a group chat keeping a tally of messages seen from everyone.

Two habits help: first, make every step and its compensation idempotent; second, compare clocks entry by entry to detect true concurrency.
