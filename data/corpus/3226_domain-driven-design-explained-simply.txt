Domain-Driven Design Explained Simply

If Domain-Driven Design sounds intimidating, relax. It is an approach to software that models the code around the real business domain, and the idea is simpler than the name suggests.

Think of Domain-Driven Design as writing software in the same vocabulary the business speaks. Keep that picture in mind as you read on.

The vocabulary has four main pieces: ubiquitous language, bounded contexts, aggregates, and entities and value objects. You do not need to master them all at once. Start with ubiquitous language, then add bounded contexts.

People reach for Domain-Driven Design when they work on complex business systems, aligning developers with domain experts, and splitting large systems.

One practical tip is to agree on shared terms with domain experts before drawing classes.

Once Domain-Driven Design feels comfortable, read about Pair Programming, which lives in the same neighborhood of ideas.
