Combining Debugging Strategies with Command Line Productivity in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Debugging Strategies and Command Line Productivity can work together in a single project.

Debugging Strategies is systematic ways of finding and fixing the cause of unexpected program behavior. Command Line Productivity is using shell tools and shortcuts to complete tasks faster.

Imagine a project that involves fixing production issues and automating repetitive tasks. Debugging Strategies brings reproducing the bug, while Command Line Productivity brings aliases.

A sensible plan is to start with debuggers for the Debugging Strategies part and bash for the Command Line Productivity part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: guessing without evidence on one side, and dangerous wildcard use on the other.

Picture the whole as a detective narrowing down suspects, working alongside learning the shortcuts on a well-used road.

Two habits help: first, change one thing at a time and record the result; second, turn commands you repeat into small scripts.
