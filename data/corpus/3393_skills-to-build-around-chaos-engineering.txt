Skills to Build Around Chaos Engineering

Knowing the definition of Chaos Engineering is only the start. These skills turn that knowledge into something useful.

Foundations: be comfortable with steady state and hypotheses, and able to explain them without notes.

Hands-on practice: spend real time with Chaos Monkey on a Chaos Engineering task, and try at least one alternative such as Gremlin.

Judgment: learn to decide when Chaos Engineering fits a problem such as testing resilience, and when something simpler is better.

Communication: practice describing it with an everyday picture, for example a fire drill where you secretly block one exit.

Habits: start with small experiments in a test environment, and write down what you learn after each project.
