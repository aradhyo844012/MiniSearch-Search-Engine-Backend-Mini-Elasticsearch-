The Trade-offs of HTTP and HTTPS

Every design choice has a cost, and HTTP and HTTPS is no exception. The term HTTP and HTTPS refers to the request and response protocol of the web, with HTTPS adding encryption. This article explains the idea in plain language and points out where it fits in real projects.

On the positive side, HTTP and HTTPS is well suited to loading web pages and calling APIs. On the other hand, it is not free. Keep these risks in mind: mixing up status codes; sending secrets in URLs; ignoring caching headers. Whether the trade is worth it depends on how much methods and status codes matter to your project.

Beginners often stumble on three things. First: mixing up status codes. Second: sending secrets in URLs. Third: ignoring caching headers.
