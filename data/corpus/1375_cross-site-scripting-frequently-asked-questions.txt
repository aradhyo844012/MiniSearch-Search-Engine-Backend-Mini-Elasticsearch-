Cross-Site Scripting: Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What is Cross-Site Scripting?
A: The term Cross-Site Scripting refers to a vulnerability where attackers get a site to run their scripts in other users' browsers.

Q: Why do developers use Cross-Site Scripting?
A: Common uses include reviewing web forms, securing comment sections, and hardening dashboards.

Q: What mistakes are most common?
A: Watch for these: inserting user text as raw HTML; trusting client-side filtering; and missing content security policy.

Q: Which tools help?
A: Popular choices include OWASP guides, CSP evaluators, and browser developer tools.

Q: What should a beginner learn first?
A: Start with reflected XSS and stored XSS, then move on to output encoding and content security policy.
