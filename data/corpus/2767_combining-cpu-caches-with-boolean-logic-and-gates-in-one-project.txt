Combining CPU Caches with Boolean Logic and Gates in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how CPU Caches and Boolean Logic and Gates can work together in a single project.

CPU Caches is small, fast memory close to the processor that stores recently used data. Boolean Logic and Gates is the basic true-or-false operations that digital circuits and program conditions are built from.

Imagine a project that involves speeding up loops and writing conditions. CPU Caches brings cache lines, while Boolean Logic and Gates brings AND, OR and NOT.

A sensible plan is to start with perf for the CPU Caches part and Logisim for the Boolean Logic and Gates part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: poor access patterns on one side, and forgetting short-circuit behavior on the other.

Picture the whole as keeping your most-used books on your desk instead of in the library, working alongside light switches wired in series or in parallel.

Two habits help: first, access arrays in the order they are stored; second, write a truth table whenever a condition feels confusing.
