Ray Tracing in Real Projects

Reading about Ray Tracing is useful, but the ideas make more sense in context. These three scenarios show how it appears in real projects.

Scenario one: movie visuals. Here, rays drives the main design choices, and the team needs to guard against slow render times.

Scenario two: architectural visualization. This time intersections matters most, and tools such as Blender Cycles help keep things manageable.

Scenario three: realistic game lighting. The key idea is reflection and refraction, and the biggest risk is noise from too few samples.

Across all three scenarios, Ray Tracing stays the same idea: a rendering technique that follows rays of light to produce realistic images. Only the details change.
