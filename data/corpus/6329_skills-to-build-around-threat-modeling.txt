Skills to Build Around Threat Modeling

Knowing the definition of Threat Modeling is only the start. These skills turn that knowledge into something useful.

Foundations: be comfortable with assets and attackers, and able to explain them without notes.

Hands-on practice: spend real time with STRIDE on a Threat Modeling task, and try at least one alternative such as OWASP Threat Dragon.

Judgment: learn to decide when Threat Modeling fits a problem such as designing new features, and when something simpler is better.

Communication: practice describing it with an everyday picture, for example walking around a house asking where a burglar might get in.

Habits: draw the data flow diagram first, and write down what you learn after each project.
