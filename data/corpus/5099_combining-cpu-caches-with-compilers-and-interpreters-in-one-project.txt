Combining CPU Caches with Compilers and Interpreters in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how CPU Caches and Compilers and Interpreters can work together in a single project.

CPU Caches is small, fast memory close to the processor that stores recently used data. Compilers and Interpreters is programs that translate or run source code written in a high-level language.

Imagine a project that involves speeding up loops and running Python and JavaScript. CPU Caches brings cache lines, while Compilers and Interpreters brings lexing.

A sensible plan is to start with perf for the CPU Caches part and GCC for the Compilers and Interpreters part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: poor access patterns on one side, and ignoring compiler warnings on the other.

Picture the whole as keeping your most-used books on your desk instead of in the library, working alongside a translator who either translates a whole book first or interprets sentence by sentence.

Two habits help: first, access arrays in the order they are stored; second, turn on compiler warnings and treat them seriously.
