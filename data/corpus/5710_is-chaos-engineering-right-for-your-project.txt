Is Chaos Engineering Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Chaos Engineering, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Chaos Engineering is deliberately causing failures in a system to learn how it responds.

It is a good fit when your work involves testing resilience or validating failover, and when your team understands steady state.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn hypotheses, or when you cannot manage these risks: experimenting without safeguards, having no steady-state metrics, and running first in production.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether blast radius and fault injection are part of the answer. If they are, Chaos Engineering is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with preparing teams for incidents and start with small experiments in a test environment.
