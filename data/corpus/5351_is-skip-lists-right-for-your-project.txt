Is Skip Lists Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Skip Lists, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Skip Lists is layered linked lists that allow fast searching by skipping over many elements.

It is a good fit when your work involves ordered in-memory indexes or sorted sets in caches, and when your team understands multiple levels.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn random level assignment, or when you cannot manage these risks: poor random number choices, memory overhead, and harder debugging than arrays.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether forward pointers and expected logarithmic time are part of the answer. If they are, Skip Lists is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with concurrent ordered maps and draw several levels by hand to see how a search moves down.
