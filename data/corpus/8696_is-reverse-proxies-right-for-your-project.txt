Is Reverse Proxies Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Reverse Proxies, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Reverse Proxies is servers that sit in front of applications and forward client requests to them.

It is a good fit when your work involves hiding backend servers or terminating HTTPS, and when your team understands upstream servers.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn proxy headers, or when you cannot manage these risks: losing the client IP address, wrong timeouts, and exposing internal paths.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether TLS termination and caching are part of the answer. If they are, Reverse Proxies is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with routing requests to several apps and forward the real client IP in a trusted header.
