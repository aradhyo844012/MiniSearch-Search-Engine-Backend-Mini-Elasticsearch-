Fuzz Testing for Students: A Study Guide

This study guide prepares you for exams, labs and interviews that cover Fuzz Testing.

Learning goals:
1. Explain Fuzz Testing in your own words.
2. Describe input generation, coverage guidance, crash triage, and corpora.
3. Give examples from testing parsers and finding memory bugs.
4. Name three problems to watch for.

Start with the definition: Fuzz Testing is feeding programs random or malformed input to uncover crashes and security bugs. Then draw shaking a machine hard to see what rattles loose on paper and label the four terms from the goals above.

Self-test questions: Why is running without sanitizers a problem? How does coverage guidance relate to crash triage? When would you choose AFL++ or libFuzzer?

Study habit worth keeping: keep every crashing input as a regression test.

When you feel ready, compare Fuzz Testing with Static Analysis and write down three differences.
