Write-Ahead Logging vs Eventual Consistency: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Write-Ahead Logging or Eventual Consistency first. Both sit in the area of Storage Engines and Databases, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Write-Ahead Logging is a technique that records changes in a log before they are applied to the main data, and it is closely tied to log records and checkpoints. Eventual Consistency, by contrast, is a guarantee that replicas will agree on the same value if updates stop for long enough, and it is closely tied to replication delay and conflict resolution.

Choose Write-Ahead Logging first if you care about surviving crashes or feeding replication streams. Choose Eventual Consistency first if you care about global social feeds or shopping cart services.

Think of Write-Ahead Logging as writing a plan in a notebook before changing the real blueprint, and of Eventual Consistency as news spreading through a village until everyone has heard it. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Write-Ahead Logging, avoid disabling fsync for speed. With Eventual Consistency, avoid assuming reads are always fresh.

A good habit for Write-Ahead Logging is to test crash recovery by killing the process on purpose. A good habit for Eventual Consistency is to decide up front how your app handles stale reads.
