Distributed Locks for Students: A Study Guide

This study guide prepares you for exams, labs and interviews that cover Distributed Locks.

Learning goals:
1. Explain Distributed Locks in your own words.
2. Describe lease times, fencing tokens, lock owners, and timeouts.
3. Give examples from running scheduled jobs once and protecting shared resources.
4. Name three problems to watch for.

Start with the definition: Distributed Locks is mechanisms that ensure only one process at a time performs a critical action across machines. Then draw a single key to a shared meeting room that expires automatically on paper and label the four terms from the goals above.

Self-test questions: Why is locks that never expire a problem? How does fencing tokens relate to lock owners? When would you choose Redis or ZooKeeper?

Study habit worth keeping: always use a timeout together with a fencing token.

When you feel ready, compare Distributed Locks with Raft Consensus and write down three differences.
