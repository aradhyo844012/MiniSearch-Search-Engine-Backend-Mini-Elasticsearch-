Process Signals and Job Control Explained Simply

If Process Signals and Job Control sounds intimidating, relax. It is messages and shell features used to stop, pause and resume running programs, and the idea is simpler than the name suggests.

Think of Process Signals and Job Control as a traffic officer's hand signals for running programs. Keep that picture in mind as you read on.

The vocabulary has four main pieces: SIGTERM and SIGKILL, foreground and background jobs, kill and trap, and process groups. You do not need to master them all at once. Start with SIGTERM and SIGKILL, then add foreground and background jobs.

People reach for Process Signals and Job Control when they work on stopping runaway programs, shutting down gracefully, and running long tasks in the background.

One practical tip is to try SIGTERM before SIGKILL so programs can clean up.

Once Process Signals and Job Control feels comfortable, read about systemd Services, which lives in the same neighborhood of ideas.
