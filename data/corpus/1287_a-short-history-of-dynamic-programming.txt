A Short History of Dynamic Programming

Ideas behind Dynamic Programming developed gradually as engineers ran into practical limits and shared what they learned. The term Dynamic Programming refers to a technique for solving problems by storing the answers to overlapping subproblems. This article explains the idea in plain language and points out where it fits in real projects.

Choosing the right data structure often matters more than clever tuning later. Interviewers and engineers alike value clear reasoning about time and space costs.

To understand Dynamic Programming, start with overlapping subproblems. Then move on to optimal substructure, and finally look at memoization and tabulation. A good way to remember overlapping subproblems is to connect it with a concrete example from your own code.

If you are comfortable with Dynamic Programming, explore Sorting Algorithms next. The two often appear in the same project.

With steady practice, Dynamic Programming will stop feeling abstract and become a tool you reach for naturally.
