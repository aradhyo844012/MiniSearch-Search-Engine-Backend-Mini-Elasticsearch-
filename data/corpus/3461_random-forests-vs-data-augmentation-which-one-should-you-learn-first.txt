Random Forests vs Data Augmentation: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Random Forests or Data Augmentation first. Both sit in the area of AI and Machine Learning, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Random Forests is ensembles of many decision trees whose predictions are combined, and it is closely tied to bagging and feature randomness. Data Augmentation, by contrast, is creating modified copies of training data to help models generalize, and it is closely tied to transformations and label preservation.

Choose Random Forests first if you care about tabular predictions or feature importance ranking. Choose Data Augmentation first if you care about image models or text classification.

Think of Random Forests as asking many different experts and taking the majority view, and of Data Augmentation as practicing a sport in different weather and on different fields. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Random Forests, avoid treating importance scores as causes. With Data Augmentation, avoid changes that alter the label.

A good habit for Random Forests is to tune the number of trees only after the basics work. A good habit for Data Augmentation is to augment only the training data.
