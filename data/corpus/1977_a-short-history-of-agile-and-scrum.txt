A Short History of Agile and Scrum

Ideas behind Agile and Scrum developed gradually as engineers ran into practical limits and shared what they learned. Put simply, Agile and Scrum covers iterative ways of planning and delivering software in short cycles. Learning it well pays off across many areas of computing.

Software spends most of its life being changed, not written for the first time. Readable code saves more time than clever code over the long run.

Four ideas sit at the heart of Agile and Scrum: sprints, backlogs, stand-ups, and retrospectives. Each one builds on the one before it. A good way to remember sprints is to connect it with a concrete example from your own code.

Start small by applying Agile and Scrum to one case, such as student team projects, before rolling it out more widely. Measure before you optimize. Use Jira or a simple timer to see where time is actually spent.

Agile and Scrum and Code Review are closely related, so learning both gives a fuller picture of Software Engineering.

None of this needs special hardware or expensive software. A laptop, curiosity and a few small experiments are enough to get comfortable with Agile and Scrum.
