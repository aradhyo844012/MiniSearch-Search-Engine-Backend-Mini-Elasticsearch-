Teaching Environment Variables to a Teammate

Explaining Environment Variables to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: sticky notes attached to a program before it starts. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Environment Variables is named values the operating system gives to programs to configure their behavior. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving storing configuration, naming variable names and scope as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain export in Environment Variables back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, committing secrets in dotfiles, and suggest this habit: print a variable to confirm its value before blaming the code.
