Computability and the Halting Problem Explained Simply

Here is Computability and the Halting Problem without the jargon: it is the study of what problems computers can and cannot solve.

Think of Computability and the Halting Problem as asking a machine to predict whether any program will ever finish. Keep that picture in mind as you read on.

The vocabulary has four main pieces: Turing machines, decidability, the halting problem, and reductions. You do not need to master them all at once. Start with Turing machines, then add decidability.

You will meet Computability and the Halting Problem in work such as understanding the limits of tools, proofs of impossibility, and theory courses.

One practical tip is to learn the diagonal argument step by step.

Once Computability and the Halting Problem feels comfortable, read about Linear Algebra for Programmers, which lives in the same neighborhood of ideas.
