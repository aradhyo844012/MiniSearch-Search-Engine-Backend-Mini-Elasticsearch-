Case Study: Applying Deadlocks in a Small Team

Imagine a small student team building a project that depends on Deadlocks. Put simply, Deadlocks covers situations where two or more tasks wait for each other forever. Learning it well pays off across many areas of computing.

Deadlocks and Threads and Concurrency are closely related, so learning both gives a fuller picture of Operating Systems.

The team first spent a week learning mutual exclusion and hold and wait. Early on they hit a familiar problem: acquiring locks in inconsistent order. After fixing it, they used thread dumps to check their work and adopted Deadlocks for database lock conflicts. Looking back, they said the biggest lesson was to document decisions as they went.

Three issues come up again and again: acquiring locks in inconsistent order; holding locks while waiting on I/O; and no timeouts on locks. Knowing them in advance saves hours of debugging.

With steady practice, Deadlocks will stop feeling abstract and become a tool you reach for naturally.
