Process Signals and Job Control in Real Projects

Reading about Process Signals and Job Control is useful, but the ideas make more sense in context. These three scenarios show how it appears in real projects.

Scenario one: stopping runaway programs. Here, SIGTERM and SIGKILL drives the main design choices, and the team needs to guard against using kill -9 first.

Scenario two: shutting down gracefully. This time foreground and background jobs matters most, and tools such as kill help keep things manageable.

Scenario three: running long tasks in the background. The key idea is kill and trap, and the biggest risk is orphaned child processes.

Across all three scenarios, Process Signals and Job Control stays the same idea: messages and shell features used to stop, pause and resume running programs. Only the details change.
