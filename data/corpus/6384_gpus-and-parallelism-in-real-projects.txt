GPUs and Parallelism in Real Projects

Reading about GPUs and Parallelism is useful, but the ideas make more sense in context. These three scenarios show how it appears in real projects.

Scenario one: training neural networks. Here, threads and blocks drives the main design choices, and the team needs to guard against slow data transfers.

Scenario two: rendering graphics. This time kernels matters most, and tools such as CUDA help keep things manageable.

Scenario three: scientific simulations. The key idea is memory transfers, and the biggest risk is branch-heavy code.

Across all three scenarios, GPUs and Parallelism stays the same idea: processors with thousands of small cores that handle many similar calculations at once. Only the details change.
