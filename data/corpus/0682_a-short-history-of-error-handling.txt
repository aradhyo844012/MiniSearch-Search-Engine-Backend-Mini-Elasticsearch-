A Short History of Error Handling

Ideas behind Error Handling developed gradually as engineers ran into practical limits and shared what they learned. Put simply, Error Handling covers planning for failures so that programs respond clearly instead of crashing. Learning it well pays off across many areas of computing.

Clear error messages and good names make programs far easier to debug.

To understand Error Handling, start with exceptions. Then move on to validation, and finally look at logging and retries. Sketching retries on paper before coding helps many learners see how the pieces fit.

A solid grasp of Error Handling also makes Functional Programming easier to understand.

Clear error messages and good names make programs far easier to debug.

None of this needs special hardware or expensive software. A laptop, curiosity and a few small experiments are enough to get comfortable with Error Handling.
