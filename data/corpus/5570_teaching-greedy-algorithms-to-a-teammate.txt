Teaching Greedy Algorithms to a Teammate

Explaining Greedy Algorithms to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: picking the closest open parking spot at every junction and hoping it works out. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Greedy Algorithms is algorithms that build a solution by always taking the locally best choice. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving interval scheduling, naming greedy choice property and optimal substructure as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain exchange arguments in Greedy Algorithms back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, assuming greedy always works, and suggest this habit: try to break your greedy idea with a small counterexample before trusting it.
