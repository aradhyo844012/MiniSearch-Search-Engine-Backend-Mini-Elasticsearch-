A Beginner's Guide to TLS Handshake

This guide is written for readers who are new to TLS Handshake and want a clear starting point. Put simply, TLS Handshake covers the exchange that lets a client and server agree on keys and set up an encrypted connection. Learning it well pays off across many areas of computing.

Simple command-line tools can reveal a lot about what is happening on the wire.

Four ideas sit at the heart of TLS Handshake: certificates, cipher suites, session keys, and certificate authorities. Each one builds on the one before it. Understanding cipher suites first often makes session keys much easier to reason about.

A team working on securing websites might adopt TLS Handshake after hitting the limits of a simpler approach. Other teams turn to it for protecting API traffic or verifying server identity.

Popular tools and libraries include OpenSSL, Let's Encrypt, and SSL Labs.

Watch out for these problems: expired certificates; disabling certificate validation; and using outdated protocol versions.

In short, careful study of TLS Handshake pays off. Spend time on certificates and cipher suites, and the rest will follow.
