Mistakes to Avoid with Test-Driven Development

Everyone learns Test-Driven Development partly through mistakes. Here are three worth avoiding.

Mistake one: writing tests that are too large. It usually appears when someone skips red, green, refactor and jumps straight to implementation. The fix is to slow down and review the basics.

Mistake two: skipping the refactor step. It tends to be noticed late, often during testing of designing small modules. A short checklist during review catches it early.

Mistake three: testing implementation details. It is easy to miss because everything seems to work at first. Keep an eye on fast feedback to spot it.

If you remember only one rule, make it this: keep each red-green cycle to a few minutes.
