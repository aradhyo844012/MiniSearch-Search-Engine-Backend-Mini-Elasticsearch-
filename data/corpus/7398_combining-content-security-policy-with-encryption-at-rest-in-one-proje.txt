Combining Content Security Policy with Encryption at Rest in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Content Security Policy and Encryption at Rest can work together in a single project.

Content Security Policy is a browser feature that limits which scripts and resources a page may load. Encryption at Rest is protecting stored data by encrypting it on disk or in a database.

Imagine a project that involves reducing the impact of XSS and protecting backups. Content Security Policy brings directives, while Encryption at Rest brings symmetric keys.

A sensible plan is to start with browser DevTools for the Content Security Policy part and LUKS for the Encryption at Rest part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: policies too broad to help on one side, and no key rotation on the other.

Picture the whole as a guest list at the door of a building, working alongside a locked safe inside a locked room.

Two habits help: first, start in report-only mode to learn what would break; second, keep keys in a separate service from the data.
