Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Students: A Study Guide

This study guide prepares you for exams, labs and interviews that cover Retrieval-Augmented Generation.

Learning goals:
1. Explain Retrieval-Augmented Generation in your own words.
2. Describe retrieval, chunking, prompts with context, and grounding.
3. Give examples from company knowledge assistants and question answering over documents.
4. Name three problems to watch for.

Start with the definition: Retrieval-Augmented Generation is a technique that gives a language model relevant documents to read before it answers. Then draw an open-book exam where the student first looks up the right pages on paper and label the four terms from the goals above.

Self-test questions: Why is poor chunking a problem? How does chunking relate to prompts with context? When would you choose LangChain or FAISS?

Study habit: evaluate retrieval quality separately from answer quality.

When you feel ready, compare Retrieval-Augmented Generation with Support Vector Machines and write down three differences.
