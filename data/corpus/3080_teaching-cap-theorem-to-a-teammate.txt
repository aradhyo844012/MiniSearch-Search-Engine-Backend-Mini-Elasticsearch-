Teaching CAP Theorem to a Teammate

Explaining CAP Theorem to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: a phone tree that must choose between giving old information or staying silent when a line is cut. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: CAP Theorem is a result stating that a distributed system cannot guarantee consistency, availability and partition tolerance all at once. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving choosing database types, naming consistency and availability as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain partition tolerance in CAP Theorem back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, treating it as a three-way menu, and suggest this habit: ask what your users prefer during a failure: stale answers or errors.
