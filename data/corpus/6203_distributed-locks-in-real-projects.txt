Distributed Locks in Real Projects

Reading about Distributed Locks is useful, but the ideas make more sense in context. These three scenarios show how it appears in real projects.

Scenario one: running scheduled jobs once. Here, lease times drives the main design choices, and the team needs to guard against locks that never expire.

Scenario two: protecting shared resources. This time fencing tokens matters most, and tools such as Redis help keep things manageable.

Scenario three: selecting a leader. The key idea is lock owners, and the biggest risk is clock drift.

Across all three scenarios, Distributed Locks stays the same idea: mechanisms that ensure only one process at a time performs a critical action across machines. Only the details change.
