Is Topological Sorting Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Topological Sorting, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Topological Sorting is an ordering of tasks in a directed graph so that every task comes after the tasks it depends on.

It is a good fit when your work involves build systems or course prerequisite planning, and when your team understands directed acyclic graphs.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn in-degree counts, or when you cannot manage these risks: ignoring cycles, assuming the order is unique, and mixing up edge directions.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether Kahn's algorithm and cycle detection are part of the answer. If they are, Topological Sorting is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with task scheduling and check for cycles first so you can report a clear error.
