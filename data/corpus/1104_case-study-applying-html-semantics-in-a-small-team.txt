Case Study: Applying HTML Semantics in a Small Team

Imagine a small student team building a project that depends on HTML Semantics. Many students first meet HTML Semantics in a classroom, but it matters just as much in production systems. The term refers to the practice of using HTML elements that describe the meaning of content.

The team first spent a week learning headings and landmarks. Early on they hit a familiar problem: using div for everything. After fixing it, they used HTML validators to check their work and adopted HTML Semantics for accessible pages. Looking back, they said the biggest lesson was to measure before optimizing.

Watch out for these problems: using div for everything; skipping heading levels; and missing labels on form inputs.

None of this needs special hardware or expensive software. A laptop, curiosity and a few small experiments are enough to get comfortable with HTML Semantics.
