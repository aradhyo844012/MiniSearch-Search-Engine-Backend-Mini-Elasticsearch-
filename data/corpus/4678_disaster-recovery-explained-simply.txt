Disaster Recovery Explained Simply

Here is Disaster Recovery without the jargon: it is the plans and systems that restore services after major failures.

Think of Disaster Recovery as a fire drill for a whole system. Keep that picture in mind as you read on.

The vocabulary has four main pieces: recovery time objective, recovery point objective, failover regions, and runbooks. You do not need to master them all at once. Start with recovery time objective, then add recovery point objective.

People reach for Disaster Recovery when they work on surviving regional outages, meeting compliance needs, and protecting critical data.

One practical tip is to run a failover exercise on a regular schedule.

Once Disaster Recovery feels comfortable, read about Autoscaling, which lives in the same neighborhood of ideas.
