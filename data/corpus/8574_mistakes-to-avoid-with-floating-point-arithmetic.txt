Mistakes to Avoid with Floating-Point Arithmetic

Everyone learns Floating-Point Arithmetic partly through mistakes. Here are three worth avoiding.

Mistake one: comparing floats with equality. It usually appears when someone skips mantissa and exponent and jumps straight to implementation. The fix is to slow down and review the basics.

Mistake two: accumulated rounding errors. It tends to be noticed late, often during testing of scientific computing. A short checklist during review catches it early.

Mistake three: using floats for money. It is easy to miss because everything seems to work at first. Keep an eye on special values to spot it.

If you remember only one rule, make it this: compare floats with a tolerance and use decimals for money.
