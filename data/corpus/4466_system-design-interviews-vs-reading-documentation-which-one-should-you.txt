System Design Interviews vs Reading Documentation: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study System Design Interviews or Reading Documentation first. Both sit in the area of Developer Career and Tools, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

System Design Interviews is discussions in which candidates design a large software system and explain their choices, and it is closely tied to requirements and scaling estimates. Reading Documentation, by contrast, is the skill of finding accurate answers in official guides and references, and it is closely tied to search and tutorials versus references.

Choose System Design Interviews first if you care about preparing for senior roles or practicing architectural thinking. Choose Reading Documentation first if you care about learning new libraries or solving errors.

Think of System Design Interviews as sketching the blueprint of a city while explaining why each road exists, and of Reading Documentation as reading the manual before assembling the furniture. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With System Design Interviews, avoid jumping into solutions before clarifying requirements. With Reading Documentation, avoid reading the wrong version.

A good habit for System Design Interviews is to clarify requirements and scale before drawing boxes. A good habit for Reading Documentation is to check that the documentation version matches your installed version.
