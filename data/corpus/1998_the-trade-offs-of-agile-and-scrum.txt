The Trade-offs of Agile and Scrum

Every design choice has a cost, and Agile and Scrum is no exception. Put simply, Agile and Scrum covers iterative ways of planning and delivering software in short cycles. Learning it well pays off across many areas of computing.

On the positive side, Agile and Scrum is well suited to student team projects and product development. On the other hand, it is not free. Keep these risks in mind: skipping retrospectives; overloading sprints; treating estimates as promises. Whether the trade is worth it depends on how much sprints and backlogs matter to your project.

Teams succeed when they agree on a few simple practices and follow them consistently.

Three issues come up again and again: skipping retrospectives; overloading sprints; and treating estimates as promises. Knowing them in advance saves hours of debugging.

Ask a teammate to explain stand-ups back to you; teaching is a quick way to find gaps. Keep an eye on this risk during code review: skipping retrospectives. Practice sprints with small exercises until it feels natural.

Keep experimenting, keep notes, and revisit Agile and Scrum whenever a new project gives you a reason.
