Inside HTTP and HTTPS: How It Works

This article walks through what happens inside HTTP and HTTPS, step by step. The term HTTP and HTTPS refers to the request and response protocol of the web, with HTTPS adding encryption. This article explains the idea in plain language and points out where it fits in real projects.

Four ideas sit at the heart of HTTP and HTTPS: methods, status codes, headers, and cookies. Each one builds on the one before it. A good way to remember methods is to connect it with a concrete example from your own code.

First, methods sets the stage for everything else. Next, status codes builds on it. After that, headers comes into play, and finally cookies ties the pieces together. Seeing the sequence once, with a tiny example, makes the whole topic easier to remember.

Simple command-line tools can reveal a lot about what is happening on the wire. Network failures are normal, so well-designed software expects timeouts and retries.

Every web request crosses several layers of protocols before it reaches an application.

If you are comfortable with HTTP and HTTPS, explore TLS Handshake next. The two often appear in the same project.

In short, careful study of HTTP and HTTPS pays off. Spend time on methods and status codes, and the rest will follow.
