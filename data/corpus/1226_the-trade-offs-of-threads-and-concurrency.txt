The Trade-offs of Threads and Concurrency

Every design choice has a cost, and Threads and Concurrency is no exception. When people talk about Threads and Concurrency, they usually mean ways of making progress on several tasks at once within a program. It appears in almost every serious software project, whether or not developers notice it.

On the positive side, Threads and Concurrency is well suited to handling many requests and keeping interfaces responsive. On the other hand, it is not free. Keep these risks in mind: forgetting to protect shared data; creating too many threads; mixing up concurrency and parallelism. Whether the trade is worth it depends on how much threads and locks matter to your project.

Three issues come up again and again: forgetting to protect shared data; creating too many threads; and mixing up concurrency and parallelism. Knowing them in advance saves hours of debugging.

Keep an eye on this risk during code review: forgetting to protect shared data. Practice threads with small exercises until it feels natural.

In short, careful study of Threads and Concurrency pays off. Spend time on threads and locks, and the rest will follow.
