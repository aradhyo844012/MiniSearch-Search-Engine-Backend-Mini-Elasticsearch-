Instruction Pipelines vs Interrupts: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Instruction Pipelines or Interrupts first. Both sit in the area of Computer Systems, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Instruction Pipelines is a technique where a processor overlaps the stages of several instructions, and it is closely tied to fetch, decode and execute stages and hazards. Interrupts, by contrast, is signals that make a processor pause its work to handle an urgent event, and it is closely tied to interrupt handlers and interrupt vectors.

Choose Instruction Pipelines first if you care about explaining CPU speed or understanding branch costs. Choose Interrupts first if you care about keyboard and mouse input or timers.

Think of Instruction Pipelines as an assembly line where several cars are in different stages at once, and of Interrupts as a phone call interrupting your reading. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Instruction Pipelines, avoid unpredictable branches. With Interrupts, avoid long handlers.

A good habit for Instruction Pipelines is to avoid unpredictable branches in hot loops. A good habit for Interrupts is to keep interrupt handlers short and defer the heavy work.
