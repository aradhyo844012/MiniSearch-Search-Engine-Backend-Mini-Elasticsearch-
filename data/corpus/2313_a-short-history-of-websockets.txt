A Short History of WebSockets

Ideas behind WebSockets developed gradually as engineers ran into practical limits and shared what they learned. The term WebSockets refers to a protocol that keeps a two-way connection open between a browser and a server. This article explains the idea in plain language and points out where it fits in real projects.

Network failures are normal, so well-designed software expects timeouts and retries. Every web request crosses several layers of protocols before it reaches an application.

To understand WebSockets, start with persistent connections. Then move on to messages, and finally look at heartbeats and reconnection. A good way to remember persistent connections is to connect it with a concrete example from your own code.

WebSockets and TCP and UDP are closely related, so learning both gives a fuller picture of Networking.

None of this needs special hardware or expensive software. A laptop, curiosity and a few small experiments are enough to get comfortable with WebSockets.
