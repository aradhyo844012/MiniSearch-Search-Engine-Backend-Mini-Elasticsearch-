Combining K-Means Clustering with Random Forests in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how K-Means Clustering and Random Forests can work together in a single project.

K-Means Clustering is an algorithm that groups data points into a chosen number of clusters around central points. Random Forests is ensembles of many decision trees whose predictions are combined.

Imagine a project that involves customer segmentation and tabular predictions. K-Means Clustering brings centroids, while Random Forests brings bagging.

A sensible plan is to start with scikit-learn for the K-Means Clustering part and scikit-learn for the Random Forests part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: unscaled features on one side, and very large forests being slow on the other.

Picture the whole as dividing students into study groups around the most central member, working alongside asking many different experts and taking the majority view.

Two habits help: first, scale features before clustering; second, tune the number of trees only after the basics work.
