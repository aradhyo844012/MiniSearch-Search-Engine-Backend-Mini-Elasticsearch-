Mistakes to Avoid with Random Forests

Everyone learns Random Forests partly through mistakes. Here are three worth avoiding.

Mistake one: treating importance scores as causes. It usually appears when someone skips bagging and jumps straight to implementation. The fix is to slow down and review the basics.

Mistake two: very large forests being slow. It tends to be noticed late, often during testing of tabular predictions. A short checklist during review catches it early.

Mistake three: ignoring class imbalance. It is easy to miss because everything seems to work at first. Keep an eye on out-of-bag error to spot it.

If you remember only one rule, make it this: tune the number of trees only after the basics work.
