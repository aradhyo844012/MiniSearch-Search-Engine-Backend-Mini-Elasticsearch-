Assembly Language Basics in 10 Minutes

You have ten minutes, so here is the fast route through Assembly Language Basics.

Minutes 1 and 2, the definition: Assembly Language Basics is low-level instructions that map closely to what a processor actually executes.

Minutes 3 and 4, the vocabulary: learn registers, instructions, memory addressing, and the stack.

Minutes 5 and 6, the picture: imagine the individual muscle movements behind a simple action. That is the intuition.

Minutes 7 and 8, the use cases: skim through understanding compiled code, debugging crashes, and writing tiny boot code.

Minutes 9 and 10, the traps and the tools: watch for wrong calling conventions and stack corruption, then try GDB for a first experiment.

If you have a spare minute, remember to read the assembly of a tiny C function to see what the compiler does.
