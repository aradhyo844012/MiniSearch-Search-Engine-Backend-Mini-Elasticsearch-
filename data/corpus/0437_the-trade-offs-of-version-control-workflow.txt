The Trade-offs of Version Control Workflow

Every design choice has a cost, and Version Control Workflow is no exception. The term Version Control Workflow refers to the habits and rules a team uses to track and combine changes to code. This article explains the idea in plain language and points out where it fits in real projects.

On the positive side, Version Control Workflow is well suited to collaborating on shared code and tracking history. On the other hand, it is not free. Keep these risks in mind: vague commit messages; committing large unrelated changes; rewriting shared history. Whether the trade is worth it depends on how much commits and branches matter to your project.
