Teaching Graph Databases to a Teammate

Explaining Graph Databases to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: a city map where intersections are records and roads are relationships. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Graph Databases is databases that store data as nodes and relationships and query connections directly. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving social networks, naming nodes and edges as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain properties in Graph Databases back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, using them for simple tabular data, and suggest this habit: limit traversal depth in every exploratory query.
