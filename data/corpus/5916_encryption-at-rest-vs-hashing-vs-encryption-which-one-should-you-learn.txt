Encryption at Rest vs Hashing vs Encryption: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Encryption at Rest or Hashing vs Encryption first. Both sit in the area of Application Security, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Encryption at Rest is protecting stored data by encrypting it on disk or in a database, and it is closely tied to symmetric keys and key management. Hashing vs Encryption, by contrast, is the difference between one-way digests and reversible protection of data, and it is closely tied to one-way functions and keys.

Choose Encryption at Rest first if you care about protecting backups or meeting compliance rules. Choose Hashing vs Encryption first if you care about storing passwords or verifying file integrity.

Think of Encryption at Rest as a locked safe inside a locked room, and of Hashing vs Encryption as a paper shredder compared with a locked suitcase. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Encryption at Rest, avoid storing keys next to the data. With Hashing vs Encryption, avoid encrypting passwords instead of hashing them.

A good habit for Encryption at Rest is to keep keys in a separate service from the data. A good habit for Hashing vs Encryption is to ask whether you will ever need the original value back.
