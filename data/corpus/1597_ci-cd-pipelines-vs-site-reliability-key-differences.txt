CI/CD Pipelines vs Site Reliability: Key Differences

CI/CD Pipelines and Site Reliability both belong to the area of Cloud and DevOps, but they solve different problems. This article compares them side by side so you can choose with confidence.

The term CI/CD Pipelines refers to automated workflows that build, test and deliver code whenever it changes. Its core ideas are build stage, test stage, and artifacts.

The term Site Reliability refers to the discipline of keeping services dependable using engineering practices. Its core ideas are service level objectives, error budgets, and incident response.

The main difference is focus. CI/CD Pipelines puts the emphasis on build stage and test stage, while Site Reliability puts the emphasis on service level objectives and error budgets.

Reach for CI/CD Pipelines when your task involves running tests on every push or shipping releases safely. Reach for Site Reliability when your task involves running production services or reducing outages.

Both have risks. With CI/CD Pipelines, watch for this: slow pipelines. With Site Reliability, watch for this: alerting on everything.

In practice you do not always have to choose. Many projects use CI/CD Pipelines alongside Site Reliability, with tools such as GitHub Actions and PagerDuty. Small, frequent releases are usually safer than rare, large ones.
