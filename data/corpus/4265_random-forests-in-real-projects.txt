Random Forests in Real Projects

Reading about Random Forests is useful, but the ideas make more sense in context. These three scenarios show how it appears in real projects.

Scenario one: tabular predictions. Here, bagging drives the main design choices, and the team needs to guard against treating importance scores as causes.

Scenario two: feature importance ranking. This time feature randomness matters most, and tools such as scikit-learn help keep things manageable.

Scenario three: strong baseline models. The key idea is voting, and the biggest risk is very large forests being slow.

Across all three scenarios, Random Forests stays the same idea: ensembles of many decision trees whose predictions are combined. Only the details change.
