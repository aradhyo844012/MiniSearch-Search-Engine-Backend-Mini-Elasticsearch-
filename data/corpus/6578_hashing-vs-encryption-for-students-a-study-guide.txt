Hashing vs Encryption for Students: A Study Guide

This study guide prepares you for exams, labs and interviews that cover Hashing vs Encryption.

Learning goals:
1. Explain Hashing vs Encryption in your own words.
2. Describe one-way functions, keys, collision resistance, and reversibility.
3. Give examples from storing passwords and verifying file integrity.
4. Name three problems to watch for.

Start with the definition: Hashing vs Encryption is the difference between one-way digests and reversible protection of data. Then draw a paper shredder compared with a locked suitcase on paper and label the four terms from the goals above.

Self-test questions: Why is encrypting passwords instead of hashing them a problem? How does keys relate to collision resistance? When would you choose SHA-256 or AES?

Study habit: ask whether you will ever need the original value back.

When you feel ready, compare Hashing vs Encryption with Encryption at Rest and write down three differences.
