Combining Connection Pooling with Database Backups and Recovery in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Connection Pooling and Database Backups and Recovery can work together in a single project.

Connection Pooling is reusing a limited set of open database connections instead of opening a new one for every request. Database Backups and Recovery is the practice of copying data regularly so that it can be restored after failures.

Imagine a project that involves web backends and recovering deleted tables. Connection Pooling brings pool size, while Database Backups and Recovery brings full and incremental backups.

A sensible plan is to start with PgBouncer for the Connection Pooling part and pg_dump for the Database Backups and Recovery part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: leaked connections on one side, and storing backups on the same disk on the other.

Picture the whole as a rental desk that lends a few shared bikes instead of buying one per visitor, working alongside a spare house key kept at a trusted neighbor's home.

Two habits help: first, always return connections in a finally block; second, schedule a restore drill on a regular basis.
