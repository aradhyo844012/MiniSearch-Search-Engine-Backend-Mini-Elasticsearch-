Content Security Policy vs Single Sign-On: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Content Security Policy or Single Sign-On first. Both sit in the area of Application Security, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Content Security Policy is a browser feature that limits which scripts and resources a page may load, and it is closely tied to directives and nonces. Single Sign-On, by contrast, is letting users log in once and access several applications, and it is closely tied to identity provider and SAML.

Choose Content Security Policy first if you care about reducing the impact of XSS or blocking unwanted scripts. Choose Single Sign-On first if you care about company intranets or reducing password fatigue.

Think of Content Security Policy as a guest list at the door of a building, and of Single Sign-On as a festival wristband that opens every gate. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Content Security Policy, avoid allowing unsafe-inline. With Single Sign-On, avoid a single point of failure.

A good habit for Content Security Policy is to start in report-only mode to learn what would break. A good habit for Single Sign-On is to plan for identity provider outages.
