Combining Data Lakes with Jupyter Notebooks in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Data Lakes and Jupyter Notebooks can work together in a single project.

Data Lakes is large storage systems that keep raw data in its original format until it is needed. Jupyter Notebooks is interactive documents that combine code, results and notes.

Imagine a project that involves storing logs and events and exploring data. Data Lakes brings raw storage, while Jupyter Notebooks brings cells.

A sensible plan is to start with Amazon S3 for the Data Lakes part and JupyterLab for the Jupyter Notebooks part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: no catalog or ownership on one side, and hidden state on the other.

Picture the whole as a big reservoir where water from many rivers is stored before treatment, working alongside a lab notebook that can run its own experiments.

Two habits help: first, register every dataset in a catalog with an owner; second, restart the kernel and run all cells before sharing.
