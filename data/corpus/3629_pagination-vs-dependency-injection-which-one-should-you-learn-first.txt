Pagination vs Dependency Injection: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Pagination or Dependency Injection first. Both sit in the area of Backend Engineering, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Pagination is splitting large result sets into smaller pages that clients request one at a time, and it is closely tied to offset pagination and cursor pagination. Dependency Injection, by contrast, is a design approach where objects receive their dependencies instead of creating them, and it is closely tied to constructor injection and interfaces.

Choose Pagination first if you care about search results or activity feeds. Choose Dependency Injection first if you care about easier unit testing or swapping implementations.

Think of Pagination as reading a long book one chapter at a time with a bookmark, and of Dependency Injection as a chef who is handed ingredients instead of shopping for them. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Pagination, avoid offsets getting slow on huge tables. With Dependency Injection, avoid hiding too much behind containers.

A good habit for Pagination is to prefer cursors for large or fast-changing data. A good habit for Dependency Injection is to inject interfaces so tests can use simple fakes.
