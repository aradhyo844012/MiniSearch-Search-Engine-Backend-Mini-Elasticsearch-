Saga Pattern in Real Projects

Reading about Saga Pattern is useful, but the ideas make more sense in context. These three scenarios show how it appears in real projects.

Scenario one: order processing. Here, local transactions drives the main design choices, and the team needs to guard against missing compensations.

Scenario two: travel bookings. This time compensating actions matters most, and tools such as Temporal help keep things manageable.

Scenario three: payment workflows. The key idea is orchestration, and the biggest risk is steps that are not idempotent.

Across all three scenarios, Saga Pattern stays the same idea: a way of managing long business operations across services using a sequence of local steps and compensating actions. Only the details change.
