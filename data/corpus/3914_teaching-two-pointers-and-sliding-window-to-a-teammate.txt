Teaching Two Pointers and Sliding Window to a Teammate

Explaining Two Pointers and Sliding Window to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: a small frame that slides along a line of text. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Two Pointers and Sliding Window is techniques that scan arrays with two moving positions to avoid nested loops. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving finding pairs with a given sum, naming left and right pointers and window boundaries as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain invariants in Two Pointers and Sliding Window back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, moving the wrong pointer, and suggest this habit: write down the window invariant before coding the loop.
