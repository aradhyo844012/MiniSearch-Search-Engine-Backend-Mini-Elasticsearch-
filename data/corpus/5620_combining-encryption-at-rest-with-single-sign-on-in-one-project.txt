Combining Encryption at Rest with Single Sign-On in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Encryption at Rest and Single Sign-On can work together in a single project.

Encryption at Rest is protecting stored data by encrypting it on disk or in a database. Single Sign-On is letting users log in once and access several applications.

Imagine a project that involves protecting backups and company intranets. Encryption at Rest brings symmetric keys, while Single Sign-On brings identity provider.

A sensible plan is to start with LUKS for the Encryption at Rest part and Okta for the Single Sign-On part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: no key rotation on one side, and misconfigured redirects on the other.

Picture the whole as a locked safe inside a locked room, working alongside a festival wristband that opens every gate.

Two habits help: first, keep keys in a separate service from the data; second, plan for identity provider outages.
