Teaching Stored Procedures and Triggers to a Teammate

Explaining Stored Procedures and Triggers to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: a house rule that fires automatically whenever something is placed on a shelf. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Stored Procedures and Triggers is code stored inside the database that runs on demand or automatically when data changes. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving enforcing rules close to the data, naming procedures and triggers as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain parameters in Stored Procedures and Triggers back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, hidden logic that surprises developers, and suggest this habit: keep triggers small and well documented.
