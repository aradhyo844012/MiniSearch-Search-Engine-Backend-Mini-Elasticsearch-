Domain-Driven Design Quick Reference

A one-screen reminder for Domain-Driven Design. Bookmark it and return when the details fade.

What it is: an approach to software that models the code around the real business domain.
Key terms: ubiquitous language, bounded contexts, aggregates, and entities and value objects.
Typical uses: complex business systems, aligning developers with domain experts, and splitting large systems.
Risks: using it for simple CRUD apps, models that never match the business, and ignoring context boundaries.
Tools: event storming, UML, and Miro.

Mental picture: writing software in the same vocabulary the business speaks.

Habit to build: agree on shared terms with domain experts before drawing classes.
