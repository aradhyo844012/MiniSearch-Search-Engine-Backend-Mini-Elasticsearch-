Rasterization Explained Simply

If Rasterization sounds intimidating, relax. It is converting shapes described by geometry into pixels on the screen, and the idea is simpler than the name suggests.

Think of Rasterization as coloring squares on graph paper to match a drawn outline. Keep that picture in mind as you read on.

The vocabulary has four main pieces: triangles, pixel coverage, the depth buffer, and interpolation. You do not need to master them all at once. Start with triangles, then add pixel coverage.

People reach for Rasterization when they work on real-time games, user interfaces, and CAD previews.

One practical tip is to draw simple shapes first to understand the pipeline.

After Rasterization, a natural next topic is Vector Graphics (SVG).
