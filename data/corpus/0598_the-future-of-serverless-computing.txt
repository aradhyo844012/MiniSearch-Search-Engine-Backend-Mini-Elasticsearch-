The Future of Serverless Computing

Predicting the future is risky, but the direction of Serverless Computing is easy to describe in general terms. Many students first meet Serverless Computing in a classroom, but it matters just as much in production systems. The term refers to running code in managed functions without maintaining servers yourself.

Small, frequent releases are usually safer than rare, large ones. Cloud resources are easy to create and just as easy to forget, which makes costs grow quietly.

Expect tools such as AWS Lambda and Cloud Functions to keep improving, and expect newer ideas to keep building on functions. The basics, though, tend to stay the same, so time spent on event triggers and cold starts is rarely wasted. Anyone working on webhooks will keep meeting Serverless Computing for years to come.

A solid grasp of Serverless Computing also makes CI/CD Pipelines easier to understand.

Read the official documentation for Cloud Functions; it often explains event triggers better than blog posts do. Automate repetitive steps so that results are easy to reproduce. Ask a teammate to explain cold starts back to you; teaching is a quick way to find gaps.

In short, careful study of Serverless Computing pays off. Spend time on functions and event triggers, and the rest will follow.
