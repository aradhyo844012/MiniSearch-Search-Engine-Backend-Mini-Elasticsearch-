Teaching WebAssembly to a Teammate

Explaining WebAssembly to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: a fast engine installed in the corner of a web page. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: WebAssembly is a compact binary format that lets code written in many languages run fast in browsers. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving running heavy computation in the browser, naming modules and linear memory as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain imports and exports in WebAssembly back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, crossing the JavaScript boundary too often, and suggest this habit: measure before moving code to WebAssembly.
