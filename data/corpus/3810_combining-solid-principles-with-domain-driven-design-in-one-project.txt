Combining SOLID Principles with Domain-Driven Design in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how SOLID Principles and Domain-Driven Design can work together in a single project.

SOLID Principles is five guidelines for designing object-oriented code that is easy to change. Domain-Driven Design is an approach to software that models the code around the real business domain.

Imagine a project that involves structuring classes and complex business systems. SOLID Principles brings single responsibility, while Domain-Driven Design brings ubiquitous language.

A sensible plan is to start with UML diagrams for the SOLID Principles part and event storming for the Domain-Driven Design part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: creating too many tiny classes on one side, and models that never match the business on the other.

Picture the whole as house rules that keep a shared kitchen tidy, working alongside writing software in the same vocabulary the business speaks.

Two habits help: first, apply them when a design starts to hurt, not before; second, agree on shared terms with domain experts before drawing classes.
