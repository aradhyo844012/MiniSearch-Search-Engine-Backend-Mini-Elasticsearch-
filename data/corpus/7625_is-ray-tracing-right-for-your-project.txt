Is Ray Tracing Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Ray Tracing, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Ray Tracing is a rendering technique that follows rays of light to produce realistic images.

It is a good fit when your work involves movie visuals or architectural visualization, and when your team understands rays.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn intersections, or when you cannot manage these risks: slow render times, noise from too few samples, and numerical precision errors.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether reflection and refraction and shadows are part of the answer. If they are, Ray Tracing is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with realistic game lighting and start with spheres and a single light before adding features.
