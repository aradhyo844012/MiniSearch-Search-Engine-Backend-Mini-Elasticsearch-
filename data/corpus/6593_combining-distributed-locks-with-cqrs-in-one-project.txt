Combining Distributed Locks with CQRS in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Distributed Locks and CQRS can work together in a single project.

Distributed Locks is mechanisms that ensure only one process at a time performs a critical action across machines. CQRS is a pattern that separates the models used to change data from the models used to read it.

Imagine a project that involves running scheduled jobs once and high-read dashboards. Distributed Locks brings lease times, while CQRS brings commands.

A sensible plan is to start with Redis for the Distributed Locks part and Axon Framework for the CQRS part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: clock drift on one side, and read models drifting out of sync on the other.

Picture the whole as a single key to a shared meeting room that expires automatically, working alongside a restaurant with one counter for orders and another for pickup.

Two habits help: first, always use a timeout together with a fencing token; second, start with a single model and split only when reads and writes truly diverge.
