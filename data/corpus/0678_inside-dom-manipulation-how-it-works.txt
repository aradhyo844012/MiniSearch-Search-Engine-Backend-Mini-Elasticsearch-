Inside DOM Manipulation: How It Works

This article walks through what happens inside DOM Manipulation, step by step. The term DOM Manipulation refers to reading and changing the structure and content of a web page from JavaScript. This article explains the idea in plain language and points out where it fits in real projects.

To understand DOM Manipulation, start with selectors. Then move on to events, and finally look at nodes and updates. A good way to remember selectors is to connect it with a concrete example from your own code.

If you are comfortable with DOM Manipulation, explore Browser Rendering next. The two often appear in the same project.

First, selectors sets the stage for everything else. Next, events builds on it. After that, nodes comes into play, and finally updates ties the pieces together. Seeing the sequence once, with a tiny example, makes the whole topic easier to remember.

Web pages must work across many browsers, devices and network conditions.

A solid grasp of DOM Manipulation also makes Browser Rendering easier to understand.
