Is Content Security Policy Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Content Security Policy, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Content Security Policy is a browser feature that limits which scripts and resources a page may load.

It is a good fit when your work involves reducing the impact of XSS or blocking unwanted scripts, and when your team understands directives.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn nonces, or when you cannot manage these risks: allowing unsafe-inline, policies too broad to help, and breaking third-party widgets.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether report-only mode and trusted sources are part of the answer. If they are, Content Security Policy is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with monitoring policy violations and start in report-only mode to learn what would break.
