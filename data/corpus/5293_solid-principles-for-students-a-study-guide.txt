SOLID Principles for Students: A Study Guide

This study guide prepares you for exams, labs and interviews that cover SOLID Principles.

Learning goals:
1. Explain SOLID Principles in your own words.
2. Describe single responsibility, the open-closed principle, Liskov substitution, and dependency inversion.
3. Give examples from structuring classes and reviewing designs.
4. Name three problems to watch for.

Start with the definition: SOLID Principles is five guidelines for designing object-oriented code that is easy to change. Then draw house rules that keep a shared kitchen tidy on paper and label the four terms from the goals above.

Self-test questions: Why is applying them rigidly to tiny scripts a problem? How does the open-closed principle relate to Liskov substitution? When would you choose UML diagrams or SonarQube?

Study habit worth keeping: apply them when a design starts to hurt, not before.

When you feel ready, compare SOLID Principles with Pair Programming and write down three differences.
