GPUs and Parallelism Explained Simply

Here is GPUs and Parallelism without the jargon: it is processors with thousands of small cores that handle many similar calculations at once.

Think of GPUs and Parallelism as a stadium of workers each doing one small identical task. Keep that picture in mind as you read on.

The vocabulary has four main pieces: threads and blocks, kernels, memory transfers, and SIMD. You do not need to master them all at once. Start with threads and blocks, then add kernels.

People reach for GPUs and Parallelism when they work on training neural networks, rendering graphics, and scientific simulations.

One practical tip is to minimize copies between CPU and GPU memory.

Once GPUs and Parallelism feels comfortable, read about System Calls, which lives in the same neighborhood of ideas.
