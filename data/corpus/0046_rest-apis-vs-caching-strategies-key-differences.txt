REST APIs vs Caching Strategies: Key Differences

REST APIs and Caching Strategies both belong to the area of Backend and APIs, but they solve different problems. This article compares them side by side so you can choose with confidence.

The term REST APIs refers to an approach to designing web APIs around resources, URLs and standard HTTP methods. Its core ideas are resources, HTTP verbs, and status codes.

The term Caching Strategies refers to approaches for storing computed results so they can be reused instead of recalculated. Its core ideas are cache-aside, write-through, and time to live.

The main difference is focus. REST APIs puts the emphasis on resources and HTTP verbs, while Caching Strategies puts the emphasis on cache-aside and write-through.

Reach for REST APIs when your task involves mobile app backends or integrating services. Reach for Caching Strategies when your task involves speeding up read-heavy APIs or reducing database load.

Both have risks. With REST APIs, watch for this: inconsistent naming. With Caching Strategies, watch for this: stale data.

In practice you do not always have to choose. Many projects use REST APIs alongside Caching Strategies, with tools such as Flask and Redis. A backend turns requests from many clients into reliable, consistent responses.
