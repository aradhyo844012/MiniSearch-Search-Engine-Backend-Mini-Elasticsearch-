Combining CSRF with Threat Modeling in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how CSRF and Threat Modeling can work together in a single project.

CSRF is an attack that tricks a logged-in user's browser into sending unwanted requests to a site. Threat Modeling is a structured way of identifying what could go wrong in a system and how to prevent it.

Imagine a project that involves protecting forms and designing new features. CSRF brings session cookies, while Threat Modeling brings assets.

A sensible plan is to start with Django CSRF middleware for the CSRF part and STRIDE for the Threat Modeling part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: skipping tokens on cookie-based APIs on one side, and ignoring data flows on the other.

Picture the whole as a forged letter that a bank clerk stamps because your ID is already on file, working alongside walking around a house asking where a burglar might get in.

Two habits help: first, combine SameSite cookies with anti-CSRF tokens; second, draw the data flow diagram first.
