The Future of CI/CD Pipelines

Predicting the future is risky, but the direction of CI/CD Pipelines is easy to describe in general terms. Many students first meet CI/CD Pipelines in a classroom, but it matters just as much in production systems. The term refers to automated workflows that build, test and deliver code whenever it changes.

A system that cannot be observed cannot be reliably operated. Automation turns risky manual steps into repeatable and reviewable processes.

Expect tools such as GitHub Actions and Jenkins to keep improving, and expect newer ideas to keep building on build stage. The basics, though, tend to stay the same, so time spent on test stage and artifacts is rarely wasted. Anyone working on running tests on every push will keep meeting CI/CD Pipelines for years to come.

If you are comfortable with CI/CD Pipelines, explore Site Reliability next. The two often appear in the same project.

Practice build stage with small exercises until it feels natural. Keep an eye on this risk during code review: slow pipelines. Automate repetitive steps so that results are easy to reproduce.

None of this needs special hardware or expensive software. A laptop, curiosity and a few small experiments are enough to get comfortable with CI/CD Pipelines.
