Dijkstra's Algorithm vs Two Pointers and Sliding Window: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Dijkstra's Algorithm or Two Pointers and Sliding Window first. Both sit in the area of Algorithms in Practice, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Dijkstra's Algorithm is a method for finding the shortest path from one node to all others in a graph with non-negative weights, and it is closely tied to priority queue and distance table. Two Pointers and Sliding Window, by contrast, is techniques that scan arrays with two moving positions to avoid nested loops, and it is closely tied to left and right pointers and window boundaries.

Choose Dijkstra's Algorithm first if you care about route planning or network routing. Choose Two Pointers and Sliding Window first if you care about finding pairs with a given sum or longest substring problems.

Think of Dijkstra's Algorithm as a GPS that keeps expanding to the nearest unvisited intersection, and of Two Pointers and Sliding Window as a small frame that slides along a line of text. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Dijkstra's Algorithm, avoid using it with negative edge weights. With Two Pointers and Sliding Window, avoid moving the wrong pointer.

A good habit for Dijkstra's Algorithm is to stop as soon as the destination is popped from the queue. A good habit for Two Pointers and Sliding Window is to write down the window invariant before coding the loop.
