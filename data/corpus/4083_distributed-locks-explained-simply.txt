Distributed Locks Explained Simply

If Distributed Locks sounds intimidating, relax. It is mechanisms that ensure only one process at a time performs a critical action across machines, and the idea is simpler than the name suggests.

Think of Distributed Locks as a single key to a shared meeting room that expires automatically. Keep that picture in mind as you read on.

The vocabulary has four main pieces: lease times, fencing tokens, lock owners, and timeouts. You do not need to master them all at once. Start with lease times, then add fencing tokens.

People reach for Distributed Locks when they work on running scheduled jobs once, protecting shared resources, and selecting a leader.

One practical tip is to always use a timeout together with a fencing token.

After Distributed Locks, a natural next topic is CAP Theorem.
