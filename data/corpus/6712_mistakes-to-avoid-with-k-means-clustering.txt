Mistakes to Avoid with K-Means Clustering

Everyone learns K-Means Clustering partly through mistakes. Here are three worth avoiding.

Mistake one: choosing k blindly. It usually appears when someone skips centroids and jumps straight to implementation. The fix is to slow down and review the basics.

Mistake two: unscaled features. It tends to be noticed late, often during testing of customer segmentation. A short checklist during review catches it early.

Mistake three: sensitivity to starting points. It is easy to miss because everything seems to work at first. Keep an eye on iterations to spot it.

If you remember only one rule, make it this: scale features before clustering.
