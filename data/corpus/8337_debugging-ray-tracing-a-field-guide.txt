Debugging Ray Tracing: A Field Guide

When something goes wrong around Ray Tracing, resist the urge to guess. Work through the problem in order.

Step 1: reproduce the problem with the smallest possible example.
Step 2: list the usual suspects: slow render times, noise from too few samples, and numerical precision errors.
Step 3: revisit rays and intersections, since misunderstandings there cause many bugs.
Step 4: use Blender Cycles or similar tools to look inside.
Step 5: write a test that fails because of the bug and passes after the fix.

Many bugs involving Ray Tracing in work such as movie visuals come down to the first suspect on the list. Check the simple causes before suspecting anything exotic.

Finally, start with spheres and a single light before adding features. It prevents a surprising number of repeat problems.
