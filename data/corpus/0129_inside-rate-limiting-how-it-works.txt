Inside Rate Limiting: How It Works

This article walks through what happens inside Rate Limiting, step by step. Many students first meet Rate Limiting in a classroom, but it matters just as much in production systems. The term refers to controlling how many requests a client can make in a given period.

Four ideas sit at the heart of Rate Limiting: token bucket, fixed window, sliding window, and 429 responses. Each one builds on the one before it. A good way to remember token bucket is to connect it with a concrete example from your own code.

First, token bucket sets the stage for everything else. Next, fixed window builds on it. After that, sliding window comes into play, and finally 429 responses ties the pieces together. Seeing the sequence once, with a tiny example, makes the whole topic easier to remember.
