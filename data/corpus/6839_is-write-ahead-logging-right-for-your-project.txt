Is Write-Ahead Logging Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Write-Ahead Logging, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Write-Ahead Logging is a technique that records changes in a log before they are applied to the main data.

It is a good fit when your work involves surviving crashes or feeding replication streams, and when your team understands log records.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn checkpoints, or when you cannot manage these risks: disabling fsync for speed, log files filling the disk, and forgetting to archive logs.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether crash recovery and fsync are part of the answer. If they are, Write-Ahead Logging is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with point-in-time recovery and test crash recovery by killing the process on purpose.
