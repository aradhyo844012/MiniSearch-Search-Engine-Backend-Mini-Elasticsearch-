The Trade-offs of NoSQL Databases

Every design choice has a cost, and NoSQL Databases is no exception. When people talk about NoSQL Databases, they usually mean a broad family of databases that do not use the traditional relational table model. It appears in almost every serious software project, whether or not developers notice it.

On the positive side, NoSQL Databases is well suited to flexible schemas and high write throughput. On the other hand, it is not free. Keep these risks in mind: assuming no schema means no design; weak consistency surprises; expensive cross-collection queries. Whether the trade is worth it depends on how much document stores and key-value stores matter to your project.

Three issues come up again and again: assuming no schema means no design; weak consistency surprises; and expensive cross-collection queries. Knowing them in advance saves hours of debugging.

Automate repetitive steps so that results are easy to reproduce. Read the official documentation for Cassandra; it often explains key-value stores better than blog posts do.

Good schema design is hard to change once real data and users depend on it. Data that must survive restarts and crashes belongs in a database rather than in memory.

None of this needs special hardware or expensive software. A laptop, curiosity and a few small experiments are enough to get comfortable with NoSQL Databases.
