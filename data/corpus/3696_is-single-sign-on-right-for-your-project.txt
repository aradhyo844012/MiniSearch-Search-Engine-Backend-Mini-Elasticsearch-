Is Single Sign-On Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Single Sign-On, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Single Sign-On is letting users log in once and access several applications.

It is a good fit when your work involves company intranets or reducing password fatigue, and when your team understands identity provider.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn SAML, or when you cannot manage these risks: a single point of failure, misconfigured redirects, and long sessions on shared devices.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether OpenID Connect and session lifetime are part of the answer. If they are, Single Sign-On is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with centralized access control and plan for identity provider outages.
