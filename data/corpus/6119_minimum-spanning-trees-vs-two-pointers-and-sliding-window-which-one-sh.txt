Minimum Spanning Trees vs Two Pointers and Sliding Window: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Minimum Spanning Trees or Two Pointers and Sliding Window first. Both sit in the area of Algorithms in Practice, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Minimum Spanning Trees is a set of edges that connects every node in a graph with the smallest possible total weight, and it is closely tied to weighted graphs and Kruskal's algorithm. Two Pointers and Sliding Window, by contrast, is techniques that scan arrays with two moving positions to avoid nested loops, and it is closely tied to left and right pointers and window boundaries.

Choose Minimum Spanning Trees first if you care about designing cable networks or clustering data. Choose Two Pointers and Sliding Window first if you care about finding pairs with a given sum or longest substring problems.

Think of Minimum Spanning Trees as laying the cheapest set of roads that still links every town, and of Two Pointers and Sliding Window as a small frame that slides along a line of text. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Minimum Spanning Trees, avoid using it on directed graphs. With Two Pointers and Sliding Window, avoid moving the wrong pointer.

A good habit for Minimum Spanning Trees is to sort the edges once and reuse the order when testing variations. A good habit for Two Pointers and Sliding Window is to write down the window invariant before coding the loop.
