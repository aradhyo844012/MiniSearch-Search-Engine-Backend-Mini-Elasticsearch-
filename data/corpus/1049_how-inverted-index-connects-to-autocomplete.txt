How Inverted Index Connects to Autocomplete

Inverted Index and Autocomplete are closely connected parts of Search and Retrieval. Seeing how they fit together helps you learn both faster.

A project involving full-text search may also involve search boxes. In such a project, vocabulary and prefixes often influence each other.

Start with Inverted Index: the term refers to a data structure that maps each term to the documents that contain it. Then look at Autocomplete, which refers to suggesting likely completions while a person types a query.

A useful exercise is to take one example, such as log search, and explain how ranking suggestions would change the design.

Common problems are shared too. For Inverted Index, watch for this: building the index without normalizing text. For Autocomplete, watch for this: offensive or irrelevant completions. Real query logs reveal what people actually look for, which is rarely what designers expect.

Spend a few hours on each topic, then combine them in one small project using Lucene or tries.
