Teaching CPU Caches to a Teammate

Explaining CPU Caches to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: keeping your most-used books on your desk instead of in the library. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: CPU Caches is small, fast memory close to the processor that stores recently used data. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving speeding up loops, naming cache lines and hit and miss rates as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain levels L1 to L3 in CPU Caches back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, false sharing, and suggest this habit: access arrays in the order they are stored.
