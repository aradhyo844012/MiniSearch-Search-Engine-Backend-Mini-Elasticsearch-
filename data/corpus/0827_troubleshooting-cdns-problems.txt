Troubleshooting CDNs Problems

When something breaks around CDNs, a calm and systematic approach saves the most time. Many students first meet CDNs in a classroom, but it matters just as much in production systems. The term refers to networks of servers around the world that deliver static content from a location close to the user.

Start by reproducing the problem with the smallest possible example. Then change one thing at a time and note what happens. Logs, tests and Cloudflare usually reveal the cause faster than guessing.

If you are comfortable with CDNs, explore Load Balancing next. The two often appear in the same project.

Common culprits: caching private data; forgetting to purge old files; misconfigured cache headers. If none of these explains the symptom, re-read the basics of edge servers and cache rules.

In practice you can start with Cloudflare, then explore Akamai or Amazon CloudFront as your needs grow.

None of this needs special hardware or expensive software. A laptop, curiosity and a few small experiments are enough to get comfortable with CDNs.
