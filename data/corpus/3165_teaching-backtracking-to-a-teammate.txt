Teaching Backtracking to a Teammate

Explaining Backtracking to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: walking a maze and returning to the last fork whenever you hit a wall. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Backtracking is a search technique that builds solutions step by step and undoes steps that lead to dead ends. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving solving Sudoku, naming decision tree and pruning as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain state restoration in Backtracking back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, forgetting to undo changes, and suggest this habit: prune as early as possible because the savings multiply.
