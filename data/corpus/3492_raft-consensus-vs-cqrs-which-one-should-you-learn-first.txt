Raft Consensus vs CQRS: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Raft Consensus or CQRS first. Both sit in the area of Distributed Systems, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Raft Consensus is an algorithm that lets a group of servers agree on a shared log of operations, and it is closely tied to leader election and log replication. CQRS, by contrast, is a pattern that separates the models used to change data from the models used to read it, and it is closely tied to commands and queries.

Choose Raft Consensus first if you care about configuration stores or distributed locks. Choose CQRS first if you care about high-read dashboards or complex domains.

Think of Raft Consensus as a committee that elects a chair and only records decisions most members confirm, and of CQRS as a restaurant with one counter for orders and another for pickup. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Raft Consensus, avoid even-sized clusters. With CQRS, avoid adding it to simple apps.

A good habit for Raft Consensus is to use an odd number of nodes. A good habit for CQRS is to start with a single model and split only when reads and writes truly diverge.
