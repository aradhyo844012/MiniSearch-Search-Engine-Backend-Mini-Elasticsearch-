Combining Raft Consensus with Saga Pattern in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Raft Consensus and Saga Pattern can work together in a single project.

Raft Consensus is an algorithm that lets a group of servers agree on a shared log of operations. Saga Pattern is a way of managing long business operations across services using a sequence of local steps and compensating actions.

Imagine a project that involves configuration stores and order processing. Raft Consensus brings leader election, while Saga Pattern brings local transactions.

A sensible plan is to start with etcd for the Raft Consensus part and Temporal for the Saga Pattern part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: slow disks causing elections on one side, and steps that are not idempotent on the other.

Picture the whole as a committee that elects a chair and only records decisions most members confirm, working alongside booking a trip with separate hotel, flight and car reservations that can each be cancelled.

Two habits help: first, use an odd number of nodes; second, make every step and its compensation idempotent.
