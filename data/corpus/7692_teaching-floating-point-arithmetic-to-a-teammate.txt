Teaching Floating-Point Arithmetic to a Teammate

Explaining Floating-Point Arithmetic to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: writing numbers in scientific notation with a limited number of digits. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Floating-Point Arithmetic is the way computers approximate real numbers with a fixed number of bits. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving scientific computing, naming mantissa and exponent and rounding as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain precision in Floating-Point Arithmetic back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, comparing floats with equality, and suggest this habit: compare floats with a tolerance and use decimals for money.
