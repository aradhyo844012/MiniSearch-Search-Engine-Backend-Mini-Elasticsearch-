The Trade-offs of DNS

Every design choice has a cost, and DNS is no exception. If you build software for long enough, you will run into DNS. The term refers to the system that translates human-friendly domain names into IP addresses.

On the positive side, DNS is well suited to mapping domains to servers and email routing. On the other hand, it is not free. Keep these risks in mind: forgetting TTLs when migrating; mistyped records; assuming changes apply instantly. Whether the trade is worth it depends on how much resolvers and records matter to your project.

Start small by applying DNS to one case, such as mapping domains to servers, before rolling it out more widely. Automate repetitive steps so that results are easy to reproduce.

In short, careful study of DNS pays off. Spend time on resolvers and records, and the rest will follow.
