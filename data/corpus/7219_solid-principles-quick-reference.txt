SOLID Principles Quick Reference

A one-screen reminder for SOLID Principles. Bookmark it and return when the details fade.

What it is: five guidelines for designing object-oriented code that is easy to change.
Key terms: single responsibility, the open-closed principle, Liskov substitution, and dependency inversion.
Typical uses: structuring classes, reviewing designs, and reducing coupling.
Risks: applying them rigidly to tiny scripts, creating too many tiny classes, and confusing interfaces with inheritance.
Tools: UML diagrams, SonarQube, and IDE refactoring tools.

Mental picture: house rules that keep a shared kitchen tidy.

Habit to build: apply them when a design starts to hurt, not before.
