Combining Webhooks with Dependency Injection in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Webhooks and Dependency Injection can work together in a single project.

Webhooks is HTTP callbacks that let one service notify another when an event happens. Dependency Injection is a design approach where objects receive their dependencies instead of creating them.

Imagine a project that involves payment notifications and easier unit testing. Webhooks brings event payloads, while Dependency Injection brings constructor injection.

A sensible plan is to start with ngrok for the Webhooks part and Spring for the Dependency Injection part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: slow handlers causing timeouts on one side, and circular dependencies on the other.

Picture the whole as a doorbell that rings when a delivery arrives instead of you checking the porch, working alongside a chef who is handed ingredients instead of shopping for them.

Two habits help: first, respond quickly and process the event in a background job; second, inject interfaces so tests can use simple fakes.
