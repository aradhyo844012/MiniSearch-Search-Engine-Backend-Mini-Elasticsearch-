Teaching Probability Basics to a Teammate

Explaining Probability Basics to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: predicting how often a dice roll will land on each face. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Probability Basics is the study of how likely events are to happen. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving randomized algorithms, naming events and conditional probability as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain independence in Probability Basics back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, confusing independent and exclusive events, and suggest this habit: simulate the problem in code to check your reasoning.
