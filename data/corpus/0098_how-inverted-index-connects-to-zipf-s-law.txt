How Inverted Index Connects to Zipf's Law

Inverted Index and Zipf's Law are closely connected parts of Search and Retrieval. Seeing how they fit together helps you learn both faster.

A project involving full-text search may also involve analyzing text corpora. In such a project, vocabulary and rank and frequency often influence each other.

Start with Inverted Index: the term refers to a data structure that maps each term to the documents that contain it. Then look at Zipf's Law, which refers to the observation that a word's frequency is roughly inversely related to its rank in natural language.

A useful exercise is to take one example, such as log search, and explain how power laws would change the design.

Common problems are shared too. For Inverted Index, watch for this: building the index without normalizing text. For Zipf's Law, watch for this: reading too much into a perfect fit. Real query logs reveal what people actually look for, which is rarely what designers expect.

Spend a few hours on each topic, then combine them in one small project using Lucene or matplotlib.
