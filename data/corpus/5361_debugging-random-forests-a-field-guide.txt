Debugging Random Forests: A Field Guide

When something goes wrong around Random Forests, resist the urge to guess. Work through the problem in order.

Step 1: reproduce the problem with the smallest possible example.
Step 2: list the usual suspects: treating importance scores as causes, very large forests being slow, and ignoring class imbalance.
Step 3: revisit bagging and feature randomness, since misunderstandings there cause many bugs.
Step 4: use scikit-learn or similar tools to look inside.
Step 5: write a test that fails because of the bug and passes after the fix.

Many bugs involving Random Forests in work such as tabular predictions come down to the first suspect on the list. Check the simple causes before suspecting anything exotic.

Finally, tune the number of trees only after the basics work. It prevents a surprising number of repeat problems.
