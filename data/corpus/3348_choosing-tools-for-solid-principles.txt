Choosing Tools for SOLID Principles

There is no single best tool for SOLID Principles. The right choice depends on your project, your team and your timeline.

UML diagrams is a common first choice, especially for structuring classes. SonarQube suits teams that already work with similar technology, and IDE refactoring tools is worth a look when you need something different.

Judge any tool by four questions: Is the documentation clear? Is the community active? Does it handle reviewing designs well? Can you leave it later if you need to?

Whichever tool you pick, test it on a small problem first, and watch for this: applying them rigidly to tiny scripts.

As a rule of thumb, apply them when a design starts to hurt, not before, no matter which tool you use.
