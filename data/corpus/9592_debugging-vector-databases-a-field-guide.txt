Debugging Vector Databases: A Field Guide

When something goes wrong around Vector Databases, resist the urge to guess. Work through the problem in order.

Step 1: reproduce the problem with the smallest possible example.
Step 2: list the usual suspects: mixing embedding models, ignoring recall trade-offs, and oversized vectors.
Step 3: revisit embeddings and approximate nearest neighbors, since misunderstandings there cause many bugs.
Step 4: use FAISS or similar tools to look inside.
Step 5: write a test that fails because of the bug and passes after the fix.

Many bugs involving Vector Databases in work such as semantic search come down to the first suspect on the list. Check the simple causes before suspecting anything exotic.

Finally, measure recall on a small labeled test set. It prevents a surprising number of repeat problems.
