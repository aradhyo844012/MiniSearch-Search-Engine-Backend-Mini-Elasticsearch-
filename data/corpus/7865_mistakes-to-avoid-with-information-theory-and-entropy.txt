Mistakes to Avoid with Information Theory and Entropy

Everyone learns Information Theory and Entropy partly through mistakes. Here are three worth avoiding.

Mistake one: confusing information with meaning. It usually appears when someone skips bits and jumps straight to implementation. The fix is to slow down and review the basics.

Mistake two: ignoring noise. It tends to be noticed late, often during testing of data compression. A short checklist during review catches it early.

Mistake three: misreading entropy values. It is easy to miss because everything seems to work at first. Keep an eye on noise to spot it.

If you remember only one rule, make it this: compute the entropy of a short text by hand.
