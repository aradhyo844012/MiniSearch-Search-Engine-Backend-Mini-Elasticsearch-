Statistical Significance for Students: A Study Guide

This study guide prepares you for exams, labs and interviews that cover Statistical Significance.

Learning goals:
1. Explain Statistical Significance in your own words.
2. Describe p-values, the null hypothesis, confidence intervals, and effect size.
3. Give examples from judging experiment results and comparing model performance.
4. Name three problems to watch for.

Start with the definition: Statistical Significance is a measure of how unlikely a result would be if there were no real effect. Then draw asking how surprised you should be if a coin were actually fair on paper and label the four terms from the goals above.

Self-test questions: Why is mistaking significance for importance a problem? How does the null hypothesis relate to confidence intervals? When would you choose SciPy or statsmodels?

Study habit: always report the effect size alongside the p-value.

When you feel ready, compare Statistical Significance with Apache Spark and write down three differences.
