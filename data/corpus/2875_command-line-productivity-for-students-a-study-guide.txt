Command Line Productivity for Students: A Study Guide

This study guide prepares you for exams, labs and interviews that cover Command Line Productivity.

Learning goals:
1. Explain Command Line Productivity in your own words.
2. Describe aliases, pipes, history search, and scripts.
3. Give examples from automating repetitive tasks and exploring files and logs.
4. Name three problems to watch for.

Start with the definition: Command Line Productivity is using shell tools and shortcuts to complete tasks faster. Then draw learning the shortcuts on a well-used road on paper and label the four terms from the goals above.

Self-test questions: Why is copying commands without understanding them a problem? How does pipes relate to history search? When would you choose bash or tmux?

Study habit: turn commands you repeat into small scripts.

When you feel ready, compare Command Line Productivity with Competitive Programming and write down three differences.
