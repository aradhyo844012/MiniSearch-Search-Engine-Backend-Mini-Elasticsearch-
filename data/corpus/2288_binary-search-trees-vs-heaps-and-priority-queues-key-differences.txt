Binary Search Trees vs Heaps and Priority Queues: Key Differences

Binary Search Trees and Heaps and Priority Queues both belong to the area of Data Structures and Algorithms, but they solve different problems. This article compares them side by side so you can choose with confidence.

The term Binary Search Trees refers to ordered trees in which each node's left subtree holds smaller keys and its right subtree holds larger keys. Its core ideas are nodes and links, in-order traversal, and tree height.

The term Heaps and Priority Queues refers to structures that always give quick access to the smallest or largest element. Its core ideas are heap property, sift up and sift down, and array representation.

The main difference is focus. Binary Search Trees puts the emphasis on nodes and links and in-order traversal, while Heaps and Priority Queues puts the emphasis on heap property and sift up and sift down.

Reach for Binary Search Trees when your task involves ordered lookups or range queries. Reach for Heaps and Priority Queues when your task involves scheduling tasks or selecting the top results from many candidates.

Both have risks. With Binary Search Trees, watch for this: unbalanced trees degrade to linked lists. With Heaps and Priority Queues, watch for this: assuming a heap is fully sorted.

In practice you do not always have to choose. Many projects use Binary Search Trees alongside Heaps and Priority Queues, with tools such as AVL trees and Python heapq. Algorithms are easiest to learn by tracing small examples on paper before writing any code.
