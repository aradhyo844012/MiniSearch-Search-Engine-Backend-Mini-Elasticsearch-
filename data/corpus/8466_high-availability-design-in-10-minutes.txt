High Availability Design in 10 Minutes

You have ten minutes, so here is the fast route through High Availability Design.

Minutes 1 and 2, the definition: High Availability Design is designing systems so they keep working when parts fail.

Minutes 3 and 4, the vocabulary: learn redundancy, failover, health checks, and no single point of failure.

Minutes 5 and 6, the picture: imagine a bridge with several cables so one breaking does not matter. That is the intuition.

Minutes 7 and 8, the use cases: skim through online stores, banking systems, and healthcare software.

Minutes 9 and 10, the traps and the tools: watch for hidden single points of failure and untested failover, then try load balancers for a first experiment.

If you have a spare minute, remember to list every component and ask what happens if it dies.
