Inside JavaScript Promises: How It Works

This article walks through what happens inside JavaScript Promises, step by step. If you build software for long enough, you will run into JavaScript Promises. The term refers to objects that represent a value that will be available later, after an asynchronous task finishes.

To understand JavaScript Promises, start with pending and resolved states. Then move on to then and catch, and finally look at async and await and chaining. Understanding then and catch first often makes async and await much easier to reason about.

First, pending and resolved states sets the stage for everything else. Next, then and catch builds on it. After that, async and await comes into play, and finally chaining ties the pieces together. Seeing the sequence once, with a tiny example, makes the whole topic easier to remember.
