Best Practices for Agile and Scrum

The practices below come from patterns that tend to work well across many teams and projects. Many students first meet Agile and Scrum in a classroom, but it matters just as much in production systems. The term refers to iterative ways of planning and delivering software in short cycles.

1. Write a few tests that show how Agile and Scrum should behave. Tests double as documentation for your teammates.
2. Measure before you optimize. Use Jira or a simple timer to see where time is actually spent.
3. Automate repetitive steps so that results are easy to reproduce.
4. Keep an eye on this risk during code review: skipping retrospectives.
5. Practice sprints with small exercises until it feels natural.

In practice you can start with Jira, then explore Trello or GitHub Projects as your needs grow.

If you are comfortable with Agile and Scrum, explore Refactoring next. The two often appear in the same project.

Keep experimenting, keep notes, and revisit Agile and Scrum whenever a new project gives you a reason.
