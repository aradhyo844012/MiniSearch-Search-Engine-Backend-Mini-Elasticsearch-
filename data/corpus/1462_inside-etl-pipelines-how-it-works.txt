Inside ETL Pipelines: How It Works

This article walks through what happens inside ETL Pipelines, step by step. Put simply, ETL Pipelines covers processes that extract data from sources, transform it and load it into a destination. Learning it well pays off across many areas of computing.

To understand ETL Pipelines, start with extraction. Then move on to transformation, and finally look at loading and scheduling. A good way to remember extraction is to connect it with a concrete example from your own code.

First, extraction sets the stage for everything else. Next, transformation builds on it. After that, loading comes into play, and finally scheduling ties the pieces together. Seeing the sequence once, with a tiny example, makes the whole topic easier to remember.

Pipelines should be repeatable so that results can be reproduced later. Data work is mostly preparation, and cleaning often takes longer than analysis.

If you are comfortable with ETL Pipelines, explore Data Cleaning next. The two often appear in the same project.

Keep experimenting, keep notes, and revisit ETL Pipelines whenever a new project gives you a reason.
