Endianness vs Interrupts: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Endianness or Interrupts first. Both sit in the area of Computer Systems, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Endianness is the order in which a computer stores the bytes of a multi-byte number, and it is closely tied to big-endian and little-endian. Interrupts, by contrast, is signals that make a processor pause its work to handle an urgent event, and it is closely tied to interrupt handlers and interrupt vectors.

Choose Endianness first if you care about network protocols or binary file formats. Choose Interrupts first if you care about keyboard and mouse input or timers.

Think of Endianness as writing a date as day-month-year or as year-month-day, and of Interrupts as a phone call interrupting your reading. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Endianness, avoid mixing byte orders. With Interrupts, avoid long handlers.

A good habit for Endianness is to always specify byte order in file and protocol formats. A good habit for Interrupts is to keep interrupt handlers short and defer the heavy work.
