Best Practices for Process Scheduling

The practices below come from patterns that tend to work well across many teams and projects. Many students first meet Process Scheduling in a classroom, but it matters just as much in production systems. The term refers to the way an operating system decides which process runs on the CPU and for how long.

Start small by applying Process Scheduling to one case, such as sharing CPUs among programs, before rolling it out more widely. Write a few tests that show how Process Scheduling should behave. Tests double as documentation for your teammates. Practice time slices with small exercises until it feels natural.

1. Automate repetitive steps so that results are easy to reproduce.
2. Start small by applying Process Scheduling to one case, such as sharing CPUs among programs, before rolling it out more widely.
3. Keep notes on what worked. A short README explaining your decisions about Process Scheduling helps the next developer.

Popular tools and libraries include top, htop, and perf.

A solid grasp of Process Scheduling also makes Containers easier to understand.

None of this needs special hardware or expensive software. A laptop, curiosity and a few small experiments are enough to get comfortable with Process Scheduling.
