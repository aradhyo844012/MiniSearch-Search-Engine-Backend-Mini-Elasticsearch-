Is Client-Side Routing Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Client-Side Routing, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Client-Side Routing is changing the visible page in the browser without a full page reload.

It is a good fit when your work involves single-page apps or dashboards, and when your team understands history API.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn route matching, or when you cannot manage these risks: broken deep links, unhandled 404 pages, and lost scroll position.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether nested routes and route guards are part of the answer. If they are, Client-Side Routing is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with multi-step flows and test refreshing the page on every route.
