Consistent Hashing Explained Simply

Here is Consistent Hashing without the jargon: it is a way of mapping keys to servers so that adding or removing servers moves very few keys.

Think of Consistent Hashing as seats around a round table where a new guest only displaces their neighbor. Keep that picture in mind as you read on.

The vocabulary has four main pieces: hash ring, virtual nodes, key placement, and rebalancing. You do not need to master them all at once. Start with hash ring, then add virtual nodes.

You will meet Consistent Hashing in work such as distributed caches, sharded databases, and load balancing.

One practical tip is to use many virtual nodes per server to even out the load.
