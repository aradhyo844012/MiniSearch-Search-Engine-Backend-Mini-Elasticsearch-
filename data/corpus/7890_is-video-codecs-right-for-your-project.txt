Is Video Codecs Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Video Codecs, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Video Codecs is programs that compress and decompress video so it can be stored and streamed.

It is a good fit when your work involves streaming services or video calls, and when your team understands frames.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn motion compensation, or when you cannot manage these risks: mismatched codec support, too low a bitrate, and ignoring hardware decoding.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether bitrate and containers are part of the answer. If they are, Video Codecs is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with archiving footage and test playback on the weakest target device.
