Combining Instruction Pipelines with GPUs and Parallelism in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Instruction Pipelines and GPUs and Parallelism can work together in a single project.

Instruction Pipelines is a technique where a processor overlaps the stages of several instructions. GPUs and Parallelism is processors with thousands of small cores that handle many similar calculations at once.

Imagine a project that involves explaining CPU speed and training neural networks. Instruction Pipelines brings fetch, decode and execute stages, while GPUs and Parallelism brings threads and blocks.

A sensible plan is to start with perf for the Instruction Pipelines part and CUDA for the GPUs and Parallelism part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: dependency chains on one side, and branch-heavy code on the other.

Picture the whole as an assembly line where several cars are in different stages at once, working alongside a stadium of workers each doing one small identical task.

Two habits help: first, avoid unpredictable branches in hot loops; second, minimize copies between CPU and GPU memory.
