Teaching Memory Hierarchy to a Teammate

Explaining Memory Hierarchy to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: a kitchen with a spice rack, a pantry and a warehouse. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Memory Hierarchy is the layers of storage from registers to disks, trading speed for size. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving designing efficient programs, naming registers and cache as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain main memory in Memory Hierarchy back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, ignoring locality, and suggest this habit: reduce how often your program goes down to the slower layers.
