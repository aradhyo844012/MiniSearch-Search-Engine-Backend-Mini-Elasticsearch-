SQL Joins vs NoSQL Databases: Key Differences

SQL Joins and NoSQL Databases both belong to the area of Databases, but they solve different problems. This article compares them side by side so you can choose with confidence.

The term SQL Joins refers to operations that combine rows from two or more tables based on related columns. Its core ideas are inner join, left join, and join conditions.

The term NoSQL Databases refers to a broad family of databases that do not use the traditional relational table model. Its core ideas are document stores, key-value stores, and column families.

The main difference is focus. SQL Joins puts the emphasis on inner join and left join, while NoSQL Databases puts the emphasis on document stores and key-value stores.

Reach for SQL Joins when your task involves linking customers to orders or building reports. Reach for NoSQL Databases when your task involves flexible schemas or high write throughput.

Both have risks. With SQL Joins, watch for this: forgetting the join condition. With NoSQL Databases, watch for this: assuming no schema means no design.

In practice you do not always have to choose. Many projects use SQL Joins alongside NoSQL Databases, with tools such as PostgreSQL and MongoDB. Backups are only useful if restoring from them has been tested.
