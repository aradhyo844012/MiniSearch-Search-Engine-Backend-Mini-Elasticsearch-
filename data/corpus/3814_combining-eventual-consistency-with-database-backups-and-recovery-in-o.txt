Combining Eventual Consistency with Database Backups and Recovery in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Eventual Consistency and Database Backups and Recovery can work together in a single project.

Eventual Consistency is a guarantee that replicas will agree on the same value if updates stop for long enough. Database Backups and Recovery is the practice of copying data regularly so that it can be restored after failures.

Imagine a project that involves global social feeds and recovering deleted tables. Eventual Consistency brings replication delay, while Database Backups and Recovery brings full and incremental backups.

A sensible plan is to start with Amazon DynamoDB for the Eventual Consistency part and pg_dump for the Database Backups and Recovery part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: unresolved write conflicts on one side, and storing backups on the same disk on the other.

Picture the whole as news spreading through a village until everyone has heard it, working alongside a spare house key kept at a trusted neighbor's home.

Two habits help: first, decide up front how your app handles stale reads; second, schedule a restore drill on a regular basis.
