Mistakes to Avoid with Minimum Spanning Trees

Everyone learns Minimum Spanning Trees partly through mistakes. Here are three worth avoiding.

Mistake one: using it on directed graphs. It usually appears when someone skips weighted graphs and jumps straight to implementation. The fix is to slow down and review the basics.

Mistake two: forgetting that the graph must be connected. It tends to be noticed late, often during testing of designing cable networks. A short checklist during review catches it early.

Mistake three: treating ties as errors. It is easy to miss because everything seems to work at first. Keep an eye on the cut property to spot it.

If you remember only one rule, make it this: sort the edges once and reuse the order when testing variations.
