Threat Modeling for Students: A Study Guide

This study guide prepares you for exams, labs and interviews that cover Threat Modeling.

Learning goals:
1. Explain Threat Modeling in your own words.
2. Describe assets, attackers, trust boundaries, and mitigations.
3. Give examples from designing new features and reviewing architecture.
4. Name three problems to watch for.

Start with the definition: Threat Modeling is a structured way of identifying what could go wrong in a system and how to prevent it. Then draw walking around a house asking where a burglar might get in on paper and label the four terms from the goals above.

Self-test questions: Why is doing it once and never again a problem? How does attackers relate to trust boundaries? When would you choose STRIDE or OWASP Threat Dragon?

Study habit: draw the data flow diagram first.

When you feel ready, compare Threat Modeling with CSRF and write down three differences.
