Combining Interrupts with Boolean Logic and Gates in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Interrupts and Boolean Logic and Gates can work together in a single project.

Interrupts is signals that make a processor pause its work to handle an urgent event. Boolean Logic and Gates is the basic true-or-false operations that digital circuits and program conditions are built from.

Imagine a project that involves keyboard and mouse input and writing conditions. Interrupts brings interrupt handlers, while Boolean Logic and Gates brings AND, OR and NOT.

A sensible plan is to start with Linux /proc/interrupts for the Interrupts part and Logisim for the Boolean Logic and Gates part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: race conditions with shared data on one side, and forgetting short-circuit behavior on the other.

Picture the whole as a phone call interrupting your reading, working alongside light switches wired in series or in parallel.

Two habits help: first, keep interrupt handlers short and defer the heavy work; second, write a truth table whenever a condition feels confusing.
