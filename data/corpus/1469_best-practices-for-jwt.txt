Best Practices for JWT

The practices below come from patterns that tend to work well across many teams and projects. The term JWT refers to a compact token format that carries signed claims between a client and a server. This article explains the idea in plain language and points out where it fits in real projects.

1. Read the official documentation for PyJWT; it often explains claims better than blog posts do.
2. Keep an eye on this risk during code review: storing tokens in insecure places.
3. Write a few tests that show how JWT should behave. Tests double as documentation for your teammates.
4. Measure before you optimize. Use jwt.io or a simple timer to see where time is actually spent.
5. Ask a teammate to explain expiry back to you; teaching is a quick way to find gaps.

In practice you can start with jwt.io, then explore PyJWT or jsonwebtoken as your needs grow.

JWT and Microservices are closely related, so learning both gives a fuller picture of Backend and APIs.
