Backtracking for Students: A Study Guide

This study guide prepares you for exams, labs and interviews that cover Backtracking.

Learning goals:
1. Explain Backtracking in your own words.
2. Describe decision tree, pruning, state restoration, and constraint checking.
3. Give examples from solving Sudoku and generating permutations.
4. Name three problems to watch for.

Start with the definition: Backtracking is a search technique that builds solutions step by step and undoes steps that lead to dead ends. Then draw walking a maze and returning to the last fork whenever you hit a wall on paper and label the four terms from the goals above.

Self-test questions: Why is forgetting to undo changes a problem? How does pruning relate to state restoration? When would you choose Python recursion or itertools?

Study habit worth keeping: prune as early as possible because the savings multiply.

When you feel ready, compare Backtracking with Dijkstra's Algorithm and write down three differences.
