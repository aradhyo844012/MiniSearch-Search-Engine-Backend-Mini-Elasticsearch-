Is Distributed Locks Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Distributed Locks, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Distributed Locks is mechanisms that ensure only one process at a time performs a critical action across machines.

It is a good fit when your work involves running scheduled jobs once or protecting shared resources, and when your team understands lease times.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn fencing tokens, or when you cannot manage these risks: locks that never expire, clock drift, and assuming the lock holder is still alive.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether lock owners and timeouts are part of the answer. If they are, Distributed Locks is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with selecting a leader and always use a timeout together with a fencing token.
