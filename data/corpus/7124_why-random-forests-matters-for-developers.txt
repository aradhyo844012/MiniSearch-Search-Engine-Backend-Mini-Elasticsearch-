Why Random Forests Matters for Developers

Developers who understand Random Forests make better decisions, because Random Forests is ensembles of many decision trees whose predictions are combined.

Three situations show why it matters. First, tabular predictions. Second, feature importance ranking. Third, strong baseline models. In each case, a solid grasp of bagging and feature randomness separates a quick fix from a lasting solution.

Ignoring it has a cost. Teams run into these problems: treating importance scores as causes, very large forests being slow, and ignoring class imbalance.

Good tools exist, including scikit-learn, XGBoost, and LightGBM, so you rarely have to start from nothing.
