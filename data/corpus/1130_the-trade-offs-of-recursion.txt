The Trade-offs of Recursion

Every design choice has a cost, and Recursion is no exception. The term Recursion refers to a technique where a function solves a problem by calling itself on smaller inputs. This article explains the idea in plain language and points out where it fits in real projects.

On the positive side, Recursion is well suited to traversing trees and divide and conquer algorithms. On the other hand, it is not free. Keep these risks in mind: missing base cases; exceeding the recursion limit; recomputing the same values. Whether the trade is worth it depends on how much base case and recursive case matter to your project.

Beginners often stumble on three things. First: missing base cases. Second: exceeding the recursion limit. Third: recomputing the same values.

Keep notes on what worked. A short README explaining your decisions about Recursion helps the next developer. Keep an eye on this risk during code review: missing base cases. Automate repetitive steps so that results are easy to reproduce.

Keep experimenting, keep notes, and revisit Recursion whenever a new project gives you a reason.
