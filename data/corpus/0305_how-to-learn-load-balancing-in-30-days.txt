How to Learn Load Balancing in 30 Days

You do not need months to become comfortable with Load Balancing. A focused plan of thirty days is enough to build a solid base. Many students first meet Load Balancing in a classroom, but it matters just as much in production systems. The term refers to spreading incoming requests across several servers so no single server is overwhelmed.

Week 1: learn round robin and health checks, and write a one-page summary.
Week 2: practice with NGINX on a small task such as scaling web applications.
Week 3: build a mini project around avoiding downtime.
Week 4: review common mistakes such as the balancer becoming a single point of failure, and explain Load Balancing to a friend.

In practice you can start with NGINX, then explore HAProxy or AWS Elastic Load Balancing as your needs grow.

Automate repetitive steps so that results are easy to reproduce. Read the official documentation for HAProxy; it often explains health checks better than blog posts do.

None of this needs special hardware or expensive software. A laptop, curiosity and a few small experiments are enough to get comfortable with Load Balancing.
