Best Practices for CDNs

The practices below come from patterns that tend to work well across many teams and projects. If you build software for long enough, you will run into CDNs. The term refers to networks of servers around the world that deliver static content from a location close to the user.

CDNs and DNS are closely related, so learning both gives a fuller picture of Networking.

1. Keep notes on what worked. A short README explaining your decisions about CDNs helps the next developer.
2. Ask a teammate to explain origin servers back to you; teaching is a quick way to find gaps.
3. Write a few tests that show how CDNs should behave. Tests double as documentation for your teammates.
4. Start small by applying CDNs to one case, such as serving images and scripts, before rolling it out more widely.
5. Read the official documentation for Akamai; it often explains cache rules better than blog posts do.

In practice you can start with Cloudflare, then explore Akamai or Amazon CloudFront as your needs grow.

A solid grasp of CDNs also makes DNS easier to understand.

In short, careful study of CDNs pays off. Spend time on edge servers and cache rules, and the rest will follow.
