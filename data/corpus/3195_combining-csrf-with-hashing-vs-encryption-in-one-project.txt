Combining CSRF with Hashing vs Encryption in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how CSRF and Hashing vs Encryption can work together in a single project.

CSRF is an attack that tricks a logged-in user's browser into sending unwanted requests to a site. Hashing vs Encryption is the difference between one-way digests and reversible protection of data.

Imagine a project that involves protecting forms and storing passwords. CSRF brings session cookies, while Hashing vs Encryption brings one-way functions.

A sensible plan is to start with Django CSRF middleware for the CSRF part and SHA-256 for the Hashing vs Encryption part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: skipping tokens on cookie-based APIs on one side, and using broken hash functions on the other.

Picture the whole as a forged letter that a bank clerk stamps because your ID is already on file, working alongside a paper shredder compared with a locked suitcase.

Two habits help: first, combine SameSite cookies with anti-CSRF tokens; second, ask whether you will ever need the original value back.
