How to Learn Relational Databases in 30 Days

You do not need months to become comfortable with Relational Databases. A focused plan of thirty days is enough to build a solid base. The term Relational Databases refers to systems that store data in tables with rows, columns and defined relationships. This article explains the idea in plain language and points out where it fits in real projects.

Week 1: learn tables and rows and primary keys, and write a one-page summary.
Week 2: practice with PostgreSQL on a small task such as storing orders and customers.
Week 3: build a mini project around reporting and analytics.
Week 4: review common mistakes such as skipping constraints, and explain Relational Databases to a friend.

In practice you can start with PostgreSQL, then explore MySQL or SQLite as your needs grow.

Measure before you optimize. Use PostgreSQL or a simple timer to see where time is actually spent. Keep notes on what worked. A short README explaining your decisions about Relational Databases helps the next developer. Ask a teammate to explain foreign keys back to you; teaching is a quick way to find gaps.

In short, careful study of Relational Databases pays off. Spend time on tables and rows and primary keys, and the rest will follow.
