Backtracking vs Two Pointers and Sliding Window: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Backtracking or Two Pointers and Sliding Window first. Both sit in the area of Algorithms in Practice, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Backtracking is a search technique that builds solutions step by step and undoes steps that lead to dead ends, and it is closely tied to decision tree and pruning. Two Pointers and Sliding Window, by contrast, is techniques that scan arrays with two moving positions to avoid nested loops, and it is closely tied to left and right pointers and window boundaries.

Choose Backtracking first if you care about solving Sudoku or generating permutations. Choose Two Pointers and Sliding Window first if you care about finding pairs with a given sum or longest substring problems.

Think of Backtracking as walking a maze and returning to the last fork whenever you hit a wall, and of Two Pointers and Sliding Window as a small frame that slides along a line of text. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Backtracking, avoid forgetting to undo changes. With Two Pointers and Sliding Window, avoid moving the wrong pointer.

A good habit for Backtracking is to prune as early as possible because the savings multiply. A good habit for Two Pointers and Sliding Window is to write down the window invariant before coding the loop.
