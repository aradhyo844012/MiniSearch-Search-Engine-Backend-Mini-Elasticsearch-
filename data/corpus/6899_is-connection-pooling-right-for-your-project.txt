Is Connection Pooling Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Connection Pooling, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Connection Pooling is reusing a limited set of open database connections instead of opening a new one for every request.

It is a good fit when your work involves web backends or serverless functions that call databases, and when your team understands pool size.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn idle connections, or when you cannot manage these risks: pools larger than the database allows, leaked connections, and long transactions that hold connections.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether timeouts and connection leaks are part of the answer. If they are, Connection Pooling is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with high-traffic APIs and always return connections in a finally block.
