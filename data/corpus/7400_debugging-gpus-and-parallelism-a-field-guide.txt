Debugging GPUs and Parallelism: A Field Guide

When something goes wrong around GPUs and Parallelism, resist the urge to guess. Work through the problem in order.

Step 1: reproduce the problem with the smallest possible example.
Step 2: list the usual suspects: slow data transfers, branch-heavy code, and launching tiny workloads.
Step 3: revisit threads and blocks and kernels, since misunderstandings there cause many bugs.
Step 4: use CUDA or similar tools to look inside.
Step 5: write a test that fails because of the bug and passes after the fix.

Many bugs involving GPUs and Parallelism in work such as training neural networks come down to the first suspect on the list. Check the simple causes before suspecting anything exotic.

Finally, minimize copies between CPU and GPU memory. It prevents a surprising number of repeat problems.
