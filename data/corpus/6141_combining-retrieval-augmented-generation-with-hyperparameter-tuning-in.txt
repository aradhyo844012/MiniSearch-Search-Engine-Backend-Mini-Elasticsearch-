Combining Retrieval-Augmented Generation with Hyperparameter Tuning in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Retrieval-Augmented Generation and Hyperparameter Tuning can work together in a single project.

Retrieval-Augmented Generation is a technique that gives a language model relevant documents to read before it answers. Hyperparameter Tuning is the process of searching for the best settings that control how a model learns.

Imagine a project that involves company knowledge assistants and improving model accuracy. Retrieval-Augmented Generation brings retrieval, while Hyperparameter Tuning brings search spaces.

A sensible plan is to start with LangChain for the Retrieval-Augmented Generation part and Optuna for the Hyperparameter Tuning part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: irrelevant retrieved text on one side, and searching too many options on the other.

Picture the whole as an open-book exam where the student first looks up the right pages, working alongside adjusting the knobs on a radio until the signal is clear.

Two habits help: first, evaluate retrieval quality separately from answer quality; second, try random search before anything fancier.
