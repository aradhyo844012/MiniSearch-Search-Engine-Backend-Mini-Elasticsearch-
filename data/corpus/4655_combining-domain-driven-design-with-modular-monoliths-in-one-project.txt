Combining Domain-Driven Design with Modular Monoliths in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Domain-Driven Design and Modular Monoliths can work together in a single project.

Domain-Driven Design is an approach to software that models the code around the real business domain. Modular Monoliths is single deployable applications built from well-separated internal modules.

Imagine a project that involves complex business systems and small teams. Domain-Driven Design brings ubiquitous language, while Modular Monoliths brings module boundaries.

A sensible plan is to start with event storming for the Domain-Driven Design part and ArchUnit for the Modular Monoliths part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: models that never match the business on one side, and no enforced boundaries on the other.

Picture the whole as writing software in the same vocabulary the business speaks, working alongside an apartment building with separate flats under one roof.

Two habits help: first, agree on shared terms with domain experts before drawing classes; second, enforce boundaries with automated checks.
