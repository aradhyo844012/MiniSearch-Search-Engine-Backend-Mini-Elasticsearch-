Webhooks vs Object-Relational Mappers: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Webhooks or Object-Relational Mappers first. Both sit in the area of Backend Engineering, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Webhooks is HTTP callbacks that let one service notify another when an event happens, and it is closely tied to event payloads and signature verification. Object-Relational Mappers, by contrast, is libraries that map database tables to objects in code, and it is closely tied to models and queries.

Choose Webhooks first if you care about payment notifications or CI triggers. Choose Object-Relational Mappers first if you care about building CRUD apps quickly or switching databases.

Think of Webhooks as a doorbell that rings when a delivery arrives instead of you checking the porch, and of Object-Relational Mappers as a translator between the language of tables and the language of objects. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Webhooks, avoid not verifying signatures. With Object-Relational Mappers, avoid N+1 queries.

A good habit for Webhooks is to respond quickly and process the event in a background job. A good habit for Object-Relational Mappers is to log the generated SQL during development.
