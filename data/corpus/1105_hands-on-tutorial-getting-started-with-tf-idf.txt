Hands-On Tutorial: Getting Started with TF-IDF

This hands-on tutorial takes you from zero to a small working experiment with TF-IDF. When people talk about TF-IDF, they usually mean a scoring method that weights terms by how often they appear in a document and how rare they are overall. It appears in almost every serious software project, whether or not developers notice it.

Step 1: Install or open scikit-learn TfidfVectorizer and create a new empty project folder.
Step 2: Read about term frequency and write down a definition in your own words.
Step 3: Build the smallest possible example that uses inverse document frequency.
Step 4: Add one test or print statement that proves it works.
Step 5: Extend the example to handle a case such as ranking search results.
Step 6: Break your example on purpose and see what happens. A good experiment: over-weighting common words.

In practice you can start with scikit-learn TfidfVectorizer, then explore Lucene or Gensim as your needs grow.

Watch out for these problems: over-weighting common words; ignoring document length; and using it on tiny corpora.

None of this needs special hardware or expensive software. A laptop, curiosity and a few small experiments are enough to get comfortable with TF-IDF.
