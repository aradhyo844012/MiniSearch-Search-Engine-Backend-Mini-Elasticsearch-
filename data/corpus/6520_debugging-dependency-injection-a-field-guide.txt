Debugging Dependency Injection: A Field Guide

When something goes wrong around Dependency Injection, resist the urge to guess. Work through the problem in order.

Step 1: reproduce the problem with the smallest possible example.
Step 2: list the usual suspects: hiding too much behind containers, circular dependencies, and injecting too many dependencies.
Step 3: revisit constructor injection and interfaces, since misunderstandings there cause many bugs.
Step 4: use Spring or similar tools to look inside.
Step 5: write a test that fails because of the bug and passes after the fix.

Many bugs involving Dependency Injection in work such as easier unit testing come down to the first suspect on the list. Check the simple causes before suspecting anything exotic.

Finally, inject interfaces so tests can use simple fakes. It prevents a surprising number of repeat problems.
