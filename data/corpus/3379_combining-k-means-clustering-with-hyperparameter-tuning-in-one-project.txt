Combining K-Means Clustering with Hyperparameter Tuning in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how K-Means Clustering and Hyperparameter Tuning can work together in a single project.

K-Means Clustering is an algorithm that groups data points into a chosen number of clusters around central points. Hyperparameter Tuning is the process of searching for the best settings that control how a model learns.

Imagine a project that involves customer segmentation and improving model accuracy. K-Means Clustering brings centroids, while Hyperparameter Tuning brings search spaces.

A sensible plan is to start with scikit-learn for the K-Means Clustering part and Optuna for the Hyperparameter Tuning part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: unscaled features on one side, and searching too many options on the other.

Picture the whole as dividing students into study groups around the most central member, working alongside adjusting the knobs on a radio until the signal is clear.

Two habits help: first, scale features before clustering; second, try random search before anything fancier.
