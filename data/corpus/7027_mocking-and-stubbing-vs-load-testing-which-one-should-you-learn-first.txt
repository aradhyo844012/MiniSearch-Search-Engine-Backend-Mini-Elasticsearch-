Mocking and Stubbing vs Load Testing: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Mocking and Stubbing or Load Testing first. Both sit in the area of Testing and Quality, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Mocking and Stubbing is replacing real dependencies with controlled fakes during tests, and it is closely tied to mocks and stubs. Load Testing, by contrast, is measuring how a system behaves under expected and heavy traffic, and it is closely tied to virtual users and throughput.

Choose Mocking and Stubbing first if you care about isolating units or simulating failures. Choose Load Testing first if you care about capacity planning or finding bottlenecks.

Think of Mocking and Stubbing as a crash test dummy standing in for a real passenger, and of Load Testing as a stress test that fills a stadium to see how the exits cope. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Mocking and Stubbing, avoid over-mocking. With Load Testing, avoid testing unrealistic traffic.

A good habit for Mocking and Stubbing is to prefer simple fakes over deep mock chains. A good habit for Load Testing is to report p95 and p99 latency, not only averages.
