The Trade-offs of Message Queues

Every design choice has a cost, and Message Queues is no exception. When people talk about Message Queues, they usually mean systems that let services send work to each other asynchronously through a buffer. It appears in almost every serious software project, whether or not developers notice it.

On the positive side, Message Queues is well suited to sending emails and processing uploads. On the other hand, it is not free. Keep these risks in mind: losing messages without acknowledgements; poison messages that retry forever; duplicate processing. Whether the trade is worth it depends on how much producers and consumers matter to your project.

Three issues come up again and again: losing messages without acknowledgements; poison messages that retry forever; and duplicate processing. Knowing them in advance saves hours of debugging.

Ask a teammate to explain acknowledgements back to you; teaching is a quick way to find gaps. Read the official documentation for Apache Kafka; it often explains consumers better than blog posts do.

Clear contracts between services make systems easier to change and test.

In short, careful study of Message Queues pays off. Spend time on producers and consumers, and the rest will follow.
