Linear Regression vs Large Language Models: Key Differences

Linear Regression and Large Language Models both belong to the area of Machine Learning, but they solve different problems. This article compares them side by side so you can choose with confidence.

The term Linear Regression refers to a method for predicting a number by fitting a straight line or plane to data. Its core ideas are coefficients, residuals, and least squares.

The term Large Language Models refers to very large neural networks trained on text that can generate and analyze language. Its core ideas are tokens, attention, and pretraining.

The main difference is focus. Linear Regression puts the emphasis on coefficients and residuals, while Large Language Models puts the emphasis on tokens and attention.

Reach for Linear Regression when your task involves predicting prices or estimating demand. Reach for Large Language Models when your task involves drafting text or answering questions.

Both have risks. With Linear Regression, watch for this: extrapolating far beyond the data. With Large Language Models, watch for this: trusting outputs without checking.

In practice you do not always have to choose. Many projects use Linear Regression alongside Large Language Models, with tools such as scikit-learn and Hugging Face. Reproducible experiments make it far easier to trust and improve a model.
