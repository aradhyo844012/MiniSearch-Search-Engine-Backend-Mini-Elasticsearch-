Network Troubleshooting Explained Simply

Here is Network Troubleshooting without the jargon: it is a step-by-step process for finding out why a network connection fails or is slow.

Think of Network Troubleshooting as a doctor who checks the heart, lungs and blood pressure in order. Keep that picture in mind as you read on.

The vocabulary has four main pieces: protocol layers, ping and traceroute, DNS checks, and packet captures. You do not need to master them all at once. Start with protocol layers, then add ping and traceroute.

You will meet Network Troubleshooting in work such as fixing office connectivity, diagnosing slow apps, and verifying firewall changes.

One practical tip is to work from the bottom layer upward.

Once Network Troubleshooting feels comfortable, read about NAT, which lives in the same neighborhood of ideas.
