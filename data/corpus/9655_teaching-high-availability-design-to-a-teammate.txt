Teaching High Availability Design to a Teammate

Explaining High Availability Design to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: a bridge with several cables so one breaking does not matter. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: High Availability Design is designing systems so they keep working when parts fail. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving online stores, naming redundancy and failover as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain health checks in High Availability Design back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, hidden single points of failure, and suggest this habit: list every component and ask what happens if it dies.
