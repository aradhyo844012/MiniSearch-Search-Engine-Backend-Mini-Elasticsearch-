A Short History of CDNs

Ideas behind CDNs developed gradually as engineers ran into practical limits and shared what they learned. The term CDNs refers to networks of servers around the world that deliver static content from a location close to the user. This article explains the idea in plain language and points out where it fits in real projects.

Every web request crosses several layers of protocols before it reaches an application. Simple command-line tools can reveal a lot about what is happening on the wire.

To understand CDNs, start with edge servers. Then move on to cache rules, and finally look at origin servers and cache purging. Sketching cache purging on paper before coding helps many learners see how the pieces fit.

CDNs and HTTP and HTTPS are closely related, so learning both gives a fuller picture of Networking.

In short, careful study of CDNs pays off. Spend time on edge servers and cache rules, and the rest will follow.
