Hands-On Tutorial: Getting Started with Zipf's Law

This hands-on tutorial takes you from zero to a small working experiment with Zipf's Law. Put simply, Zipf's Law covers the observation that a word's frequency is roughly inversely related to its rank in natural language. Learning it well pays off across many areas of computing.

Zipf's Law and Edit Distance are closely related, so learning both gives a fuller picture of Search and Retrieval.

Step 1: Install or open matplotlib and create a new empty project folder.
Step 2: Read about rank and frequency and write down a definition in your own words.
Step 3: Build the smallest possible example that uses power laws.
Step 4: Add one test or print statement that proves it works.
Step 5: Extend the example to handle a case such as analyzing text corpora.
Step 6: Break your example on purpose and see what happens. A good experiment: testing it on random text.

In practice you can start with matplotlib, then explore NumPy or pandas as your needs grow.

Watch out for these problems: testing it on random text; reading too much into a perfect fit; and ignoring small samples.

None of this needs special hardware or expensive software. A laptop, curiosity and a few small experiments are enough to get comfortable with Zipf's Law.
