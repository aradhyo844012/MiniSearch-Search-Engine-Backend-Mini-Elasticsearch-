How to Learn Autocomplete in 30 Days

You do not need months to become comfortable with Autocomplete. A focused plan of thirty days is enough to build a solid base. The term Autocomplete refers to suggesting likely completions while a person types a query. This article explains the idea in plain language and points out where it fits in real projects.

Week 1: learn prefixes and ranking suggestions, and write a one-page summary.
Week 2: practice with tries on a small task such as search boxes.
Week 3: build a mini project around command palettes.
Week 4: review common mistakes such as slow suggestions, and explain Autocomplete to a friend.

Popular tools and libraries include tries, Redis sorted sets, and Elasticsearch suggesters.

Real query logs reveal what people actually look for, which is rarely what designers expect. Search quality depends on how text is prepared before it is ever indexed.

In short, careful study of Autocomplete pays off. Spend time on prefixes and ranking suggestions, and the rest will follow.
