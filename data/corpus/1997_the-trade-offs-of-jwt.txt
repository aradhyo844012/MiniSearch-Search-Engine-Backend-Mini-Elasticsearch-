The Trade-offs of JWT

Every design choice has a cost, and JWT is no exception. When people talk about JWT, they usually mean a compact token format that carries signed claims between a client and a server. It appears in almost every serious software project, whether or not developers notice it.

On the positive side, JWT is well suited to stateless API authentication and service-to-service calls. On the other hand, it is not free. Keep these risks in mind: storing tokens in insecure places; skipping signature checks; very long expiry times. Whether the trade is worth it depends on how much header, payload and signature and claims matter to your project.

Beginners often stumble on three things. First: storing tokens in insecure places. Second: skipping signature checks. Third: very long expiry times.

Ask a teammate to explain expiry back to you; teaching is a quick way to find gaps. Keep notes on what worked. A short README explaining your decisions about JWT helps the next developer. Practice header, payload and signature with small exercises until it feels natural.

None of this needs special hardware or expensive software. A laptop, curiosity and a few small experiments are enough to get comfortable with JWT.
