Random Forests Explained Simply

If Random Forests sounds intimidating, relax. It is ensembles of many decision trees whose predictions are combined, and the idea is simpler than the name suggests.

Think of Random Forests as asking many different experts and taking the majority view. Keep that picture in mind as you read on.

The vocabulary has four main pieces: bagging, feature randomness, voting, and out-of-bag error. You do not need to master them all at once. Start with bagging, then add feature randomness.

People reach for Random Forests when they work on tabular predictions, feature importance ranking, and strong baseline models.

One practical tip is to tune the number of trees only after the basics work.

After Random Forests, a natural next topic is Support Vector Machines.
