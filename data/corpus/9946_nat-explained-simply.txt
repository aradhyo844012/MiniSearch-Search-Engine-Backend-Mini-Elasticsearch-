NAT Explained Simply

If NAT sounds intimidating, relax. It is a technique that lets many devices share one public IP address by rewriting addresses, and the idea is simpler than the name suggests.

Think of NAT as a receptionist who forwards calls for many desks through one phone number. Keep that picture in mind as you read on.

The vocabulary has four main pieces: private addresses, translation tables, port mapping, and port forwarding. You do not need to master them all at once. Start with private addresses, then add translation tables.

You will meet NAT in work such as home routers, sharing limited IPv4 addresses, and hiding internal networks.

One practical tip is to document every port forward you create.

Once NAT feels comfortable, read about Packet Switching, which lives in the same neighborhood of ideas.
