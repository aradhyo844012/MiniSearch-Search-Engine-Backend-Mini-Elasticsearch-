Background Jobs Explained Simply

Here is Background Jobs without the jargon: it is tasks that run outside the request cycle so users do not have to wait for them.

Think of Background Jobs as dropping laundry at a service and collecting it later. Keep that picture in mind as you read on.

The vocabulary has four main pieces: queues, workers, retries, and scheduling. You do not need to master them all at once. Start with queues, then add workers.

People reach for Background Jobs when they work on sending emails, resizing images, and generating reports.

One practical tip is to give every job a timeout and a retry limit.

Once Background Jobs feels comfortable, read about API Versioning, which lives in the same neighborhood of ideas.
