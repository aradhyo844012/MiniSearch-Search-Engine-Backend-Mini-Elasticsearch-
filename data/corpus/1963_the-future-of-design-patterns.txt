The Future of Design Patterns

Predicting the future is risky, but the direction of Design Patterns is easy to describe in general terms. Many students first meet Design Patterns in a classroom, but it matters just as much in production systems. The term refers to reusable solutions to common problems in software design.

Teams succeed when they agree on a few simple practices and follow them consistently.

Expect tools such as UML diagrams and refactoring guides to keep improving, and expect newer ideas to keep building on singleton. The basics, though, tend to stay the same, so time spent on factory and observer is rarely wasted. Anyone working on structuring applications will keep meeting Design Patterns for years to come.

Start small by applying Design Patterns to one case, such as structuring applications, before rolling it out more widely. Ask a teammate to explain observer back to you; teaching is a quick way to find gaps.
