Debugging Procedural Generation: A Field Guide

When something goes wrong around Procedural Generation, resist the urge to guess. Work through the problem in order.

Step 1: reproduce the problem with the smallest possible example.
Step 2: list the usual suspects: levels that are unfair or unwinnable, repetitive results, and bugs that cannot be reproduced without seeds.
Step 3: revisit random seeds and noise functions, since misunderstandings there cause many bugs.
Step 4: use Perlin noise or similar tools to look inside.
Step 5: write a test that fails because of the bug and passes after the fix.

Many bugs involving Procedural Generation in work such as roguelike dungeons come down to the first suspect on the list. Check the simple causes before suspecting anything exotic.

Finally, log the seed so any generated level can be reproduced. It prevents a surprising number of repeat problems.
