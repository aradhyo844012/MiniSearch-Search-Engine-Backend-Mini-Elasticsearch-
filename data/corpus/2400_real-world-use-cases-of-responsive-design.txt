Real-World Use Cases of Responsive Design

The best way to appreciate Responsive Design is to see where it is actually used. When people talk about Responsive Design, they usually mean building pages that adapt smoothly to phones, tablets and large screens. It appears in almost every serious software project, whether or not developers notice it.

People use Responsive Design for websites used on many devices, for dashboards, and for e-commerce pages.

Consider websites used on many devices. In a case like this, Responsive Design gives the work a clear structure, and understanding media queries makes the design easier to explain. A different example is dashboards, where fluid grids often decides how well the solution scales.

Small improvements in markup and styling often have a large effect on usability. Web pages must work across many browsers, devices and network conditions.

Popular tools and libraries include Chrome DevTools device mode, Bootstrap grid, and Lighthouse.

Keep experimenting, keep notes, and revisit Responsive Design whenever a new project gives you a reason.
