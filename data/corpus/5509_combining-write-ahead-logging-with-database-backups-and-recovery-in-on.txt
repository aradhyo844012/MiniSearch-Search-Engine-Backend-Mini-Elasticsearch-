Combining Write-Ahead Logging with Database Backups and Recovery in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Write-Ahead Logging and Database Backups and Recovery can work together in a single project.

Write-Ahead Logging is a technique that records changes in a log before they are applied to the main data. Database Backups and Recovery is the practice of copying data regularly so that it can be restored after failures.

Imagine a project that involves surviving crashes and recovering deleted tables. Write-Ahead Logging brings log records, while Database Backups and Recovery brings full and incremental backups.

A sensible plan is to start with PostgreSQL WAL for the Write-Ahead Logging part and pg_dump for the Database Backups and Recovery part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: log files filling the disk on one side, and storing backups on the same disk on the other.

Picture the whole as writing a plan in a notebook before changing the real blueprint, working alongside a spare house key kept at a trusted neighbor's home.

Two habits help: first, test crash recovery by killing the process on purpose; second, schedule a restore drill on a regular basis.
