Practice Exercises for Computability and the Halting Problem

These exercises build real understanding of Computability and the Halting Problem. Do them in order, and write down your answers.

Exercise 1: Explain how the halting problem and reductions differ, using a drawing.
Exercise 2: List three situations from your own experience that resemble understanding the limits of tools or proofs of impossibility.
Exercise 3: Create a small example that deliberately triggers this problem: assuming everything is solvable. Then fix it.
Exercise 4: Compare textbooks and proof sketches for theory courses and write down your conclusion.
Exercise 5: Pick Turing machine simulators and complete its getting-started tutorial.

When you finish, compare your answers with the definition: Computability and the Halting Problem is the study of what problems computers can and cannot solve.
