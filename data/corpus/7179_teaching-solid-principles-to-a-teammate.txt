Teaching SOLID Principles to a Teammate

Explaining SOLID Principles to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: house rules that keep a shared kitchen tidy. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: SOLID Principles is five guidelines for designing object-oriented code that is easy to change. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving structuring classes, naming single responsibility and the open-closed principle as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain Liskov substitution in SOLID Principles back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, applying them rigidly to tiny scripts, and suggest this habit: apply them when a design starts to hurt, not before.
