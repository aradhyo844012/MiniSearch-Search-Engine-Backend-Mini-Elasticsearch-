Debugging Rust Ownership: A Field Guide

When something goes wrong around Rust Ownership, resist the urge to guess. Work through the problem in order.

Step 1: reproduce the problem with the smallest possible example.
Step 2: list the usual suspects: fighting the borrow checker with needless clones, confusing moves and copies, and overly complex lifetime annotations.
Step 3: revisit ownership and borrowing, since misunderstandings there cause many bugs.
Step 4: use Cargo or similar tools to look inside.
Step 5: write a test that fails because of the bug and passes after the fix.

Many bugs involving Rust Ownership in work such as systems programming come down to the first suspect on the list. Check the simple causes before suspecting anything exotic.

Finally, read the compiler's error messages closely because they often explain the fix. It prevents a surprising number of repeat problems.
