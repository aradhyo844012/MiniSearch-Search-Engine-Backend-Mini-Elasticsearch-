Encryption at Rest vs Threat Modeling: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Encryption at Rest or Threat Modeling first. Both sit in the area of Application Security, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Encryption at Rest is protecting stored data by encrypting it on disk or in a database, and it is closely tied to symmetric keys and key management. Threat Modeling, by contrast, is a structured way of identifying what could go wrong in a system and how to prevent it, and it is closely tied to assets and attackers.

Choose Encryption at Rest first if you care about protecting backups or meeting compliance rules. Choose Threat Modeling first if you care about designing new features or reviewing architecture.

Think of Encryption at Rest as a locked safe inside a locked room, and of Threat Modeling as walking around a house asking where a burglar might get in. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Encryption at Rest, avoid storing keys next to the data. With Threat Modeling, avoid doing it once and never again.

A good habit for Encryption at Rest is to keep keys in a separate service from the data. A good habit for Threat Modeling is to draw the data flow diagram first.
