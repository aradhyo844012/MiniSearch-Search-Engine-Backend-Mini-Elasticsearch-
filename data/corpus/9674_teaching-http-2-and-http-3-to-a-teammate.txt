Teaching HTTP/2 and HTTP/3 to a Teammate

Explaining HTTP/2 and HTTP/3 to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: a multi-lane highway replacing a single-lane road. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: HTTP/2 and HTTP/3 is newer versions of the web protocol that load pages faster over fewer connections. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving faster page loads, naming multiplexing and header compression as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain QUIC in HTTP/2 and HTTP/3 back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, assuming old tricks like sprite sheets still help, and suggest this habit: keep a working HTTP/1.1 fallback.
