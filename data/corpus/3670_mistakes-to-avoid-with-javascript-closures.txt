Mistakes to Avoid with JavaScript Closures

Everyone learns JavaScript Closures partly through mistakes. Here are three worth avoiding.

Mistake one: loop variables captured by mistake. It usually appears when someone skips lexical scope and jumps straight to implementation. The fix is to slow down and review the basics.

Mistake two: memory leaks from long-lived closures. It tends to be noticed late, often during testing of keeping private state in modules. A short checklist during review catches it early.

Mistake three: accidentally sharing mutable state. It is easy to miss because everything seems to work at first. Keep an eye on memory retention to spot it.

If you remember only one rule, make it this: use let instead of var inside loops.
