Combining Random Forests with Transformers in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Random Forests and Transformers can work together in a single project.

Random Forests is ensembles of many decision trees whose predictions are combined. Transformers is neural network architectures that use attention to relate all parts of an input to each other.

Imagine a project that involves tabular predictions and language translation. Random Forests brings bagging, while Transformers brings self-attention.

A sensible plan is to start with scikit-learn for the Random Forests part and Hugging Face for the Transformers part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: very large forests being slow on one side, and limited context windows on the other.

Picture the whole as asking many different experts and taking the majority view, working alongside a reader who can look back at any word of a paragraph while reading each new word.

Two habits help: first, tune the number of trees only after the basics work; second, begin with small pretrained models.
