Understanding Semantic Versioning Through an Everyday Analogy

Abstract ideas are easier to hold onto when you attach them to something familiar. So picture the edition number on a textbook that tells you whether you must relearn anything.

In that picture, major, minor and patch numbers is the first rule you follow, breaking changes is the next step, and pre-release tags and version ranges keep everything running smoothly.

Every analogy breaks down eventually. Real Semantic Versioning is a numbering scheme where major, minor and patch numbers signal how much changed, and it involves details such as breaking changes in minor releases that no everyday picture captures.

Use the analogy to remember the shape of the idea, and use documentation for semver.org to learn the exact behavior. The same picture helps when you explain publishing libraries to a friend.
