Combining Interrupts with Assembly Language Basics in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Interrupts and Assembly Language Basics can work together in a single project.

Interrupts is signals that make a processor pause its work to handle an urgent event. Assembly Language Basics is low-level instructions that map closely to what a processor actually executes.

Imagine a project that involves keyboard and mouse input and understanding compiled code. Interrupts brings interrupt handlers, while Assembly Language Basics brings registers.

A sensible plan is to start with Linux /proc/interrupts for the Interrupts part and GDB for the Assembly Language Basics part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: race conditions with shared data on one side, and stack corruption on the other.

Picture the whole as a phone call interrupting your reading, working alongside the individual muscle movements behind a simple action.

Two habits help: first, keep interrupt handlers short and defer the heavy work; second, read the assembly of a tiny C function to see what the compiler does.
