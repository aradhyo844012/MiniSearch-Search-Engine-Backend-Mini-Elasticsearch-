Teaching Bash Scripting to a Teammate

Explaining Bash Scripting to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: a recipe card that the computer follows without help. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Bash Scripting is writing shell scripts that automate tasks by chaining commands, variables and control flow. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving automating deployments, naming variables and conditionals and loops as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain exit codes in Bash Scripting back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, unquoted variables, and suggest this habit: start scripts with set -euo pipefail and quote every variable.
