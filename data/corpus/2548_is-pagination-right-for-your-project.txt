Is Pagination Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Pagination, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Pagination is splitting large result sets into smaller pages that clients request one at a time.

It is a good fit when your work involves search results or activity feeds, and when your team understands offset pagination.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn cursor pagination, or when you cannot manage these risks: offsets getting slow on huge tables, unstable sort order, and missing total counts when users need them.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether page size and stable ordering are part of the answer. If they are, Pagination is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with admin tables and prefer cursors for large or fast-changing data.
