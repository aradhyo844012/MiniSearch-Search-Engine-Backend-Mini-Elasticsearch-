Case Study: Applying Autocomplete in a Small Team

Imagine a small student team building a project that depends on Autocomplete. Put simply, Autocomplete covers suggesting likely completions while a person types a query. Learning it well pays off across many areas of computing.

The team first spent a week learning prefixes and ranking suggestions. Early on they hit a familiar problem: slow suggestions. After fixing it, they used tries to check their work and adopted Autocomplete for search boxes. Looking back, they said the biggest lesson was to measure before optimizing.

Beginners often stumble on three things. First: slow suggestions. Second: offensive or irrelevant completions. Third: ignoring typos in the prefix.

In short, careful study of Autocomplete pays off. Spend time on prefixes and ranking suggestions, and the rest will follow.
