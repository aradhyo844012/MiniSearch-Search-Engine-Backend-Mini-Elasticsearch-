Teaching Endianness to a Teammate

Explaining Endianness to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: writing a date as day-month-year or as year-month-day. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Endianness is the order in which a computer stores the bytes of a multi-byte number. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving network protocols, naming big-endian and little-endian as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain byte order marks in Endianness back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, mixing byte orders, and suggest this habit: always specify byte order in file and protocol formats.
