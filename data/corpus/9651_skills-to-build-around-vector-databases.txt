Skills to Build Around Vector Databases

Knowing the definition of Vector Databases is only the start. These skills turn that knowledge into something useful.

Foundations: be comfortable with embeddings and approximate nearest neighbors, and able to explain them without notes.

Hands-on practice: spend real time with FAISS on a Vector Databases task, and try at least one alternative such as pgvector.

Judgment: learn to decide when Vector Databases fits a problem such as semantic search, and when something simpler is better.

Communication: practice describing it with an everyday picture, for example a map where similar ideas sit near each other.

Habits: measure recall on a small labeled test set, and write down what you learn after each project.
