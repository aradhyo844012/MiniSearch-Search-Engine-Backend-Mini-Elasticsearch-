Mistakes to Avoid with Probability Basics

Everyone learns Probability Basics partly through mistakes. Here are three worth avoiding.

Mistake one: confusing independent and exclusive events. It usually appears when someone skips events and jumps straight to implementation. The fix is to slow down and review the basics.

Mistake two: ignoring base rates. It tends to be noticed late, often during testing of randomized algorithms. A short checklist during review catches it early.

Mistake three: falling for the gambler's fallacy. It is easy to miss because everything seems to work at first. Keep an eye on expected value to spot it.

If you remember only one rule, make it this: simulate the problem in code to check your reasoning.
