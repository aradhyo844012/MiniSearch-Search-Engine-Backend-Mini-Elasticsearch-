Best Practices for Refactoring

The practices below come from patterns that tend to work well across many teams and projects. Put simply, Refactoring covers improving the structure of existing code without changing its behavior. Learning it well pays off across many areas of computing.

1. Practice small steps with small exercises until it feels natural.
2. Measure before you optimize. Use IDE refactoring tools or a simple timer to see where time is actually spent.
3. Keep notes on what worked. A short README explaining your decisions about Refactoring helps the next developer.
4. Automate repetitive steps so that results are easy to reproduce.
5. Start small by applying Refactoring to one case, such as reducing duplication, before rolling it out more widely.

Popular tools and libraries include IDE refactoring tools, pytest, and linters.

A solid grasp of Refactoring also makes Design Patterns easier to understand.

Readable code saves more time than clever code over the long run.

In short, careful study of Refactoring pays off. Spend time on small steps and tests as a safety net, and the rest will follow.
