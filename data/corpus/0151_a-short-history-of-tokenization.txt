A Short History of Tokenization

Ideas behind Tokenization developed gradually as engineers ran into practical limits and shared what they learned. Put simply, Tokenization covers splitting text into smaller units such as words that a program can count and compare. Learning it well pays off across many areas of computing.

Fast search comes from precomputing structures so that queries do very little work. Real query logs reveal what people actually look for, which is rarely what designers expect.

Four ideas sit at the heart of Tokenization: lowercasing, splitting on punctuation, stop words, and normalization. Each one builds on the one before it. Understanding splitting on punctuation first often makes stop words much easier to reason about.

Tokenization and Stop Words are closely related, so learning both gives a fuller picture of Search and Retrieval.

In short, careful study of Tokenization pays off. Spend time on lowercasing and splitting on punctuation, and the rest will follow.
