Skills to Build Around High Availability Design

Knowing the definition of High Availability Design is only the start. These skills turn that knowledge into something useful.

Foundations: be comfortable with redundancy and failover, and able to explain them without notes.

Hands-on practice: spend real time with load balancers on a High Availability Design task, and try at least one alternative such as Kubernetes.

Judgment: learn to decide when High Availability Design fits a problem such as online stores, and when something simpler is better.

Communication: practice describing it with an everyday picture, for example a bridge with several cables so one breaking does not matter.

Habits: list every component and ask what happens if it dies, and write down what you learn after each project.
