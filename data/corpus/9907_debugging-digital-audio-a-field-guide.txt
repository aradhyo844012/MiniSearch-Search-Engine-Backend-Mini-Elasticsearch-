Debugging Digital Audio: A Field Guide

When something goes wrong around Digital Audio, resist the urge to guess. Work through the problem in order.

Step 1: reproduce the problem with the smallest possible example.
Step 2: list the usual suspects: clipping from too high a level, aliasing at low sample rates, and mixing sample rates.
Step 3: revisit sampling rate and bit depth, since misunderstandings there cause many bugs.
Step 4: use Audacity or similar tools to look inside.
Step 5: write a test that fails because of the bug and passes after the fix.

Many bugs involving Digital Audio in work such as music production come down to the first suspect on the list. Check the simple causes before suspecting anything exotic.

Finally, keep recordings below the clipping level. It prevents a surprising number of repeat problems.
