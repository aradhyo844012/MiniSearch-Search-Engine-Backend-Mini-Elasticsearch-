Mistakes to Avoid with Computability and the Halting Problem

Everyone learns Computability and the Halting Problem partly through mistakes. Here are three worth avoiding.

Mistake one: assuming everything is solvable. It usually appears when someone skips Turing machines and jumps straight to implementation. The fix is to slow down and review the basics.

Mistake two: confusing hard with undecidable. It tends to be noticed late, often during testing of understanding the limits of tools. A short checklist during review catches it early.

Mistake three: hand-wavy proofs. It is easy to miss because everything seems to work at first. Keep an eye on reductions to spot it.

If you remember only one rule, make it this: learn the diagonal argument step by step.
