Common Mistakes When Working with Agile and Scrum

Almost everyone makes a few avoidable errors when first working with Agile and Scrum. Many students first meet Agile and Scrum in a classroom, but it matters just as much in production systems. The term refers to iterative ways of planning and delivering software in short cycles.

Watch out for these problems: skipping retrospectives; overloading sprints; and treating estimates as promises.

Consider the first problem: skipping retrospectives. A short test or a careful review comment usually catches it. The second problem is similar: overloading sprints. The third problem is this: treating estimates as promises. It tends to appear only once a project grows, so it is worth planning for early.

Read the official documentation for Trello; it often explains backlogs better than blog posts do. Measure before you optimize. Use Jira or a simple timer to see where time is actually spent.

Start small by applying Agile and Scrum to one case, such as student team projects, before rolling it out more widely. Keep an eye on this risk during code review: skipping retrospectives.

In short, careful study of Agile and Scrum pays off. Spend time on sprints and backlogs, and the rest will follow.
