Inside Agile and Scrum: How It Works

This article walks through what happens inside Agile and Scrum, step by step. Put simply, Agile and Scrum covers iterative ways of planning and delivering software in short cycles. Learning it well pays off across many areas of computing.

Teams succeed when they agree on a few simple practices and follow them consistently. Software spends most of its life being changed, not written for the first time.

Four ideas sit at the heart of Agile and Scrum: sprints, backlogs, stand-ups, and retrospectives. Each one builds on the one before it. A good way to remember sprints is to connect it with a concrete example from your own code.

First, sprints sets the stage for everything else. Next, backlogs builds on it. After that, stand-ups comes into play, and finally retrospectives ties the pieces together. Seeing the sequence once, with a tiny example, makes the whole topic easier to remember.

Small, frequent feedback loops catch problems while they are still cheap to fix.

A solid grasp of Agile and Scrum also makes Refactoring easier to understand.

With steady practice, Agile and Scrum will stop feeling abstract and become a tool you reach for naturally.
