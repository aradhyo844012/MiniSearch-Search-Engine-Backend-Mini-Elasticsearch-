A Short History of Decision Trees

Ideas behind Decision Trees developed gradually as engineers ran into practical limits and shared what they learned. When people talk about Decision Trees, they usually mean models that make predictions by asking a sequence of simple questions about the data. It appears in almost every serious software project, whether or not developers notice it.

A model is only as good as the data used to train and test it.

To understand Decision Trees, start with splits. Then move on to entropy and Gini impurity, and finally look at leaf nodes and pruning. A good way to remember splits is to connect it with a concrete example from your own code.

Results should always be checked on data the model has never seen. Reproducible experiments make it far easier to trust and improve a model.

A solid grasp of Decision Trees also makes Feature Engineering easier to understand.

None of this needs special hardware or expensive software. A laptop, curiosity and a few small experiments are enough to get comfortable with Decision Trees.
