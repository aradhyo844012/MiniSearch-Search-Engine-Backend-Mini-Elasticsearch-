Memory Hierarchy vs GPUs and Parallelism: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Memory Hierarchy or GPUs and Parallelism first. Both sit in the area of Computer Systems, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Memory Hierarchy is the layers of storage from registers to disks, trading speed for size, and it is closely tied to registers and cache. GPUs and Parallelism, by contrast, is processors with thousands of small cores that handle many similar calculations at once, and it is closely tied to threads and blocks and kernels.

Choose Memory Hierarchy first if you care about designing efficient programs or explaining slow I/O. Choose GPUs and Parallelism first if you care about training neural networks or rendering graphics.

Think of Memory Hierarchy as a kitchen with a spice rack, a pantry and a warehouse, and of GPUs and Parallelism as a stadium of workers each doing one small identical task. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Memory Hierarchy, avoid ignoring locality. With GPUs and Parallelism, avoid slow data transfers.

A good habit for Memory Hierarchy is to reduce how often your program goes down to the slower layers. A good habit for GPUs and Parallelism is to minimize copies between CPU and GPU memory.
