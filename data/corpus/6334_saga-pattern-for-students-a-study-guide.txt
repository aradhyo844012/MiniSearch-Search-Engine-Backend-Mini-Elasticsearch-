Saga Pattern for Students: A Study Guide

This study guide prepares you for exams, labs and interviews that cover Saga Pattern.

Learning goals:
1. Explain Saga Pattern in your own words.
2. Describe local transactions, compensating actions, orchestration, and choreography.
3. Give examples from order processing and travel bookings.
4. Name three problems to watch for.

Start with the definition: Saga Pattern is a way of managing long business operations across services using a sequence of local steps and compensating actions. Then draw booking a trip with separate hotel, flight and car reservations that can each be cancelled on paper and label the four terms from the goals above.

Self-test questions: Why is missing compensations a problem? How does compensating actions relate to orchestration? When would you choose Temporal or Camunda?

Study habit: make every step and its compensation idempotent.

When you feel ready, compare Saga Pattern with Consistent Hashing and write down three differences.
