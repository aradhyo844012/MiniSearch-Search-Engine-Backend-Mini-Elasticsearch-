Teaching Star Schema Modeling to a Teammate

Explaining Star Schema Modeling to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: a wheel with a hub of numbers and spokes of descriptions. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Star Schema Modeling is a way of organizing analytics data into a central fact table surrounded by dimension tables. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving business intelligence reports, naming fact tables and dimension tables as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain surrogate keys in Star Schema Modeling back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, mixing different grains, and suggest this habit: state the grain of the fact table before designing anything else.
