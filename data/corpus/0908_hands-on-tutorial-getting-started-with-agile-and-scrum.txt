Hands-On Tutorial: Getting Started with Agile and Scrum

This hands-on tutorial takes you from zero to a small working experiment with Agile and Scrum. The term Agile and Scrum refers to iterative ways of planning and delivering software in short cycles. This article explains the idea in plain language and points out where it fits in real projects.

Step 1: Install or open Jira and create a new empty project folder.
Step 2: Read about sprints and write down a definition in your own words.
Step 3: Build the smallest possible example that uses backlogs.
Step 4: Add one test or print statement that proves it works.
Step 5: Extend the example to handle a case such as student team projects.
Step 6: Break your example on purpose and see what happens. A good experiment: skipping retrospectives.

In practice you can start with Jira, then explore Trello or GitHub Projects as your needs grow.

If you are comfortable with Agile and Scrum, explore Unit Testing next. The two often appear in the same project.

Watch out for these problems: skipping retrospectives; overloading sprints; and treating estimates as promises.

None of this needs special hardware or expensive software. A laptop, curiosity and a few small experiments are enough to get comfortable with Agile and Scrum.
