Combining Topological Sorting with Backtracking in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Topological Sorting and Backtracking can work together in a single project.

Topological Sorting is an ordering of tasks in a directed graph so that every task comes after the tasks it depends on. Backtracking is a search technique that builds solutions step by step and undoes steps that lead to dead ends.

Imagine a project that involves build systems and solving Sudoku. Topological Sorting brings directed acyclic graphs, while Backtracking brings decision tree.

A sensible plan is to start with Python graphlib for the Topological Sorting part and Python recursion for the Backtracking part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: assuming the order is unique on one side, and missing pruning rules on the other.

Picture the whole as a recipe that must list every step after the steps it depends on, working alongside walking a maze and returning to the last fork whenever you hit a wall.

Two habits help: first, check for cycles first so you can report a clear error; second, prune as early as possible because the savings multiply.
