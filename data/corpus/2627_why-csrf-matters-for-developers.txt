Why CSRF Matters for Developers

Developers who understand CSRF make better decisions, because CSRF is an attack that tricks a logged-in user's browser into sending unwanted requests to a site.

Three situations show why it matters. First, protecting forms. Second, securing account settings. Third, hardening admin panels. In each case, a solid grasp of session cookies and anti-CSRF tokens separates a quick fix from a lasting solution.

Ignoring it has a cost. Teams run into these problems: using GET requests for state changes, skipping tokens on cookie-based APIs, and misconfigured SameSite settings.

Good tools exist, including Django CSRF middleware, Flask-WTF, and OWASP guides, so you rarely have to start from nothing.
