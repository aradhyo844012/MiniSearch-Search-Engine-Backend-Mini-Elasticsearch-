Domain-Driven Design in 10 Minutes

You have ten minutes, so here is the fast route through Domain-Driven Design.

Minutes 1 and 2, the definition: Domain-Driven Design is an approach to software that models the code around the real business domain.

Minutes 3 and 4, the vocabulary: learn ubiquitous language, bounded contexts, aggregates, and entities and value objects.

Minutes 5 and 6, the picture: imagine writing software in the same vocabulary the business speaks. That is the intuition.

Minutes 7 and 8, the use cases: skim through complex business systems, aligning developers with domain experts, and splitting large systems.

Minutes 9 and 10, the traps and the tools: watch for using it for simple CRUD apps and models that never match the business, then try event storming for a first experiment.

If you have a spare minute, remember to agree on shared terms with domain experts before drawing classes.
