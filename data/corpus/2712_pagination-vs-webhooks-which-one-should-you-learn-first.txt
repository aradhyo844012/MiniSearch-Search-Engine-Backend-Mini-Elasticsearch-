Pagination vs Webhooks: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Pagination or Webhooks first. Both sit in the area of Backend Engineering, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Pagination is splitting large result sets into smaller pages that clients request one at a time, and it is closely tied to offset pagination and cursor pagination. Webhooks, by contrast, is HTTP callbacks that let one service notify another when an event happens, and it is closely tied to event payloads and signature verification.

Choose Pagination first if you care about search results or activity feeds. Choose Webhooks first if you care about payment notifications or CI triggers.

Think of Pagination as reading a long book one chapter at a time with a bookmark, and of Webhooks as a doorbell that rings when a delivery arrives instead of you checking the porch. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Pagination, avoid offsets getting slow on huge tables. With Webhooks, avoid not verifying signatures.

A good habit for Pagination is to prefer cursors for large or fast-changing data. A good habit for Webhooks is to respond quickly and process the event in a background job.
