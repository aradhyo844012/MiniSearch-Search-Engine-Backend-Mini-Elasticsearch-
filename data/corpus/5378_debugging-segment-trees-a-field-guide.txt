Debugging Segment Trees: A Field Guide

When something goes wrong around Segment Trees, resist the urge to guess. Work through the problem in order.

Step 1: reproduce the problem with the smallest possible example.
Step 2: list the usual suspects: off-by-one errors in ranges, wrong tree size allocation, and forgetting to push lazy updates.
Step 3: revisit tree nodes covering ranges and range queries, since misunderstandings there cause many bugs.
Step 4: use C++ arrays or similar tools to look inside.
Step 5: write a test that fails because of the bug and passes after the fix.

Many bugs involving Segment Trees in work such as range sum queries come down to the first suspect on the list. Check the simple causes before suspecting anything exotic.

Finally, test your tree against a slow brute-force version on small inputs. It prevents a surprising number of repeat problems.
