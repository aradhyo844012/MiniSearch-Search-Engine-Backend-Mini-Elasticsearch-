Greedy Algorithms Quick Reference

A one-screen reminder for Greedy Algorithms. Bookmark it and return when the details fade.

What it is: algorithms that build a solution by always taking the locally best choice.
Key terms: greedy choice property, optimal substructure, exchange arguments, and local versus global optimum.
Typical uses: interval scheduling, Huffman coding, and making change with standard coins.
Risks: assuming greedy always works, missing counterexamples, and skipping the correctness proof.
Tools: pen and paper proofs, Python sorted, and unit tests.

Mental picture: picking the closest open parking spot at every junction and hoping it works out.

Habit to build: try to break your greedy idea with a small counterexample before trusting it.
