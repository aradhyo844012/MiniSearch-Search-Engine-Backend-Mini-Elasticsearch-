Chaos Engineering for Students: A Study Guide

This study guide prepares you for exams, labs and interviews that cover Chaos Engineering.

Learning goals:
1. Explain Chaos Engineering in your own words.
2. Describe steady state, hypotheses, blast radius, and fault injection.
3. Give examples from testing resilience and validating failover.
4. Name three problems to watch for.

Start with the definition: Chaos Engineering is deliberately causing failures in a system to learn how it responds. Then draw a fire drill where you secretly block one exit on paper and label the four terms from the goals above.

Self-test questions: Why is experimenting without safeguards a problem? How does hypotheses relate to blast radius? When would you choose Chaos Monkey or Gremlin?

Study habit: start with small experiments in a test environment.

When you feel ready, compare Chaos Engineering with Regression Testing and write down three differences.
