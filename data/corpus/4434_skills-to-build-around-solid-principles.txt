Skills to Build Around SOLID Principles

Knowing the definition of SOLID Principles is only the start. These skills turn that knowledge into something useful.

Foundations: be comfortable with single responsibility and the open-closed principle, and able to explain them without notes.

Hands-on practice: spend real time with UML diagrams on a SOLID Principles task, and try at least one alternative such as SonarQube.

Judgment: learn to decide when SOLID Principles fits a problem such as structuring classes, and when something simpler is better.

Communication: practice describing it with an everyday picture, for example house rules that keep a shared kitchen tidy.

Habits: apply them when a design starts to hurt, not before, and write down what you learn after each project.
