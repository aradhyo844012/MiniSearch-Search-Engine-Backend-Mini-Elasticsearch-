Mistakes to Avoid with Greedy Algorithms

Everyone learns Greedy Algorithms partly through mistakes. Here are three worth avoiding.

Mistake one: assuming greedy always works. It usually appears when someone skips greedy choice property and jumps straight to implementation. The fix is to slow down and review the basics.

Mistake two: missing counterexamples. It tends to be noticed late, often during testing of interval scheduling. A short checklist during review catches it early.

Mistake three: skipping the correctness proof. It is easy to miss because everything seems to work at first. Keep an eye on local versus global optimum to spot it.

If you remember only one rule, make it this: try to break your greedy idea with a small counterexample before trusting it.
