Combining Leader Election with CQRS in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Leader Election and CQRS can work together in a single project.

Leader Election is a process by which nodes in a cluster choose one node to coordinate the work. CQRS is a pattern that separates the models used to change data from the models used to read it.

Imagine a project that involves database primaries and high-read dashboards. Leader Election brings heartbeats, while CQRS brings commands.

A sensible plan is to start with ZooKeeper for the Leader Election part and Axon Framework for the CQRS part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: flapping leaders on one side, and read models drifting out of sync on the other.

Picture the whole as a classroom picking a monitor when the teacher leaves, working alongside a restaurant with one counter for orders and another for pickup.

Two habits help: first, tune heartbeat intervals against real network delays; second, start with a single model and split only when reads and writes truly diverge.
