Combining Encryption at Rest with Threat Modeling in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Encryption at Rest and Threat Modeling can work together in a single project.

Encryption at Rest is protecting stored data by encrypting it on disk or in a database. Threat Modeling is a structured way of identifying what could go wrong in a system and how to prevent it.

Imagine a project that involves protecting backups and designing new features. Encryption at Rest brings symmetric keys, while Threat Modeling brings assets.

A sensible plan is to start with LUKS for the Encryption at Rest part and STRIDE for the Threat Modeling part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: no key rotation on one side, and ignoring data flows on the other.

Picture the whole as a locked safe inside a locked room, working alongside walking around a house asking where a burglar might get in.

Two habits help: first, keep keys in a separate service from the data; second, draw the data flow diagram first.
