Hashing vs Encryption vs Single Sign-On: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Hashing vs Encryption or Single Sign-On first. Both sit in the area of Application Security, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Hashing vs Encryption is the difference between one-way digests and reversible protection of data, and it is closely tied to one-way functions and keys. Single Sign-On, by contrast, is letting users log in once and access several applications, and it is closely tied to identity provider and SAML.

Choose Hashing vs Encryption first if you care about storing passwords or verifying file integrity. Choose Single Sign-On first if you care about company intranets or reducing password fatigue.

Think of Hashing vs Encryption as a paper shredder compared with a locked suitcase, and of Single Sign-On as a festival wristband that opens every gate. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Hashing vs Encryption, avoid encrypting passwords instead of hashing them. With Single Sign-On, avoid a single point of failure.

A good habit for Hashing vs Encryption is to ask whether you will ever need the original value back. A good habit for Single Sign-On is to plan for identity provider outages.
