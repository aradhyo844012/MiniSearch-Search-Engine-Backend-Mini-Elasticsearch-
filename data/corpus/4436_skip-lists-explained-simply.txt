Skip Lists Explained Simply

Here is Skip Lists without the jargon: it is layered linked lists that allow fast searching by skipping over many elements.

Think of Skip Lists as an express train line layered over a local train line. Keep that picture in mind as you read on.

The vocabulary has four main pieces: multiple levels, random level assignment, forward pointers, and expected logarithmic time. You do not need to master them all at once. Start with multiple levels, then add random level assignment.

People reach for Skip Lists when they work on ordered in-memory indexes, sorted sets in caches, and concurrent ordered maps.

One practical tip is to draw several levels by hand to see how a search moves down.

Once Skip Lists feels comfortable, read about Segment Trees, which lives in the same neighborhood of ideas.
