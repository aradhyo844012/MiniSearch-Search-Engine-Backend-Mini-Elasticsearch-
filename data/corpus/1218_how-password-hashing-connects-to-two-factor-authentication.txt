How Password Hashing Connects to Two-Factor Authentication

Password Hashing and Two-Factor Authentication are closely connected parts of Security. Seeing how they fit together helps you learn both faster.

A project involving user login systems may also involve protecting email accounts. In such a project, salts and one-time codes often influence each other.

Start with Password Hashing: the term refers to storing passwords as one-way hashes so the originals are never kept. Then look at Two-Factor Authentication, which refers to requiring a second proof of identity in addition to a password.

A useful exercise is to take one example, such as protecting data after a breach, and explain how authenticator apps would change the design.

Common problems are shared too. For Password Hashing, watch for this: reusing salts. For Two-Factor Authentication, watch for this: no recovery plan. Never trust input that comes from outside your own code.

Spend a few hours on each topic, then combine them in one small project using bcrypt or Google Authenticator.
