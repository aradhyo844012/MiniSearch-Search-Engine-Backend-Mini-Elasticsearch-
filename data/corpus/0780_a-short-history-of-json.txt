A Short History of JSON

Ideas behind JSON developed gradually as engineers ran into practical limits and shared what they learned. Put simply, JSON covers a lightweight text format for exchanging structured data between programs. Learning it well pays off across many areas of computing.

Clear contracts between services make systems easier to change and test. Good APIs are boring, predictable and well documented.

To understand JSON, start with objects. Then move on to arrays, and finally look at strings and numbers and serialization. A good way to remember objects is to connect it with a concrete example from your own code.

If you are comfortable with JSON, explore JWT next. The two often appear in the same project.

Automate repetitive steps so that results are easy to reproduce. Practice objects with small exercises until it feels natural. Ask a teammate to explain strings and numbers back to you; teaching is a quick way to find gaps.

Keep experimenting, keep notes, and revisit JSON whenever a new project gives you a reason.
