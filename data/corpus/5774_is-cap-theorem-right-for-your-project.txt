Is CAP Theorem Right for Your Project?

Before adopting CAP Theorem, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that CAP Theorem is a result stating that a distributed system cannot guarantee consistency, availability and partition tolerance all at once.

It is a good fit when your work involves choosing database types or designing multi-region services, and when your team understands consistency.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn availability, or when you cannot manage these risks: treating it as a three-way menu, ignoring latency trade-offs in normal operation, and assuming partitions never happen.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether partition tolerance and trade-offs are part of the answer. If they are, CAP Theorem is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with explaining system behavior in interviews and ask what your users prefer during a failure: stale answers or errors.
