The Trade-offs of Large Language Models

Every design choice has a cost, and Large Language Models is no exception. Many students first meet Large Language Models in a classroom, but it matters just as much in production systems. The term refers to very large neural networks trained on text that can generate and analyze language.

On the positive side, Large Language Models is well suited to drafting text and answering questions. On the other hand, it is not free. Keep these risks in mind: trusting outputs without checking; vague prompts; sending sensitive data carelessly. Whether the trade is worth it depends on how much tokens and attention matter to your project.

Three issues come up again and again: trusting outputs without checking; vague prompts; and sending sensitive data carelessly. Knowing them in advance saves hours of debugging.

Read the official documentation for API playgrounds; it often explains attention better than blog posts do. Automate repetitive steps so that results are easy to reproduce.

If you are comfortable with Large Language Models, explore Overfitting next. The two often appear in the same project.

In short, careful study of Large Language Models pays off. Spend time on tokens and attention, and the rest will follow.
