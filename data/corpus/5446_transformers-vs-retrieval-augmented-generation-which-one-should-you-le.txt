Transformers vs Retrieval-Augmented Generation: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Transformers or Retrieval-Augmented Generation first. Both sit in the area of AI and Machine Learning, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Transformers is neural network architectures that use attention to relate all parts of an input to each other, and it is closely tied to self-attention and positional encoding. Retrieval-Augmented Generation, by contrast, is a technique that gives a language model relevant documents to read before it answers, and it is closely tied to retrieval and chunking.

Choose Transformers first if you care about language translation or text generation. Choose Retrieval-Augmented Generation first if you care about company knowledge assistants or question answering over documents.

Think of Transformers as a reader who can look back at any word of a paragraph while reading each new word, and of Retrieval-Augmented Generation as an open-book exam where the student first looks up the right pages. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Transformers, avoid huge compute requirements. With Retrieval-Augmented Generation, avoid poor chunking.

A good habit for Transformers is to begin with small pretrained models. A good habit for Retrieval-Augmented Generation is to evaluate retrieval quality separately from answer quality.
