Debugging Linear Algebra for Programmers: A Field Guide

When something goes wrong around Linear Algebra for Programmers, resist the urge to guess. Work through the problem in order.

Step 1: reproduce the problem with the smallest possible example.
Step 2: list the usual suspects: mixing row and column conventions, shape mismatches, and forgetting that matrix multiplication is not commutative.
Step 3: revisit vectors and matrices, since misunderstandings there cause many bugs.
Step 4: use NumPy or similar tools to look inside.
Step 5: write a test that fails because of the bug and passes after the fix.

Many bugs involving Linear Algebra for Programmers in work such as 3D graphics come down to the first suspect on the list. Check the simple causes before suspecting anything exotic.

Finally, check shapes before multiplying. It prevents a surprising number of repeat problems.
