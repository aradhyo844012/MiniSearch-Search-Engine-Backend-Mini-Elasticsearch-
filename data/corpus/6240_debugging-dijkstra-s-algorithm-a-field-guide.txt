Debugging Dijkstra's Algorithm: A Field Guide

When something goes wrong around Dijkstra's Algorithm, resist the urge to guess. Work through the problem in order.

Step 1: reproduce the problem with the smallest possible example.
Step 2: list the usual suspects: using it with negative edge weights, skipping the priority queue and getting slow code, and not stopping early when only one target is needed.
Step 3: revisit priority queue and distance table, since misunderstandings there cause many bugs.
Step 4: use Python heapq or similar tools to look inside.
Step 5: write a test that fails because of the bug and passes after the fix.

Many bugs involving Dijkstra's Algorithm in work such as route planning come down to the first suspect on the list. Check the simple causes before suspecting anything exotic.

Finally, stop as soon as the destination is popped from the queue. It prevents a surprising number of repeat problems.
