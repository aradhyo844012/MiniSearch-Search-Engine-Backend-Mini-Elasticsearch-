Teaching Linear Algebra for Programmers to a Teammate

Explaining Linear Algebra for Programmers to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: a set of arrows and stretching machines acting on space. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Linear Algebra for Programmers is the math of vectors and matrices used in graphics and machine learning. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving 3D graphics, naming vectors and matrices as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain dot products in Linear Algebra for Programmers back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, mixing row and column conventions, and suggest this habit: check shapes before multiplying.
