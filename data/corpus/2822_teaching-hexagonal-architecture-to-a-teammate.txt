Teaching Hexagonal Architecture to a Teammate

Explaining Hexagonal Architecture to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: a power strip that lets any plug connect to the same core. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Hexagonal Architecture is a design that keeps core logic separate from outside systems through ports and adapters. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving testable business logic, naming ports and adapters as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain the core domain in Hexagonal Architecture back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, too many layers for small apps, and suggest this habit: keep framework imports out of the core domain.
