Combining Encryption at Rest with Hashing vs Encryption in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Encryption at Rest and Hashing vs Encryption can work together in a single project.

Encryption at Rest is protecting stored data by encrypting it on disk or in a database. Hashing vs Encryption is the difference between one-way digests and reversible protection of data.

Imagine a project that involves protecting backups and storing passwords. Encryption at Rest brings symmetric keys, while Hashing vs Encryption brings one-way functions.

A sensible plan is to start with LUKS for the Encryption at Rest part and SHA-256 for the Hashing vs Encryption part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: no key rotation on one side, and using broken hash functions on the other.

Picture the whole as a locked safe inside a locked room, working alongside a paper shredder compared with a locked suitcase.

Two habits help: first, keep keys in a separate service from the data; second, ask whether you will ever need the original value back.
