Is Form Validation Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Form Validation, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Form Validation is checking user input for correctness before it is accepted.

It is a good fit when your work involves sign-up forms or payment pages, and when your team understands client-side checks.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn server-side checks, or when you cannot manage these risks: validating only on the client, vague error messages, and rejecting valid but unusual input.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether error messages and constraint attributes are part of the answer. If they are, Form Validation is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with search filters and validate on the server even when the browser checks too.
