Combining Random Forests with Data Augmentation in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Random Forests and Data Augmentation can work together in a single project.

Random Forests is ensembles of many decision trees whose predictions are combined. Data Augmentation is creating modified copies of training data to help models generalize.

Imagine a project that involves tabular predictions and image models. Random Forests brings bagging, while Data Augmentation brings transformations.

A sensible plan is to start with scikit-learn for the Random Forests part and Albumentations for the Data Augmentation part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: very large forests being slow on one side, and augmenting the test set on the other.

Picture the whole as asking many different experts and taking the majority view, working alongside practicing a sport in different weather and on different fields.

Two habits help: first, tune the number of trees only after the basics work; second, augment only the training data.
