Understanding Client-Side Routing Through an Everyday Analogy

Abstract ideas are easier to hold onto when you attach them to something familiar. So picture swapping the picture in a frame without replacing the frame.

In that picture, history API is the first rule you follow, route matching is the next step, and nested routes and route guards keep everything running smoothly.

Every analogy breaks down eventually. Real Client-Side Routing is changing the visible page in the browser without a full page reload, and it involves details such as broken deep links that no everyday picture captures.

Use the analogy to remember the shape of the idea, and use documentation for React Router to learn the exact behavior. The same picture helps when you explain single-page apps to a friend.
