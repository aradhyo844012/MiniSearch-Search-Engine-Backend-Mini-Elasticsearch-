Teaching Skip Lists to a Teammate

Explaining Skip Lists to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: an express train line layered over a local train line. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Skip Lists is layered linked lists that allow fast searching by skipping over many elements. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving ordered in-memory indexes, naming multiple levels and random level assignment as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain forward pointers in Skip Lists back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, poor random number choices, and suggest this habit: draw several levels by hand to see how a search moves down.
