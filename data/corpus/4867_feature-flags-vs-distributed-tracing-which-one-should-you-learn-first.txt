Feature Flags vs Distributed Tracing: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Feature Flags or Distributed Tracing first. Both sit in the area of DevOps and Cloud, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Feature Flags is switches in configuration that turn features on or off without redeploying code, and it is closely tied to flag evaluation and targeting rules. Distributed Tracing, by contrast, is following a single request as it passes through many services, and it is closely tied to spans and trace IDs.

Choose Feature Flags first if you care about dark launches or A/B tests. Choose Distributed Tracing first if you care about finding slow services or understanding dependencies.

Think of Feature Flags as light switches wired to every room of a house, and of Distributed Tracing as a parcel tracking page that shows every stop on the route. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Feature Flags, avoid never removing old flags. With Distributed Tracing, avoid missing context propagation.

A good habit for Feature Flags is to give every flag an owner and a removal date. A good habit for Distributed Tracing is to propagate trace IDs through every outgoing call.
