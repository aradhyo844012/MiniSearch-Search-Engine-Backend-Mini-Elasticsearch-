A Short History of Deadlocks

Ideas behind Deadlocks developed gradually as engineers ran into practical limits and shared what they learned. The term Deadlocks refers to situations where two or more tasks wait for each other forever. This article explains the idea in plain language and points out where it fits in real projects.

Understanding what the kernel does explains many surprising performance problems.

The vocabulary of Deadlocks is small. Learn mutual exclusion, hold and wait, circular wait, and lock ordering, and most documentation will start to make sense. Sketching lock ordering on paper before coding helps many learners see how the pieces fit.
