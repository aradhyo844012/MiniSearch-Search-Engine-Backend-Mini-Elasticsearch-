Is Hexagonal Architecture Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Hexagonal Architecture, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Hexagonal Architecture is a design that keeps core logic separate from outside systems through ports and adapters.

It is a good fit when your work involves testable business logic or swapping databases, and when your team understands ports.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn adapters, or when you cannot manage these risks: too many layers for small apps, leaking framework types into the core, and confusing ports with adapters.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether the core domain and dependency direction are part of the answer. If they are, Hexagonal Architecture is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with supporting several interfaces and keep framework imports out of the core domain.
