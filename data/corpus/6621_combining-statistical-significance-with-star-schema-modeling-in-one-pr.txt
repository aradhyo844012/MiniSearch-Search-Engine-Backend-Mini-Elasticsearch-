Combining Statistical Significance with Star Schema Modeling in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Statistical Significance and Star Schema Modeling can work together in a single project.

Statistical Significance is a measure of how unlikely a result would be if there were no real effect. Star Schema Modeling is a way of organizing analytics data into a central fact table surrounded by dimension tables.

Imagine a project that involves judging experiment results and business intelligence reports. Statistical Significance brings p-values, while Star Schema Modeling brings fact tables.

A sensible plan is to start with SciPy for the Statistical Significance part and dbt for the Star Schema Modeling part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: p-hacking on one side, and over-normalized dimensions on the other.

Picture the whole as asking how surprised you should be if a coin were actually fair, working alongside a wheel with a hub of numbers and spokes of descriptions.

Two habits help: first, always report the effect size alongside the p-value; second, state the grain of the fact table before designing anything else.
