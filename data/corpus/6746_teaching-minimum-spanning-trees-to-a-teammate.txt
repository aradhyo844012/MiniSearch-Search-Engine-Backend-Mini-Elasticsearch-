Teaching Minimum Spanning Trees to a Teammate

Explaining Minimum Spanning Trees to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: laying the cheapest set of roads that still links every town. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Minimum Spanning Trees is a set of edges that connects every node in a graph with the smallest possible total weight. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving designing cable networks, naming weighted graphs and Kruskal's algorithm as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain Prim's algorithm in Minimum Spanning Trees back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, using it on directed graphs, and suggest this habit: sort the edges once and reuse the order when testing variations.
