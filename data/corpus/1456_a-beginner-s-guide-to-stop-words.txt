A Beginner's Guide to Stop Words

This guide is written for readers who are new to Stop Words and want a clear starting point. When people talk about Stop Words, they usually mean very common words such as the and of that are often ignored by search systems. It appears in almost every serious software project, whether or not developers notice it.

Fast search comes from precomputing structures so that queries do very little work. Search quality depends on how text is prepared before it is ever indexed.

Four ideas sit at the heart of Stop Words: frequency lists, index size, query relevance, and language-specific lists. Each one builds on the one before it. Understanding index size first often makes query relevance much easier to reason about.

People use Stop Words for shrinking indexes, for speeding up queries, and for reducing noise in ranking.

Popular tools and libraries include NLTK stopwords, scikit-learn, and custom word lists.
