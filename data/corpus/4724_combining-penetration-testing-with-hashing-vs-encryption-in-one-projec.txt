Combining Penetration Testing with Hashing vs Encryption in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Penetration Testing and Hashing vs Encryption can work together in a single project.

Penetration Testing is authorized attempts to find and exploit weaknesses in a system before real attackers do. Hashing vs Encryption is the difference between one-way digests and reversible protection of data.

Imagine a project that involves pre-launch security checks and storing passwords. Penetration Testing brings scoping, while Hashing vs Encryption brings one-way functions.

A sensible plan is to start with OWASP ZAP for the Penetration Testing part and SHA-256 for the Hashing vs Encryption part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: ignoring the findings afterward on one side, and using broken hash functions on the other.

Picture the whole as hiring a locksmith to test your doors, working alongside a paper shredder compared with a locked suitcase.

Two habits help: first, get written authorization before any test; second, ask whether you will ever need the original value back.
