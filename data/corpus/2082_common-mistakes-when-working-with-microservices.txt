Common Mistakes When Working with Microservices

Almost everyone makes a few avoidable errors when first working with Microservices. Many students first meet Microservices in a classroom, but it matters just as much in production systems. The term refers to an architecture that splits an application into small services that can be deployed independently.

Watch out for these problems: splitting too early; chatty services; and hard debugging across services.

Consider the first problem: splitting too early. A short test or a careful review comment usually catches it. The second problem is similar: chatty services. The third problem is this: hard debugging across services. It tends to appear only once a project grows, so it is worth planning for early.

Measure before you optimize. Use Docker or a simple timer to see where time is actually spent. Practice service boundaries with small exercises until it feels natural.

Keep experimenting, keep notes, and revisit Microservices whenever a new project gives you a reason.
