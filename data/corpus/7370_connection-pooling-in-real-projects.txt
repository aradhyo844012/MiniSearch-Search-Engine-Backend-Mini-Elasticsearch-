Connection Pooling in Real Projects

Reading about Connection Pooling is useful, but the ideas make more sense in context. These three scenarios show how it appears in real projects.

Scenario one: web backends. Here, pool size drives the main design choices, and the team needs to guard against pools larger than the database allows.

Scenario two: serverless functions that call databases. This time idle connections matters most, and tools such as PgBouncer help keep things manageable.

Scenario three: high-traffic APIs. The key idea is timeouts, and the biggest risk is leaked connections.

Across all three scenarios, Connection Pooling stays the same idea: reusing a limited set of open database connections instead of opening a new one for every request. Only the details change.
