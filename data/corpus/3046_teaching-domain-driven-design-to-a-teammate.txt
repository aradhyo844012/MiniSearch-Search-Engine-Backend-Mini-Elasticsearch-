Teaching Domain-Driven Design to a Teammate

Explaining Domain-Driven Design to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: writing software in the same vocabulary the business speaks. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Domain-Driven Design is an approach to software that models the code around the real business domain. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving complex business systems, naming ubiquitous language and bounded contexts as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain aggregates in Domain-Driven Design back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, using it for simple CRUD apps, and suggest this habit: agree on shared terms with domain experts before drawing classes.
