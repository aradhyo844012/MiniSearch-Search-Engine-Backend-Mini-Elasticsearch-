Event-Driven Architecture in 10 Minutes

You have ten minutes, so here is the fast route through Event-Driven Architecture.

Minutes 1 and 2, the definition: Event-Driven Architecture is a style in which components communicate by publishing events and reacting to them.

Minutes 3 and 4, the vocabulary: learn producers and consumers, event brokers, loose coupling, and asynchronous handling.

Minutes 5 and 6, the picture: imagine a notice board where people post news and others react when they see it. That is the intuition.

Minutes 7 and 8, the use cases: skim through order pipelines, real-time notifications, and integrating many services.

Minutes 9 and 10, the traps and the tools: watch for hard-to-trace flows and duplicate events, then try Apache Kafka for a first experiment.

If you have a spare minute, remember to document every event name and schema.
