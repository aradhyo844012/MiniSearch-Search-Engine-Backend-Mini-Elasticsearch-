The Importance of Threads and Concurrency in Modern Software

It is easy to underestimate Threads and Concurrency until something goes wrong without it. Many students first meet Threads and Concurrency in a classroom, but it matters just as much in production systems. The term refers to ways of making progress on several tasks at once within a program.

Logs and system monitors are the first place to look when a machine misbehaves.

Understanding what the kernel does explains many surprising performance problems.

Typical applications include handling many requests, keeping interfaces responsive, and parallel computation. Small projects and large systems both benefit.

Watch out for these problems: forgetting to protect shared data; creating too many threads; and mixing up concurrency and parallelism.

If you are comfortable with Threads and Concurrency, explore Containers next. The two often appear in the same project.

In short, careful study of Threads and Concurrency pays off. Spend time on threads and locks, and the rest will follow.
