Case Study: Applying Site Reliability in a Small Team

Imagine a small student team building a project that depends on Site Reliability. If you build software for long enough, you will run into Site Reliability. The term refers to the discipline of keeping services dependable using engineering practices.

The team first spent a week learning service level objectives and error budgets. Early on they hit a familiar problem: alerting on everything. After fixing it, they used PagerDuty to check their work and adopted Site Reliability for running production services. Looking back, they said the biggest lesson was to write tests earlier.

Beginners often stumble on three things. First: alerting on everything. Second: blaming people instead of systems. Third: no written postmortems.

None of this needs special hardware or expensive software. A laptop, curiosity and a few small experiments are enough to get comfortable with Site Reliability.
