Skills to Build Around Support Vector Machines

Knowing the definition of Support Vector Machines is only the start. These skills turn that knowledge into something useful.

Foundations: be comfortable with margin and support vectors, and able to explain them without notes.

Hands-on practice: spend real time with scikit-learn on a Support Vector Machines task, and try at least one alternative such as LIBSVM.

Judgment: learn to decide when Support Vector Machines fits a problem such as text classification, and when something simpler is better.

Communication: practice describing it with an everyday picture, for example drawing the widest road between two neighborhoods.

Habits: scale features and try a linear kernel first, and write down what you learn after each project.
