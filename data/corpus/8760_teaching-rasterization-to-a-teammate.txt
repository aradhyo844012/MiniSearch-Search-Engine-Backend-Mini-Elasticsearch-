Teaching Rasterization to a Teammate

Explaining Rasterization to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: coloring squares on graph paper to match a drawn outline. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Rasterization is converting shapes described by geometry into pixels on the screen. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving real-time games, naming triangles and pixel coverage as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain the depth buffer in Rasterization back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, aliasing along edges, and suggest this habit: draw simple shapes first to understand the pipeline.
