Skills to Build Around Domain-Driven Design

Knowing the definition of Domain-Driven Design is only the start. These skills turn that knowledge into something useful.

Foundations: be comfortable with ubiquitous language and bounded contexts, and able to explain them without notes.

Hands-on practice: spend real time with event storming on a Domain-Driven Design task, and try at least one alternative such as UML.

Judgment: learn to decide when Domain-Driven Design fits a problem such as complex business systems, and when something simpler is better.

Communication: practice describing it with an everyday picture, for example writing software in the same vocabulary the business speaks.

Habits: agree on shared terms with domain experts before drawing classes, and write down what you learn after each project.
