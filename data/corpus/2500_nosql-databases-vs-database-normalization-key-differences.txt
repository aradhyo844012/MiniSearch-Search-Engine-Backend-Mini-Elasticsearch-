NoSQL Databases vs Database Normalization: Key Differences

NoSQL Databases and Database Normalization both belong to the area of Databases, but they solve different problems. This article compares them side by side so you can choose with confidence.

The term NoSQL Databases refers to a broad family of databases that do not use the traditional relational table model. Its core ideas are document stores, key-value stores, and column families.

The term Database Normalization refers to the practice of organizing tables to reduce duplicated data and update anomalies. Its core ideas are first normal form, second normal form, and third normal form.

The main difference is focus. NoSQL Databases puts the emphasis on document stores and key-value stores, while Database Normalization puts the emphasis on first normal form and second normal form.

Reach for NoSQL Databases when your task involves flexible schemas or high write throughput. Reach for Database Normalization when your task involves designing clean schemas or avoiding update anomalies.

Both have risks. With NoSQL Databases, watch for this: assuming no schema means no design. With Database Normalization, watch for this: over-normalizing reporting tables.

In practice you do not always have to choose. Many projects use NoSQL Databases alongside Database Normalization, with tools such as MongoDB and ER diagrams. Data that must survive restarts and crashes belongs in a database rather than in memory.
