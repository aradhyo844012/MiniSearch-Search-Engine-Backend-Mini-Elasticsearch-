Combining Domain-Driven Design with Test-Driven Development in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Domain-Driven Design and Test-Driven Development can work together in a single project.

Domain-Driven Design is an approach to software that models the code around the real business domain. Test-Driven Development is a workflow where you write a failing test first and then write code to make it pass.

Imagine a project that involves complex business systems and designing small modules. Domain-Driven Design brings ubiquitous language, while Test-Driven Development brings red, green, refactor.

A sensible plan is to start with event storming for the Domain-Driven Design part and pytest for the Test-Driven Development part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: models that never match the business on one side, and skipping the refactor step on the other.

Picture the whole as writing software in the same vocabulary the business speaks, working alongside writing the exam questions before teaching the lesson.

Two habits help: first, agree on shared terms with domain experts before drawing classes; second, keep each red-green cycle to a few minutes.
