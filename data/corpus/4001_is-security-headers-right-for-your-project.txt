Is Security Headers Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Security Headers, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Security Headers is HTTP response headers that tell browsers to enforce extra protections.

It is a good fit when your work involves hardening websites or reducing clickjacking, and when your team understands Strict-Transport-Security.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn X-Content-Type-Options, or when you cannot manage these risks: setting headers on only some routes, breaking embeds, and copying configurations without understanding them.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether frame protections and referrer policy are part of the answer. If they are, Security Headers is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with enforcing HTTPS and test the headers on every environment.
