A Short History of Load Balancing

Ideas behind Load Balancing developed gradually as engineers ran into practical limits and shared what they learned. Many students first meet Load Balancing in a classroom, but it matters just as much in production systems. The term refers to spreading incoming requests across several servers so no single server is overwhelmed.

Every web request crosses several layers of protocols before it reaches an application. Simple command-line tools can reveal a lot about what is happening on the wire.

Four ideas sit at the heart of Load Balancing: round robin, health checks, sticky sessions, and layer 4 and layer 7. Each one builds on the one before it. A good way to remember round robin is to connect it with a concrete example from your own code.

Load Balancing and WebSockets are closely related, so learning both gives a fuller picture of Networking.

With steady practice, Load Balancing will stop feeling abstract and become a tool you reach for naturally.
