Debugging Assembly Language Basics: A Field Guide

When something goes wrong around Assembly Language Basics, resist the urge to guess. Work through the problem in order.

Step 1: reproduce the problem with the smallest possible example.
Step 2: list the usual suspects: wrong calling conventions, stack corruption, and platform differences.
Step 3: revisit registers and instructions, since misunderstandings there cause many bugs.
Step 4: use GDB or similar tools to look inside.
Step 5: write a test that fails because of the bug and passes after the fix.

Many bugs involving Assembly Language Basics in work such as understanding compiled code come down to the first suspect on the list. Check the simple causes before suspecting anything exotic.

Finally, read the assembly of a tiny C function to see what the compiler does. It prevents a surprising number of repeat problems.
