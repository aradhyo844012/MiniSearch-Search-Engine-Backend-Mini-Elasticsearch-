Combining Hashing vs Encryption with Single Sign-On in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Hashing vs Encryption and Single Sign-On can work together in a single project.

Hashing vs Encryption is the difference between one-way digests and reversible protection of data. Single Sign-On is letting users log in once and access several applications.

Imagine a project that involves storing passwords and company intranets. Hashing vs Encryption brings one-way functions, while Single Sign-On brings identity provider.

A sensible plan is to start with SHA-256 for the Hashing vs Encryption part and Okta for the Single Sign-On part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: using broken hash functions on one side, and misconfigured redirects on the other.

Picture the whole as a paper shredder compared with a locked suitcase, working alongside a festival wristband that opens every gate.

Two habits help: first, ask whether you will ever need the original value back; second, plan for identity provider outages.
