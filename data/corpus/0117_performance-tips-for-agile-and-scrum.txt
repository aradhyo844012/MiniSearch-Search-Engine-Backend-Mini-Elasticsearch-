Performance Tips for Agile and Scrum

Fast software rarely happens by accident, and Agile and Scrum offers several places to gain speed. The term Agile and Scrum refers to iterative ways of planning and delivering software in short cycles. This article explains the idea in plain language and points out where it fits in real projects.

Start by measuring. Use Jira or a simple timer to find out where time is really spent, rather than guessing. Pay special attention to sprints and backlogs, because they usually decide how well things scale. For workloads such as student team projects, even small improvements add up quickly.

The vocabulary of Agile and Scrum is small. Learn sprints, backlogs, stand-ups, and retrospectives, and most documentation will start to make sense. Sketching retrospectives on paper before coding helps many learners see how the pieces fit.

Write a few tests that show how Agile and Scrum should behave. Tests double as documentation for your teammates. Keep an eye on this risk during code review: skipping retrospectives.

Measure before you optimize. Use Jira or a simple timer to see where time is actually spent. Ask a teammate to explain stand-ups back to you; teaching is a quick way to find gaps.

Watch out for these problems: skipping retrospectives; overloading sprints; and treating estimates as promises.

With steady practice, Agile and Scrum will stop feeling abstract and become a tool you reach for naturally.
