Feature Flags vs Log Aggregation: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Feature Flags or Log Aggregation first. Both sit in the area of DevOps and Cloud, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Feature Flags is switches in configuration that turn features on or off without redeploying code, and it is closely tied to flag evaluation and targeting rules. Log Aggregation, by contrast, is collecting logs from many machines into one searchable place, and it is closely tied to shippers and indexing.

Choose Feature Flags first if you care about dark launches or A/B tests. Choose Log Aggregation first if you care about troubleshooting across services or security investigations.

Think of Feature Flags as light switches wired to every room of a house, and of Log Aggregation as gathering notes from every desk into a single searchable binder. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Feature Flags, avoid never removing old flags. With Log Aggregation, avoid unbounded storage growth.

A good habit for Feature Flags is to give every flag an owner and a removal date. A good habit for Log Aggregation is to use structured JSON logs so that fields are searchable.
