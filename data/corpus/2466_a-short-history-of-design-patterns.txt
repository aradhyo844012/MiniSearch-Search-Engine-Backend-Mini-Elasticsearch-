A Short History of Design Patterns

Ideas behind Design Patterns developed gradually as engineers ran into practical limits and shared what they learned. Put simply, Design Patterns covers reusable solutions to common problems in software design. Learning it well pays off across many areas of computing.

Software spends most of its life being changed, not written for the first time.

Four ideas sit at the heart of Design Patterns: singleton, factory, observer, and strategy. Each one builds on the one before it. Sketching strategy on paper before coding helps many learners see how the pieces fit.

If you are comfortable with Design Patterns, explore Refactoring next. The two often appear in the same project.

None of this needs special hardware or expensive software. A laptop, curiosity and a few small experiments are enough to get comfortable with Design Patterns.
