Reading Documentation Explained Simply

Here is Reading Documentation without the jargon: it is the skill of finding accurate answers in official guides and references.

Think of Reading Documentation as reading the manual before assembling the furniture. Keep that picture in mind as you read on.

The vocabulary has four main pieces: search, tutorials versus references, examples, and version numbers. You do not need to master them all at once. Start with search, then add tutorials versus references.

You will meet Reading Documentation in work such as learning new libraries, solving errors, and verifying answers from other sources.

One practical tip is to check that the documentation version matches your installed version.

Once Reading Documentation feels comfortable, read about Competitive Programming, which lives in the same neighborhood of ideas.
