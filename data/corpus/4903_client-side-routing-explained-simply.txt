Client-Side Routing Explained Simply

If Client-Side Routing sounds intimidating, relax. It is changing the visible page in the browser without a full page reload, and the idea is simpler than the name suggests.

Think of Client-Side Routing as swapping the picture in a frame without replacing the frame. Keep that picture in mind as you read on.

The vocabulary has four main pieces: history API, route matching, nested routes, and route guards. You do not need to master them all at once. Start with history API, then add route matching.

People reach for Client-Side Routing when they work on single-page apps, dashboards, and multi-step flows.

One practical tip is to test refreshing the page on every route.

Once Client-Side Routing feels comfortable, read about Service Workers, which lives in the same neighborhood of ideas.
