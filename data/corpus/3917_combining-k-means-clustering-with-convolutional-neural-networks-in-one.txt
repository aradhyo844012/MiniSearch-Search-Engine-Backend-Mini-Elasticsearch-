Combining K-Means Clustering with Convolutional Neural Networks in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how K-Means Clustering and Convolutional Neural Networks can work together in a single project.

K-Means Clustering is an algorithm that groups data points into a chosen number of clusters around central points. Convolutional Neural Networks is neural networks that scan images with small filters to detect patterns.

Imagine a project that involves customer segmentation and image classification. K-Means Clustering brings centroids, while Convolutional Neural Networks brings filters.

A sensible plan is to start with scikit-learn for the K-Means Clustering part and PyTorch for the Convolutional Neural Networks part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: unscaled features on one side, and ignoring data augmentation on the other.

Picture the whole as dividing students into study groups around the most central member, working alongside a magnifying glass sweeping across a photo looking for edges and shapes.

Two habits help: first, scale features before clustering; second, start from a pretrained model.
