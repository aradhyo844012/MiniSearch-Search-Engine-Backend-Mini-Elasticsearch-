Compilers and Interpreters vs GPUs and Parallelism: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Compilers and Interpreters or GPUs and Parallelism first. Both sit in the area of Computer Systems, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Compilers and Interpreters is programs that translate or run source code written in a high-level language, and it is closely tied to lexing and parsing. GPUs and Parallelism, by contrast, is processors with thousands of small cores that handle many similar calculations at once, and it is closely tied to threads and blocks and kernels.

Choose Compilers and Interpreters first if you care about running Python and JavaScript or building native programs. Choose GPUs and Parallelism first if you care about training neural networks or rendering graphics.

Think of Compilers and Interpreters as a translator who either translates a whole book first or interprets sentence by sentence, and of GPUs and Parallelism as a stadium of workers each doing one small identical task. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Compilers and Interpreters, avoid assuming interpreted means slow without measuring. With GPUs and Parallelism, avoid slow data transfers.

A good habit for Compilers and Interpreters is to turn on compiler warnings and treat them seriously. A good habit for GPUs and Parallelism is to minimize copies between CPU and GPU memory.
