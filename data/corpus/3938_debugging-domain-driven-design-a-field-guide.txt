Debugging Domain-Driven Design: A Field Guide

When something goes wrong around Domain-Driven Design, resist the urge to guess. Work through the problem in order.

Step 1: reproduce the problem with the smallest possible example.
Step 2: list the usual suspects: using it for simple CRUD apps, models that never match the business, and ignoring context boundaries.
Step 3: revisit ubiquitous language and bounded contexts, since misunderstandings there cause many bugs.
Step 4: use event storming or similar tools to look inside.
Step 5: write a test that fails because of the bug and passes after the fix.

Many bugs involving Domain-Driven Design in work such as complex business systems come down to the first suspect on the list. Check the simple causes before suspecting anything exotic.

Finally, agree on shared terms with domain experts before drawing classes. It prevents a surprising number of repeat problems.
