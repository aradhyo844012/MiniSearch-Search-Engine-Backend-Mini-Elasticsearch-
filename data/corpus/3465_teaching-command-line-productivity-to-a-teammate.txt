Teaching Command Line Productivity to a Teammate

Explaining Command Line Productivity to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: learning the shortcuts on a well-used road. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Command Line Productivity is using shell tools and shortcuts to complete tasks faster. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving automating repetitive tasks, naming aliases and pipes as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain history search in Command Line Productivity back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, copying commands without understanding them, and suggest this habit: turn commands you repeat into small scripts.
