Practice Exercises for Domain-Driven Design

These exercises build real understanding of Domain-Driven Design. Do them in order, and write down your answers.

Exercise 1: Create a small example that deliberately triggers this problem: using it for simple CRUD apps. Then fix it.
Exercise 2: Write a short paragraph describing Domain-Driven Design and include the term ubiquitous language.
Exercise 3: Compare UML and Miro for splitting large systems and write down your conclusion.
Exercise 4: Pick event storming and complete its getting-started tutorial.
Exercise 5: List three situations from your own experience that resemble complex business systems or aligning developers with domain experts.

When you finish, compare your answers with the definition: Domain-Driven Design is an approach to software that models the code around the real business domain.
