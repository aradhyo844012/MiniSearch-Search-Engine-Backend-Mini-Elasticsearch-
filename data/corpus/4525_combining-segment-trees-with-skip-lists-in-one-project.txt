Combining Segment Trees with Skip Lists in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Segment Trees and Skip Lists can work together in a single project.

Segment Trees is a tree structure that answers range queries and handles updates in logarithmic time. Skip Lists is layered linked lists that allow fast searching by skipping over many elements.

Imagine a project that involves range sum queries and ordered in-memory indexes. Segment Trees brings tree nodes covering ranges, while Skip Lists brings multiple levels.

A sensible plan is to start with C++ arrays for the Segment Trees part and Redis sorted sets for the Skip Lists part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: wrong tree size allocation on one side, and memory overhead on the other.

Picture the whole as a tournament bracket that remembers the winner of every group of players, working alongside an express train line layered over a local train line.

Two habits help: first, test your tree against a slow brute-force version on small inputs; second, draw several levels by hand to see how a search moves down.
