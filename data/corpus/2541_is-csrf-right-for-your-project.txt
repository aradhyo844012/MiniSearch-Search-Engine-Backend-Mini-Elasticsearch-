Is CSRF Right for Your Project?

Before adopting CSRF, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that CSRF is an attack that tricks a logged-in user's browser into sending unwanted requests to a site.

It is a good fit when your work involves protecting forms or securing account settings, and when your team understands session cookies.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn anti-CSRF tokens, or when you cannot manage these risks: using GET requests for state changes, skipping tokens on cookie-based APIs, and misconfigured SameSite settings.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether SameSite cookies and state-changing requests are part of the answer. If they are, CSRF is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with hardening admin panels and combine SameSite cookies with anti-CSRF tokens.
