Why Background Jobs Matters for Developers

Developers who understand Background Jobs make better decisions, because Background Jobs is tasks that run outside the request cycle so users do not have to wait for them.

Three situations show why it matters. First, sending emails. Second, resizing images. Third, generating reports. In each case, a solid grasp of queues and workers separates a quick fix from a lasting solution.

Ignoring it has a cost. Teams run into these problems: jobs that are not idempotent, unbounded retries, and losing jobs on restarts.

Good tools exist, including Celery, Sidekiq, and BullMQ, so you rarely have to start from nothing.
