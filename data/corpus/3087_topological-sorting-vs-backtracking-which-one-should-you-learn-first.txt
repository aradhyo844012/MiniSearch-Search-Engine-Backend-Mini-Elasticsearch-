Topological Sorting vs Backtracking: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Topological Sorting or Backtracking first. Both sit in the area of Algorithms in Practice, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Topological Sorting is an ordering of tasks in a directed graph so that every task comes after the tasks it depends on, and it is closely tied to directed acyclic graphs and in-degree counts. Backtracking, by contrast, is a search technique that builds solutions step by step and undoes steps that lead to dead ends, and it is closely tied to decision tree and pruning.

Choose Topological Sorting first if you care about build systems or course prerequisite planning. Choose Backtracking first if you care about solving Sudoku or generating permutations.

Think of Topological Sorting as a recipe that must list every step after the steps it depends on, and of Backtracking as walking a maze and returning to the last fork whenever you hit a wall. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Topological Sorting, avoid ignoring cycles. With Backtracking, avoid forgetting to undo changes.

A good habit for Topological Sorting is to check for cycles first so you can report a clear error. A good habit for Backtracking is to prune as early as possible because the savings multiply.
