Combining Hashing vs Encryption with Threat Modeling in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Hashing vs Encryption and Threat Modeling can work together in a single project.

Hashing vs Encryption is the difference between one-way digests and reversible protection of data. Threat Modeling is a structured way of identifying what could go wrong in a system and how to prevent it.

Imagine a project that involves storing passwords and designing new features. Hashing vs Encryption brings one-way functions, while Threat Modeling brings assets.

A sensible plan is to start with SHA-256 for the Hashing vs Encryption part and STRIDE for the Threat Modeling part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: using broken hash functions on one side, and ignoring data flows on the other.

Picture the whole as a paper shredder compared with a locked suitcase, working alongside walking around a house asking where a burglar might get in.

Two habits help: first, ask whether you will ever need the original value back; second, draw the data flow diagram first.
