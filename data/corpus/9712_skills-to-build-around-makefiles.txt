Skills to Build Around Makefiles

Knowing the definition of Makefiles is only the start. These skills turn that knowledge into something useful.

Foundations: be comfortable with targets and prerequisites, and able to explain them without notes.

Hands-on practice: spend real time with GNU Make on a Makefiles task, and try at least one alternative such as CMake.

Judgment: learn to decide when Makefiles fits a problem such as compiling C projects, and when something simpler is better.

Communication: practice describing it with an everyday picture, for example a to-do list that knows which steps must come first.

Habits: use phony targets for tasks like test and clean, and write down what you learn after each project.
