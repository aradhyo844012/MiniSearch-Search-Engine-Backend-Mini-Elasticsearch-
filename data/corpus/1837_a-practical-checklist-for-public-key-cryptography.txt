A Practical Checklist for Public Key Cryptography

Use this checklist before you rely on Public Key Cryptography in a project.

1. Confirm that your team agrees on what Public Key Cryptography should accomplish.
2. Try the approach on a small case, for example HTTPS.
3. Ask a teammate to review the work, focusing on digital signatures.
4. Guard against this risk: trusting unverified keys.
5. Pick a tool to start with, such as OpenSSL or GnuPG.
6. Guard against this risk: using keys that are too short.
7. Document your decisions in the project README.
8. Guard against this risk: losing private keys.
9. Measure results before and after any change.
