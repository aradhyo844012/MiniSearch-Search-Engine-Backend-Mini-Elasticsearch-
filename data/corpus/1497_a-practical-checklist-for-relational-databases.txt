A Practical Checklist for Relational Databases

Use this checklist before you rely on Relational Databases in a project.

1. Measure results before and after any change.
2. Guard against this risk: missing indexes on filtered columns.
3. Confirm that your team agrees on what Relational Databases should accomplish.
4. Guard against this risk: skipping constraints.
5. Guard against this risk: storing lists in a single column.
6. Try the approach on a small case, for example storing orders and customers.
7. Pick a tool to start with, such as PostgreSQL or MySQL.
8. Review the core ideas: tables and rows, primary keys, foreign keys, and schemas.
