Is CPU Caches Right for Your Project?

Before adopting CPU Caches, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that CPU Caches is small, fast memory close to the processor that stores recently used data.

It is a good fit when your work involves speeding up loops or explaining performance differences, and when your team understands cache lines.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn hit and miss rates, or when you cannot manage these risks: false sharing, poor access patterns, and assuming memory is uniformly fast.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether levels L1 to L3 and locality are part of the answer. If they are, CPU Caches is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with designing data layouts and access arrays in the order they are stored.
