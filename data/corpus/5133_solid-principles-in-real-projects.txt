SOLID Principles in Real Projects

Reading about SOLID Principles is useful, but the ideas make more sense in context. These three scenarios show how it appears in real projects.

Scenario one: structuring classes. Here, single responsibility drives the main design choices, and the team needs to guard against applying them rigidly to tiny scripts.

Scenario two: reviewing designs. This time the open-closed principle matters most, and tools such as UML diagrams help keep things manageable.

Scenario three: reducing coupling. The key idea is Liskov substitution, and the biggest risk is creating too many tiny classes.

Across all three scenarios, SOLID Principles stays the same idea: five guidelines for designing object-oriented code that is easy to change. Only the details change.
