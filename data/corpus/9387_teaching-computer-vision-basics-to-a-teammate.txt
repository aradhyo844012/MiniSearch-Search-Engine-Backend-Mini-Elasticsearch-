Teaching Computer Vision Basics to a Teammate

Explaining Computer Vision Basics to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: teaching a computer to notice shapes the way eyes do. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Computer Vision Basics is teaching computers to extract information from images and video. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving face detection, naming pixels and filters and edges as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain feature detection in Computer Vision Basics back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, lighting changes, and suggest this habit: visualize intermediate steps such as edges or masks.
