Computability and the Halting Problem for Students: A Study Guide

This study guide prepares you for exams, labs and interviews that cover Computability and the Halting Problem.

Learning goals:
1. Explain Computability and the Halting Problem in your own words.
2. Describe Turing machines, decidability, the halting problem, and reductions.
3. Give examples from understanding the limits of tools and proofs of impossibility.
4. Name three problems to watch for.

Start with the definition: Computability and the Halting Problem is the study of what problems computers can and cannot solve. Then draw asking a machine to predict whether any program will ever finish on paper and label the four terms from the goals above.

Self-test questions: Why is assuming everything is solvable a problem? How does decidability relate to the halting problem? When would you choose Turing machine simulators or textbooks?

Study habit: learn the diagonal argument step by step.

When you feel ready, compare Computability and the Halting Problem with Graph Theory Basics and write down three differences.
