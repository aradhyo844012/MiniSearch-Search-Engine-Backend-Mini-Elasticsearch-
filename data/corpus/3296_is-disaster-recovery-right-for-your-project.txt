Is Disaster Recovery Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Disaster Recovery, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Disaster Recovery is the plans and systems that restore services after major failures.

It is a good fit when your work involves surviving regional outages or meeting compliance needs, and when your team understands recovery time objective.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn recovery point objective, or when you cannot manage these risks: untested plans, outdated runbooks, and dependencies missed in the plan.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether failover regions and runbooks are part of the answer. If they are, Disaster Recovery is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with protecting critical data and run a failover exercise on a regular schedule.
