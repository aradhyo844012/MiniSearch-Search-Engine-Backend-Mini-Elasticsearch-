Combining B-Trees on Disk with Time-Series Databases in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how B-Trees on Disk and Time-Series Databases can work together in a single project.

B-Trees on Disk is balanced tree structures designed to read large blocks from disk efficiently. Time-Series Databases is databases optimized for storing and querying data points ordered by time.

Imagine a project that involves database indexes and server metrics. B-Trees on Disk brings pages, while Time-Series Databases brings timestamps.

A sensible plan is to start with PostgreSQL for the B-Trees on Disk part and InfluxDB for the Time-Series Databases part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: oversized keys on one side, and keeping raw data forever on the other.

Picture the whole as a filing cabinet where each drawer tells you which drawer to open next, working alongside a long logbook where every line starts with the time.

Two habits help: first, keep indexed keys short so more of them fit on a page; second, include a time range in every query.
