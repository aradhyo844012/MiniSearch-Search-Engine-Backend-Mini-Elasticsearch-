Connection Pooling for Students: A Study Guide

This study guide prepares you for exams, labs and interviews that cover Connection Pooling.

Learning goals:
1. Explain Connection Pooling in your own words.
2. Describe pool size, idle connections, timeouts, and connection leaks.
3. Give examples from web backends and serverless functions that call databases.
4. Name three problems to watch for.

Start with the definition: Connection Pooling is reusing a limited set of open database connections instead of opening a new one for every request. Then draw a rental desk that lends a few shared bikes instead of buying one per visitor on paper and label the four terms from the goals above.

Self-test questions: Why is pools larger than the database allows a problem? How does idle connections relate to timeouts? When would you choose PgBouncer or HikariCP?

Study habit worth keeping: always return connections in a finally block.

When you feel ready, compare Connection Pooling with Graph Databases and write down three differences.
