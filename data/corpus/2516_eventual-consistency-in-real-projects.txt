Eventual Consistency in Real Projects

Reading about Eventual Consistency is useful, but the ideas make more sense in context. These three scenarios show how it appears in real projects.

Scenario one: global social feeds. Here, replication delay drives the main design choices, and the team needs to guard against assuming reads are always fresh.

Scenario two: shopping cart services. This time conflict resolution matters most, and tools such as Amazon DynamoDB help keep things manageable.

Scenario three: DNS records. The key idea is read-your-writes, and the biggest risk is unresolved write conflicts.

Across all three scenarios, Eventual Consistency stays the same idea: a guarantee that replicas will agree on the same value if updates stop for long enough. Only the details change.
