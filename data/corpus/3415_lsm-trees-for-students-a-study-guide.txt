LSM Trees for Students: A Study Guide

This study guide prepares you for exams, labs and interviews that cover LSM Trees.

Learning goals:
1. Explain LSM Trees in your own words.
2. Describe memtable, sorted string tables, compaction, and write amplification.
3. Give examples from write-heavy databases and time-ordered data.
4. Name three problems to watch for.

Start with the definition: LSM Trees is storage structures that buffer writes in memory and merge them into sorted files on disk. Then draw a mail room that sorts incoming letters in batches rather than one by one on paper and label the four terms from the goals above.

Self-test questions: Why is read amplification a problem? How does sorted string tables relate to compaction? When would you choose RocksDB or LevelDB?

Study habit: watch compaction metrics before blaming the application.

When you feel ready, compare LSM Trees with Column-Oriented Storage and write down three differences.
