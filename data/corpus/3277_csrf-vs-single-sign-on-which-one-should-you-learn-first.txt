CSRF vs Single Sign-On: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study CSRF or Single Sign-On first. Both sit in the area of Application Security, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

CSRF is an attack that tricks a logged-in user's browser into sending unwanted requests to a site, and it is closely tied to session cookies and anti-CSRF tokens. Single Sign-On, by contrast, is letting users log in once and access several applications, and it is closely tied to identity provider and SAML.

Choose CSRF first if you care about protecting forms or securing account settings. Choose Single Sign-On first if you care about company intranets or reducing password fatigue.

Think of CSRF as a forged letter that a bank clerk stamps because your ID is already on file, and of Single Sign-On as a festival wristband that opens every gate. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With CSRF, avoid using GET requests for state changes. With Single Sign-On, avoid a single point of failure.

A good habit for CSRF is to combine SameSite cookies with anti-CSRF tokens. A good habit for Single Sign-On is to plan for identity provider outages.
