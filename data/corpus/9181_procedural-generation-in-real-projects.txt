Procedural Generation in Real Projects

Reading about Procedural Generation is useful, but the ideas make more sense in context. These three scenarios show how it appears in real projects.

Scenario one: roguelike dungeons. Here, random seeds drives the main design choices, and the team needs to guard against levels that are unfair or unwinnable.

Scenario two: infinite terrain. This time noise functions matters most, and tools such as Perlin noise help keep things manageable.

Scenario three: varied item drops. The key idea is rules and constraints, and the biggest risk is repetitive results.

Across all three scenarios, Procedural Generation stays the same idea: creating game content such as levels and terrain automatically with algorithms. Only the details change.
