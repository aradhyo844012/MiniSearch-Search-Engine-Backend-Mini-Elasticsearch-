Combining Hexagonal Architecture with Software Estimation in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Hexagonal Architecture and Software Estimation can work together in a single project.

Hexagonal Architecture is a design that keeps core logic separate from outside systems through ports and adapters. Software Estimation is predicting how much time and effort a piece of work will take.

Imagine a project that involves testable business logic and sprint planning. Hexagonal Architecture brings ports, while Software Estimation brings story points.

A sensible plan is to start with Spring for the Hexagonal Architecture part and planning poker for the Software Estimation part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: leaking framework types into the core on one side, and ignoring unknowns on the other.

Picture the whole as a power strip that lets any plug connect to the same core, working alongside forecasting the weather rather than reading a timetable.

Two habits help: first, keep framework imports out of the core domain; second, give ranges instead of single numbers.
