Combining Random Forests with Reinforcement Learning in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Random Forests and Reinforcement Learning can work together in a single project.

Random Forests is ensembles of many decision trees whose predictions are combined. Reinforcement Learning is a way of training agents to choose actions by rewarding good outcomes.

Imagine a project that involves tabular predictions and game playing. Random Forests brings bagging, while Reinforcement Learning brings agent.

A sensible plan is to start with scikit-learn for the Random Forests part and Gymnasium for the Reinforcement Learning part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: very large forests being slow on one side, and slow training on the other.

Picture the whole as asking many different experts and taking the majority view, working alongside training a dog with treats for good behavior.

Two habits help: first, tune the number of trees only after the basics work; second, start with simple environments.
