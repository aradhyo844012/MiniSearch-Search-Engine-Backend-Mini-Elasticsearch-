Teaching Write-Ahead Logging to a Teammate

Explaining Write-Ahead Logging to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: writing a plan in a notebook before changing the real blueprint. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Write-Ahead Logging is a technique that records changes in a log before they are applied to the main data. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving surviving crashes, naming log records and checkpoints as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain crash recovery in Write-Ahead Logging back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, disabling fsync for speed, and suggest this habit: test crash recovery by killing the process on purpose.
