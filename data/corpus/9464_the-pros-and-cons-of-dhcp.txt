The Pros and Cons of DHCP

No technology is free of trade-offs, and DHCP is no exception. Here is an honest summary.

Advantages:
- Supports home and office networks
- Makes guest Wi-Fi easier
- Helps with booting machines over a network

Drawbacks and risks:
- Risk: rogue DHCP servers
- Risk: exhausted address pools
- Risk: conflicting static addresses

Weigh the two lists against your own project. If the advantages match your needs, such as home and office networks, and you can manage the risks, DHCP is a sound choice. Whatever you decide, reserve fixed addresses for servers and printers.
