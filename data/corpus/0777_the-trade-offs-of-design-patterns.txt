The Trade-offs of Design Patterns

Every design choice has a cost, and Design Patterns is no exception. Many students first meet Design Patterns in a classroom, but it matters just as much in production systems. The term refers to reusable solutions to common problems in software design.

On the positive side, Design Patterns is well suited to structuring applications and decoupling components. On the other hand, it is not free. Keep these risks in mind: forcing patterns where they are unnecessary; over-engineering; copying patterns without understanding the problem. Whether the trade is worth it depends on how much singleton and factory matter to your project.

Three issues come up again and again: forcing patterns where they are unnecessary; over-engineering; and copying patterns without understanding the problem. Knowing them in advance saves hours of debugging.

Write a few tests that show how Design Patterns should behave. Tests double as documentation for your teammates. Ask a teammate to explain observer back to you; teaching is a quick way to find gaps.

With steady practice, Design Patterns will stop feeling abstract and become a tool you reach for naturally.
