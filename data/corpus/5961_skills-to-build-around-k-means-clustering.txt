Skills to Build Around K-Means Clustering

Knowing the definition of K-Means Clustering is only the start. These skills turn that knowledge into something useful.

Foundations: be comfortable with centroids and distance measures, and able to explain them without notes.

Hands-on practice: spend real time with scikit-learn on a K-Means Clustering task, and try at least one alternative such as NumPy.

Judgment: learn to decide when K-Means Clustering fits a problem such as customer segmentation, and when something simpler is better.

Communication: practice describing it with an everyday picture, for example dividing students into study groups around the most central member.

Habits: scale features before clustering, and write down what you learn after each project.
