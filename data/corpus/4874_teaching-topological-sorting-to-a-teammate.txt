Teaching Topological Sorting to a Teammate

Explaining Topological Sorting to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: a recipe that must list every step after the steps it depends on. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Topological Sorting is an ordering of tasks in a directed graph so that every task comes after the tasks it depends on. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving build systems, naming directed acyclic graphs and in-degree counts as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain Kahn's algorithm in Topological Sorting back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, ignoring cycles, and suggest this habit: check for cycles first so you can report a clear error.
