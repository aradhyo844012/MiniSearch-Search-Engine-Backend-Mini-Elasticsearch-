Hexagonal Architecture Explained Simply

Here is Hexagonal Architecture without the jargon: it is a design that keeps core logic separate from outside systems through ports and adapters.

Think of Hexagonal Architecture as a power strip that lets any plug connect to the same core. Keep that picture in mind as you read on.

The vocabulary has four main pieces: ports, adapters, the core domain, and dependency direction. You do not need to master them all at once. Start with ports, then add adapters.

You will meet Hexagonal Architecture in work such as testable business logic, swapping databases, and supporting several interfaces.

One practical tip is to keep framework imports out of the core domain.

Once Hexagonal Architecture feels comfortable, read about Event-Driven Architecture, which lives in the same neighborhood of ideas.
