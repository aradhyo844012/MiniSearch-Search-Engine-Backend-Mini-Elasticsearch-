Teaching NAT to a Teammate

Explaining NAT to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: a receptionist who forwards calls for many desks through one phone number. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: NAT is a technique that lets many devices share one public IP address by rewriting addresses. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving home routers, naming private addresses and translation tables as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain port mapping in NAT back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, breaking peer-to-peer connections, and suggest this habit: document every port forward you create.
