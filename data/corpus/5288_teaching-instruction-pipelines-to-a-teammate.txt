Teaching Instruction Pipelines to a Teammate

Explaining Instruction Pipelines to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: an assembly line where several cars are in different stages at once. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Instruction Pipelines is a technique where a processor overlaps the stages of several instructions. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving explaining CPU speed, naming fetch, decode and execute stages and hazards as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain branch prediction in Instruction Pipelines back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, unpredictable branches, and suggest this habit: avoid unpredictable branches in hot loops.
