Domain-Driven Design vs Modular Monoliths: Which One Should You Learn First?

Learners often ask whether to study Domain-Driven Design or Modular Monoliths first. Both sit in the area of Software Design, so the honest answer is that it depends on your goals.

Domain-Driven Design is an approach to software that models the code around the real business domain, and it is closely tied to ubiquitous language and bounded contexts. Modular Monoliths, by contrast, is single deployable applications built from well-separated internal modules, and it is closely tied to module boundaries and public interfaces.

Choose Domain-Driven Design first if you care about complex business systems or aligning developers with domain experts. Choose Modular Monoliths first if you care about small teams or early-stage products.

Think of Domain-Driven Design as writing software in the same vocabulary the business speaks, and of Modular Monoliths as an apartment building with separate flats under one roof. The two pictures are very different, which is a useful reminder that the topics solve different problems.

Both have traps. With Domain-Driven Design, avoid using it for simple CRUD apps. With Modular Monoliths, avoid modules reaching into each other's tables.

A good habit for Domain-Driven Design is to agree on shared terms with domain experts before drawing classes. A good habit for Modular Monoliths is to enforce boundaries with automated checks.
