Debugging NAT: A Field Guide

When something goes wrong around NAT, resist the urge to guess. Work through the problem in order.

Step 1: reproduce the problem with the smallest possible example.
Step 2: list the usual suspects: breaking peer-to-peer connections, exhausted translation tables, and misconfigured port forwarding.
Step 3: revisit private addresses and translation tables, since misunderstandings there cause many bugs.
Step 4: use iptables or similar tools to look inside.
Step 5: write a test that fails because of the bug and passes after the fix.

Many bugs involving NAT in work such as home routers come down to the first suspect on the list. Check the simple causes before suspecting anything exotic.

Finally, document every port forward you create. It prevents a surprising number of repeat problems.
