The Future of Query Optimization

Predicting the future is risky, but the direction of Query Optimization is easy to describe in general terms. When people talk about Query Optimization, they usually mean the process of making database queries return results with less time and fewer resources. It appears in almost every serious software project, whether or not developers notice it.

Good schema design is hard to change once real data and users depend on it. Most slow pages in data-driven applications trace back to a handful of slow queries.

Expect tools such as EXPLAIN ANALYZE and pg_stat_statements to keep improving, and expect newer ideas to keep building on query plans. The basics, though, tend to stay the same, so time spent on statistics and index usage is rarely wasted. Anyone working on speeding up dashboards will keep meeting Query Optimization for years to come.

Keep an eye on this risk during code review: selecting more columns than needed. Ask a teammate to explain index usage back to you; teaching is a quick way to find gaps.

Keep experimenting, keep notes, and revisit Query Optimization whenever a new project gives you a reason.
