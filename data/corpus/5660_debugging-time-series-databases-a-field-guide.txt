Debugging Time-Series Databases: A Field Guide

When something goes wrong around Time-Series Databases, resist the urge to guess. Work through the problem in order.

Step 1: reproduce the problem with the smallest possible example.
Step 2: list the usual suspects: high-cardinality tags, keeping raw data forever, and querying without time bounds.
Step 3: revisit timestamps and retention policies, since misunderstandings there cause many bugs.
Step 4: use InfluxDB or similar tools to look inside.
Step 5: write a test that fails because of the bug and passes after the fix.

Many bugs involving Time-Series Databases in work such as server metrics come down to the first suspect on the list. Check the simple causes before suspecting anything exotic.

Finally, include a time range in every query. It prevents a surprising number of repeat problems.
