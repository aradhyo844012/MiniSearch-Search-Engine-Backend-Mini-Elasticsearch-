Teaching Boolean Logic and Gates to a Teammate

Explaining Boolean Logic and Gates to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: light switches wired in series or in parallel. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Boolean Logic and Gates is the basic true-or-false operations that digital circuits and program conditions are built from. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving writing conditions, naming AND, OR and NOT and truth tables as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain De Morgan's laws in Boolean Logic and Gates back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, operator precedence mistakes, and suggest this habit: write a truth table whenever a condition feels confusing.
