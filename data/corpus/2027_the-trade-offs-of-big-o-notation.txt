The Trade-offs of Big O Notation

Every design choice has a cost, and Big O Notation is no exception. Put simply, Big O Notation covers a way of describing how running time or memory grows as the input size grows. Learning it well pays off across many areas of computing.

On the positive side, Big O Notation is well suited to comparing algorithms and predicting scaling problems. On the other hand, it is not free. Keep these risks in mind: ignoring constant factors entirely; mixing up best and worst case; measuring only tiny inputs. Whether the trade is worth it depends on how much upper bounds and constant, linear and logarithmic growth matter to your project.

Three issues come up again and again: ignoring constant factors entirely; mixing up best and worst case; and measuring only tiny inputs. Knowing them in advance saves hours of debugging.
