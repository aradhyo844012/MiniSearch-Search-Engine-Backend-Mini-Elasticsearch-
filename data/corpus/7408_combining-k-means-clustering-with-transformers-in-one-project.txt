Combining K-Means Clustering with Transformers in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how K-Means Clustering and Transformers can work together in a single project.

K-Means Clustering is an algorithm that groups data points into a chosen number of clusters around central points. Transformers is neural network architectures that use attention to relate all parts of an input to each other.

Imagine a project that involves customer segmentation and language translation. K-Means Clustering brings centroids, while Transformers brings self-attention.

A sensible plan is to start with scikit-learn for the K-Means Clustering part and Hugging Face for the Transformers part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: unscaled features on one side, and limited context windows on the other.

Picture the whole as dividing students into study groups around the most central member, working alongside a reader who can look back at any word of a paragraph while reading each new word.

Two habits help: first, scale features before clustering; second, begin with small pretrained models.
