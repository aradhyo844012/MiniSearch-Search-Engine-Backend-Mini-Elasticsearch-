Computability and the Halting Problem Quick Reference

A one-screen reminder for Computability and the Halting Problem. Bookmark it and return when the details fade.

What it is: the study of what problems computers can and cannot solve.
Key terms: Turing machines, decidability, the halting problem, and reductions.
Typical uses: understanding the limits of tools, proofs of impossibility, and theory courses.
Risks: assuming everything is solvable, confusing hard with undecidable, and hand-wavy proofs.
Tools: Turing machine simulators, textbooks, and proof sketches.

Mental picture: asking a machine to predict whether any program will ever finish.

Habit to build: learn the diagonal argument step by step.

See also: Floating-Point Arithmetic, which sits close to Computability and the Halting Problem in the area of Mathematics for Computing.
