Teaching Logging Best Practices to a Teammate

Explaining Logging Best Practices to someone else is one of the best ways to test your own understanding. Use this simple outline.

Start with a picture: a ship's logbook that explains what happened and when. Ask your teammate what the picture suggests.

Then give the plain definition: Logging Best Practices is recording useful, structured information about what an application is doing. Avoid jargon at this stage.

Walk through an example involving debugging production issues, naming log levels and structured logs as they come up.

Ask your teammate to explain correlation IDs in Logging Best Practices back to you. Gaps in their explanation show you what to clarify.

Finish by pointing out a trap, logging sensitive data, and suggest this habit: include a request ID in every log line.
