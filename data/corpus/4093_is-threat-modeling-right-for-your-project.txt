Is Threat Modeling Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Threat Modeling, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Threat Modeling is a structured way of identifying what could go wrong in a system and how to prevent it.

It is a good fit when your work involves designing new features or reviewing architecture, and when your team understands assets.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn attackers, or when you cannot manage these risks: doing it once and never again, ignoring data flows, and focusing only on technical threats.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether trust boundaries and mitigations are part of the answer. If they are, Threat Modeling is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with prioritizing security work and draw the data flow diagram first.
