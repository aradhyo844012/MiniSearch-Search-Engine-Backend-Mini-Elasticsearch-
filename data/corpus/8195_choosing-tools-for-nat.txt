Choosing Tools for NAT

There is no single best tool for NAT. The right choice depends on your project, your team and your timeline.

iptables is a common first choice, especially for home routers. router admin pages suits teams that already work with similar technology, and conntrack is worth a look when you need something different.

Judge any tool for NAT by four questions: Is the documentation clear? Is the community active? Does it handle sharing limited IPv4 addresses well? Can you leave it later if you need to?

Whichever tool you pick for NAT, test it on a small problem first, and watch for this: breaking peer-to-peer connections.

As a rule of thumb, document every port forward you create, no matter which tool you use.
