Debugging Write-Ahead Logging: A Field Guide

When something goes wrong around Write-Ahead Logging, resist the urge to guess. Work through the problem in order.

Step 1: reproduce the problem with the smallest possible example.
Step 2: list the usual suspects: disabling fsync for speed, log files filling the disk, and forgetting to archive logs.
Step 3: revisit log records and checkpoints, since misunderstandings there cause many bugs.
Step 4: use PostgreSQL WAL or similar tools to look inside.
Step 5: write a test that fails because of the bug and passes after the fix.

Many bugs involving Write-Ahead Logging in work such as surviving crashes come down to the first suspect on the list. Check the simple causes before suspecting anything exotic.

Finally, test crash recovery by killing the process on purpose. It prevents a surprising number of repeat problems.
