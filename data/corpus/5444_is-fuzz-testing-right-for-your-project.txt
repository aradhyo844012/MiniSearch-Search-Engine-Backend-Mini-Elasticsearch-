Is Fuzz Testing Right for Your Project?

Before adopting Fuzz Testing, ask whether it fits the problem. Remember that Fuzz Testing is feeding programs random or malformed input to uncover crashes and security bugs.

It is a good fit when your work involves testing parsers or finding memory bugs, and when your team understands input generation.

Be cautious when the project is very small, when deadlines leave no room to learn coverage guidance, or when you cannot manage these risks: running without sanitizers, crashes that cannot be reproduced, and ignoring slow inputs.

A simple decision test: write down the problem in one sentence, then check whether crash triage and corpora are part of the answer. If they are, Fuzz Testing is probably worth a closer look.

If you decide to go ahead, start small with security research and keep every crashing input as a regression test.
