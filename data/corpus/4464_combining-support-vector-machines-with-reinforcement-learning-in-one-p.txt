Combining Support Vector Machines with Reinforcement Learning in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Support Vector Machines and Reinforcement Learning can work together in a single project.

Support Vector Machines is models that classify data by finding the boundary with the widest margin between classes. Reinforcement Learning is a way of training agents to choose actions by rewarding good outcomes.

Imagine a project that involves text classification and game playing. Support Vector Machines brings margin, while Reinforcement Learning brings agent.

A sensible plan is to start with scikit-learn for the Support Vector Machines part and Gymnasium for the Reinforcement Learning part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: poor kernel choices on one side, and slow training on the other.

Picture the whole as drawing the widest road between two neighborhoods, working alongside training a dog with treats for good behavior.

Two habits help: first, scale features and try a linear kernel first; second, start with simple environments.
