NoSQL Databases vs Database Replication: Key Differences

NoSQL Databases and Database Replication both belong to the area of Databases, but they solve different problems. This article compares them side by side so you can choose with confidence.

The term NoSQL Databases refers to a broad family of databases that do not use the traditional relational table model. Its core ideas are document stores, key-value stores, and column families.

The term Database Replication refers to copying data from one database server to others to improve availability and read capacity. Its core ideas are primary and replica, replication lag, and failover.

The main difference is focus. NoSQL Databases puts the emphasis on document stores and key-value stores, while Database Replication puts the emphasis on primary and replica and replication lag.

Reach for NoSQL Databases when your task involves flexible schemas or high write throughput. Reach for Database Replication when your task involves surviving server failures or scaling read traffic.

Both have risks. With NoSQL Databases, watch for this: assuming no schema means no design. With Database Replication, watch for this: reading stale data from replicas.

In practice you do not always have to choose. Many projects use NoSQL Databases alongside Database Replication, with tools such as MongoDB and PostgreSQL streaming replication. Backups are only useful if restoring from them has been tested.
