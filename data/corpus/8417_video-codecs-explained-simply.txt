Video Codecs Explained Simply

If Video Codecs sounds intimidating, relax. It is programs that compress and decompress video so it can be stored and streamed, and the idea is simpler than the name suggests.

Think of Video Codecs as a flip book that only redraws what changed between pages. Keep that picture in mind as you read on.

The vocabulary has four main pieces: frames, motion compensation, bitrate, and containers. You do not need to master them all at once. Start with frames, then add motion compensation.

You will meet Video Codecs in work such as streaming services, video calls, and archiving footage.

One practical tip is to test playback on the weakest target device.

Once Video Codecs feels comfortable, read about Ray Tracing, which lives in the same neighborhood of ideas.
