Combining Encryption at Rest with Dependency Vulnerabilities in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how Encryption at Rest and Dependency Vulnerabilities can work together in a single project.

Encryption at Rest is protecting stored data by encrypting it on disk or in a database. Dependency Vulnerabilities is security weaknesses that come from third-party libraries a project uses.

Imagine a project that involves protecting backups and auditing projects. Encryption at Rest brings symmetric keys, while Dependency Vulnerabilities brings CVE identifiers.

A sensible plan is to start with LUKS for the Encryption at Rest part and Dependabot for the Dependency Vulnerabilities part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: no key rotation on one side, and never updating on the other.

Picture the whole as a locked safe inside a locked room, working alongside checking every ingredient supplier for recalls.

Two habits help: first, keep keys in a separate service from the data; second, automate update pull requests.
