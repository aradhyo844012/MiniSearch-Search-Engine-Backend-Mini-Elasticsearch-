Kubernetes vs Cloud Storage: Key Differences

Kubernetes and Cloud Storage both belong to the area of Cloud and DevOps, but they solve different problems. This article compares them side by side so you can choose with confidence.

The term Kubernetes refers to a system for deploying, scaling and managing containerized applications. Its core ideas are pods, deployments, and services.

The term Cloud Storage refers to storing files and objects on remote servers that scale on demand. Its core ideas are buckets, objects, and access policies.

The main difference is focus. Kubernetes puts the emphasis on pods and deployments, while Cloud Storage puts the emphasis on buckets and objects.

Reach for Kubernetes when your task involves running many containers or rolling updates. Reach for Cloud Storage when your task involves backups or serving images.

Both have risks. With Kubernetes, watch for this: resource limits missing. With Cloud Storage, watch for this: public buckets by mistake.

In practice you do not always have to choose. Many projects use Kubernetes alongside Cloud Storage, with tools such as kubectl and Amazon S3. A system that cannot be observed cannot be reliably operated.
