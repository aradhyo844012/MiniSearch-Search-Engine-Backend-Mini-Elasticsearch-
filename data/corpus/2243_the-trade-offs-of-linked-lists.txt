The Trade-offs of Linked Lists

Every design choice has a cost, and Linked Lists is no exception. Many students first meet Linked Lists in a classroom, but it matters just as much in production systems. The term refers to chains of nodes where each node stores a value and a reference to the next node.

On the positive side, Linked Lists is well suited to queues and stacks and undo histories. On the other hand, it is not free. Keep these risks in mind: losing the head pointer; off-by-one errors during insertion; poor cache locality. Whether the trade is worth it depends on how much nodes and head and tail pointers matter to your project.

Three issues come up again and again: losing the head pointer; off-by-one errors during insertion; and poor cache locality. Knowing them in advance saves hours of debugging.

Keep an eye on this risk during code review: losing the head pointer. Automate repetitive steps so that results are easy to reproduce.

None of this needs special hardware or expensive software. A laptop, curiosity and a few small experiments are enough to get comfortable with Linked Lists.
