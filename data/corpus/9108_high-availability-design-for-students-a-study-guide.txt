High Availability Design for Students: A Study Guide

This study guide prepares you for exams, labs and interviews that cover High Availability Design.

Learning goals:
1. Explain High Availability Design in your own words.
2. Describe redundancy, failover, health checks, and no single point of failure.
3. Give examples from online stores and banking systems.
4. Name three problems to watch for.

Start with the definition: High Availability Design is designing systems so they keep working when parts fail. Then draw a bridge with several cables so one breaking does not matter on paper and label the four terms from the goals above.

Self-test questions: Why is hidden single points of failure a problem? How does failover relate to health checks? When would you choose load balancers or Kubernetes?

Study habit worth keeping: list every component and ask what happens if it dies.

When you feel ready, compare High Availability Design with Cloud-Native Applications and write down three differences.
