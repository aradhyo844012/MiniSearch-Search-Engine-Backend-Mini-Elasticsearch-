Threat Modeling Quick Reference

A one-screen reminder for Threat Modeling. Bookmark it and return when the details fade.

What it is: a structured way of identifying what could go wrong in a system and how to prevent it.
Key terms: assets, attackers, trust boundaries, and mitigations.
Typical uses: designing new features, reviewing architecture, and prioritizing security work.
Risks: doing it once and never again, ignoring data flows, and focusing only on technical threats.
Tools: STRIDE, OWASP Threat Dragon, and data flow diagrams.

Mental picture: walking around a house asking where a burglar might get in.

Habit to build: draw the data flow diagram first.

See also: Penetration Testing, which sits close to Threat Modeling in the area of Application Security.
