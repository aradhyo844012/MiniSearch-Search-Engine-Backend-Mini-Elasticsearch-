Mistakes to Avoid with Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Everyone learns Retrieval-Augmented Generation partly through mistakes. Here are three worth avoiding.

Mistake one: poor chunking. It usually appears when someone skips retrieval and jumps straight to implementation. The fix is to slow down and review the basics.

Mistake two: irrelevant retrieved text. It tends to be noticed late, often during testing of company knowledge assistants. A short checklist during review catches it early.

Mistake three: no evaluation of the answers. It is easy to miss because everything seems to work at first. Keep an eye on grounding to spot it.

If you remember only one rule, make it this: evaluate retrieval quality separately from answer quality.
