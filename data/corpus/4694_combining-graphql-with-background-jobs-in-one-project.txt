Combining GraphQL with Background Jobs in One Project

Real projects rarely use one idea in isolation. This article shows how GraphQL and Background Jobs can work together in a single project.

GraphQL is a query language for APIs that lets clients ask for exactly the fields they need. Background Jobs is tasks that run outside the request cycle so users do not have to wait for them.

Imagine a project that involves mobile apps with limited bandwidth and sending emails. GraphQL brings schema, while Background Jobs brings queues.

A sensible plan is to start with Apollo for the GraphQL part and Celery for the Background Jobs part, and to keep the two sides connected through a small, well-tested interface.

Be prepared for combined risks: unbounded query depth on one side, and unbounded retries on the other.

Picture the whole as ordering a la carte instead of picking a fixed set menu, working alongside dropping laundry at a service and collecting it later.

Two habits help: first, use a data loader to avoid N+1 queries; second, give every job a timeout and a retry limit.
