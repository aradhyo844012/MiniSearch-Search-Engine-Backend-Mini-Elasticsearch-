LSM Trees in 10 Minutes

You have ten minutes, so here is the fast route through LSM Trees.

Minutes 1 and 2, the definition: LSM Trees is storage structures that buffer writes in memory and merge them into sorted files on disk.

Minutes 3 and 4, the vocabulary: learn memtable, sorted string tables, compaction, and write amplification.

Minutes 5 and 6, the picture: imagine a mail room that sorts incoming letters in batches rather than one by one. That is the intuition.

Minutes 7 and 8, the use cases: skim through write-heavy databases, time-ordered data, and key-value stores.

Minutes 9 and 10, the traps and the tools: watch for read amplification and compaction stalls, then try RocksDB for a first experiment.

If you have a spare minute, remember to watch compaction metrics before blaming the application.
