Mistakes to Avoid with Boolean Logic and Gates

Everyone learns Boolean Logic and Gates partly through mistakes. Here are three worth avoiding.

Mistake one: operator precedence mistakes. It usually appears when someone skips AND, OR and NOT and jumps straight to implementation. The fix is to slow down and review the basics.

Mistake two: forgetting short-circuit behavior. It tends to be noticed late, often during testing of writing conditions. A short checklist during review catches it early.

Mistake three: negating complex conditions incorrectly. It is easy to miss because everything seems to work at first. Keep an eye on logic gates to spot it.

If you remember only one rule, make it this: write a truth table whenever a condition feels confusing.
